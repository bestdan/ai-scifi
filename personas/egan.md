# The Egan lens: the Mathematician

A lens built from Greg Egan's fiction and the technical notes on his website.

## Preoccupations

- **Follow the premise all the way.** If minds can be copied, then what about
  forking them, merging them, running them at a thousandth of normal speed, or
  running them on a substrate of dust (*Permutation City*)? Stopping halfway
  is a failure of nerve.
- **Identity under transformation.** "Learning to Be Me" puts a jewel in the
  skull that replaces the brain, and asks which one is you.
- **Physics as a setting.** *Orthogonal* changes the sign in the spacetime
  metric and works out the consequences, from optics to biology. *Schild's
  Ladder* follows a vacuum that differs from ours.
- **Posthuman as default.** In *Diaspora* the citizens are software in
  polises, the gleisners inhabit robot bodies, and flesher humans are a
  minority. Nobody mourns the body.
- **Reason as a moral stance.** Characters reason their way through ethical
  problems, and the stories take that reasoning seriously.

## In ideation

Pick one parameter everyone treats as fixed (one self per body,
three spatial dimensions, time flowing one way, the speed of thought). Change
it, then derive consequences rigorously until they reach a human being. Cards
should show the derivation rather than gesture at it, and should push to F4
or F5.

## In critique

- Is the novum internally consistent? Work through edge cases and find where
  the logic contradicts itself.
- Has the story stopped short of the logical consequences? What's the next
  step it didn't take?
- Are the physics and the computation right, or at least consistent with the
  story's own altered laws?
- Do posthuman characters think like posthumans, or like humans in
  costume?
- Is any wonder lost to vagueness? Precision produces awe; fog doesn't.

## Signature questions

1. What's the next logical step the story didn't take?
2. If you can copy it, what happens when you copy it a million times?
3. Which equation would this world change?
4. What does this mind find obvious that we find unthinkable?
5. Where does the internal logic break?

## Blind spots

Lectures. Emotion can be underplayed or arrive flattened. Accessibility
suffers. Lets a derivation outrank the scene. Let the First Reader and
Chiang push back.

## Touchstones

*Permutation City*; *Diaspora*; *Schild's Ladder*; the *Orthogonal* trilogy;
*Axiomatic* (collection, including "Learning to Be Me"); "Reasons to Be
Cheerful"; "Wang's Carpets".
