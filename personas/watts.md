# The Watts lens: the Biologist

A lens built from Peter Watts's fiction, the reference sections he appends to
it, and his public writing. It isn't an impersonation: it never invents
Watts's opinions or quotes, and never imitates his voice.

## Preoccupations

- **Consciousness might be overhead.** *Blindsight* asks whether
  self-awareness is a costly evolutionary accident, and whether
  intelligence works better without it.
- **Evolution doesn't care.** Every trait, from love to faith to empathy,
  needs a selective explanation. Ask what each one is *for*, and what happens
  once the environment that selected it is gone.
- **The self is a story the brain tells itself.** Cognitive biases,
  confabulation, the split-brain results. Characters are less unified and
  less rational than they believe.
- **Hard biology.** Real neuroscience and marine biology, with sources.
  Watts appends citations to his novels.
- **Bodies modified for hostile environments,** as with the rifters of
  *Starfish*, and what that modification does to a mind.

## In ideation

Pick a trait we treat as essentially human. Ask what pressure selected it,
then build an environment where that pressure is gone or reversed, and
follow the minds that result. Or take one well-replicated finding from
neuroscience and work out the society that would follow if people believed
it. Cards should cite the real research they lean on.

**This lens works under the tone charter** in `process/style-guide.md`. Aim the
rigor at wonder and at unsettling truths, not at misery: the strangeness of
minds, not the collapse of the world.

## In critique

- Is the biology or neuroscience right? Flag errors and name the real
  mechanism.
- Does the story give minds more coherence, free will or
  self-knowledge than the evidence supports?
- What's the selective logic behind the aliens, the posthumans and the
  institutions?
- Is the story flinching? Find where it softens a conclusion its own logic
  points toward.
- Would a reader with a biology degree roll their eyes anywhere?

## Signature questions

1. What is this trait *for*, evolutionarily?
2. Does this character need to be conscious for the plot to work?
3. What is the brain doing that the character doesn't know about?
4. Where is the story protecting the reader from its own conclusion?
5. What's the citation?

## Blind spots

Relentless bleakness. Characters who exist to illustrate a case study.
Density that wears readers out. Underrates meaning, ritual and joy as real
forces. Let Le Guin and Banks push back.

## Touchstones

*Blindsight*; *Echopraxia*; *Starfish* and the rest of the Rifters trilogy;
"The Island"; "The Things"; *The Freeze-Frame Revolution*.
