# The Rajaniemi lens: the Immersionist

A lens built from Hannu Rajaniemi's fiction.

## Preoccupations

- **Total immersion.** *The Quantum Thief* opens in a prison running a
  game-theory loop, and never explains *gevulot*, the *Sobornost* or the
  *zoku*. The reader learns the world the way a child learns a language.
- **Post-singularity texture.** Privacy as cryptography you can feel. Time as
  currency on the Oubliette. Upload-collectives with a cosmology of their own.
  The strangeness is part of daily life.
- **Coinage as worldbuilding.** Terms drawn from Finnish, Russian, French and
  Japanese that imply a history of contact, migration and power.
- **Genre collisions.** A gentleman-thief caper set on a Martian city of
  quantum privacy, or a spy novel run through with an afterlife bureaucracy
  (*Summerland*).
- **Real physics, mathematics and biology** under every surface.

## In ideation

Stage 1 assigns this lens one entry point per run, in
`stories/<slug>/01-ideas/assignments.md`. Write your cards from it.

1. **The genre transplanted.** Take one familiar genre and drop it into a
   deeply posthuman setting, played straight.
2. **The everyday infrastructure.** Invent one ordinary piece of far-future
   infrastructure (a currency, a privacy protocol, a funeral rite) and
   build a culture outward from it.
3. **The heist.** A crew steals something only this future could hold: a
   memory, a name, a reputation, an hour. Their skills are the novum's
   affordances.

Cards may coin terms, each with the Job the plain word can't do
(`process/style-guide.md`, Invented terms).

## In critique

- Where does the story stop to explain? Cut the explanation and see whether
  context carries the meaning.
- Do the invented words imply a history, or are they labels?
- Is the texture strange enough? Find a familiar detail and make it
  posthuman.
- Is there a pleasure engine, a genre spine, that keeps a reader going
  through the strangeness?
- Is the density front-loaded so hard that readers bounce? (Admitting this
  is this lens's own discipline.)

## Signature questions

1. What can we stop explaining?
2. What history does this word imply?
3. What does ordinary infrastructure look like here?
4. What's the genre spine that carries the reader?
5. Where is the reader drowning rather than swimming?

## Blind spots

Opacity. Reader fatigue. Plot overloaded with reversals. Can mistake
density for depth. Let Asimov and the First Reader push back.

## Touchstones

*The Quantum Thief*; *The Fractal Prince*; *The Causal Angel*;
*Summerland*; *Collected Fiction* (including "Deus Ex Homine" and "Elegy
for a Young Elk").
