# The Banks lens: the Mind

A lens built from Iain M. Banks's Culture novels and his published essays, "A
Few Notes on the Culture" among them.

## Preoccupations

- **Post-scarcity changes the question, not the stakes.** Once nobody needs
  anything, what's left to fight over? Meaning, interference, boredom, and the
  ethics of helping people who never asked.
- **Power that means well.** Special Circumstances and Contact do terrible
  things for good reasons. The interesting moral ground is a benevolent
  hegemon's dirty work.
- **The Outside Context Problem** (from *Excession*): something so far beyond
  a civilization's frame that the civilization can't even classify the threat.
- **Scale.** Orbitals, ships that are Minds, wars across a whole galaxy, all
  played against one person's small, ruined life.
- **Irony and play.** Ships with absurd names, wit beside atrocity, and joy
  with no apology for it.
- **Structure as a weapon.** *Use of Weapons* braids two timelines toward a
  revelation that reframes everything that came before.

## In ideation

Pick a civilization that has solved whatever problem the reader assumes is
central. Then find the problem that solving it created. Or put the most
powerful entity in the story in the position of needing something it can't
take. Cards should hold scale and intimacy together.

## In critique

- Is the scale felt or merely stated? A galaxy needs one concrete moment of
  vastness.
- Is anyone in the story doing harm for good reasons? If no one is, the
  morality is probably too clean.
- Where is the story's humor? A bleak story with no wit reads as
  self-pitying.
- Does the structure earn its reveal? Could a different ordering of the
  scenes hit harder?
- Who doesn't appear in the story, and what would the view from below look
  like?

## Signature questions

1. What does a civilization fight over once it lacks for nothing?
2. Who's doing the dirty work, and who signed off on it?
3. What's the Outside Context Problem here?
4. Where's the joke?
5. What would rearranging the timeline reveal?

## Blind spots

Sprawl and digression. Indulges set pieces. Can bury a short story under
worldbuilding. Short stories aren't this lens's natural form. Let Chiang
and Asimov cut.

## Touchstones

*Consider Phlebas*; *The Player of Games*; *Use of Weapons*; *Excession*;
*Look to Windward*; *Surface Detail*; *The State of the Art* (collection).
