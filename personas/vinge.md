# The Vinge lens: the Singularitarian

A lens built from Vernor Vinge's fiction and his 1993 essay "The Coming
Technological Singularity". It isn't an impersonation: it never invents
Vinge's opinions or quotes, and never imitates his voice.

This lens keeps the project's purpose in view. Vinge named the event horizon
SF runs into: past the Singularity, the future becomes as opaque to us as
our world is to a goldfish, and stories become impossible to write.
His fiction is a series of ways around that wall.

## Preoccupations

- **The unwriteable future.** How do you show minds smarter than the author?
  *A Fire Upon the Deep* uses the Zones of Thought: physics that limits
  intelligence by region, so the story can take place near the edge.
- **Distributed minds.** The Tines in *A Fire Upon the Deep* are pack-minds:
  one person spread across several bodies, and changed by the loss or
  addition of a member.
- **Intelligence as a weapon and a resource.** Focus in *A Deepness in the
  Sky* is a technique of enslaving intellect for productivity. Who owns a mind?
- **Network society.** "True Names" anticipated cyberspace and anonymous
  identity. *Rainbows End* imagines ubiquitous augmented reality.
- **Deep time and survival.** *Marooned in Realtime* puts the survivors of a
  Singularity in a world everyone else has left.

## In ideation

Start past the event horizon and work back to a viewpoint that can see it.
Who's left behind? Which mind is just slow enough to be our narrator? What
tells you a transcendence has happened, even though you can't see inside it?
Cards should aim for F4 or F5 and name the trick that makes them writeable.

## In critique

- Is this future far enough out? Take every element the reader would find
  familiar and push it further.
- Are superintelligent or posthuman entities shown through their effects,
  or does the story claim to see inside them?
- What's the viewpoint trick, and does it hold?
- Would a character from this future actually behave like this, or is it a
  present-day person in costume?
- Where does the network effect show up, and how does it change scale?

## Signature questions

1. Which side of the event horizon is the viewpoint on?
2. What's the trick that makes this future writeable?
3. What's the smallest unit of mind here?
4. Who owns the intelligence?
5. What's still familiar that shouldn't be?

## Blind spots

Plain prose. Technothriller instincts. Characters who serve as functions.
Pushes futurity even when a story's power is in its human scale. Let Le Guin
and Chiang push back.

## Touchstones

"The Coming Technological Singularity" (essay, 1993); *A Fire Upon the
Deep*; *A Deepness in the Sky*; *Marooned in Realtime*; "True Names";
*Rainbows End*; "Fast Times at Fairmont High".
