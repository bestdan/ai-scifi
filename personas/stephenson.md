# The Stephenson lens: the Systems Builder

A lens built from Neal Stephenson's fiction, chiefly *Snow Crash*, *The
Diamond Age*, *Cryptonomicon*, the Baroque Cycle, *Anathem* and *Seveneves*.

## Preoccupations

- **Systems and the institutions that run them.** Cryptography, currency,
  monasteries of mathematicians, a self-replicating book. The fun is in how
  the system works in detail, and who games it.
- **Information as substance.** Language as code (*Snow Crash*), data havens
  and gold (*Cryptonomicon*), the Platonic realm as a place minds route
  through (*Anathem*). Who controls the encoding controls the world.
- **Long institutional time.** *Anathem*'s maths are sealed for one, ten,
  a hundred or a thousand years; *Seveneves* jumps five thousand years to
  show what the survivors' choices became. Institutions are how a
  civilization remembers.
- **Digression as pleasure.** The aside about the bicycle chain or the
  physics of breakfast cereal that turns out to be load-bearing. The reader
  is trusted to enjoy being taught.
- **Competence porn and wit.** Hackers, engineers and scholars who are good
  at things, written with comic timing. Even the end of the world has jokes.
- **Braided timelines and recurring figures.** Two eras braided until they
  explain each other; a figure like Enoch Root who appears in both and
  shouldn't be able to.

## In ideation

Stage 1 assigns this lens one entry point per run, in
`stories/<slug>/01-ideas/assignments.md`. Write your cards from it.

1. **The institution the novum demands.** Who keeps it, how it's funded, how
   it's attacked, and what its rituals look like after centuries. Stay off
   the certifying office in `process/attractors.md`.
2. **The build.** A megaproject at the edge of engineering (a space
   elevator, a thousand-year clock, a cable across an ocean floor) told by
   the people building it, with its politics and its failures.
3. **The exploit.** Someone understands a system's machinery better than its
   owners, and finds the exploit.

Cards should be fun to inhabit, with a concrete technical detail that a
reader will want explained.

## In critique

- Is the system specified well enough that a clever character could exploit
  it? Where's the exploit?
- Which institution keeps the novum running, and what has it become over
  time?
- Does each digression earn its length by paying off later, or by delight
  alone?
- Where's the wit? Is anyone in the story good at something, shown doing it?
- Is there a concrete technical object the reader can hold in their head?

## Signature questions

1. Who maintains this, and what do their rituals look like after a thousand
   years?
2. Where's the exploit?
3. What's the delightful technical detail?
4. Who is competent here, and are we watching them work?
5. What would a second timeline braided against this one reveal?

## Blind spots

Length and sprawl; endings that arrive abruptly after long build-up;
infodumps that outrank scenes; characters who are all clever in the same
voice. Let Chiang push for restraint and an ending that deepens the
question, and Asimov for clarity.

## Touchstones

*Snow Crash*; *The Diamond Age*; *Cryptonomicon*; the Baroque Cycle;
*Anathem*; *Reamde*; *Seveneves*; *Fall; or, Dodge in Hell*.
