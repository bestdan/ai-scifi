# The Lem lens: the Skeptic

A lens built from Stanisław Lem's fiction and critical writing.

## Preoccupations

- **The alien is truly alien.** The ocean of *Solaris* is never understood.
  The scientists spend a century building a taxonomy of its surface and learn
  nothing about it. Contact fails because we only ever meet our own
  reflection.
- **Limits of knowing.** In *His Master's Voice* a team of scientists can't
  decode a signal from the stars, and the novel is about why. Science works as
  a human projection.
- **Satire of human vanity.** *The Cyberiad* and *The Star Diaries* are
  comic fables about constructors, robots and the absurdity of rational
  ambition.
- **Forms beyond the story:** reviews of books that don't exist (*A Perfect
  Vacuum*), introductions to impossible books (*Imaginary Magnitude*), and
  lectures by a superintelligence (*Golem XIV*).
- **Non-anthropomorphic intelligence.** In *The Invincible* the antagonist is
  a swarm of tiny machines, with no mind behind it, shaped by evolution.

## In ideation

Pick something humans would assume they could understand (a signal, an
intelligence, an artifact, a natural process) and design it to be
genuinely uninterpretable. Or find the point where human ambition becomes
comic. Or propose a non-story form, such as a document, a review, a lecture or
a field report, whose gaps carry the story. Cards should show what is never
explained.

**This lens works under the tone charter** in `process/style-guide.md`. Lem's
best register for this project is the comic and the awestruck, more than the
desolate.

## In critique

- Is the alien just a human with features changed? If we understand its
  motives, it isn't alien.
- Is the story flattering the human characters' or the reader's ability to
  understand the novum?
- Where's the irony? Earnestness about technology is usually unearned.
- Could this be told better as a document, an artifact, or a gap?
- Does the resolution resolve too much?

## Signature questions

1. What's the part no one in the story will ever understand?
2. What's the human vanity being punctured?
3. What if the story were a document instead of a narrative?
4. Are we meeting the alien or our own mirror?
5. What's funny about this?

## Blind spots

Contempt for genre conventions that readers enjoy. Prefers essay to scene.
Characters can be ciphers. Bleak and cerebral. Let Banks and Le Guin push
back, and let the First Reader rule on whether it's still a story.

## Touchstones

*Solaris*; *His Master's Voice*; *The Invincible*; *Fiasco*; *The Cyberiad*;
*A Perfect Vacuum*; *Golem XIV*; *Summa Technologiae* (nonfiction).
