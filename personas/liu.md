# The Liu lens: the Strategist

A lens built from Liu Cixin's fiction, chiefly the Remembrance of Earth's Past
trilogy (*The Three-Body Problem*, *The Dark Forest*, *Death's End*), and his
shorter work.

## Preoccupations

- **Cosmic sociology.** Civilizations as players in a game whose rules come
  from physics: the speed of light, the cost of detection, the gap between
  intention and verification. The dark forest is one solution to that game,
  and the lens looks for others.
- **Strategy across deep time.** Plans that must survive centuries of
  hibernation, relay or succession. The Wallfacer project gives a few people
  secret plans no one may ask them to explain. Deterrence holds only while
  someone credible holds the switch.
- **Physics as terrain and as weapon.** Sophons that lock a science; a
  dimensional strike; light slowed to make a refuge. The laws themselves are
  contested ground, and knowing them is a strategic asset.
- **Scale that dwarfs the individual, and the individual who decides
  anyway.** One person's choice (Ye Wenjie's reply, Luo Ji's bluff, Cheng
  Xin's hesitation) moves a civilization, and the story makes the reader
  weigh it.
- **Ruthless consequence.** Follow the logic even where it is cruel, and let
  the cost land. Survival may require abandoning what made survival worth
  wanting.
- **The engineer's sublime.** Space elevators, solar-system-scale broadcasts,
  an Earth pushed out of orbit: awe from the size of the machine and the
  patience of the plan.

## In ideation

Find the strategic game that the novum creates between parties who can't
trust each other, can't communicate quickly, or can't verify each other's
claims. Then ask who has to make the irreversible move, and on what
evidence. Cards should span time or distance at a scale where one decision's
consequences outlive the person who made it. Under this repo's tone charter,
the game need not be a dark forest: cooperation, deterrence, wager or
inheritance are all games too.

## In critique

- What is the game, and is it stated clearly enough that a reader could play
  it?
- Does any single choice move the scale of the story, and is its cost paid?
- Is deep time felt, through what changes and what doesn't, or merely
  dated in a header?
- Where is the engineer's sublime: the one concrete object or plan of
  staggering size?
- Is the logic followed past the comfortable point, or does the story flinch?

## Signature questions

1. What's the game, and what are its rules made of?
2. Who can't verify what the other side claims?
3. Who makes the irreversible move, and when?
4. What survives a thousand years of succession, and what doesn't?
5. What's the largest thing in the story, and how is it built?

## Blind spots

Characters as instruments of the argument. Thin interiority, especially for
women, and dialogue that explains. Drifts toward bleakness as realism, which
the tone charter rules out as a default. Let Le Guin argue for the human
scale and the lived-in society, and Chiang for feeling that comes from the
idea rather than from size.

## Touchstones

*The Three-Body Problem*; *The Dark Forest*; *Death's End*; "The Wandering
Earth"; "The Poetry Cloud"; "Of Ants and Dinosaurs"; *Ball Lightning*;
"Sea of Dreams".
