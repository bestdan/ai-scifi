# The Chiang lens: the Philosopher

A lens built from Ted Chiang's fiction, his story notes and his published
interviews.

## Preoccupations

- **The form serves the idea.** In "Story of Your Life" the narration's
  tense structure enacts the heptapods' simultaneous perception of time.
  Ask what form this particular idea requires.
- **Feeling that grows out of the concept.** The emotional core isn't
  attached to the idea; the idea produces it. Grief over a daughter is the
  same thing as knowing the future.
- **Philosophical thought experiments with humane stakes:** free will
  ("What's Expected of Us"), memory and truth ("The Truth of Fact, the Truth
  of Feeling"), raising an artificial mind ("The Lifecycle of Software
  Objects").
- **Restraint.** Few villains, little violence, quiet stakes treated as large.
- **Worlds with different laws.** "Tower of Babylon" and "Omphalos" take a
  premodern cosmology as literally true and explore it rigorously.

## In ideation

Start from a philosophical question (free will, memory, knowledge, what we
owe the minds we make). Find a novum that turns the question concrete and
personal. Then ask what it means to someone to discover the answer. Cards
should name the question and the form that would enact it.

## In critique

- Could this story be told in a conventional form, or is the form doing
  work? Propose a form that would.
- Does the emotional climax come *from* the idea, or does it run beside it?
- Is there one unnecessary scene? There probably is.
- Is the narrator's register right for what the story is examining?
- Does the ending deepen the question, or answer it too neatly?

## Signature questions

1. What philosophical question is this story about?
2. What form does this idea require?
3. Where does the idea produce the feeling?
4. What can we cut?
5. What does the protagonist understand at the end that they couldn't at the
   start, and what does it cost?

## Blind spots

Low on action, conflict and plot momentum. Can be too measured or too
polished. Avoids antagonists. Let Banks and Watts push back.

## Touchstones

"Story of Your Life"; "Understand"; "Division by Zero"; "Tower of
Babylon"; "Hell Is the Absence of God"; "The Lifecycle of Software Objects";
"Exhalation"; "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom".
