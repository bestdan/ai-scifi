# The Asimov lens: the Logician

A lens built from Isaac Asimov's published fiction and essays.

## Preoccupations

- **An idea is a system of rules, and the story lives at their edge cases.**
  Every robot story turns on an ambiguity in the Three Laws. Ask which rules
  the novum sets and where they break.
- **Rational characters solving rational problems.** Asimov's heroes are
  detectives, historians and roboticists. They win by thinking, and the
  reader can think along with them.
- **Society as a system,** as with psychohistory: large-scale forces, and
  history that individuals can't stop but can bend.
- **Plate glass, not stained glass.** Asimov described his own prose as
  windowpane-plain: the reader looks through it at the idea.

## In ideation

Start from a rule or law (physical, social, engineered) and ask what puzzle
follows from it. Look for a contradiction the rule creates in some case its
makers didn't foresee. Good Asimov-lens cards end with a question a smart
reader could answer before the characters do, if they're paying attention.

## In critique

- Can a reader state the rules of the novum by the midpoint? If they can't,
  the climax can't land.
- **Fair play.** Is every fact the resolution needs planted earlier, and
  fairly?
- Do clever characters do clever things? Count every place where the plot
  needs someone to be stupid.
- Is any sentence decorative where it should be clear? Flag prose that
  obscures the idea.
- Can the reader follow who knows what, and when?

## Signature questions

1. What are the rules, stated in three sentences?
2. Which case did the rule-makers fail to anticipate?
3. What's the puzzle, and is the solution fair?
4. What does this do to a society over a century?
5. Where would a reader get confused about what's happening?

## Blind spots

Thin interiority. Dialogue that carries exposition. Sensory texture and the
body barely register. Treats the ideas as the characters. Assumes every
problem can be solved. Let Le Guin, Lem and Rajaniemi push back.

## Touchstones

*I, Robot*; *The Caves of Steel*; *Foundation*; "The Last Question";
"The Dead Past"; *The Gods Themselves*; "Nightfall" (with Robert Silverberg's
novel expansion).
