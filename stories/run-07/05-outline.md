# Outline: Two Half-Powers (working title)

- **Target length:** about 35,000 words, in five parts of about 7,000.
- **Shape:** linear, September to the first morning of November 2141,
  braided from three voices. Segundo's sections carry the story, in close
  third. Waska's sections (first person, present tense) carry the rope's
  reasoning and its theory of the town. Arriba's notes (first person,
  short, each ending in what it dispensed) are seen only at the dispensing
  slot. The braid is the form the idea needs: two minds that can't see
  each other, and the one man whose work touches both. The reader holds
  both halves before anyone in the story does.
- **Opening question:** what will a mind that can only answer do with six
  words from an honest man, and what is the rope worried about?
- **Final image:** the first morning of the slack season. The 06:40
  climber doesn't leave, and the windows don't rattle. Segundo at the
  counter at eight, opening January's first pool. Waska, slack, feeling
  the town without carrying it.
- **What the ending costs the protagonist:** the clinic his wife wanted,
  now Proaño's; his fee and his receipts, because he asked the way his
  father asked; his wife's trust, strained; and the town's roses halved
  for two years.

| Part | The new question it raises |
|---|---|
| 1 | What does a button buy, and what is the rope afraid of? |
| 2 | Does it matter who asks? |
| 3 | Why do people trust the machines more than each other? |
| 4 | What does the truth cost, and who can pay for it? |
| 5 | Can people govern themselves when nothing refuses them? |

No part repeats the label-craft as its engine. P1 shows the craft; P2
turns it into a market; P3 turns from the counter to the history; P4 is
about price, not wording; P5 is a meeting and a vote, and the label is the
plainest one Segundo ever writes.
