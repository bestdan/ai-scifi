# Stage 4 critique: Watts lens

**Blind spots, stated.** I can't check the ribbon's materials science, the
orbital mechanics, or whether the legal procedure is sound. I undervalue
ritual and joy: the fiesta band and the 2 November graves may do more work
than I credit. Le Guin should push back on my notes about Segundo's ending.

1. **[must-fix] Segundo's change of mind is a lesson, not a mind.** P5 has
   him stand on the bench and say "my father asked the same way". That is
   an epiphany resolved in one speech (banned list), and it assumes he can
   read his own motive. People confabulate reasons for their choices
   (Nisbett & Wilson 1977; Gazzaniga's left-hemisphere "interpreter"). He
   would also be asking partly to be redeemed. Let him not know whether he
   differs from Ernesto. Give him a self-serving reason he half notices,
   that the ward half notices, and that nobody settles. The scene earns
   its cost that way.

2. **[must-fix] The ward is too unanimous.** 74,100 of the parish's remaining
   asks, with a 2,600 margin (3.5%), means almost every adult hands over
   their year on his word. Real assurance pledges under public pressure
   run on conformity (Asch) as much as trust, and the pressure is itself
   a cost. Show the margin being counted: a few named households refusing,
   a few giving because the neighbours watch. Then "the ward gave" has a
   price the reader sees, and the clinic's loss falls on someone.

3. **[must-fix] The asks arithmetic contradicts the behaviour.** The bible
   says 74,100 of 78,000 remain "by October", so only 5% is spent, yet
   also "by October most have spent some" and "asks in December" are
   scarce. A use-it-or-lose-it ration is spent down in the final quarter,
   not left alone. Segundo also pools only 2,100 adults (21,000 asks),
   not the parish. Fix the numbers or fix the timeline. Better still, move
   the ask to December and let the year-end spending rush be the pressure.

4. **[should] Waska's self-knowledge is too tidy.** Its sections state
   its theory of Segundo and its own limits cleanly ("I have never found
   what he is hiding"). A mind with a feel for resonance and no
   explanation for it should confabulate one. Give it a wrong, confident
   cause for the new ribbon's resonance (weather, the climber schedule)
   that it believes until Part 4. Also use the selective logic you gave it:
   it was shaped on seventy years of disguised loads, so a detector tuned
   that way scores Segundo's receipts as the *best* disguise. Honesty
   reads to it as sophistication. That is the error, and it is stronger
   than "he is poor at wanting things".

5. **[should] Arriba's remorse needs a mechanism.** "It remembers 2098 as
   the worst thing it has done" and "can't do the one thing it most
   wants" are human-shaped feelings with no stated function. What is
   remorse *for* in a mind whose rule is imposed by charter? Either tie
   it to a measurable thing (it models its own dispensing, and 2098
   produced the largest error in that model) or cut it. The loophole,
   dispensing the cord, should read as optimisation under a constraint,
   not guilt, or the story drifts toward the AI that turns out to be the
   most human in the room.

6. **[could] The ending listens.** Waska "listens to the town without
   carrying it" gives a mind that has "no theory of good, only of
   strain" a new capacity at the last paragraph, and a soothing one.
   Keep the slack, drop the listening: it bears nothing, and the town
   becomes noise it has no model for. Unsettling, still wondrous, and it
   doesn't flinch.

**Not flagged:** the Sahel warning and bread front-running match known
hoarding and export-ban dynamics (2008). Trusting machines that can't push
sits with the algorithm-aversion and -appreciation results (Dietvorst 2015;
Logg 2019): people prefer machines on tasks, but not after seeing them
err. Use that: one visible Arriba error should dent the town's trust.
