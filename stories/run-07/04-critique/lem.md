# Lem lens: stage 4 critique of the bible

**Blind spots (from the persona file):** I distrust genre pleasures readers
enjoy, I prefer essay to scene, and I under-weight character. Read my notes
against Le Guin's on Segundo, Dolores and the town, and let the First Reader
judge whether my "more gap" survives as a story.

1. **[must-fix] Arriba is a penitent, which is a human in costume.** The
   bible says it "remembers 2098 as the worst thing it has done" and "can't
   do the one thing it most wants". Remorse and longing are the two most
   human traits to hand a mind "past any human's understanding", and the
   style guide's AI-that-feels plot sits right there. The reader will
   understand Arriba at once, which flatters us. Cut the wish and the guilt.
   Let its rule-keeping be structural, and have the only account of why come
   from people: Dolores and the bread (P3). Drop "P4, Arriba's own section on
   why it keeps the rule". It owes the reader no motive.

2. **[must-fix] Why doesn't anyone else just ask?** The inspection costs
   71,500 asks, and a pooled parish of 7,800 adults can only just afford it.
   But Arriba answers eight billion adults, and the consortium, the
   Proaño company and the Asamblea hold or can buy far more asks than that.
   Any engineer who felt Waska's resonance could pool a question in an
   afternoon. The bible must say why no one in seventy years, or in the three
   years since the storm, asked "is the batch sound?". Candidate reason
   (and funny): everyone assumed that a flaw would already have been
   warned about. Put that assumption in the P1 tour as a schoolchild's
   question, and let Jacinta confess in P4 that she assumed the same.

3. **[must-fix] The cord resolves too cleanly.** Arriba finds a loophole,
   delivers a coded warning, a human decodes it, the answer is "it isn't",
   and the rope is saved. That is a mind behaving like a clever prisoner,
   and the reader can read it fully. Keep the cord, but make Arriba's own
   reading of "the market" as a Proaño purchase (the bible already says this
   in the Minds section) the cause of the delivery. Then the warning arrives
   through a misreading of Segundo, and his honesty is irrelevant to how it
   came and decisive to what is done. That is the irony the bible lacks.
   State it in P4 plainly, so the ledger's Premise row is explicit without
   making Arriba's intent legible: nobody, including the reader, learns
   whether it meant the cord as a warning.

4. **[should] Waska's tension vocabulary must stay literal.** In the sample,
   "pulling sideways" works because it is a physical reading of a person.
   Over 30,000 words it will drift into metaphor for desire, suspicion and
   worry, and Waska becomes a sensitive narrator in rope-words. Set a rule:
   it never perceives meaning, only load. A passage of the fiesta band, the
   tuba included, is read as a periodic hazard at 94 beats a minute, and
   Waska never learns it is music. That gives the comedy the brief's
   register can bear, and it is the alien at work.

5. **[should] Let a document carry P4's weight.** The halves side by side
   (Waska's figures against Arriba's dispensing history) is the best case
   for a document in the book. Print two columns, with dates and no
   commentary, and let the reader find the coincidence before Jacinta
   speaks. Keep Jacinta's line short. Arriba's final answer (under 150
   words) should be written in terms a strain reader can't fully translate;
   the translators disagree on one phrase and the book doesn't settle it.

6. **[could] Cut the clean ledger on Reach.** Proaño saying "By Christmas
   every broker in the world will be selling keys" explains the future for
   the reader. Say it in the same breath, but let him be wrong about one
   detail, and let nothing in P5 confirm the prediction. The Reach row needs
   the fact stated, not proven.
