# Stage 4 critique: Stephenson lens (systems builder)

**Blind spots:** I favour machinery and digression, and I'm weak on length discipline, on whether the ending lands emotionally, and on whether Segundo, Dolores and Jacinta sound different from one another. Read my notes against Le Guin and Chiang on those. Everything below is about whether the system holds up under a clever person leaning on it.

1. **[must-fix] The cheap-question exploit breaks the climax.** The bible says "a question costs what answering it takes", and the soil-survey example scales with work. The inspection covers 312 sections for 71,500 asks, about 229 asks per section. Segundo's whole craft is label-writing, so he would ask "Is section 40 of batch seven sound?" for roughly 229 asks, one broker's pool, with no ward vote and no ruin. The ending's price falls apart. Don't patch it by fiat; make it a scene. The flaw is spotty (it depends on where each section sat in the curing oven during the March 2138 storm), so a sample comes back true and useless. Add a rule that Arriba never lies and answers the question literally. Then P4 has Segundo spending 229 asks and receiving "Section 40 is sound", and Arriba, which cannot volunteer, has to hand over a true answer that misleads. Only then does the full 71,500 look like the cost of the plain question. State in the bible what counts as a "warning" (the cord is one, so what else is?).

2. **[must-fix] "Why not just…" holes around the question.**
   - Jacinta has 2,000 m of ribbon offcut and a materials lab. Why not fatigue-test it for free? Give the reason: the defects sit below the lab's test method, and only Arriba's orbital instruments resolve them. Then the lab test comes back "within tolerance", which is the same tolerance Waska feels the resonance in. That is the system's real trap, and it is a scene.
   - Why does the ward fund this, when Proaño has 3,000 workers' asks and the anchor employs a quarter of the town? Name who could ask and what stops them (the consortium trusts Arriba's own QA, and Arriba made the batch, so it is inspecting itself).
   - 71,500 asks is nothing against Earth's adults. The reason has to be that only people with something at stake are placed to ask, and the labels are public.

3. **[must-fix] The slack season is the unexamined exploit.** In it Waska "bears nothing, so refuses nothing". Then what stops the Asamblea or the consortium from installing batch 7 anyway? The ending says the season "goes ahead with old-design stock", but who decides that, with the veto off? Two more gaps:
   - Why is legislation not simply frozen for 40 days? Give a reason that goes back to 2101: this is the first replacement in 75 years, and the referendum's drafters never wrote a slack clause.
   - A reader will ask how Arriba's inspection becomes Waska's "1.00 / 0.00". Waska can't see Arriba's records. Say plainly that the climb is resubmitted with the defect map as a derated capacity, so the number is just arithmetic.

4. **[must-fix] The numbers contradict each other.** 74,100 is 78,000 × 0.95, but the bible says "by October most have spent some". Segundo also pools for only 2,100 adults (21,000 asks), not the parish. A reader who checks gets either of two answers. Choose one: Segundo needs the other brokers' pools, including Wilmer's, which makes the ward meeting a negotiation among brokers. Or the figures are recomputed from real spend-down. Trace the days too: how long does the inspection run between 28 Oct and 1 Nov?

5. **[should] P2 and P3 need their own exploits.** "A market in vague labels" is the middle of the book and has no mechanism yet. Give Wilmer a controlled experiment: he copies "the market" and gets no cord. That shows Arriba reads the broker's history (it takes Segundo for Proaño's agent), so the cord was addressed to Segundo, and the reader sees the dispensing depends on who asks. Jacinta can design the control, and the scene is competence with no lecture. Label reputation then becomes the tradeable asset, and the "skim" turns into the sale of a good history.

6. **[could] Waska's wrongness costs nothing.** It misreads Segundo as a disguised load, but no plot event turns on that. Let the misreading have a price, such as Waska refusing his awning load for a cord-irrelevant reason, so the reader watches its theory fail in public. Also, "the six words" craft is a digression with a payoff: the end button's plainness should mark the one time his craft is useless.
