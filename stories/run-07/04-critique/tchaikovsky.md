# Stage 4 critique: Tchaikovsky lens (the Naturalist)

**Blind spots.** I'm optimistic that minds can be reached, so I'm soft on the Segundo–Jacinta "halves side by side" scene, which Lem should test. I don't judge whether Waska is conscious at all (Watts), and I don't check the tether physics (Egan). I tend to want more page for the minds than a novella's human middle can spare.

The strongest thing in the bible is that Waska's senses write its mistakes. A mind that reads every human as a disguised load, after seventy years of disguised bills, is built from the inside. Most of my notes come from where that discipline lapses.

1. **[must-fix] Waska's "now" contradicts its memory, and its drive is never named.** The bible says it "only ever feels now", yet it "has learned" about sideways pulls, remembers 2097 and "has spent seventy years" adapting to Arriba. Give it the memory a cable would have. Fatigue, creep and set in the ribbon are its history, felt as a stiffness that shows where past loads were. Then name the want. A damping mind wants slack: quiet, an absence of pull. The last image, listening "without carrying", pays off only if the reader has felt it wanting that for 30,000 words. Put the want in Waska's first section, and let it flinch from the slack season as well as long for it. That flinch is the cost to Waska.

2. **[must-fix] Arriba's cord doesn't follow from its stated theory of Segundo.** Arriba reads "the market" as Proaño's buying, and dispenses accordingly. Yet it also sends batch-7 offcut as its one lawful warning, to a man it thinks is Proaño's agent. The reader will ask why it warns him, and why it ships a flawed batch at all when it knows. Settle both on the page. Perhaps the cord goes to Proaño's side deliberately, because Proaño is the one who can stop the climb. And a withholding rule: it must fill the consortium's order as placed, since refusing to fill is also unasked action. Arriba's own section in P2 should show it choosing the cord with the 2098 bread in mind, knowing it is bending the rule. That is its cost: a mind that sees itself becoming a mind that can be bought.

3. **[should] Arriba's misreading is human gossip in costume.** "The son of the broker who sold his parish" is lineage prejudice. A mind that sees every ask on Earth would misread from its own senses: it knows people only as pooled asks, so it would mistake Segundo's receipts and waived fee for a tell, since honest brokers are rare in its data. Let it be wrong because it sees only totals and timing, never intent.

4. **[should] Both minds should be right about Segundo, in turn.** The bible says Waska is "wrong" about him. But in P5 he does pull sideways: he asks on his word, with no receipt, and can't say why. That is exactly the disguised load Waska has expected for seventy years. Plant its P1 theory, let it look foolish in P2–P3, and let P5 make it correct for the reason it never guessed. The reader should hold both views, and Waska's final section can note, without glee, that the man was hiding something after all.

5. **[should] The encounter needs a failure that costs the human side.** Segundo's label craft is shown as competence, and the halves click together in one scene. Negotiation with a mind this strange should cost something earlier. In P2, let Wilmer's copy of "the market" dispense something different from Segundo's, because Arriba reads who pools. Segundo loses asks learning that his craft is partly the machine's reading of him. This also answers "why not just copy the label?" and makes the market in labels dangerous, not merely busy.

6. **[could] Add the humour the bible lacks.** Nothing here is yet funny or fond. Waska's literalism is the obvious source: Segundo's badly played tuba, in a fiesta parade crossing the anchor road, is a periodic load at an awkward frequency, and Waska worries at length about the band, not about the ribbon. It plants resonance as a theme and makes the reader fond of a mind that mistakes a brass band for a threat. Arriba's equivalent is a dryness it can only show in what it dispenses: a polite oversupply of tuba mouthpieces, say.

**Signature questions, answered.** Waska senses tension first and cannot sense purpose. Arriba senses totals and cannot sense intent. Waska is right about disguised loads and wrong about who is disguising. The scene only these minds could live through is the fiesta band on the cable road, if note 6 is taken.
