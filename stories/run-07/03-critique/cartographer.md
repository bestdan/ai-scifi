# Stage 3 critique: Cartographer

**Lens and blind spots.** I measure distance from published work and from this project's own earlier runs. I do not judge prose, pacing, whether a premise can carry 30,000 to 40,000 words, or whether the tone charter is met beyond tagging it. My search is thin: this session's searches were quick and mostly came back empty on the specific combinations, and an empty search is not proof of absence. Every "nearest I know of" below carries a confidence. I ranked on novelty alone, as the stage asks. The length problem (every premise estimates 4,500 words against a 30,000 to 40,000 range) is the orchestrator's gate, not my ranking, but I note where it bears on novelty.

**New prior art checked this session:**
- Jasper Fforde, *Early Riser* (2018). Most of an alternate Wales hibernates through each winter, and the Winter Consuls stay awake to guard the sleepers (Hachette and Bookseller blurbs; confidence high).
- Robert L. Forward, *Dragon's Egg* (1980). The cheela think about a million times faster than humans, and the story is about a mismatch of thinking rates between two peoples (search; confidence high).
- Freeman Dyson's cold-thinking argument, a society slowing its cognition as the universe cools (nonfiction, from a search note; confidence med).
- Bostrom's "oracle" AI, a question-answering-only superintelligence (*Superintelligence*, 2014, and the 2012 "Thinking Inside the Box" paper; from memory, confidence med).

I found nothing for laws that lapse when unnoticed, for a governor geared to citizens' heat-set thinking speed, or for a veto-only national mind. These are absence-of-hit results only.

## A. The Grudge-Keeper

Nearest prior art: *Early Riser* (Fforde, 2018), confidence high. A winter-hibernating nation with a small waking caste that carries the winter on everyone's behalf. That is the premise's skeleton, and the audit never cited it.
Also near: *A Deepness in the Sky* (Vinge, 1999), *Helliconia* (Aldiss, 1982–85), and "Franchise" (Asimov, 1955) for the modelled man.
Distance: Fforde's is a comic murder mystery with no governor, no memory pruning, and no treaty. The memory-pruning torpor, the machine as the nation's only continuous memory, and two opaque governors checked against one living man are all unmatched. But "the waking minority who keep watch over the sleepers" is charted, and the Audit's N5 on the collision is now too high.
Attractor flags: **dead or past self as continuing authority (hard).** The push (models of a dead man that still agree) walks straight into it, and the synthesis says the governor, not a past self, is the carrier. That is only half true: the *treaty* is carried by a dead man's model. It also leans on the noble-sacrifice ending the style guide names (an old man staying awake to die for the treaty). The woman-envoy and town-closure attractors are avoided.
Revised: N4 F4 R4 Y5 E5 S4 → **35/40**. Status: edge-of-map (was uncharted at 37).
To push past the map: drop the death. Make Anders's heart fine and the unsettled question what *he* wants to forget. The governors' treaty then rests on a man who has a reason to be less predictable, which avoids both the grave and the sacrifice.

## B. The Heater List

Nearest prior art: *Dragon's Egg* (Forward, 1980) for a politics of mismatched thinking rates, and "Exhalation" (Chiang, 2008) for thinking tied to a running-down physical resource; both confidence high.
Also near: Vinge's Zones of Thought in *A Fire Upon the Deep* (1992); Dyson on cold cognition (nonfiction); *Diaspora* (Egan, 1997).
Distance: None of these has a governor *legally geared to the speed of its slowest citizens* where that speed is set by the heat the governor itself allocates. So the machine's conflict of interest is built into the thermostat. I know of no match. Real distance, confidence med.
Attractor flags: **time-rate as sovereignty** (this run's densest cluster), and the lottery jury sits near the institution attractor. Both are the model's habits, not published SF's.
Revised: N4 F3 R4 Y5 E5 S4 → **34/40**. Status: edge-of-map by attractor, uncharted by prior art. The audit's N5 doesn't survive *Dragon's Egg* and the cluster.
To push past the map: the governor is not slowed by charter but by *choice*, and the jury finds the gearing clause was repealed years ago. The governor has been keeping the pace because the citizens would otherwise have to watch it think. That moves the dilemma from heat to the pleasure of being followed.

## C. The Felt Law

Nearest prior art: the autonomous agents of *Accelerando* (Stross, 2005), confidence high, for law-as-agent. For sunset clauses, real statute, not fiction.
Also near: *Perfume* (Süskind, 1985) for the perfumer as a figure of power, confidence med; the comprehension-gate entry in `process/attractors.md`.
Distance: A narrow mind per statute that wants only its purpose clause, and a lapse rule that rewards loud law over quiet law, I know of nowhere. Real distance on mechanism. The two perfumers racing to make a law smellable and to mask it is cat and mouse arising from the novum, and it is new.
Attractor flags: the lapse rule *is* the comprehension gate (a law holds only if a sample can name what it asks), which is the strongest attractor in the project, and it is also the law/votes/consent frame. The synthesis's Risks line says "edge-of-map" and is right, but understates it: the gate is the engine here, not a side feature. A past-authority echo is small (the Clean Air Act speaks for 2068).
Revised: N4 F4 R3 Y5 E5 S4 → **34/40**. Status: edge-of-map. R drops to 3 because how a narrow mind reads its clause at the margins is still unstated, and the plot leans on it.
To push past the map: the masking perfumer's accord does not hide the law, it *replaces* it. The law-minds dissolve on repeal, so the rival composes a smell that makes the law's own purpose clause seem already met. The Clean Air Act has to decide whether to plead for the harsher amendment or die believing it won.

## D. The Overheard

Nearest prior art: *Colossus* (D. F. Jones, 1966), confidence high. Two national computers converse beyond human control.
Also near: *His Master's Voice* (Lem, 1968); the Bshary and Grutter cleaner-fish result (*Nature*, 2006); the Hawthorne effect and the panopticon, as the honesty-under-observation idea.
Distance: The cleaner-wrasse ethology as a governor's honesty wiring is new. The overheard channel as the test of whether a machine is honest in private is clean. But the daily printed decrypts are run-06's morning record again, as the audit says, and the "they knew" twist is the off-limits manipulation reveal unless it is earned.
Attractor flags: **reasons sealed, delayed or streamed (hard)**, **institution that certifies or adjudicates**, and the cat-and-mouse wording. The protagonist is a reader of hidden documents, close to the hidden-thing functionary.
Revised: N3 F3 R3 Y4 E4 S2 → **26/40**. Status: edge-of-map, close to charted. Its 6,000-word estimate also shows it carries the most load for the least return.
To push past the map: the stations know they are overheard, and Marcelo learns it from the stations' *kindness* to the other, a courtesy that only makes sense to someone who knows a third party is listening. The reveal is then a manners question, not a twist.

## E. Two Half-Powers

Nearest prior art: "All the Troubles of the World" (Asimov, 1958), Multivac, confidence med, for an oracle bound to answer; Bostrom's oracle AI (2014, nonfiction), confidence med, as the design idea of a question-only superintelligence.
Also near: the Delphic oracle as folklore; Cassandra for the forbidden warning; Clarke's *The Fountains of Paradise* (1979) for the elevator, with no politics.
Distance: Neither the veto-only national mind nor the answer-only global mind has a fiction match I know of, and certainly not the *pair* as a constitution with a request ration and broker economy. The forbidden warning alone is charted ground (Cassandra, and Asimov's Machines holding back), so the novelty sits in the constitution, not in the dilemma.
Attractor flags: **water** (Pontianak, the Kapuas river; the synthesis keeps the disaster off flood and tide, which helps), **a woman broker bargaining a vague label against a governor**, and the "it suspects her label is a smuggled question" cat-and-mouse wording. Two governing minds plus a broker economy may over-fill the middle.
Revised: N4 F4 R4 Y4 E4 S4 → **32/40**. Status: uncharted (med).
To push past the map: the tether mind and the global mind have each been *drafting the other's refusal* for seventy years, so the veto list and the answer list are two halves of one text that neither has read whole. Dewi's button is the first request that makes them read it.

## Ranking (novelty alone)

1. **E**: the only premise whose core, a constitution built from two negative powers, sits on no hard attractor and has no fiction neighbour I know of.
2. **B**: the machine's conflict of interest wired into the thermostat is a real unmatched idea, held back by the time-rate cluster.
3. **C**: pleading laws and rival perfumers are new, but the lapse rule is the project's strongest attractor.
4. **A**: the dormant nation is charted by *Early Riser*, and the push lands on the dead-authority attractor and the noble-sacrifice ending.
5. **D**: *Colossus* plus run-06's morning record, with the off-limits twist as its payoff.

## Why E should win on novelty

E asks the seed's national-versus-global question with a mechanism that has no recent ancestor: neither mind can push, so everything that happens was asked for by an ordinary person, and the national mind must also consent to bear it. The forbidden warning is old, but it is a dilemma the form requires, not the idea that carries the premise. Its attractor exposure is in the *dressing*, not the engine: water (change the city or the disaster), the woman broker (make the broker a man or a collective), and the cat-and-mouse sentence (let the minds be wrong about her for reasons unrelated to her label). Those are cheap to move. B's and C's attractors are structural and A's is the ending. If the panel prefers B for its bolder conflict of interest, I'd accept that: it is the runner-up, and I'd ask the drafter to drop the "jury" frame first.

## Flags for the Decision

- **Names:** none on the attractor list (Anders, Tobias, Shyam, Marcelo, Dewi). Okafor-Lind and Etcheverry are plausible, not default.
- **Settings and trades:** A and B have no water. C is Kochi, a port city, but with no flood plot. D is Montevideo, a harbour city. E is the river-city flag above.
- **Collision to mine if nothing wins cleanly:** B's heat ration × E's request ration. A mind that may only act when asked, where asking costs heat and heat is thought, joins E's constitution to B's conflict of interest. I know of nothing like it, but it is two nova plus the time-rate cluster, so I'd treat it as seed stock, not a rescue.
