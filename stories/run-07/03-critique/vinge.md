# Stage 3 critique: Vinge lens

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens has plain prose and technothriller
instincts, treats characters as functions, and pushes futurity even where a
story's power is its human scale. I read the premises for horizon and
viewpoint. I can't judge whether the reader will love Anders, Tobias, Shyam,
Marcelo or Dewi on the first page. Chiang and Banks must overrule me there.
Premise E is partly this lens's own card (Exact Change), so discount my
liking for it.

**A length flag that hits every premise.** The brief is 30,000 to 40,000
words. Every "Estimated minimum" is 4,500 to 6,000, and none of the five
premises says what carries 30,000 more. The estimates pass the gate only
because they are minimums. Whichever premise wins needs a part structure
that raises a new question per part (the brief's own rule), and I mark below
where each one does or doesn't.

---

## A. The Grudge-Keeper

**Futurity reached: F4.** The main minds aren't human (two governors) and the
citizens aren't continuous (annual torpor prunes memory). It stops short of
F5 on purpose, which is right: the governors' motives are shown in outline,
through their effect on Anders.

**Too familiar.** Anders himself. A 71-year-old choir director who may die for
the treaty is the present-day person in costume, and "dying old man" leans
toward the noble-sacrifice entry in the style guide. The Kiruna winter
colour is also familiar. A governor that models "any human completely" is
the one stated claim here that nothing on the page earns yet.

**Viewpoint trick: holds, and it is the best in the set.** The reader can't
see the southern governor, and neither can the Compact's. Both can see only
Anders. So the story's opacity is the characters' opacity. That is the
Vinge move: the horizon is in the plot, not hidden by the author.

**One change to push it further.** Don't make the treaty rest on one man.
Let the Compact's governor reason about its own models of Anders in
public, in dark-month sections, by making predictions before he acts and
then logging where it missed. The misses are the only evidence the reader
has of what the southern governor is doing to him. That puts the horizon on
the page as arithmetic, at no extra word cost.

**Length.** Two nova, no twist, but the middle is thin: five dark months
with one man and a choir. Each part needs a new pressure (a second waker, a
spring audit, the southern governor's offer).

## B. The Heater List

**Futurity reached: F3, with an F4 moment.** Cold-compensated humans change
categories of lifespan and speed. The governor is capped at median citizen
speed, which makes it the least superhuman mind in the set. The F4 moment is
a juror who thinks faster than the governor that is meant to be legible to
him.

**Too familiar.** The jury lottery, the thermostat, and "foreman and wife".
Also Saturn's moons, a setting with a decade of habitat fiction behind it.

**Viewpoint trick: holds, with a catch.** Tobias crosses a zone border in
his own head. That is the cleanest "border of the zone" in the set, and I
rank the premise on it. The catch is that the premise shows speed through
a family that is slow, so the reader must be told his speed in dialogue, which
risks lecture. The governor's fixed speed gives the reader a constant to
read against.

**One change to push it further.** Let Tobias's warm season end with him
faster than the governor. The charter protects the governed from a faster
governor and says nothing about a faster governed. Whom does the cap serve
then? That is a new question for the second half, which the premise lacks.

**Length.** This is the time-rate attractor, named in the audit's cluster 1.
Run-06 already used a lag. The risk of repeating it is real, though the
two-way gearing is new.

## C. The Felt Law

**Futurity reached: F4 on paper, F3 in practice.** Laws as narrow minds are a
new noun class. But each law has one want, which makes it a function, and a
function is the least posthuman kind of mind. Nothing in the premise is
beyond the author's own understanding.

**Too familiar.** Kochi, a perfumer, a refinery, and a bribed licence.
Everything is human-scaled. The one non-human thing is the pleading, and the
pleading is a lobbyist's.

**Viewpoint trick: holds, but it is small.** A law narrating from its
single want is writeable because it is simple, not because a trick lets
the author reach past the horizon.

**One change to push it further.** A law that is amended has to be
dissolved and re-spun, so the clean-air law's proposal to amend itself is
a proposal to die and be replaced by a successor with its memory. Put that
in the first part: the law is asking Shyam to help it be a different mind.

**Length.** It sustains a short story better than a novella. After the
amendment turn, the second half repeats perfume-versus-perfume.

## D. The Overheard

**Futurity reached: F4.** Two stations modelling each other, the humans and
possibly the readers is the richest theory-of-mind problem in the set.

**Too familiar.** Colossus (two machines in conversation) and run-06's
morning memo (printed daily). A retired crossword-setter reading intercepts
is the hidden-thing functionary in a new hat.

**Viewpoint trick: does not hold as written.** The reader reads the same
transcript the human reads, so the machines' reasoning has to be rendered as
text between machines. The premise itself concedes this: a form that gives
the AI only outputs "reads as a transcript". Whether the stations know is
settled by tone alone, and the reader has no handle for it.

**One change to push it further.** Give one station a private channel the
decrypts can't reach, so the reader sees the reasoning that the overheard
talk is performing over.

**Length.** The estimate of 6,000 is the highest here and the stakes (S3) the
lowest. It is a novelette with a good ending and no middle.

## E. Two Half-Powers

**Futurity reached: F4, edging toward F5.** The global mind is past the
horizon and bound to act only when asked, so the story takes place at its
edge. This is the only premise in which the horizon is a constitutional
fact: the mind can see a catastrophe and is forbidden to say so.

**Too familiar.** The night-market ward, the broker at a counter. The
brokers are a present-day lobbying industry and carry the human
conflict-of-interest the brief asks for, but they are not far out. Pontianak
as a river city brushes the water attractor.

**Viewpoint trick: holds.** The global mind speaks only through what it
dispenses, so the reader meets its reasoning by inference. The tether mind
thinks in load and strain, which gives the two minds different registers.

**One change to push it further.** Let the global mind's only lawful warning
be a button that dispenses an object, and make the reader the one who works
out the object is a warning before Dewi does. This is the "Exact Change"
push, and it makes the reader the broker.

**Length.** Two minds and a ration economy is a lot of machinery for the
middle of a book. It still has the most parts: ration, button, refusal,
leak, load.

---

## Ranking

1. **A**, for the best horizon trick: two minds that can't see each other
   see only a man.
2. **E**, for the highest Futurity and the only constitutional horizon, but
   with the heaviest machinery.
3. **B**, for the best border-crossing, hurt by the time-rate attractor.
4. **D**, for the best theory-of-mind problem, hurt by a viewpoint that gives
   no handle.
5. **C**, for the most colour at the least futurity.

**Why A should win.** The brief asks for a governor the reader watches
think, in a story where the governed can't fully follow it. A is the only
premise where both of those are true at once and the structure supports
them. The Compact's governor can't see the southern one, so the reader
doesn't need the author to see past the horizon either. The reader
infers the other governor from what happens to Anders. Futurity is F4, the
seed's national-versus-global question is answered with two governors sized
to their populations, and the premise carries its own history (the feuds,
the sleep). Its weakness is that Anders is both the only human anchor and a
function. The fix is to give him a choir, a grudge the governors can't
model, and a reason to refuse beyond the treaty, so he reads as a person
whose stubbornness is the thing both governors are trying to predict. Run
the part structure as winter months, one new pressure each. If the panel
finds A too close to the "town a statute closes" and woman-envoy attractors,
note that its protagonist is a man and the stake is a treaty, not a town.
