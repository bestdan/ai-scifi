# Brief: run-07

- **Form and length range:** novella, 30,000 to 40,000 words. The range's
  top is the drafter's hard ceiling. Drafting goes in parts (see Notes).
- **Gates:** proxy. The orchestrator decides every gate after this brief
  and the human reads the story blind. The human knows the seed, so
  "blind" here means the angle, the characters and the ending.
- **Futurity floor:** F3 (see `process/rubric.md`). The change should
  reshape what a citizen, a government or a nation means, not only who
  holds office.
- **Tone:** earnest and morally serious, a thought experiment about consent
  and governance in the Le Guin register. The tone charter in
  `process/style-guide.md` always applies: neither the stock
  machine-tyranny dystopia nor a frictionless utopia.
- **Seed (from issue #9, verbatim):** People are becoming more fractious.
  They trust their government less and less, and they aren't wrong, lots of
  conflicts of interest. Can AI be the benevolent dictator? Will people
  accept it's policy? How will nations have their own vs global? Will
  people understand how they are being governed?
- **Seed adherence:** every card engages the seed's questions: an AI that
  governs, whether people accept its policy, the national-versus-global
  split, and whether the governed can understand how they are governed. A
  card may answer any of them in the negative, but it may not drop them.
  Lenses may wander in mechanism, scale, who builds it and what follows.
- **Off-limits:** nothing beyond the style guide. The rogue-AI uprising,
  the secret puppet-master reveal, and the "it was manipulating everyone
  all along" twist count as the stock darkness the tone charter rules out,
  unless a card makes one of them its uncharted novum. So are caricatured
  camps, such as luddites against singularity optimists. People who resist
  or embrace the AI do so for reasons the story takes seriously, and
  positions cut across those lines.
- **Must include:**
  - **The mechanics of governing on the page,** shown rather than
    summarised: how a law is drafted, amended, costed, passed and
    administered, and the bureaucracy that carries it out. Procedure can be
    the action of a scene. **In moderation:** no single mechanism carries
    the middle of the book, and each part raises a question the earlier
    parts didn't.
  - **The AI visibly reasons.** Whatever form its voice takes, the reader
    sees it think: what it believes the humans want, what it suspects, and
    whether it knows it is in a game of cat and mouse when one is being
    played. Outputs alone (memos, rulings, logs) read as a transcript.
  - **Why people hand power to the AI is lived, not stated.** Distrust of
    human government, its conflicts of interest and people's inability to
    be unbiased, are dramatised in scenes a character lives through.
  - **The world's shape is explained.** Why power sits where it does, and
    why it covers these functions and not those, with a history the reader
    meets on the page. Every rule the plot leans on has an in-world reason.
  - **More colour.** No idea that matters gets only a sentence. The world
    has streets, food, weather and work the reader can stand in.
- **Open question for the cards: does the AI have a voice?** Each card
  says whether the governing AI, or some component of it, gets a voice or
  a point of view in the story (narration, documents, dialogue, interior
  monologue, none), and why that choice serves the novum. Stage 3 weighs
  this choice explicitly, and tests it against "does it seem to think?"
- **Novelty:** run-06 (`stories/run-06/`) is prior art for this project.
  The Cartographer gets `stories/run-06/03-synthesis.md` and the core of
  `stories/run-06/04-bible.md` as charted ground, alongside
  `process/attractors.md`. Run-06's seed stock (B to E in
  `stories/run-06/STATUS.md`) may be mined, but the stage 2 audit clears
  it like any card.
- **Panel changes:** add the **Stephenson** lens (`personas/stephenson.md`:
  systems and institutions, digression as pleasure, technical virtuosity,
  wit).
  - Stage 1: the default nine plus Stephenson, 3 cards each.
  - Stage 3: add Stephenson.
  - Stage 4: add Stephenson.
  - Stage 7: add Stephenson.
- **Notes (long-form adaptations, carried over from run-06):**
  - **Two-level outline.** An arc map comes first, one row per part,
    tracking the idea arc, the character arc and the pressure arc. A scene
    table per part sits under it. Each part turns and lands on its own.
  - **Bible additions:** a timeline, a global payoff ledger plus one per
    part, and at most about three new names per part.
  - **Drafting in parts.** The same drafter is resumed for each part, and a
    running `drafts/continuity.md` is updated after each one, voice notes
    included, so a fresh drafter can carry on if the first is lost.
  - **Review.** Lens notes on each part as it lands, then a full-draft
    review with a fresh First Reader and the comprehension gate.
  - **Lost-list threshold** scales with length: 6 entries at this length.
  - **Lint budgets** apply per 1,000 words (`budget_per_1000` in
    `scripts/lint_prose.py`).
  - **The run is unattended.** The orchestrator logs each gate decision,
    with its runner-up, and each stage's token use in `STATUS.md`. Nothing
    the human sees before reading reveals the angle.
