## Part One revisions (applied before Part Two)

1. Arriba's note: "Pooled: 1,995, on 7 September." It assigns the goods
   from "a climber eleven days down the rope [with] an unassigned hold,
   due at the anchor on 15 September". So 7 Sep + 8 days = 15 Sep, and
   11 + 8 = 19 days of descent.
2. Waska's rule is now **"nothing that rings past tolerance should be
   borne"**, so bearing batch 7 in 3.4 is the rule working.
3. Waska's detector is **pattern, not meaning.** A label is "a mark" to
   it. It knows which marks come down with which weights at this and other
   counters. Four-one-nine's loads "fall like no others filed under marks
   like his". The drum kit stays, "under a mark that at every other
   counter has come down as stone". Waska never knows why.
4. Waska no longer diagnoses its blind spot. It "files [the ring] as
   noise", and "the four hundredths sit in me unweighed, like a load that
   has been announced and not yet sent." **The irony of what it can't feel
   is saved for 4.5.**

## Form conventions

- **Title:** *What the Rope Will Bear* (`# `). Part headings:
  `## Part One: The Button`, `## Part Two: The Label Market`,
  `## Part Three: The Skim`, `## Part Four: The Price of a Question`,
  `## Part Five: The Slack Season`.
- **One paragraph per line** in the file.
- **Section headers.** Every section opens with one italic line, alone as a
  paragraph, and no other scene-break marker.
  - **Segundo:** a receipt, exactly
    `*Received: 1 ask, from <Full Name>. Label: <label>. Fee: 0.05. Pooled: 0.95.*`
    Labels inside a header are not italicised again. Variant used in 2.1
    for asks Arriba returns: `*Received: 5 asks, returned by Arriba. Label:
    the market. Fee: none. Pooled: none.*` Multi-ask receipts keep the
    arithmetic (131 gross → fee 6.55 → pooled 124.45). **For 4.2:** if the
    section-forty pool must reach 131 after the fee, he needs 138 asks
    (138 × 0.95 = 131.1). State the gross or the net plainly.
    **From 5.2 on**, Segundo's sections open with a blank receipt,
    `*Received: . Label: . Fee: . Pooled: .*`. The prose never mentions it.
  - **Waska:** a load figure only, `*0.62 / 0.38*`, with spaces round the
    slash and a true minus sign (−) for a negative margin. Its last header
    is `*0.00 / 1.00*`.
  - **Arriba:** `*<Label>. <price> asks.*`, e.g. `*The market. 1,990 asks.*`
    A question label keeps its question mark.
- **Arriba's sections are its interior, for the reader only.** People in the
  story never see them. What people see is **the board**.
- **The board** (italic paragraph per row, quoted as the board shows it):
  - Dispensing rows: `*The market (broker 0419). Pooled 1,995. Priced
    1,990. Frames and canvas, forty awnings, 540. … Cord, grey, 2,000
    metres, surplus stock, disposal: nothing. Returned 5.*` The broker's
    number is added when two labels must be told apart. **The board never
    names batch seven for the cord.** It says only "surplus stock,
    disposal". The batch is identified in P4 from Waska's ring, not from
    the board.
  - Load rows, at the bottom of the board, where the anchor posts what it
    holds or decides: `*Cold store, one (broker 0419). 0.31 / 0.69. Held
    for weighing.*`, then `Borne.` or `Refused.`
  - Laws: `*Tariff, orbital steel. 0.97 / 0.03.*`
  - Co-signed labels show two broker numbers beside them.
  - Answers to questions: an italic paragraph, one exact sentence where
    possible.
- **Labels** in prose are italicised: *the market*, *our children's
  mornings*, *the dispensary's nights*, *Ayora's clinic*, *The sheds'
  roofs*, *Proaño's packing shed*.
- **Numbers:** asks and prices as numerals with commas; narration spells
  out small counts and times. Load figures always to two decimals.
- **Verse** is set one italic line per paragraph (the rhyme in 1.1).
- **Spanish** is rendered in English. Only "Señora", "Don" and
  "Mamá"/"Papá" stay. No other Spanish or Kichwa beyond the glossary
  (Waska, Arriba, minga). Food takes the plain word: "the long dry biscuits
  Cayambe was known for", lupin beans, toasted maize, pork skins.
- **Em-dashes:** none so far. Keep it at zero.
- **Contrast-shape:** none so far after fixes; about 5 allowed in the whole
  story. Watch for "She was not X. She was Y." in character description
  (one was caught in 2.4).
- **"the way" similes:** 3 in Part One, 0 in Part Two. Prefer "as".
- **Lint:** the human runs `just lint`. The drafter checks the
  banned-phrase, name, em-dash, contrast-shape and "the way" rules by hand
  with `rg`.

## Voice notes

### Segundo's sections: close third, past tense

- **Distance.** Close on Segundo, never inside anyone else. Plain
  judgements in narration ("It was the most important thing he knew");
  self-deception shown as deciding a thing twice ("he had decided it meant
  nothing … and decided it again").
- **Texture.** Warm, dry, crowded with objects, their materials and where
  they came from: the oak bench, the carbon receipt books, the four-metre
  aluminium arch, the paper twist of sugared peanuts, the poncho over a
  grey suit and muddy rubber boots. Every scene gets weather (wind off the
  snow, low gold sun, cold coming down at night), food and a material.
  Long "and" sentences for movement and habit; short ones for judgement.
- **Wit** is deadpan and in dialogue: the baker, Sisa ("She's your
  *wife*."), the pork man's wife, his mother ("You were flat."), Wilmer
  ("I want a receipt."). Segundo is funny by understatement.
- **Dialogue friction.** People answer beside the question. Segundo gives
  the honest short answer and thinks the long one ("Because it's mine," he
  said finally). He hates saying *I don't know* to a client.
- **His interior in Part Two:** envy of Wilmer's ease, "so badly that he
  could feel it in his jaw"; the receipts now feel "as if someone had been
  reading his letters"; "something small and warm and mean" at Wilmer's
  refusal, ashamed and still feeling it. Keep his motives mixed and
  physical.
- **Exposition** only where someone in the scene needs it (a new client,
  nine-year-olds, the stallholders, Proaño explaining the slack to a man
  who half knows).
- **Sample lines:**
  > He could see their headlamps moving inside it, small and yellow, a
  > whole town of people bent over looking for something they'd dropped.

  > It was far too strong for what it weighed. He could think of nothing in
  > the world that was that strong for what it weighed, except one.

### Waska: first person, present tense

- **Perception.** Only tension, load, ring, slack and wear, along all
  36,000 km at once. It knows the world through what pulls: climbers up
  and down, the counterweight, laws (the bill, its costing, its schedule),
  manifests, and the test line the engineers wired to it. It can read a
  **manifest's contents** (a refrigeration plant, roses, fittings), but
  **not purposes or the meaning of labels**: "A label is a mark to me. I
  don't know what its words mean." It doesn't know what a market is.
- **People** are broker numbers on manifests: Segundo is
  **"four-one-nine"**, Wilmer **"one-one-seven-two"**. Since the label
  market, numbers arrive "two at a time, paired on one load", which looks
  to it "like people learning to hide the true direction together".
- **Its theory of Segundo (wrong, never corrected):** his loads "fall like
  no others filed under marks like his", which it reads as a hidden
  direction, so it weighs him at full margin. After two weeks: "I have
  found nothing, which is what I always find with him, and I bear it now
  … I will watch it go and keep looking."
- **The Asamblea:** it recognises the habit of cutting a load into pieces
  to fit under it ("Each piece is honest. The whole is dressed."). It
  bears the pieces, "because each one is under the line, and I can only
  weigh what is sent". This is Proaño's 18-month truck bill seen from
  below.
- **The ring:** batch 7 rings four hundredths more, tolerance is a tenth,
  and its rule is "nothing that rings past tolerance should be borne". It
  files the ring as noise every morning. **It must not state its blind
  spot** before 4.5. Unease is allowed, never a verdict.
- **Refusals** are arithmetic and impersonal: "It is not a judgement on the
  shed. I have no judgements. There is no room on me in October."
- **Want:** slack. "I have never been without a load. I want it as I want
  nothing else." It counts the days ("Thirty-four days to November" on
  28 Sep).
- **Vocabulary and syntax:** pull, bear, carry, hold, let go, fall, slack,
  ring, wear, margin, angle, direction, dressed. Fractions in words
  ("ninety-four hundredths"); decimals only in headers and when quoting
  what the board will say. Short present-tense declaratives;
  one-line paragraphs for turns.
- **Memory is wear** (the 2133 lamps are "a scuff of wear in a joint eleven
  kilometres up"). It can't keep what doesn't load it.
- **Sample lines:**
  > Between the reel and the counterweight I am stretched. I have never not
  > been stretched. That is what I am.

  > I don't know why two numbers would share a load. I know what it looks
  > like. It looks like people learning to hide the true direction
  > together.

### Arriba: short first-person notes, present tense, about 400–550 words

- **Shape.** Header (label, price). First line: counter, broker number,
  label, pool, date filled. Reasoning at the dispensing slot only. Last
  paragraph begins "Dispensed:" and lists items with prices, then
  "Returned to the pool: N".
- **Register.** Courteous, exact, dry, one dry line per note. **No
  grievance, remorse or longing**: the Accord's rules are stated flat
  ("The Accord permits me to read intent, within what plausibly
  answers.").
- **Reasoning.** It reads a label in the light of who asks (the broker's
  history, the pool's members, their employers, the place) and states its
  theory of the asker plainly. It **cannot see Waska's records**. It models
  Waska only from the public board, and gets Waska wrong as Waska gets
  Segundo wrong.
- **Holding back.** At most one bare factual line per note that the reader
  may feel as pointed ("The anchor town reads ribbon."), never explained,
  never followed by a wink. The question of whether the cord was a warning
  stays open.
- **On the page next:** 4.2 is the only Arriba in Part Four, and it appears
  only as the posted answer on the board. 5.3 is a full note. 5.7 is the
  last short note.
- **Sample lines:**
  > A careful man who never pools for a firm, writing *the market* for a
  > pool of that firm's workers. His father wrote what he was buying. I
  > think the son is buying the same thing and declining to write it.

  > The anchor town reads ribbon.

## Arithmetic so far

- **Asks:** 10 per adult on 1 January, lapsing 31 December. Sisa gets **2**

  page in 1.4).
- **Fee:** one in twenty, 0.05 per ask on each receipt.
- **The flour question:** 4 bakeries × 3 = **12** asks. Answer verbatim in
  1.1 ("…milled at Ortega's mill in Cayambe on 30 August 2141…").
- ***The market* (0419):** 2,100 × 1 → fee 105 → pooled **1,995**; priced
  **1,990**: frames and canvas for 40 awnings 540, 60 lamps 300, a cold
  store 1,150, cord 2,000 m nothing; returned **5**, to "three cheese
  sellers, a widower and my wife" (Lourdes is among the first five names in
  his book).
- ***The market* (1172, Wilmer):** 1,600 × 1 → fee 80 → pooled **1,520**;
  priced **1,510**: frames and canvas for 30 stalls 330, 80 lamps 400,
  tables and benches 60 sets 780; returned **10**. No cold store, no cord;
  the frames are stall-sized (2 m).
- **Frames:** Segundo's are 4 m, auction-bay size. The auction has **24
  bays** and took 24 frames; the night market took **16**, each covering
  two 2-m stalls. 60 lamps hang over the whole plaza.
- **Cord:** **10 coils × 200 m.** Grey "of a wet stone", lighter than
  expected, cold, slippery, slides in an ordinary knot, holds in the
  girls' **bunch knot**, burns palms "like tying wire", does not stretch.
- **Arriba's read of the pool:** 1,240 of 2,100 clients work for the grower
  or the anchor. The auditors sample 1 dispensation in 400.
- **Load figures on the page:** the 06:40 `0.62 / 0.38`; steel tariff
  `0.97 / 0.03`; second climber yard `1.06 / −0.06` (March 2141); the cold
  store `0.31 / 0.69`, held 15–28 Sep and borne 28 Sep (posted 29 Sep);
  Proaño's truck subsidy as 18 monthly pieces at `0.94`; *Proaño's packing
  shed* (Wilmer alone) `1.02 / −0.02`, refused because October's climbs
  are full.
- **The cable:** 36,000 km; climbers at 80 km/h, about 19 days up and 19
  down; one ascent a day (the 06:40), so 19 rising at once. The crew chief:
  the lowest ~40 km "moves … sways … shivers … hums"; "up top it's dead
  still".
- **The test line:** batches 5, 6, 7 and 8, 2,000 cycles a day; batch 7
  rings **0.04** more; tolerance **0.10**; 2 million cycles ≈ 1,000 days.
- **The slack season:** 1 Nov – 10 Dec, 40 days, the first replacement
  ever. No climbers; beamed power continues. 2140 rule: laws passed in
  slack take effect provisionally and go to the rope when it returns.
  Proaño's first containers ride the new ribbon in December, on three-year
  contracts "written on it". During slack he flies roses from Quito at
  four times the price.
- **Ages and years:** Segundo 52, licence 0419 for 27 years; Wilmer 29,
  1,600 clients, licence 1172; Proaño "sixty or so" (61), 19 years in the
  Asamblea for Pichincha; the crew chief 20 years on the six-forty. His
  mother's stall has stood by the church steps for fifty years.
