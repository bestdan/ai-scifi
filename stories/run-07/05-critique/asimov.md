# Stage 5 critique: Asimov (the Logician)

**Blind spots:** I read for rules, clues and who-knows-what. I under-read
interiority, texture and the body, so Le Guin, Banks and Lem should judge
whether the family scenes and Waska's voice live. I also assume the puzzle
can be solved, which flatters this outline.

1. **[must-fix] The climax has a cheaper solution the outline never
   rejects.** 35 of 312 sections are flawed, and a section costs 131 asks.
   Ten sections (1,310 asks) find a flaw about 70% of the time, and twenty
   (2,620) about 91%. A smart reader will do this sum before 4.2 ends. One
   hit would also let anyone, the consortium included, order the full
   inspection. Also, 4.2 has Segundo pay 131 asks from "his own family's
   asks", but the household holds about 20 a year. Fix: in 4.2 Segundo and
   Jacinta run the sampling odds and reject them on the page. Candidates:
   a miss is public and reads as "sound"; the consortium certifies only on
   a complete defect map (the 4.1 derating plant); a lone hit gets
   discounted as a rogue section. Pay for section 40 from a small pooled
   label, not the family.

2. **[must-fix] Someone has to be stupid: the Gabon break.** The bible has
   a sister section of batch 7 breaking on Gabon's test line in 2140. No
   scene mentions it. The consortium and Gabon's mind would know, and the
   reader will ask why nobody tested Ayora's batch. Fix: Jacinta says in 4.1
   that the break was logged as a splice fault on a different line, and
   nobody asked Arriba. The answer in 5.3 also has to carry the storm, the
   north-wall voids, the cycle count, the 2-to-3-year horizon and Gabon.
   Its 150 words are the only place "Batch 7" is made explicit, so list
   those facts in the outline.

3. **[must-fix] The climax rule is learned after the midpoint.** That
   a label can be a question, priced at the work of answering and answered
   literally, first appears in 4.2, at about 60% of the story. The
   outline's own first-third list leaves it out. Fix: plant it in 1.1. The
   old woman, or a rose-cutter, posts "Is the well safe?", priced at 30
   asks and answered in one exact sentence. Use the bread story to tie it
   to "never lies".

4. **[must-fix] The middle repeats and stalls.** 2.2 ("history is worth
   something") and 3.1 (Wilmer rents the history) are the same tradeoff.
   Between 2.6 and 3.5 about 13,000 words go by with the reader knowing the
   cord is ribbon and the cast not knowing. Jacinta is in the plaza in 2.2,
   under awnings of replacement-stock cord, and notices nothing. Segundo
   then handles the cord in 2.6 and thinks "nothing". Fix: cut 3.1 into
   2.4. Have Jacinta's hand stop on the awning cord at the end of 2.2, so
   the cord is identified at about 40%. P3 then turns on the lab test
   failing. Several scenes have no turn: 2.3, 3.3 (Segundo leaves without
   an apology), 3.4, 4.6 and 5.5.

5. **[should] The ending's logic and the final question are thin.** In 5.4
   the consortium submits a climb it already knows exceeds the derated
   batch, just so Waska can refuse. Have it submit the original plan on
   31 October, before the answer reaches its engineers, so the refusal
   is natural. Part 5 asks "can people govern themselves?", and the outline
   answers it in 900 words of notice and two laws (5.5). The bible says the
   Asamblea "forgot how to say yes", but no earlier scene shows that. Fix:
   in 2.4 Proaño shows Segundo a bill drafted to pass a refusal, and give
   5.5 a scene of its own.
