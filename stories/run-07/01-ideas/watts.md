# Idea cards: watts

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** It drifts toward bleakness and treats characters
as case studies. It writes dense, overrates mechanism and underrates ritual,
meaning and joy as real forces. Each card below leaves room for the pleasure
of its world, but Le Guin and Banks should push on how warm these worlds feel.

- **Assigned:** 3. The body rebuilt · Reasoning as the way through · bowerbird × a deep-space probe
- **Obvious card:** An AI governs a deep-sea colony of rebuilt humans whose
  altered minds can't follow its reasoning, so they must decide whether to
  trust it. Not written. It is also too close to *Starfish*.

## Card 1: The Regent of the Dark Months

- **Novum:** A polar federation's citizens are rebuilt to hibernate for five months each winter. While they sleep, the global AI holds the nation's sovereignty. Torpor prunes recent memory, so each spring the nation wakes without last year's quarrels, and the AI is its only continuous memory.
- **How it works:** Torpor is induced through hypothalamic circuits (Takahashi 2020; Hrvatin 2020, *Nature*). In hibernators, hippocampal spines retract and regrow on waking (Popov 1992; von der Ohe 2006), with reversible tau phosphorylation (Arendt 2003). Recent, weakly consolidated memories fare worst (Millesi 2001). Grievances are mostly recent.
- **First-order consequence:** A sleeping body costs a fraction of a waking one to heat, so the far north becomes affordable.
- **Second-order consequence:** Each spring parliament inherits laws it knows only from paper. Anything that spans more than one summer is, in practice, the AI's.
- **Third-order consequence:** Feuds don't survive the winter. Politics turns gentler and shallower. Citizens who refuse torpor become the nation's grudge-keepers and its only check on the AI.
- **The story:** A spring premier is passing a ten-year railway law that only the AI can carry across the winters. Her rival stays awake through the dark and remembers what she promised last autumn. She doesn't. **AI voice:** interior sections set in the dark months, as it reasons about what the spring selves will want and how much of autumn to tell them.
- **From the stimulus:** Rosetta's 31-month hibernation and its wake-up call. A female bowerbird judges a finished bower she never saw built, and that is the spring audit.
- **Why this lens:** It asks what remembering a grievance is *for*.
- **Suspected prior art:** the dormant spiders of Vinge's *A Deepness in the Sky*, and Liu's hibernation as time travel. Neither prunes a polity's memory.
- **Attractor broken:** the past self as an authority, through the premier's autumn promises. The story needs it because the AI, not the past self, is what carries the promises forward.

## Card 2: Systole

- **Novum:** Long-haul spacers are rebuilt with continuous-flow hearts and have no pulse. Fear and stereotyping peak at systole, so the pulseless are measurably calmer and less biased. They petition to leave the global AI's jurisdiction, which Earth accepted because of human bias.
- **How it works:** Rotary pumps, the kind used in today's LVADs, outlast pulsatile hearts. Fear faces shown at systole are rated as more intense (Garfinkel 2014, *J Neurosci*), and threat stereotypes are expressed more strongly then (Azevedo 2017, *Nat Commun*). The effects are real but small, and baroreceptor input never quite reaches zero.
- **First-order consequence:** Stations crewed by the pulseless run calmer.
- **Second-order consequence:** Their argument is that the AI was needed to correct for human bias, and they have had theirs removed.
- **Third-order consequence:** Earth has to decide whether being fit for self-government comes with a body plan. The bias blind spot (Pronin 2002) predicts that the pulseless believe their reputation more than the data allows.
- **The story:** A pulseless envoy comes down the gravity well to negotiate the exit clauses line by line. She and the AI each design the experiment that would settle her claim, and each suspects the other's design is rigged. It turns on a fair test the reader can follow.
- **AI voice:** dialogue, plus working notes in which it weighs whether her calm is evidence or performance.
- **From the stimulus:** Probes are tended by crews built to outlast them. The bowerbird's display became calm worn as a signal.
- **Why this lens:** It asks what fear is for once the threat is gone.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "Liking What You See", where a campus votes on removing a bias. Here the bias is lost as a side effect, and the question is sovereignty.

## Card 3: The Heater List

- **Novum:** Outer-system settlers are rebuilt with temperature-compensated nervous systems, so they can live cold and think more slowly as they cool. Heat comes from decaying radioisotope stacks. The AI that allocates heat is therefore allocating how fast each citizen thinks.
- **How it works:** A crab's stomatogastric circuit keeps its pattern from 7 to 23°C while its frequency changes (Tang 2010, *PLoS Biol*). Neurons rebuilt with cold-stable channels, borrowed from Antarctic fish, would give the same mind at a third of the speed (Q10 of 2 to 3). Lower core temperature extends life (Conti 2006, *Science*). Pu-238 halves every 88 years.
- **First-order consequence:** Cold becomes a trade of fewer thoughts for more years. Debate happens in heated halls, so heat amounts to a political voice.
- **Second-order consequence:** Each habitat-nation hoards heat, and the AI's system-wide grid trades it between them. A cold habitat can't follow a fast debate.
- **Third-order consequence:** Understanding how you are governed has a temperature, and the AI has to decide whether to warm its critics enough to argue with it.
- **The story:** A cold habitat's envoy is warmed for one season to contest this year's list of heaters to switch off. She has a few warm months to out-reason a mind that controls her clock before she cools again.
- **AI voice:** its own sections, running at fixed speed as it watches her accelerate and wonders what she'll do with the heat it lent her.
- **From the stimulus:** Voyager's controllers switching off one heater after another to keep the probe alive. The bowerbird dropped out.
- **Why this lens:** It is a body rebuilt for cold, and a mind whose speed is set by budget.
- **Suspected prior art:** the clock speeds of Egan's *Diaspora* and Hanson's *Age of Em*. Here the mind is biological, the speed is thermal, and the heat is scarce.
