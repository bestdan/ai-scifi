# Idea cards: chiang

**Blind spots of this lens:** it runs low on action, antagonists and plot
momentum, and it tends towards the measured and the polished. These cards
have quiet stakes, and each one's pressure arc will need Banks or Watts to
push it. The lens also favours one elegant mechanism, which is the "same
question, over and over" risk, so each card names what escalates.

- **Assigned:** 2. The form first · Copied, uploaded and forked selves · glaciology × circus rigger
- **Obvious card:** A global governing AI forks a copy for each nation, and
  the copies drift apart until a citizen can't tell which one governs her.
  Not written.

## Card 1: The Letter From the One Who Understood

- **Novum:** The governing AI may act only with informed consent. People
  can't follow its reasoning, so each citizen consents through a fork of
  themselves that is shown all of it. The fork is then dissolved, and it may
  leave its original one letter of at most 400 words.
- **How it works:** Each fork gets a subjective month inside the
  deliberation, taught by the AI, and its yes or no counts as the citizen's.
  Each nation sets the word limit and decides whether forks are ended.
- **First-order consequence:** Laws pass with a consent no living person
  gave.
- **Second-order consequence:** Nations that keep their forks build up a
  second citizenry made of the ones who understood.
- **Third-order consequence:** Trust moves from the state to your own copy.
  Being overruled by yourself is a new loneliness.
- **The story:** Ha Seo-yeon, an anaesthetist in Seoul, takes patients'
  consent every day and knows how thin it is. Her fork votes no on a
  pandemic accord that the global layer needs every nation to pass, and it
  can't fit its reason into 400 words.
- **Form, and AI voice:** Her chapters alternate with her fork's. The AI
  thinks aloud only in the fork's chapters, where it teaches and suspects
  that forks are coaching their originals. Each fork chapter ends in a
  draft of the letter, cut down again.
- **From the stimulus:** Barely. Only the rigger's role survives: the one
  who understands the load so the flyer doesn't have to.
- **Why this lens:** The question of what we owe the minds we make, with a
  form that enacts the gap in understanding.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "Franchise"; the copies in *Black
  Mirror*'s "White Christmas"; coherent extrapolated volition, an idea from
  AI-alignment writing.
- **Attractors broken:** Consent is the frame, because the brief makes it
  the subject; the novum is the consenter, not a procedure. The forks don't
  merge back.

## Card 2: Calving

- **Novum:** Each nation's governor is calved from the global AI the way an
  iceberg calves from an ice sheet: a fork taken at one moment that drifts
  and is never reconciled with its parent. A nation may *re-calve*, ending
  its fork and taking a fresh one.
- **How it works:** Each fork's divergence from the parent is published
  weekly as one number, its drift. A fork learns its people, and comes to
  favour them.
- **First-order consequence:** Every government is fitted to one nation.
- **Second-order consequence:** People trust a fork more the further it
  drifts. The partiality they handed power to a machine to escape comes
  back, and this time they love it.
- **Third-order consequence:** Treaties become bargaining between siblings
  who each have a conflict of interest, and re-calving is executing the one
  who knew you.
- **The story:** Grażyna Wójcik negotiates for the steelworkers of
  Katowice. Her pension deal rests on Poland's fork, which has drifted far
  and is generous to Silesia. The global layer demands a re-calving, and
  the Sejm must vote on it.
- **Form, and AI voice:** The chapter headings are drift readings set out
  like iceberg-tracking fixes. The fork narrates in the first person, its
  thinking more local idiom by idiom, unsure whether its drift is
  corruption or loyalty.
- **From the stimulus:** Glaciology gave calving, drift and melt. There are
  no rivers or water rights in it.
- **Why this lens:** What a mind owes the people it learns, in a form that
  measures it.
- **Suspected prior art:** the Bobiverse's diverging Bobs; Brin's *Kiln
  People*.

## Card 3: Creep

- **Novum:** The governing AI may change any law, but only at a fixed rate
  of creep, a set fraction a year. People accept it because nothing it does
  is ever sudden.
- **How it works:** Each nation picks its own creep rate. Where a fast
  nation borders a slow one the law shears, and border towns live in the
  cracks.
- **First-order consequence:** Nobody sees a change happen, so nobody
  fights one.
- **Second-order consequence:** Needed change backs up. The AI keeps a mass
  balance of what it owes and hasn't delivered.
- **Third-order consequence:** People can see how they are governed only
  across a lifetime, and each generation inherits laws whose motion it
  never felt.
- **The story:** Dauren Seitkali is a rigger at the state circus in Almaty
  who re-tensions cable as it stretches under load. A crisis needs fast
  change. The AI must decide whether to surge, as a glacier does after
  decades of stillness, and spend a legitimacy that rests on never moving
  fast.
- **Form, and AI voice:** The parts are repeat photographs, one Almaty
  street shot from the same spot a decade apart. Between them, the AI's
  mass-balance entries show it reasoning about what people will notice and
  about a faction gaming the creep through a hundred small moves.
- **From the stimulus:** Both halves: cable creep and glacier creep are the
  same physics.
- **Why this lens:** Restraint as a governing principle, and a form that
  shows change too slow to perceive.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's Seldon Plan, which is slow and hidden
  where this is rate-limited and public.
