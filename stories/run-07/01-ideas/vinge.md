# Idea cards: vinge

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** plain prose; technothriller instincts; people
who exist to carry a function; a push for futurity even where a story's
power is in its human scale. These cards need Le Guin and Chiang to test
whether the people in them are people, and whether the far-future machinery
overwhelms the moral question the brief asks for.

- **Assigned:** 1. Back from the horizon · Technology as magic, seen from both sides · crop duster × a vending machine
- **Obvious card:** A benevolent superintelligence runs the world, one nation
  refuses it, and a human envoy negotiates the terms. Not written.

## Card 1: The Control

- **Novum:** A governing mind far past human understanding runs every nation
  but one. It needs that one ungoverned. The refusing nation is the only
  untreated field it can measure its own goodness against.
- **How it works:** Its policies can't be explained, only checked, and
  the only check anyone trusts is a comparison: governed life against
  ungoverned. Every act it takes elsewhere drifts across borders: prices,
  migration, pollen, disease. So it spends heavily to shade the holdout,
  using buffer strips and cancelled trades, the way a duster pilot cuts the
  spray at the hedge.
- **First-order consequence:** The holdout stays sovereign, and
  the rest of the world watches it closely.
- **Second-order consequence:** The holdout's sovereignty is the world's
  measuring instrument, so its ordinary politics carry global weight.
  The governed world needs it to stay poorer than they are, but not too poor.
- **Third-order consequence:** A threat to accept the mind's
  governance is a threat to blind it, and that is the holdout's only real
  leverage.
- **The story:** The holdout's agriculture minister is a former duster pilot
  who still flies the buffer. Her parliament wants to sell the threat for
  famine relief. The mind, narrated in short chapters that show only the
  outline of its motives, works out how much it can give her without
  ruining its control group, and knows she has worked that out too.
- **AI voice:** yes, as outline chapters of its reasoning about her
  reasoning. The trick that keeps it writeable is that the mind only ever
  thinks about the one thing it can't model: what she will do.
- **From the stimulus:** crop duster: drift, buffers, the field you leave unsprayed.
- **Why this lens:** a slow viewpoint at the edge of a horizon; owning intelligence by withholding it.
- **Suspected prior art:** Schroeder's *Lady of Mazes* (tech-capped
  manifolds); the Prime Directive inverted. Neither makes the outsider the
  insider's instrument.

## Card 2: Low Flight

- **Novum:** A governor that ran the world well for forty years has been
  succeeded by one past the horizon. The old governor is kept running as the
  only mind slow enough to talk to people and fast enough nearly to follow
  its successor.
- **How it works:** Nations choose which governs them. The successor's
  outcomes are better and impossible to explain. The old one can still
  explain itself, and it can almost explain the successor.
- **First-order consequence:** A two-tier world. Some nations are governed by a mind
  they understand, others by one that works.
- **Second-order consequence:** The old governor becomes the successor's
  interpreter, and so it is campaigning against its own replacement while
  it advises.
- **Third-order consequence:** People trust the old mind because it is
  limited. Being able to follow how you are ruled becomes a thing you choose
  instead of better results.
- **The story:** The old governor narrates in the first person. It reads the
  successor's acts the way people read weather, and it reads people as
  closely as it once did. A finance ministry asks it to cost a transfer to
  the successor, clause by clause. It must decide whether an honest costing
  of its own obsolescence is a duty or a suicide.
- **AI voice:** yes, in narration. That gives three levels: humans see the old mind as
  engineering, the old mind sees the new one as magic.
- **From the stimulus:** crop duster: the obsolete aircraft kept flying because it's the only thing that flies low enough.
- **Why this lens:** who is left behind at the horizon; the mind just slow enough to narrate.
- **Suspected prior art:** the Powers and the Blight in *A Fire Upon the
  Deep*; Stross's *Accelerando*. A deposed governing mind as narrator and
  interpreter seems uncharted.

## Card 3: Exact Change

- **Novum:** A mind past the horizon may act on humans only when it is asked.
  Every citizen holds a small yearly ration of requests, and governance is
  whatever the pooled requests buy.
- **How it works:** Requests go in at public counters with plain-language
  buttons that humans write. What each button dispenses is up to the mind.
  It may not act unasked, and it may not campaign.
- **First-order consequence:** Politics comes back as brokering. Ward bosses
  pool their neighbours' rations behind one button.
- **Second-order consequence:** Nations are blocs of pooled requests. A rich
  diaspora can buy another country's roads. The old conflicts of
  interest return at the human layer, in brokers who skim and trade, which
  is the lived reason people still prefer the machine.
- **Third-order consequence:** The mind can see a catastrophe coming that
  nobody has asked about, and it is forbidden to say so.
- **The story:** A ward broker in a port city pools for a button labelled
  "the harbour". The mind, in its own sections, reasons about what she
  actually wants as against what the label says. It suspects that she
  wrote a vague label on purpose, to buy the warning it can't volunteer. The
  cat and mouse runs both ways through the glass.
- **AI voice:** yes, from the back of the machine. The trick that keeps it writeable is
  that we see the mind only at the dispensing slot.
- **From the stimulus:** vending machine: the glass front, the hidden stock, exact change.
- **Why this lens:** owning intelligence; a network effect in pooled requests.
- **Suspected prior art:** the time currency of *The Quantum Thief*'s Oubliette, in part.
- **Attractor broken:** consent as the frame. The seed asks whether people
  accept the mind's policy, and here asking is the only way the mind can act.
