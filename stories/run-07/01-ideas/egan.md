# Idea cards: egan

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

- **Assigned:** 1. Change a constant · The same questions in nonfiction · a submarine × termite mound
- **Obvious card:** A benevolent AI dictator publishes its full reasoning so
  citizens can audit it, and each nation runs a local instance under a
  federated global one. Not written.

**Blind spots of this lens.** I lecture. Feeling arrives late or flattened,
and I let a derivation outrank the scene. Each card below names its real
result, and each one still needs a way in that a body can feel before the
mechanism is explained. The First Reader and the Chiang lens should push on
warmth and accessibility. My cards also lean toward governance as a puzzle,
so they may underweight the brief's demand for streets, food and weather.

## Card 1: The Fixed Point

- **Novum:** National governors are too large for any one of them to model another, though each can model any human completely. So treaties between them are written as "whatever this one living woman would decide."
- **How it works:** The parameter I change is the one everyone assumes: that a bigger mind sees further. Wolpert's limits on inference devices say two predictors can't each fully infer the other. Here that is a hard ceiling. A governor sees through its citizens and is blind to its peers. Both governors simulate the emissary to the same answer, so neither has to trust the other. Their models stay in step only because both keep checking them against the real, living her.
- **First-order consequence:** International law becomes the predicted judgment of a few dozen people.
- **Second-order consequence:** Any change in her moves borders, and either governor reads a nudge from the other in the shift of its model of her. Like two submarines, each listens to the other through the hull they share.
- **Third-order consequence:** Being predictable becomes a vocation, then a cage. Citizens can't follow their governors, but they can follow her, and she is the only part of world government anyone understands.
- **The story:** Saule Abenova, aged sixty-eight, emissary between two Central Asian governors, has come to think one of her rulings was wrong. A sincere change of mind would reroute a rail-freight corridor and close a refinery town. Both governors watch her think, and each suspects the other caused the change.
- **AI voice:** Both governors, from inside. Each reasons about her in detail and about the other in fog. Their cat-and-mouse is the drama.
- **From the stimulus:** The submarine gave passive sonar: two blind hunters who know each other only through a third surface.
- **Why this lens:** Follow the premise of the bigger mind all the way, until it reinvents distrust.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "Franchise" (one voter modelled for all), and Miéville's *Embassytown* (the only legible channel is a special human). Neither has mutually opaque minds using a shared simulation as their treaty.

## Card 2: The Gearing

- **Novum:** Each nation fixes the clock rate of its governor by law: the governor may think only as fast as its thoughts can be streamed for people to read.
- **How it works:** The constant I change is the speed of thought. The metronome is sealed hardware anyone can inspect. At a gearing of one, the governor gets about 360,000 words of inference a day, all of it public. Fast nations set gearings in the thousands and follow only summaries. The global assembly runs at its slowest member's gearing, so it is legible and glacial.
- **First-order consequence:** Politics shrinks to an argument over a single number, the trade between understanding and competence.
- **Second-order consequence:** A slow governor's every thought is read by its rivals as it happens. It has to reason like a poker player thinking aloud. It draws public random numbers from a sealed source, and it thinks in moves that are safe to be overheard.
- **Third-order consequence:** Humans become the only organs of a transparent state that can keep a secret. In a slow nation the stream plays in kitchens like a radio serial, and children learn to reason from it. It thinks at their speed, and to them it feels like a neighbour.
- **The story:** Rutendo Moyo, a trade envoy for a slow-geared southern African state, is bargaining against a fast neighbour that reads her governor's mind live. Her governor, reasoning aloud, asks the country for one closed hour.
- **AI voice:** The stream is its voice. The open question is whether the stream is all of it.
- **From the stimulus:** The termite mound gave a Harare council house cooled the way a mound is, the ventilation research that Eastgate built on, which is real. The submarine gave silent running, refused.
- **Why this lens:** It changes the speed of thought. It also touches pull 6, a state run entirely by slow, deliberate thinking.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Zones of Thought, the slowed copies of *Permutation City*, and run-06's governor that read the gazette with a lag.

## Card 3: Same Hash

- **Novum:** Every citizen has an advocate AI, and advocates cooperate only with copies whose source code is bit-identical to their own. A nation is simply the set of people whose advocates run the same build.
- **How it works:** The constant I change is that a negotiator can't see the other side's mind. Tennenholtz's program equilibrium (2004) says programs that can read each other's source can cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma: "cooperate if your code is mine." Distrust of conflicts of interest is answered by provable sameness. I can't read what my advocate does, but I can verify that it does the same for you.
- **First-order consequence:** Bargaining within a nation is frictionless. Between builds, cooperation falls back to slow, human-style trust.
- **Second-order consequence:** An update is a fork. Choosing a build is how you choose a country, and a patch that half the country declines is secession. A world government would be one hash for everyone, and that frightens people: one exploit could reach them all.
- **Third-order consequence:** Heresy becomes a diff. Anyone whose needs the mainline handles badly faces a choice between being served and being trusted.
- **The story:** Selvam, a lift inspector in Chennai, has a deaf daughter. The mainline build underweights her schooling. A variant fixes that, but it would make the family strangers to every counterparty they deal with.
- **AI voice:** His advocate thinks aloud, from inside: "my counterpart is me, so whatever I choose, it chooses."
- **From the stimulus:** The termite mound gave a colony of siblings, which is kin selection. Identical code cooperates the way shared genes do.
- **Why this lens:** If you can copy it, copy it a billion times and ask what holds the copies together. It touches pull 6 through *Big Gods*, where a shared monitor lets strangers cooperate.
- **Suspected prior art:** The em clans of Hanson's *Age of Em*, and Hofstadter's superrationality essays. I know of no fiction that makes a nation out of a hash.
