## The one thing
Segundo is a label-maker, and the story never has him spend that skill when
it matters. 4.1 tells him the test pieces came from the north-wall rack and
that those are the ones that ring. A man who turned *the school* into *our
children's mornings* would write a narrow label, such as "Are the sections
cured at the north wall of batch seven's oven sound?", at perhaps a sixth of
the 40,872. That saves the clinic, and a system-minded reader will think of it
before the page does. Close it on the page: they try it, and Arriba's price
shows it cannot locate the wall without inspecting the whole batch, or the
rack map is Arriba's half and costs as much to read. Otherwise the sacrifice
reads as a failure of craft.

## Notes

1. **3.3, the fatigue rig (structural, rigor):** It runs "one a second".
   Libreville's piece parted at 1,710,000 cycles, about twenty days at that
   rate, and the offcut arrived 15 September against a 30 October splice.
   "I can't make a night into three years" is true, but she had weeks. State
   a limit on the rig (heat, strain rate) or a reason she didn't run it.
2. **1.1 and 3.3, "button" (line-level):** The section is titled "The Button"
   and Segundo says "fill buttons", but the page never says what one is. The
   first reader never learned. Gloss it once in the Quishpe scene, or use the
   plain word.
3. **1.2, Waska's opening (line-level):** The voice arrives before its
   identity. Put "rope" in the first sentence.
4. **Waska's sections (structural):** Each repeats the same beats: the town as
   a rounding, an Asamblea bill, four-one-nine's angle, the ring put down. Give
   each one new fact. Only 2.4's refusal at 1.02 turns.
5. **2.4, the eighteen-piece bill (line-level):** It pays off only loosely at
   Proaño's "dressed up" line in 5.6. Tie the freight relief to that shape, or cut.
6. **1.5, the audit (line-level):** "one dispensation in four hundred" is the
   line the first reader skimmed. Make it something to hold.

## What to protect

- "The logs say what the oven did. They don't say what it made."
- Rules 4 and 5 on the lab wall, read three times in the night.
- "I remember a thing by feeling where it has worn me," and the flat place in
  the reel bearings.
- "ninety-two parts of a smaller number, which is a hundred and four."
- "No fee," and the clerk changing the row unasked.
- The 8,960,000,000 price and "Somebody does that every year."

## Blind spot disclosure
I hunt loopholes a general reader may never see. The north-wall label and the
rig speed may matter only to system-minded readers; weigh them against the
cost of explaining them. I also under-weight the people.
