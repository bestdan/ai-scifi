# Comprehension answers: "What the Rope Will Bear", draft 2

Written from memory, without rereading the draft.

## 1. What is this story about, in two sentences?

Segundo Farinango is an honest small-town broker who pools people's yearly "asks" so they can request things from a mind at the top of a space-elevator cable. He comes to suspect that a new batch of cable ribbon is flawed, and he spends his whole parish's savings on a question that proves it. The question saves the cable and costs the town its clinic, and it costs him his wife's goodwill and part of his own good name.

## 2. What does the protagonist stand to gain or lose, and what happens if they fail?

If he asks and is wrong, he loses the clinic his wife has wanted for years and the trust of the whole parish. He may also ruin the valley's rose contracts for nothing. If he doesn't ask, the new ribbon goes into the bottom of the rope. In two or three years it parts, the climbers on that length fall, and the anchor and the valley's living go with them. He asks and he is right. He loses the clinic, his wife's warmth for a while, and the rose business at half rate. The proof comes with a price: Proaño then asks him to write a label for a farm-funded clinic, and he does.

## 3. Why did the people or powers who set this situation up do it that way?

After the years of two budgets and the famine caused by the mind's unasked warning, the country split the power in two. The rope can only say no. Arriba can only answer what is asked. No one holds both halves, so no one can take the whole thing over and run it for money. The Accord gave every adult ten asks a year. Brokers pool them. The ribbon is made by one half and tested by the other, so each checks the other. The cost is that nothing gets said unless someone pays to ask.

## 4. What did you learn late that changed how you saw the earlier part, if anything?

Several things. The cord from the awnings was new ribbon from a bad batch, and Jacinta knew it the moment she touched it. The "ring" the rope kept noticing and putting down was the first warning. It was inside tolerance, so it could not be refused. The test pieces had been cut from the bad end of the oven, so they rang. The rope's long suspicion of Segundo was wrong all along: the honest one was the one it was weighing hardest. And Dolores's story about the payslips and the famine made me see why everyone, her especially, trusts the rope and fears Arriba.

## 5. What happened at the end, and why?

The parish gave its December asks to Segundo on his word. Dolores refused, and so did the Pilataxis. He waived his fee to cover the price. Arriba inspected all 312 sections and said batch seven was not sound: 35 sections, in six runs, had voids from the March 2138 solar storm. The consortium lowered the batch's rating by the share of bad sections. The rope then refused the proof climb, because the same load was now over the limit. The new ribbon was taken out of the foot, and the valley will fly at half load for two years. Proaño asked Segundo to write the label for a clinic funded by the farms ("Ayora's clinic, from the farms"), and he wrote it. The slack season began, the windows stopped rattling, and the rope hung loose at the bottom for the first time.

## 6. Who is each named character, in one line each?

- **Segundo Farinango:** broker 0419, the honest one who gives a receipt for everything. He is 52 and plays the tuba badly.
- **Dolores Farinango:** his mother. She sells lupin beans, cashed only one of the double payslips, and trusts the rope and not Arriba.
- **Ernesto Farinango:** his father, a former fixer, in the parish home. He sold the December asks for Proaño's road and says people trust a face and not a receipt.
- **Lourdes Imbaquingo:** his wife, who runs the dispensary and wants the clinic.
- **Sisa Farinango:** their daughter, who turns sixteen on the 26th. She works at the auction and is embarrassed by her father's receipts.
- **Jacinta Ulcuango:** his cousin, a strain reader at the anchor with twin nine-year-old boys. She is the one who recognises the cord.
- **Wilmer Cuzco:** a younger broker who rents other brokers' histories. He is charming and keeps asking for a receipt.
- **Gustavo Proaño:** owner of the big rose farm and a member of the Asamblea. He is affable, and he asks Segundo to write the label for the farms' clinic.
- **Rosaura Quishpe:** an old cheese seller who pools for "our children's mornings".
- **Luz Caiza:** a nineteen-year-old cutter from Tabacundo who trusts him without being told why.
- **The clerk:** runs the board at the counter, wears a hair net, and quietly sets up the meeting row.
- **The crew chief:** Proaño's friend on the six-forty, with a son on the down runs.
- **The baker:** the one with the flour answer.
- **The pork man's wife:** the stallholder who complains about the cold store.
- **The fifth-bench broker:** the widower with the white moustache.
- **The certifier:** the thin man from the station who rewrites the plan.
- **The rope / Waska:** the mind that can only say no.
- **Arriba:** the mind at the top that can only answer what is asked.

## 7. What are you still unsure about?

- Whether Lourdes will forgive him, and what she said to Dolores at the board.

- Why the story gives so much weight to the rope's wish to be slack.
- The exact ordering of the old events (2068, 2097, 2098, 2101, 2104).
- What "farm label" does to how Arriba reads him.

## 8. Which words or terms didn't you know from the story itself, and where did you get their meaning, if you got it?

- **"Button" / "button history":** never learned. I guessed it was the record of past asks.
- **"Ask":** from Segundo's talk with Luz. Ten a year, each yours, used up on the thirty-first.
- **"Label," "pool," "broker":** from Segundo's explanation to Luz and from watching the board.
- **"Held for weighing":** from Jacinta, who said the rope sits on his loads.
- **"0.31 / 0.69":** from the cousin on the tour. The first number is how much of what the rope will bear, and the second is what's left.
- **"Strain reader":** from the cousin ("we turn the rope's numbers into Spanish").
- **"Accord":** from Arriba's own section, loosely. It binds Arriba to answer only what is asked.
- **"Minga" and "December pool":** from the lines about the parish's yearly shared label, though "minga" I took from context.
- **"Offcut":** from Jacinta.
- **"Ring":** from the test shed. The hum a section makes under the pull.
- **"Slack season":** from Proaño at the bakery.
- **"Derated":** from the explanation of rule five, and it was fine once Jacinta did the sum.
- **"Waska":** the old word for rope, from Segundo.

## 9. What does the story's central change mean for people beyond its main characters?

For the valley: half the freight for two years, so half the roses and half the work for the whole parish. The crews get half pay by a law that nothing weighed, and the exporters get freight relief. Luz is moved to night packing and has her name written in a book. The parish has no clinic this year, and the farms will fund one with their name on it. For the wider country: the rope showed that it can say no to a number it could not feel. The question shows what a straight question costs. Anyone who can pay finds out the truth, and almost nobody can afford it. A good name has become something people can sell. And the habitats at the top lose some of their roses, while the other two tethers (Libreville and Kalimantan) now know about the batch they tested.
