# First Reader report: "What the Rope Will Bear"

I read it once, straight through, to the end of Part Five. There was no drafter's-notes section where I stopped.

## Where I got lost

1. **The opening line of italics (the "Received: 1 ask…" lines).** I didn't know what they were. The next page showed Segundo writing exactly that on a receipt, so I worked it out there. The blank versions in Part Five ("Received: . Label: . Fee: . Pooled: .") I read as "no receipts being given now." I think that's right, but I was guessing.

2. **The first section that says "Ninety tonnes leave the reel and I take them, because I am the rope".** The heading "0.62 / 0.38" meant nothing to me. For a paragraph or two I didn't know who "I" was. The sentence about being the rope told me. The numbers only made sense much later, when Segundo's cousin explains a row on the board ("1.06 / −0.06 … six parts too many"). Until then I took them as a kind of fraction the rope reports. I worked out the rest in pieces from the cousin and from Jacinta.

3. **"Four-one-nine pulls sideways."** I didn't know who or what 419 was. I worked it out from Segundo's card (licence 0419) and from "a broker's licence number." I still wasn't clear why the rope was suspicious of him. I took "he's too different from the others, so he must be hiding something" from the rope's own words. It struck me as a strange thing for a rope to think about a man who writes receipts.

4. **The Arriba section after the market label ("Two words. The Accord allows six…").** This was the hardest stretch for me.
   - I followed that it was reading Segundo's two words as meaning the flower auction. I didn't follow why it would price "frames and canvas for forty awnings" and then add 2,000 metres of "cord" as "disposal."
   - "Dispose of it by dispensing it" and "an unassigned hold" on a climber "eleven days down the rope" lost me. I didn't understand where the goods were physically, or why a pool filled on 7 September produced a delivery on 15 September.
   - The line "I made the replacement ribbon… Nobody has asked me about it" I felt as ominous, but I didn't know what it was warning me about.
   - I worked out what the cord was only when Segundo felt it in the plaza ("except one") and Jacinta pocketed a length. I understood it fully in the lab scene.

5. **The Asamblea "eighteen pieces" paragraph in the second rope section.** I followed that the Asamblea cuts big loads into small honest-looking ones. I never saw why it mattered to the story. I took it as colour and moved on.

6. **Pilataxi.** The Pilataxis' pickup takes Lourdes's sick baby to Cayambe. A Pilataxi family sold earrings in the bread year. Then "Pilataxi was the big farm's foreman." I wasn't sure whether these were one family or the same name used for different people. I never settled it.

7. **The 1.9 / 2097 numbers versus the later 1.04.** I got the idea, though not the arithmetic. The rope refuses anything over 1.00. I followed Jacinta's explanation of why the climber was refused on the 31st ("ninety-two parts of a smaller number"), but I did it by nodding along, not by checking it.

8. **The sampling talk at Segundo's bench (twenty sections, one in ten, nine times in ten).** I followed the point: asking about a few sections would prove nothing and could look reassuring. I did not follow the numbers.

9. **The bench scene where "the fifth bench" is added to Wilmer's number.** I wasn't sure at first what "pool alone or jointly" had done to the old man. I worked it out from his waving the receipt. Even so, I wasn't sure what the scene was for.

## Where I got bored

- **Part One, from Segundo's explanation to the girl from Tabacundo through the school trip.** There's a lot of explaining how the counter works, what a button is, what asks are, what the rope and Arriba are for. I was patient with it because I liked the voice, but I was waiting for something to go wrong. The first time I felt anything pulling me forward was the rope's own first section.
- **The school tour at the anchor.** The string demonstration is nice and I liked "it can't push." But the cousin's board, the brass plate and Segundo's speech all say the same thing a second time. I was waiting for the hook to land, and I didn't yet know what I was meant to be worried about.
- **The cord-test night, the first two thirds.** The numbers, the weave measurements and the rig counting were the slowest part for me. I was waiting for her to find out whether the cord was bad. Her relief, and then "I don't like it," woke me up again.
- **The rope's sections, by the third time.** Each one repeats the four hundredths on batch seven ("seven rings… I put it down with the noise"). By the second one I was ahead of the rope and wanted it to do something with the information. By the third I was waiting for the voice to change. It did change, in the last two, and the last one's refusal got to me.
- **Wilmer's second and third pitches.** I liked him, but by the end I felt I'd heard the point already.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The mother's biscuit tin and the payslips** (Part Three, under the stall in the rain). The two payslips with different stamps, and "a woman who cashes both is a thief; a woman who cashes neither is a fool." Her saying "I trust the rope. The other one can stay up there." I was delighted by "In my day we called that a budget."
- **Her right arm "stops there… She serves beans with her left."** This is where the road and the missing tank became real to me. Before that, the father's argument was a puzzle. After it, I was on the mother's side and furious on her behalf.
- **The father under the lemon tree: "Receipts are what you give people who don't know you."** And the remembered fiesta right after it, with the tuba note alone in the silent plaza and the mother with her hands in her lap. That was the best scene in the story for me. It made me stop and look at Segundo's whole habit of writing things down differently.
- **Luz Caiza: "I don't need to know. You wrote it down."** I was moved, and it came as a surprise from a character I'd mostly seen in the background.
- **Lourdes touching her screen six times without looking at him, and Sisa's "Two."**
- **The 8,960,000,000 price, and Segundo laughing.** Surprised and delighted.
- **The crowd waking up on its own before four in the morning, and Dolores handing Lourdes the cup of hot purple drink.** Lourdes took it without looking at it, and neither woman spoke.
- **The rope holding the climber and the windows not rattling, then "Only their number has changed… I could not have found it."** This hit me hard because I'd been hearing the rope say "if something were wrong, I would feel it" for most of the story, and here it finds it was wrong about itself.
- **The clapping-game rhyme at the start.** I liked it and was glad it came back.
- **"The only tuba in the province who played every note in the right place and none of them at the right time."**
- **The cord.** I guessed early that it mattered, but I didn't guess *how* until Segundo said "He could think of nothing in the world that was that strong for what it weighed, except one."

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about **Segundo** most, mostly through his mother and his father. I cared about **Dolores** (his mother) more than I expected, and about **Lourdes**, who says very little and whose silences carry weight. **Jacinta** I came to care about at the bench with the cord. **Luz** I cared about in small flashes. And I cared about **the rope**, which I didn't expect. I wanted it to have its slack.

I'm not sure I'd have kept reading past page 2 if I hadn't been told to. Page 2 is the bakery, the flour and the mountain. I liked the cold bench, the baker and his "Ortega" noise, and the mountain at dawn with the cutters' headlamps like "a whole town of people bent over looking for something they'd dropped." But I was reading a world being explained, and I didn't yet know what was at stake. If I'd stopped at the end of the first page of explaining the button to Luz, I'd have put it down as "nice but a lot of rules." What kept me was the rope's voice and, a bit further on, the cord. After the first rope section I would have kept reading anywhere. So: yes, but only just, and mainly because of the voice.

## The question I'm left with

Did the mind at the top (Arriba) send that cord on purpose? It can only answer what it's asked, and it says it only "disposed" of surplus. But it also says "nobody has asked me about it," and it sent the two thousand metres to the one man who would ask. The story never says whether that was luck, tidiness or a kind of hint. I'm also left with batch eight: it "rings true," and nobody is going to pay to ask. And I'd like to know whether Lourdes ever looks at him the way she did before.
