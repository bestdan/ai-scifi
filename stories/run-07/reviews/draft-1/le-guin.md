## The one thing

Make the cost to people elsewhere visible. Dolores says of the 2098 famine
that "People died there." The line is true, and nobody looks at it again.
Every household in Ayora grieves its own tripled bread, and no one in the
story asks who starved in the Sahel while the parish learned to distrust the
voice that warned them. Omelas has its child in the cellar. This world has
one, and the story walks past it. The habitats that buy the roses and the
cutters who pick them are in the same position: they pay and we hardly see
them. Give the Sahel one scene, or one person who is not a Farinango. A
cutter's rest day (Luz's, "the first in eleven") could carry it. This does
not make the story grim. It makes the bargain honest, and the Accord ought
to be the ambiguous utopia the bible says it is.

## Notes

1. **Structural. Part One, the licence speech to Luz ("On the first of
   January, ten asks land on your screen") and the school tour.** Two
   lectures, with the veto history delivered a second time by Segundo on the
   gallery. The first reader called it homework. Characters who have lived
   with this don't explain it, and the clapping rhyme already carries more
   than the speech does. Cut the speech to what Luz doesn't know, and put the
   rest in the Señora Quishpe scene, where a label is rewritten
   (*the school* to *our children's mornings*). That scene dramatises the
   rule.
2. **Structural. Language.** Six words per label is a grammar. The culture's
   idiom shows it ("A question is a smell", the rhyme, "Arriba is only the
   shop"), but only three or four times. What can't these people say? They
   can't say a want aloud without paying. I'd like more proverb, more
   "don't-ask" courtesy, and children's games of label-making. The Sahel, if
   added, would sit well with this.
3. **Structural. Weight.** Proaño funds the clinic ("from the farms") four
   pages after Lourdes loses it. The reader's grief is paid off by the man
   whose interests the question harmed, who has also just taken a freight
   relief law. That is ambiguous and good. But the clinic is back by Friday.
   Let Lourdes's loss last longer, as the oxygen machine is already "at
   least that", or let the label cost Segundo something besides his fee.
4. **Line-level. Rope sections.** "Five rings true. Six rings true. Eight
   rings true. Seven rings four hundredths more" repeats five times. Keep
   the first, the last, and one in between. The first reader skimmed the
   middle.
5. **Line-level. Terms.** "pasillo" (the record Dolores brings) is not in
   the bible's Glossary. Use "waltz" or "record". Stage 7's pre-check
   applies.
6. **Line-level. Wilmer's thread.** His joint-signing scheme ends in one
   funny line ("Two brokers and one ask... a budget") and then nothing. It
   deserves a payoff in Part Five beyond "I don't take a fee."

## What to protect

- Dolores and the two payslips: "I cashed the first one, and I kept the
  second one, and I never cashed it." It is the best answer to *why* in the
  story, and a woman says it over beans.
- "Her right arm stops there." This is who paid for the road.
- The ward meeting, where neighbours watch each other touch their screens:
  gathering, not fight.
- "A whole town of people bent over looking for something they'd dropped."
- The tuba, which Segundo plays "every note in the right place and none of
  them at the right time."
- The rope's "I remember the flat place", memory as wear.

## Blind spot disclosure

I weigh society over plot, so I forgive slowness the first reader didn't:
Part One is probably over-long for a cold reader whatever I say. I'm less
able to judge the physics of the ring and the derating. Egan and Watts
should test whether 0.92 / (277/312) is the right arithmetic. My demand for
a Sahel scene can tip into instruction, and Banks may say the story is
already carrying enough.

Structural problem remains: yes, the Part One exposition and the unpaid
cost at the edge of the world.
