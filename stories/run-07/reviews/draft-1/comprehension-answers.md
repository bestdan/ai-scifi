# Comprehension answers, "What the Rope Will Bear", draft 1

Written from memory, without rereading the draft.

## 1. What is this story about, in two sentences?

In a town at the foot of a space elevator, people pool their yearly allotment of "asks" through a broker to get a godlike mind at the top to answer questions or supply goods, and a second mind, the rope itself, can only refuse loads. Segundo, a scrupulous broker, comes to suspect that the new ribbon going into the bottom of the rope is flawed. He has to spend his whole parish's savings, and the clinic his wife wanted, on one expensive question to prove it, since the mind at the top will only answer what it is asked.

## 2. What does the protagonist stand to gain or lose, and what happens if they fail?

He stands to lose his name and the trust he built over twenty-seven years of receipts. If he is wrong, nobody will give him an ask again. He also loses the clinic, which is his wife's long wish, and with it possibly his wife's good opinion of him.

If he fails to ask, the flawed ribbon goes into the bottom of the rope. In two or three years sections would part, climbers and crews would fall, and the valley would lose the anchor and its livelihood.

## 3. Why did the people or powers who set this situation up do it that way?

- **The rope's veto:** Ecuador asked for it when the elevator was built. It feared the consortium would run the rope to ruin for money. The consortium feared Ecuador would take the rope away. A mind that can only refuse can do neither.
- **The Asamblea sending every law to the rope:** after the double-budget year of 2097, when two coalitions each passed a budget, everyone was paid twice, and slots on the rope were sold twice. The rope held the climbers for eleven days. A referendum in 2101 then sent every law to the rope, because it was the only thing that had said no.
- **Arriba answering only what is asked:** I got this in part from the bread story. Arriba once told the world about a famine unasked, the traders who heard first bought up the wheat, and bread cost three times as much in Cayambe. So it was made to speak only when asked. I'm not sure whether that rule was a deliberate design or something Arriba decided on itself.
- **Keeping the rope and Arriba from seeing each other's records:** each is meant to check the other, and Jacinta says that if they could see each other they could agree. This leaves a gap nobody is paid to read across.
- **Brokers posting fees and giving receipts:** a rule made after Segundo's father's cash-for-road deal. It came from his father's behaviour.

## 4. What did you learn late that changed how you saw the earlier part, if anything?

- **The cord was the ribbon.** The grey 2,000 metres of "surplus" tied around the auction awnings was offcut from the new ribbon. Jacinta's hand closing on it was the first hint, and the fatigue test and weave confirmed it.
- **The ring in the rope's sections was the clue.** The four hundredths that the rope kept "putting down with the noise" was the sign of batch seven's flaw. It was inside tolerance, so the rope could not act on it.
- **Segundo's father's version of the bench scene.** Segundo had sworn off asking "on his word" because of a scene at the fiesta. His father says the receipts don't matter, it was the face that made them trust him, and the method worked. Segundo ends up using that method himself.
- **Dolores and Ernesto were more complicated than the road story suggested.** Dolores's refusal to cash the second payslip and her carrying water for eight years changed how I saw her. Ernesto's easy case for the road looked worse after the shoulder.
- **The rope's suspicion of Segundo.** It held his loads because they fell unlike others, and it could never find what he was hiding. That looked different once Arriba's reading showed he was only careful. The held loads were also the one place the rope might have a feeling.

## 5. What happened at the end, and why?

- **The vote:** at the ward meeting, Segundo asked the parish for its December asks on his word without telling them why. Most gave. His mother and the Pilataxis did not. Lourdes gave her six and left. He waived his fee to make up the shortfall of 687.
- **The question:** he asked "Is batch seven sound?" Arriba answered that it was not. The batch was cured during a solar storm, and 35 of its 312 sections have voids that open after about two million cycles, as with the Libreville test piece that parted.
- **The refusal:** the consortium derated the batch by the bad share. The proof climb the next morning then came out at 1.04 of what the foot would bear, and the rope refused it. Nothing had changed in the ribbon. Only the rating had.
- **After:** the consortium pulled the batch, kept the old ribbon at the bottom nine kilometres, and put the whole rope on half load for about two years. The slack season began, with the Asamblea passing laws at once, unweighed.
- **Proaño and the clinic:** Proaño's association will pay for the clinic, and he asked Segundo to write the label. Segundo wrote "Ayora's clinic, from the farms," the first label he ever wrote for a firm.
- **Last scenes:** Lourdes is cold to him. January's pool is open with no label. Batch eight is probably fine and nobody will pay to check. The rope is slack at the bottom for the first time.

## 6. Who is each named character, in one line each?

- **Segundo Farinango:** the broker, bench two, licence 0419, who gives a receipt to everyone.
- **Lourdes Imbaquingo:** his wife, who runs the dispensary and wants the clinic.
- **Sisa:** his sixteen-year-old daughter, who ties roses at the auction. She is embarrassed by him and gives her two asks on his word.
- **Dolores Farinango:** his mother, who sells lupin beans and refuses to ask Arriba anything. She holds the uncashed payslip.
- **Ernesto Farinango:** his father, a retired fixer in the parish home, who cashed both payslips and sold the parish's December for a road.
- **Jacinta Ulcuango:** his cousin, a strain reader at the anchor, who has twin nine-year-old boys. She tests the cord and finds the Libreville clue.
- **Wilmer Cuzco:** a younger, charming broker on the third bench, who rents other brokers' histories. He copies Segundo's label and later helps at the meeting.
- **Gustavo Proaño:** the owner of the big farm and an Asamblea member for Pichincha. He asks Segundo to write the clinic label.
- **Luz Caiza:** a nineteen-year-old cutter from Tabacundo, and Segundo's client. She says "You wrote it down."
- **Señora Rosaura Quishpe:** an old cheese seller, a client of Segundo's.
- **The baker, the pork man's wife, the clerk, and the fifth-bench broker:** townspeople. The fifth-bench broker is the widower with a white moustache whose number Wilmer rents.
- **The Pilataxis:** a family led by the big farm's foreman.
- **The crew chief:** the chief on the six-forty, who has a son on the down runs.

## 8. Which words or terms didn't you know from the story itself, and where did you get their meaning, if you got it?

- **Ask:** a unit of a person's yearly allotment, ten a year. I got it from Segundo's speech to Luz.
- **Pool / label / broker:** from the same speech. A label is a short phrase, six words at most, that people pool behind.
- **The Accord:** the rules governing Arriba and the rope. I never got a full account of it, only that it allows reading intent and audits one in 400.
- **Arriba:** the mind at the top. The name is clear from the children's rhyme and the clerk.
- **Waska:** the rope's mind. I got it on the school tour. It is the old word for rope.
- **Strain reader:** someone who turns the rope's numbers into Spanish. Jacinta explained it.
- **The "0.62 / 0.38" numbers:** the share of capacity used and what is left. The cousin explained this on the tour, but I only half understood it before then.
- **Tolerance:** the allowed limit, here a tenth. I understood it from Jacinta's bulletin.
- **Ring:** the sound a section of ribbon makes under load. I got it from the headset scene.
- **Derate:** to lower a rating. I got it from Jacinta's description of the consortium's rule.
- **Slack season:** forty days with no tension on the bottom. Proaño explained it.
- **Minga:** the work day each household owes the parish. The story explains it as the origin of the December pool.
- **Asamblea:** the legislature. Clear from context.
- **The anchor / the station / the counterweight:** the base, the point in orbit, and the far end. These were fine from the tour.
- **Offcut:** trimmed ribbon. Jacinta explained it.
- **Batch seven and section forty:** sections of the new ribbon. Jacinta explained the numbering.
- **Pasillo:** I took it as a kind of song from the record Dolores gave Sisa. I didn't look further.
- **"Bunch knot":** the knot for rose stems. The girls showed Sisa.
- **Lupin beans:** food. I guessed a kind of bean from the cones of beans.
- **Day of the Dead items (bread dolls, purple maize drink, the graves):** I knew these from outside the story.

## 9. What does the story's central change mean for people beyond its main characters?

- **For the valley:** the valley's livelihood runs on the rope. The flawed ribbon would have meant a failure in two or three years, with the crews on it. The question and the derating prevent that. The cost is real: half the freight for about two years, half pay for the crews, and the parish's December gone.
- **For Proaño and the exporters:** their habitat buyers cut contracts to half the day the label went up. The question itself did the damage, before the answer. Proaño predicted this, and he is bitter about it.
- **For everyone:** the story shows that a question is expensive and public. Only the rich can afford to ask whether the maker's work is sound. Ayora could afford it once.
- **The gap is still there:** nobody is paid to read the rope's records and Arriba's together, so batch eight and anything else unasked about stays unchecked.
- **For the Asamblea:** for forty days they can pass laws without anything weighing them. Proaño used it at once for his own benefit.
- **The other two tethers:** I'd guess they used pieces from the same batch, but the story doesn't say.
