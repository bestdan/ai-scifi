## The one thing
Make the ribbon's defect spend the exploit you built. Arriba's loophole is a
disposal clause with a one-in-four-hundred audit. Wilmer's is "alone or
jointly". Both are first-rate, but neither is ever used on the climax.
Segundo wins by paying the sticker price, and nobody games the system at the
point where it matters. Give someone, Jacinta or Wilmer, one clever move
against the Accord's separation of the rope's half and Arriba's half.

## Notes

1. **Part Two, Wilmer's co-signed histories (structural):** This is the
   best exploit in the draft, a market in broker reputations that costs the
   old man his chosen broker. It is resolved by one refusal at 1.02 and then
   disappears. Wilmer's "I want to be in the room" pays off, but the
   histories market never reaches the ending. Either let it matter in the
   ward-meeting tally or cut the subplot.
2. **Arriba's section, "The anchor town reads ribbon." (line-level):** The
   First Reader lost the whole section here. The line is a hint with no
   referent. Say what the disposal clause allows, plainly. Also decide
   whether Arriba's cord is a nudge. The page offers only "plausibly needs",
   so the reader has no way to choose between the readings the notes claim.
3. **The unsound-section list, "9, 18, 27 … 306 and 312" (structural, rigor):**
   Thirty-four multiples of nine, then 312, which is not one. If that's
   deliberate (a rack geometry plus a tail), a strain reader should notice it
   in a scene. If not, it reads as a numbering slip in the climax's most
   exact line. Also, a regular every-ninth pattern makes Jacinta's random
   sampling of twenty look cleverer or dumber than the page says, and no one
   remarks on it.
4. **Proaño's coffee, 2.3 (structural):** The slack-season rules and the
   18-month truck bill are explained to Segundo, who is only listening. Waska
   already carried the eighteen-piece bill in the section before. Pick one
   telling. Waska's version has the better detail, so keep it and let Proaño
   pitch the bill in two lines.
5. **The receipts-vs-face choice (structural):** Dolores's refusal, Ernesto's
   speech and Segundo at the bench are the same tradeoff three times. The
   fiesta memory is the one that turns it, and the meeting only restates it.
6. **Sampling arithmetic, 4.2 (line-level):** The "derate needs a complete
   map" rule is the best institutional detail, but Jacinta delivers it while
   crossing out boxes on a carbon, and she is the scientist explaining to a
   less-informed colleague. Put the rule in a certifier's mouth under
   pressure, or have Segundo already know it.
7. **Rope's refrain "Five rings true…" (line-level):** The first reader
   skimmed it by the third. Vary it, or let it drop out for Part Three so its
   return at the slack means something.
8. **Weight:** Proaño's clinic is paid for by the farms in the same chapter
   that Lourdes learns what it cost. Hold the offer back so her cost lingers,
   or make the farms' clinic cost Segundo something visible.

## What to protect

- The literal-answer flour sheet, the "alone or jointly" clause, and
  Arriba's one-in-four-hundred audit.
- The string that folds when pushed, which pays off in the rope that can
  only refuse.
- The flat place in the reel bearings as the rope's memory: "I remember a
  thing by feeling where it has worn me."
- "The logs say what the oven did. They don't say what it made."
- The 8,960,000,000 price, and the clerk pulling it off the board.
- The refusal as pure arithmetic: "ninety-two parts of a smaller number,
  which is a hundred and four."

## Blind spot disclosure
I reward system detail and will forgive length that a Chiang or Asimov
reader would cut. I may have under-weighted how much of Parts Two and Four
is briefing, and I take the competence of Jacinta and Segundo at the same
register, which is the sameness-of-voice trap my lens is prone to.
