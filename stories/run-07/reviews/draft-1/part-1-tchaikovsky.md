# Part One check: Tchaikovsky lens

Verdict on the brief's demand: Waska is written from the inside, with a body and a want. Arriba's reasoning is visible and wrong in an interesting way. Neither is a human in costume for long. The faults are in Waska's logic and in two or three Arriba lines.

## Notes

1. **Must-fix, structural. Waska's detector needs semantic understanding the bible denies it.** The bible says it "can't think about what a load is *for*". The page has it reason: "Six crates of paper for a label about mornings. A cold chest of vaccine for a label about goats." It also says "I can feel the school in it, heavy, square." Then: "I do not care what steel is for" and "I don't know what a market is for." A mind that reads fit between a label's meaning and a cargo knows what mornings and goats are. Rebuild the heuristic as pattern. Waska sees that his loads weigh differently from every other load filed under similar labels, and it never knows why. That is a weaker and funnier mind, and the reader can see it is wrong.

2. **Must-fix, structural. Waska diagnoses its own blind spot too neatly.** "It is not a load, and a thing that is not a load is not a thing I can refuse." And: "there is nothing to say no to." The outline's 4.5 says it "doesn't know there is anything it can't feel." Here it knows exactly. This is the "everyone self-aware" habit, and it spends the 4.5 irony in Part One. Keep the mechanism, "My memory is my wear... every morning I notice it again". Cut the verdict. Let it file the ring as noise, with an unease it can't weigh.

3. **Should, line-level. Arriba's note tilts the open question.** The bible allows the reason (disposal) and one fact ("The anchor town reads ribbon"). The draft adds "Of batch seven I have been asked nothing." That is a third item. It names the batch the reader just heard ring, and it reads as a mind saying it would speak if asked. Cut it, or fold it into the disposal logic. Two other lines carry grievance, which the bible rules out ("no remorse and no longing"): "it is very nearly all I am permitted" and "That is the rope's choice. I don't make the rope's choices." Trim both to flat rule-statements. The disposal reason is strong enough that both readings still stand once the tilt is gone, and the note then holds the question open correctly.

4. **Should, line-level. Arriba knows the rope's behaviour, so it reads like Waska's counterpart too early.** "I can't see its records, and it can't see mine, so I don't know why" is a clean exposition of the Accord's separation, and a bleak one. Let Arriba infer the two-week hold from the board only. It then models Waska as Waska models Segundo, wrongly.

## Protect

- "I take them the way I take everything, all along me at once." The opening of Waska's first section, and the town as "a rounding".
- "I let the coil go. It weighs nothing worth holding." The reader sees the irony before the page does.
- "and that is exactly what a well-hidden load would look like." Waska's unfalsifiable suspicion is a believable machine error.
- "I read the label as the auction's needs first... a man's silences are part of his label." Arriba's wrong theory is built from checkable facts: 1,240 clients, the father's road.
- "He watched the greenhouses go by and decided it again." Segundo's "It can't want anything" against Waska's section gives both views at once.
- "Two words. The Accord allows six." The one dry line.

## Blind spot

I reward minds that can be reached from the inside, so I am soft on Waska's legibility. Lem would say a mind this readable has been translated. Watts would ask whether either note shows anyone home. I have not judged Part One's exposition density (the tour's 2068 paragraph) or its pacing.
