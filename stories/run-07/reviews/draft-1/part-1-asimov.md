# Asimov lens: Part One check (draft 1)

Verdict from the lens: a reader can state the rules by the end of Part One. Asks, six-word labels, the fee, literal priced answers, laws as loads, and refusal as a number are all on the page and all dramatised. The problems are two fair-play breaks and two plants.

## Notes

1. **must-fix, structural: the delivery date can't be met.**
   - Waska says: "I feel it from the station down, nineteen days of descent ending in the last hour."
   - Arriba says: "Pooled: 1,995, on the sixth day" (so about 7 September) and "Dispensed, from the descending climber due at the anchor on 15 September."
   - That is eight days, against a nineteen-day descent. The Part 2 and Part 4 calendar sits on the 15th, and a reader who did the sum will stop trusting every other number.
   - Fix with one line in Arriba's note: it assigns stock from a climber already eleven days down with an unassigned hold. Or move the delivery to about 26 September and ripple the bible.

2. **must-fix, structural: Waska's rule contradicts itself.**
   - "I know one thing without having to weigh it: nothing that rings should be borne."
   - Two paragraphs earlier: "Inside tolerance, a ring is nothing."
   - In Part 3 Waska bears the plan, batch 7 included. As written, Waska then breaks its own stated rule, and the reader will count that as the rope being stupid. It needs to be the rule's edge case.
   - Make it "nothing that rings past tolerance should be borne". Then the later bearing is the rule working, and the edge case is fair.

3. **should, line-level: who is Luz Caiza?**
   - The second header reads "Received: 1 ask, from Luz Caiza. Label: our children's mornings." The Tabacundo girl is never named, and she only "put her thumb to his contract." Nothing says she pooled behind that label.
   - The reader can't tell who knows what. Name her in the bench scene and have her take the mornings label, or change the header.

4. **should, structural: the clinic arithmetic is missing an input.**
   - Part 1 gives "Everyone in Ayora held back six of their ten" and "Priced 44,000 asks." It never gives the parish's adult count.
   - Without that, the reader can't test later whether 42,300 or 47,000 is plausible. Add one clause in 1.4, for example "seven thousand eight hundred adults, six each."

## Protect

- "He had watched it go every morning of his life that he could remember, and he had never once seen it come out the top."
- "A question costs what answering it takes. Ask about one day's flour and you pay for one day." The rule, clean and fair, with the flour sentence as its proof.
- "'It can't want anything,' Segundo said. 'It's a rope.'" Then: "he decided it again." Both the plant and the irony are fair.
- "Of batch seven I have been asked nothing." and "The anchor town reads ribbon." The cord is planted without being solved.
- The cousin's "Six parts too many. It doesn't say if the yard was a good idea." This is the numbers-not-reasons rule, in one example.
- Arriba's receipt: 540 + 300 + 1,150 = 1,990, with 1,995 − 1,990 = 5 returned. It adds up, so keep it.

## Blind spot

I check rules, sums and fair play. I'm weak on whether Waska's lyric passages carry feeling or just decorate, on the body and the cold, and on the family scene. The licence speech to the rose-cutter is exposition-in-dialogue, which my lens tolerates and Le Guin and Rajaniemi may not. Defer to them on those.
