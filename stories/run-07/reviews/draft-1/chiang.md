## The one thing
Trust the form and cut what restates it. The story's question is who can afford to know, and three devices carry it: the receipts, the two half-minds and the price. Several scenes say aloud what those devices already enact. Cut or shrink them, starting with Jacinta's post-refusal explanation and Proaño's truck-bill coffee.

## Notes
1. **Structural. Jacinta on the gallery ("It's arithmetic... Everybody's going to say the rope knew").** Waska's section just before it carries the idea: "I find I have weighed something that I did not feel... It came from outside me, and it was right, and I could not have found it." That is the philosophical payoff: a correct refusal with no knowing in it. Jacinta then explains it again. Keep the 0.92 to 1.04 sum, since the gate needs it, but cut the "it didn't know" gloss. Let the reader feel the gap between the two passages.
2. **Structural. Proaño's coffee, the phased truck bill.** Waska's "eighteen pieces... ninety-four, ninety-four" already shows the shape from below, and better. The scene is stated twice. Keep the slack-season rule and cut the bill.
3. **Structural. Part Two, the fifth-bench widower and the old man with two broker numbers.** It restates "histories are rented", which Wilmer's pitch already carries. It is a good small injury, but the first reader's boredom began here. One of Wilmer's co-signs is enough.
4. **Structural, Weight. Ending, Proaño's clinic.** Segundo's cost is paid back within the same part, and by the man he refused. The firm's label is his one broken rule, so something is still lost, but the page moves on from it quickly. Stay on his hand holding the slate longer, or let the clinic's fate go unsettled.
5. **Line-level. Arriba's "The anchor town reads ribbon."** The first reader never decoded it. It fits neither reading of the cord (a warning, or disposal). Make it parse: say what the town does with ribbon, or cut it. Right now the ambiguity is a blur, not two readings.
6. **Line-level. "Seven rings a little more."** The first reader took "rings" as a count. The shed scene says it is a sound, but that comes far too late. Plant the sound once in Part One, in Waska's body: a struck note, a hum.
7. **Line-level. The answer's list (9, 18, 27... 306, 312).** The multiples of nine run out at 306, and 312 is the odd one. It reads as authorial pattern, not a physical layout along the oven's north wall. A little irregularity would read as an oven.
8. **Line-level. Part Five's empty headers.** The first reader took them as "nothing received". They could be the receipts Segundo no longer writes. Say so once, in a line, or the device drifts into blankness.

## What to protect
- Waska's present-tense body-sense: "My memory is my wear... I remember a thing by feeling where it has worn me." This is the form serving the idea.
- "I do not remember the shouting. I remember the flat place."
- "I have not approved anything. I have borne it. There is a difference, and nobody in the town has ever known it."
- The literal-answer gag: "*Section forty of batch seven is sound.*" and the 8,960,000,000 price, with Segundo laughing.
- Dolores's payslips, "I cashed the first one, and I kept the second one, and I never cashed it", and Ernesto's "Receipts are what you give people who don't know you." That line is the story's argument about trust, and it costs the protagonist.
- "Nobody was going to ask." Keep batch eight open.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour the idea and the form over momentum and conflict, so I may be too forgiving of the Segundo and Wilmer rivalry and the long middle. I have standing on the idea and the form, not on Segundo's interior. Watts and Banks should test whether the cuts above remove friction the story needs.
