# First Reader report: "What the Rope Will Bear", draft 1

Read once, straight through, to the last line ("For forty days nothing rings."). The file had no Drafter's notes heading, so I read to the end. This is what happened while I read.

## Where I got lost

1. **The first section after the Luz Caiza scene ("0.62 / 0.38", "Ninety tonnes leave the reel and I take them").** I didn't know who was talking. I read "I am stretched... That is what I am" and guessed a cable or the rope. I was sure about two pages later, when "the six-forty" and "the town" turned up. I didn't know what "Four-one-nine" was until I worked out it was Segundo's licence number from the card on his bench. I worked it out from context.
2. **The numbers at the top of those sections (0.62 / 0.38).** The first time, I had no idea what they were. The cousin explained them on the school tour, much later. Until then they were just decoration.
3. **"Seven rings a little more."** In the rope's first section I read "rings" as a number of rings, like a tree. It took the headset scene, a long way on, before I understood it as a sound. The first few times it was a phrase I let wash over me.
4. **The "ring... inside tolerance... noise" business.** I understood that the rope noticed something it couldn't act on. I never fully followed why it can't act on a thing inside the limit. I accepted it because the story kept repeating it.
5. **The Arriba section after the market label ("Ayora counter, broker 0419. Label: *the market*...").** A different "I" with no introduction. I worked out it was Arriba, the one at the top, from "I read a label in the light of who asks it". Then I spent the section trying to work out why Arriba included the "offcut" cord. It says it is disposing of surplus, and I wasn't sure whether it was being sneaky, helpful, or just tidy. The line "The anchor town reads ribbon" stopped me. I couldn't tell who reads ribbon, or what it meant, and I never got it. I didn't work it out.
6. **That same section says the offcut is "from batch seven".** Later Jacinta says offcut has no marks and she can't tell which batch it is. I noticed the mismatch when she said it. I half-wondered if I had misread, and then let it go. I didn't reread.
7. **The timeline of the last part.** The question is posted on a Saturday, the answer comes "Tuesday... thirty-first of October", the splice was "Monday". I lost track of which day was which and relied on the sense that it was all happening fast.
8. **Segundo's age against the stories about him.** His father's road was 2110 and he was 21 then. The water-carrying years run 2115 on, and Segundo says he "was twenty-one" while carrying drums. The numbers didn't quite sit, but I kept going.
9. **The empty headers, "Received: . Label: . Fee: . Pooled: ."** In the last part. I took them to mean nothing was received, and that something had gone blank. I wasn't sure it was on purpose.
10. **The sums at the meeting (fee, 42,300, 40,872, 687 short).** I followed it roughly: they were just short, and he gave up his fee. I did not check the figures. I nodded at them.
11. **Why the rope refuses the climb.** Jacinta explains it as arithmetic, and I followed it. But the rope's own paragraph says "I find I have weighed something that I did not feel", and I wasn't sure what I was meant to take from that. I liked it without being sure what it meant.

## Where I got bored

1. **Pages 1 to 3 of Part One, mostly the speech to Luz Caiza** ("On the first of January ten asks land on your screen..."). It reads as a lesson in how the system works, and I was waiting for something to happen to someone. The bakery scene before it had some life. The speech didn't.
2. **The school tour at the anchor**, from the bus to the string demonstration to the cousin's explanation of the board and the history of the veto. A lot of it was new rules, and I wasn't yet attached to anyone. The string was nice. The rest I was waiting to be done with.
3. **Proaño's coffee in the bakery**, the slack-season rules and the truck bill phased over eighteen months. I took in the rule and waited for the story to come back.
4. **Jacinta's sampling arithmetic** ("say one section in ten is bad... twenty... nine times in ten"). I skimmed the figures, and I got the point.
5. **The repeated rope lines** ("Five rings true. Six rings true. Eight rings true. Seven rings four hundredths more."). By the third time I was skimming it. By the last, in the rope's section about the change, I recognised it and it did something, a kind of ticking, but in the middle I wanted it to stop.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The clapping rhyme** about Arriba and the bread: "It told us once when no one asked, and the bread cost three times in Cayambe." I liked it at once and it paid off later.
- **"A whole town of people bent over looking for something they'd dropped"**, the headlamps under the rose plastic. Also the "hair of light" going up out of the anchor.
- **Segundo writing a receipt for his own wife**, and "Then she'll have no trouble proving it."
- **Jacinta closing her hand on the cord and cutting a piece for "the boys".** I didn't know it was the ribbon yet, but I knew something had happened. That was the first time the story had me leaning forward.
- **Dolores and the two payslips.** The best thing in the story for me. "I cashed the first one, and I kept the second one, and I never cashed it." Her flat voice, the bean ladle, the tin.
- **"Her right arm stops there."** The line about the water drums and the shoulder, and Segundo saying it to his father. It landed hard, and it undercut the father's easy argument.
- **The father at the card table and the fiesta memory.** Faces lifted "the way you lift a face to a fire", and the mother with her hands in her lap. Segundo realising that the thing he swore against was a method, and that it worked.
- **Wilmer: "I want a receipt."**
- **Luz Caiza: "I don't need to know. You wrote it down."** That one got me. And Segundo not wanting the kindness.
- **The price of "What aren't you telling us?"** and Segundo laughing until he had to sit down. I didn't expect it, and I laughed.
- **The ward meeting.** Lourdes touching her screen six times without a word, Sisa shouting "Two," and the dam going. The way you watched your neighbours touch their screens. Also Segundo feeling the ugly satisfaction of being right and not liking it.
- **Dolores bringing Lourdes the cup of maize drink** at four in the morning and standing beside her, saying nothing.
- **The windows not rattling.** On the roof, the dog barking once, a woman going out on her step in her dressing gown. I felt that.
- **The rope's refusal**, in its own voice: it held the climber and felt the old flat place go round. "It came from outside me, and it was right, and I could not have found it."
- **The sixteen-year-old's receipt book** ("the most embarrassing present"). Small and good.
- **Proaño asking Segundo to write the label** for the clinic. I didn't see it coming and I couldn't decide whether it was a bribe, a kindness or both. I liked not being sure.
- **Arriba's "I revise it. It is not the first time a label has told me more about its writer than his history did."** A strange small pleasure.

## Who I cared about, and would I have kept reading past page 2

**I cared most about Dolores.** Then Segundo, then Lourdes. After those, Luz Caiza and Jacinta. I liked Wilmer a lot, more than I think I was supposed to. I cared about the rope, but at a distance. It was strange and interesting more than it was someone I worried over, until the last third, when I did.

**Would I have kept reading past page 2?** Yes, but only just. Page 2 is mostly the baker and a long explanation, and I was reading it as homework. What pulled me on was the bench, the carbon copies, the card on its nail ("RECEIPTS GIVEN"), and then the rope's first section, which was odd enough that I wanted to know what it was. If those two hadn't been there I might have drifted off during the speech to Luz. By the end of Part One I was in. Part Two I read fast. From Dolores's payslips on I couldn't have stopped.

## The question I'm left with

Did Arriba mean to warn them? It couldn't speak unasked, and it gave away the ribbon as "surplus" in a cheap, odd label. After that it revised its reading of Segundo when he finally asked. I can't tell whether it was nudging them, or whether the nudge is just something I wanted to be there. And if it was, what does it mean that the thing that "never tells" found a way to tell?

A second question: does the rope want anything? Segundo says "it's a rope", and then the rope says it wants the slack "as I want nothing else". I finished unsure whether that was a want or a count.

Smaller: batch eight rings true and nobody will pay to check it. I wanted someone to ask.
