## The one thing
Make the sampling door shut for a reason the reader can check, not by assertion. The whole clinic-versus-question dilemma rests on it.

## Notes
1. **4.2, the carbon-copy arithmetic (structural):** Twenty sections cost 2,620 asks and hit with probability ~0.88. That is 6% of the clinic price for proof. Jacinta waves it off with "thank you, that's one section, we'll leave it out", but the consortium has the Gabon page and the ring in hand, and nothing on the page says it would shrug. The other objection, "twenty *sound* answers make it look safer", is a worry about reputation, not a rule. A smart reader solves this before Segundo does, and that is a character being stupid. Make it a rule: for example, the Accord bars the consortium from acting on a partial map, or a "sound" answer must be taken as covering its own scope only. Then plant that rule early.
2. **Parts 1–3, reader ahead of cast (structural):** Arriba's note says "offcut ... from batch seven". Waska says "Seven rings a little more." By the end of Part One the reader holds the whole solution except the mechanism. Parts Two and Three then wait for Jacinta to catch up. Consider having Arriba say "replacement ribbon" only, so that pairing cord with ring is the reader's deduction in Part Four. If the irony is deliberate, spend it on something the reader doesn't already know.
3. **4.1–4.2, door cascade (structural):** Consortium, oven logs, sampling and certification are four closed doors, delivered mostly as Jacinta's lecture. Only section forty and the catch-all are *tried*. Let Segundo attempt one more and fail at a cost.
4. **Timeline (line-level):** Segundo says "Yes" on 19 Oct. The meeting is on 27 Oct and the answer lands two and a half hours before the proof climb. Nothing says why he can't call a sitting on the 20th. Also, the question has been public since Saturday, yet the consortium splices seven in on Monday. Add a rule that fixes the meeting date, and a reason the consortium doesn't pause.
5. **5.3, the answer (line-level):** Section forty's answer stops "at the edge of the wall he's paid for". The batch answer volunteers the storm, the mechanism, the cycles and Libreville. Plant a rule that a finding of "not sound" must carry its basis. The storm of March 2138 is never planted before the answer, so it lands as news. One early mention would make it recognition.
6. **5.5, the nine kilometres (line-level):** If only 35 of 312 sections are bad, about 1.1 km is lost, not nine. "The 277 sound ones go higher" contradicts "a batch short at the bottom". The ending's cost, half the roses for two years, rests on this. Fix either the arithmetic or the reason.
7. **Planting the ring (line-level):** Waska's section 1 never says "ring" is a sound, and never ties "four-one-nine" to the card. The first reader read "rings" as a count. Add one clause on the first test pull: the ribbon sings when it's pulled. Also, if only 11% of seven's sections have voids, why do all three seven test frames ring 0.06? Say they were cut from the north-wall end.
8. **Section numbers (line-level):** The bad sections are every ninth, plus 312. A careful reader will want the oven's layout to explain the pattern.

## What to protect
- "The auction's bays were four." The mismatch of frame to stall is the rules acting on the page. It's a fair clue.
- "A rope can pull ... It can't push." The string demonstration plants the whole novum.
- *Section forty of batch seven is sound.* Exact and useless, and it is also price discovery.
- Waska: "It came from outside me, and it was right, and I could not have found it."
- "a whole town of people bent over looking for something they'd dropped."

## Blind spot disclosure
I weigh rules over bodies. I may undervalue the family scenes, the Dolores payslips and the ward-meeting tally, which the first reader found strongest. I also tolerate rule-exposition that this reader found slow. Let Le Guin and Lem judge whether the speeches in Parts One and Four are dramatised enough.
