# Part Two check: Chiang lens

Per-part check, draft 1. Part Two read in full, Part One skimmed. Bible and outline as context.

**Does it turn, and does it raise a new question?** The scenes mostly turn. The delivery turns on the frames fitting the auction's bays. "Because it's mine" turns Wilmer's copy. The night market turns on the cord, and the part ends on the receipt Wilmer asks for. The new question is real but arrives late. "Does it matter who asks?" is answered by the end of the first board reading. What the part leaves open is Proaño's "It's the one thing in this country I've never had to check."

## Notes

1. **must-fix, line-level.** Jacinta delivers the theme: "So it isn't the words. It's you. The same label from two of you buys two different things." The reader already has this from Arriba's note ("I read a label in the light of who asks it") and from the two posted rows. The line also trips the contrast-shape pattern. Let her read "Stall size... Bay size" and stop. Segundo's paragraph about the carbon copies ("as if someone had been reading his letters") already carries the feeling. The sentence after it, "That's worth a lot, if you're Wilmer", gives the next scene away.

2. **must-fix, line-level.** Proaño states two theses aloud. First: "We don't write laws any more, Farinango. We write what the rope will bear. The question in committee is never whether a thing is good. It's whether it will fit." Second: "If there were anything wrong with that ribbon, the rope wouldn't bear it." The truck bill, with its last truck arriving a year late, already dramatises the first. The ring from Part One already makes the second ironic. Keep "It's the one thing in this country I've never had to check", and cut the explanation either side of it.

3. **should, structural.** The same refusal comes back three times: Proaño's offer, Wilmer's pitch, then Sisa's "you said no because of Grandpa." The outline promised the good-name sale "in one scene". Cut Proaño's offer down to "Your father didn't say no", or let Wilmer's pitch carry the whole argument. Sisa's "At least he does things" repeats her Part One contempt for his receipts. Keep only the road argument and Dolores's "She's right about the road."

4. **should, structural.** A market in histories matters to the Reach row, and it gets one speech plus one sentence: "two numbers beside them instead of one." Waska's paragraph about numbers arriving in pairs is the best of it. Give us one other broker actually signing, or one client looking confused at a double name. The idea then costs a stranger something, not only Segundo.

## Protect

- "Because it's mine," and Wilmer laughing and posting it.
- The two-metre stalls against the four-metre bays. The reader works it out before the manager says it.
- Jacinta's hand closing on the cord, and "For the boys. They like string."
- "He envied him the ease, the ability to look at a thing he had and see only what it would fetch."
- Waska: "It looks like people learning to hide the true direction together."
- Wilmer's closing "I want a receipt."

## Blind spot

I read for the idea and the form, so I undervalue the comic business, which is Wilmer, the tuba and the pork man's wife. A cold reader may enjoy exactly the passages I'd trim. I did not check the arithmetic or the calendar (Waska's "thirty-four days to November" against the dates on the board). I also don't judge momentum: Part Two may sag in the middle for readers who aren't tracking the idea.
