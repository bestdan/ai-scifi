# First Reader report: "What the Rope Will Bear", draft 3

I read it once, straight through, up to the end of Part Five.

## Where I got lost

Items marked (worked out) are ones I sorted out later. I say how.

1. **The opening receipt, "Received: 1 ask… Fee: 0.05. Pooled: 0.95."** It sits above the first scene and meant nothing to me. I didn't know what an "ask" was or why anyone was getting a receipt. (Worked out: Segundo explains asks, pooling and labels to Luz about a page and a half later. The receipts kept appearing as headings, and I stopped noticing them.)
2. **The first italic number, "0.62 / 0.38", followed by "Ninety tonnes leave the reel and I take them, because I am the rope they climb."** I lost a beat on who "I" was. I didn't know whether it was a person, a machine or a poem. (Worked out within a few paragraphs: "I am the rope," the weight, the reel. It's the rope speaking, and it's one of the two minds Segundo described to the children. I took that from the plaque and the "can only say no" talk.)
3. **The rope's description of loads that "pull sideways" and "hide the true direction".** I followed the feeling but not the mechanism. The rope seems to treat unusual-looking requests as suspicious. I took it as a mind with a hunch about one broker, and I was never sure what exactly it was reacting to. (Partly worked out later when the cord shows up. The rope's suspicion of Segundo still reads as a separate, odd thread.)
3b. **The rope hums "seven" and says it files it as noise.** I didn't know what "seven" was. I only half knew it was a piece of new ribbon on a test line. (Worked out in Part Four, when Jacinta explains the batches. Until then it was a puzzle I'd put down.)
4. **The Arriba section after "The market. 1,990 asks."** This one lost me the most. Arriba says it read the label as the auction's needs first, and it also says it priced "forty awnings, lamps, a cold store". Then it says a disposal "is due" and 2,000 metres of offcut can go to him. I didn't understand why it was bringing in the ribbon at all. The line "I made the replacement ribbon… Nobody has asked me about it" hit me as something important, but I couldn't say what. (Worked out much later, in Part Four, when Segundo and Jacinta reason out that the cord was a message Arriba couldn't say in words. I was only sure by the time Arriba's answer came.)
5. **The pool count.** "By midnight the pool count stood at four hundred and twelve," and then Arriba says it filled on 7 September. I couldn't tell how much time had gone by. (Worked out by assuming a few days or weeks passed. It's never said.)
6. **Many names at once in Parts Two and Three.** Pilataxi (a pickup, a family, the farm foreman), Proaño, the fifth-bench widower, the pork man's wife, Señora Quishpe, Luz Caiza and "the Caiza boy". I mixed up the Pilataxis for a while. I was slow to learn that Señora Quishpe is "Rosaura". (Worked out by context.)
7. **The all-night talk about rules four and five, and then the sampling talk at his bench.** I followed that checking twenty sections buys one swapped section if you're lucky, and that a run of "sound, sound, sound" would look worse. I wasn't sure I'd got the whole logic. I trusted Jacinta. I didn't follow why a batch gets rated down by the share that's bad, until Jacinta explains it again on the gallery.
8. **"1.04 / −0.04" and why the rope refuses the climb.** I got it when Jacinta explained the arithmetic. Before that I read it as magic, with the rope seeming to sense the flaw. The rope's own account ("I find I have weighed something that I did not feel") helped. (Worked out from her explanation.)
9. **Who was "Arriba" and who was "the rope" when the clerk and the children use both.** In Part One I confused the two for a stretch. I thought Arriba was the thing at the top that answers and the rope was the cable. (Worked out from the fourth-grade tour, and then clear.)
10. **How the Asamblea's laws, the rope and the "slack" fit together at the end.** The laws passed during the slack season and the rope refusing them later made sense to me as a set-up for trouble. I'm not sure I know what happens on the tenth of December. I wasn't lost so much as left holding it.

## Where I got bored

- **The explaining to Luz and Señora Quishpe in Part One, from "She knew about the asks" to the receipt scene.** Roughly the first third of Part One. I was waiting for something to happen. I liked Segundo, the bench and the cold, so I didn't mind much. It's still a long stretch of someone telling someone else how a system works.
- **The tour of the anchor with the fourth grade.** The string, the plate, "Waska" and the two-sentence version of the whole country. It's nice, but it's the third time someone has laid out the rules. I was glad of the string.
- **Proaño's coffee at the bakery in Part Two.** The slack season dates, the contracts, the new rope. I was waiting for him to do something. A lot of information, little tension.
- **The all-night wait in the lab with the fatigue rig.** The counter ticking up (*Four hundred. Five hundred.*) kept me, because I wanted the result. Before it, the rig description went on a bit.
- **The rope's sections, around the fourth or fifth time.** "I notice it again as if for the first time" was a good line the first time. By the fifth, I was waiting for the section to be over. I did like the later ones better, because things had changed by then.
- **Proaño telling Segundo, in Part Four, that a question is a smell.** Then Wilmer and Proaño's conversation said some of it again. I'd already understood.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The clapping rhyme** ("*Arriba, up the rope, never lies and never tells…*"), and the smallest girl missing the hard part. Delightful, and it told me the rules without a lecture.
- **"the only tuba in the province who played every note in the right place and none of them at the right time."** I laughed out loud.
- **"Then she'll have no trouble proving it,"** when Segundo gives his wife a receipt. And Lourdes putting it under the salt.
- **Dolores and the two payslips**, the blue one cashed and the green one never. The whole stall scene: holding up the sagging canvas with a broom handle while she tells him, in her bean-price voice, what happened. "A woman who cashes both is a thief. A woman who cashes neither is a fool. And I had a boy." This is the part I'd have told someone about.
- **The water drums, and "Her right arm stops there. It has since 2115."** I hadn't noticed it, and neither had he. I went back in my head over every time she'd served beans with her left. (It's mentioned again later at the vigil, with her "bad arm", and I felt it.)
- **The fiesta memory:** the band stopping, Segundo left holding one note on the tuba alone in a silent plaza, his mother with her hands in her lap, his father seeing it. And then, "He had not known… that the thing he had sworn against was a method, and that it worked." That floored me.
- **"Receipts are what you give people who don't know you."**
- **"What aren't you telling us?" and the price, 8,960,000,000.** Segundo's helpless laugh. Jacinta folding the 40,872 receipt into his pocket and patting it. Funny and sad at once. I hadn't expected a joke there.
- **Lourdes unfolding her arms and touching her screen six times.** Then Sisa, loudly: "Two." Then the crowd going "as a dam goes." I teared up.
- **Segundo noticing he is glad to be right, and wanting his father to have been wrong.** I recognised it, and I disliked him a little and trusted him more for it.
- **Luz Caiza: "I don't need to know. You wrote it down."** And that it was "the kindest thing anyone had said to him that month and he didn't want it."
- **The board changing at ten past four in the morning**, the parish standing in the frost in blankets, Lourdes reading it "as you would read the names on a list from a mine", Dolores arriving with the maize drink. No one says anything.
- **The rope refusing a climb because of a number it didn't feel.** I hadn't seen that coming, and I liked that it admits it couldn't have found it.
- **The windows not rattling.** The silence on the first morning, the dog barking once. Lovely.
- **"Give her a year. Then give her another one."** The bread dolls with Grandmother's face.
- **"Dispensed: an answer."** A small shock of recognition.
- **Writing "Ayora's clinic, from the farms."** He wrote "Ayora's clinic," stopped, and then added the rest. I felt that he had lost something and gained something.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared most about **Dolores**, then **Segundo**, then **Lourdes**. I also cared about **Luz Caiza**, **Sisa** and **Jacinta**. I cared about the rope more than I expected, mostly because of the "flat place" from 2097 and the way it can't stop noticing the hum.

I liked Wilmer without wanting to. I never quite trusted Proaño, but I liked that he wasn't a villain.

Yes, I'd have kept reading past page 2. The cold bench at six, the card on its nail with the two extra words, the baker's flour sheet, the mountain and the headlamps under the plastic: I was in. I did have a stretch in the first third where I was patient rather than gripped, and I kept going because of Segundo and the voice. The first place the story really took hold of me was Dolores and the payslips. If I'd put it down before that, it would have been in the middle of Part Two, in the aluminium-frames and cord-tying stretch.

## The question I'm left with

Did the mind at the top, Arriba, mean to warn him? The cord came down "surplus" and the answer was written in careful words. It reads to me as if it knew, and could only get it across by sending a thing that couldn't be a sentence. I'm not sure whether it did that deliberately, or whether I've read my wish into it.

Next to that: what happens when Proaño's association gets a clinic with its name on it, and the rope bears again on the tenth of December. And whether anyone will ever ask about batch eight.
