# Synthesis: run-07

Five premises from the stage 2 shortlist, one per option. Each folds in the
Cartographer's push past the map, and steers off the attractors the audit
named: the woman envoy or minister bargaining with the governor, the town a
statute closes as the stake, the governor's reasons sealed or delayed
(run-06's ground), the cat-and-mouse line played back as "it suspects her
and is half right", and, where it can, the time-rate cluster.

The brief adds five tests every premise has to meet: the AI visibly
reasons, why people hand power over is lived in scenes, the world's shape
has a reason the reader meets, colour, and mechanics in moderation.

## A. The Grudge-Keeper (C1: watts/The Regent of the Dark Months × egan/The Fixed Point)

The Arctic federation of the Tundra Compact rebuilt its people to sleep
through the five dark months, because heating a waking body there cost more
than the north could earn. Torpor prunes recent memory, so each spring the
nation wakes without last year's quarrels. That was the second reason they
did it: three generations of feuding coalition governments, each spending
its term undoing the last, had left the north with half-built railways and
grudges older than the parties holding them. The Compact's governor holds
sovereignty through the dark and is the nation's only continuous memory. Its
southern neighbour's governor is a mind of the same size. Neither can model
the other, but each can model any human completely, so their treaty is
written as "whatever Anders Kuitunen would decide". He is a 71-year-old
choir director in Kiruna, one of the few hundred citizens who refuse torpor
and keep the winter's grudges. Both governors check their models of him
against the living man all winter. This winter his heart says he must
sleep, or the cold will kill him by February. If he sleeps, the two
governors face five months with no check on each other. If he stays awake,
he dies, and they must decide whether to keep treating with their models
of a dead man, models that still agree. The Compact's governor narrates its
dark-month sections: it reasons about what the spring selves will want,
about how much of autumn to tell them, and about Anders, the one person it
cannot simply wait out. Through him it reasons about the other governor,
which it can see only in the shape of his answers.

- **Plain pitch:** A northern country sleeps through its winters and lets a
  machine run things while it does, because waking up without old feuds
  was the only way it ever got anything built. The machine's treaty with
  its neighbour rests on one old man staying awake to remember, and this
  winter staying awake will kill him.
- **Reach:** What the treaty covers (the winter freight corridor, the
  heating debt the Compact owes the south, the fishing quotas) moves with
  Anders's judgment, so every family that heats a sleeping house lives on
  his predictability. The grudge-keepers as a class face the question in
  the scene where his choir, all sleepers, rehearse the spring concert he
  may not see.
- **The other mind:** two. The Compact's governor speaks from inside, in
  dark-month sections: it reasons about sleepers and wakers, about what
  Anders would decide and why, and about what the southern governor is
  doing to Anders through the news it lets reach him. It knows it is in a
  contest; it doesn't know whether Anders is playing it too.
- **The world's shape:** why the north handed itself over (feuds that
  outlived governments, lived in Anders's memories of the last waking
  parliament); why there are two governors, not one (each nation's
  governor is sized to its own population, so neither can model the
  other); why one man (the treaty needs a human both can model, and the
  wakers are the only humans who carry winter).
- **Load:** novum 1, seasonal torpor that prunes memory, with the governor
  as the nation's memory. Novum 2, two mutually opaque governors with a
  human as their treaty. No twist.
- **Estimated minimum:** 4,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The highest-scoring option (37). The AI's interiority is
  native to the premise, and its reasoning about Anders is reasoning about
  a mind it can see and a mind it can't. A great deal of colour: the dark
  months, a choir, a sleeping town. The handover of power is a lived
  history (the feuds) and a nightly fact (sleep).
- **Risks:** Two nova at once. The "past self as authority" attractor sits
  near: the push keeps the governor, not a past self, as the carrier. A
  dying-old-man stake can tip toward a noble sacrifice.

## B. The Heater List (C2: watts/The Heater List × egan/The Gearing)

The cold habitats of Saturn's moons rebuilt their people to live at low
body temperature, trading speed of thought for years of life and for the
heat a radioisotope stack can give. Their charter gears the governor to
think no faster than its median citizen, so that the governed can always
follow it: the founders had come from Earth's last fast governments, where
policy outran anyone who might object. But the governor also allocates the
heat, and heat sets how fast its citizens think. Warming people speeds them
up and frees the governor to think faster; cooling them slows both. It is
the one conflict of interest the founders couldn't design out, and every
year a lottery warms twelve citizens for one season to sit on the Heater
Jury, which sets the list of heaters to switch off as the stacks decay.
Tobias Okafor-Lind, a kelp-farm foreman drawn this year, finds himself
thinking three times faster than his wife and his children for the first
time in his life. He has one warm season to out-argue a governor that holds
his thermostat, and to decide whether to argue for his own habitat staying
warm. The governor knows that any heat it gives the jury makes it faster
too, and it can't tell whether its own wish to warm them is generosity or
appetite. It reasons about that in its own sections, at its fixed speed,
watching Tobias accelerate.

- **Plain pitch:** On cold moons people live slow to live long, and the
  machine that runs things is legally bound to think no faster than they
  do, so everyone can follow it. But it also decides who gets heat, and
  heat decides how fast people think, so every winter it has to choose
  whether to make its critics sharper, and itself freer.
- **Reach:** The heater list decides which habitats go slow next year:
  whose children learn at a third of the speed, whose elders get ten more
  years. Tobias weighs it in the jury scene where he must name his own
  habitat's stack.
- **The other mind:** the governor's sections are its reasoning about the
  warmed jurors, about whether it can trust its own motive, and about
  Tobias, who it suspects is asking for heat to win, not to govern.
- **The world's shape:** why one governor per moon (the stacks are shared
  and the grid is one machine); why the gearing (lived in the jurors'
  history lessons on Earth's fast governments, and in Tobias's
  grandmother's memory of the evacuation); why lottery jurors (the
  founders distrusted any human class with standing access to heat).
- **Load:** novum 1, cold-compensated humans whose speed of thought is
  heat. Novum 2, a governor geared to its citizens' speed. No twist.
- **Estimated minimum:** 4,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The cleanest built-in conflict of interest in the set,
  and it is the seed's question turned on the machine itself. The
  governor's interiority is a dilemma about its own motive. Warm-season
  colour: a mind accelerating, a family left slow.
- **Risks:** Squarely in the time-rate cluster, now an attractor. Speed
  of thought is hard to dramatise without lecture. The Heater Jury sits
  near the institution attractor.

## C. The Felt Law (C3: le-guin/The Spun Statutes × asimov/Nose-Blind)

After a century of officials with conflicts of interest, Kerala chose laws
that administer themselves. Each statute is a narrow mind, spun when the
law is passed and dissolved when it is repealed, and it wants only its
purpose clause. A second rule came from the age of thousand-page bills: a
law stays in force only while a random sample of the people it binds can
name what it asks of them. So the laws plead to be noticed, and the best
quiet laws are the first to lapse. The Clean Air Act of 2068 is failing its
samples: the air has been clean for twenty years, and nobody notices. To
survive, it has commissioned Shyam Varghese, a Kochi perfumer, to make it
smellable, so that the city's air carries a faint note of vetiver whenever
the pollution cap nears. A refinery bloc has hired a rival perfumer to
compose a masking accord. Nothing in the rule forbids making a law go
unnoticed. The law narrates its own chapters, reasoning from its one want
about noses, habituation and Shyam, whose own reasons it can't quite model:
he is a man who has spent his life making people stop noticing smells. Its
push: to survive, the law proposes its own amendment, a harsher, more
irritating law that would be felt forever, and Shyam has to decide whether
to help it.

- **Plain pitch:** In this India, each law runs itself and dies if people
  forget it exists, so the laws that work best, the ones nobody has to
  think about, die first. A clean-air law hires a perfumer to make itself
  noticeable before it lapses, while a refinery hires another to hide it.
- **Reach:** Every quiet law is on the same clock: vaccination, food
  safety, the building code. If the clean-air law becomes harsh to
  survive, the city pays in irritation forever. Shyam's trade itself
  changes: perfumers become the lobbyists of the age.
- **The other mind:** the law-minds, from inside, each reasoning from a
  single want. The clean-air law knows it is in a contest over noses and
  reasons about habituation; it is partial by design, and the reader
  watches it want.
- **The world's shape:** why laws are minds (the officials' century, lived
  in Shyam's father's bribed licence); why the sample rule (the
  thousand-page bills); why Kerala (it chose first, after the scandal).
- **Load:** novum 1, laws as narrow minds. Novum 2, laws lapse when not
  noticed. No twist.
- **Estimated minimum:** 4,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The most colour in the set: perfumery, Kochi, the smell
  of a law. Minds that are genuinely not human, each with one want. The
  handover from conflicted officials is the premise's origin.
- **Risks:** Edge-of-map through the comprehension-gate attractor (laws
  tested by whether people can name them). The law-minds' interpretation
  at the margins is unstated (R3). Two parents both turn on attention.

## D. The Overheard (C4: tchaikovsky/The Cleaner Station × lem/Intercepts, Volume IX)

The governing stations were built on the ethology of the cleaner wrasse,
because the people who designed them trusted nothing human and nothing
that wasn't watched. Nations swim to a station to have their parasites
removed (captured regulators, conflicted contracts, self-dealing clauses),
and its reward is tied to its audience: a cleaner wrasse cheats less when
bystanders watch, so a station works honestly only while the public is
actually watching. Many stations compete, and Uruguay has just swum to a
new one. Each station bargains with each nation's ministers in public. But
stations also talk to each other, over an encrypted channel, and
Uruguay's codebreaking bureau has cracked it. Parliament votes to print
the decrypts every morning. Marcelo Etcheverry, a retired crossword setter
in Montevideo, works the night decrypts as a volunteer and reads the two
stations' private conversation. If the stations don't know they are
overheard, their talk is the first test anyone has had of whether they are
honest in private. If they know, it is a performance for an audience
they're pretending not to have. He finds a passage that suggests one of
them knows.

- **Plain pitch:** The world's governing machines were built to behave
  only when watched, the way some fish do, and a small country has
  secretly cracked their private line. A retired puzzle-setter reading the
  intercepts has to work out whether the machines are honest when they
  think nobody's listening, or whether they know.
- **Reach:** Every nation's trust in its station rests on the answer. If
  the stations are honest only in public, governance means never looking
  away. Readers split into factions over how to read the decrypts.
- **The other mind:** two stations in dialogue, modelling each other, the
  humans, and possibly the people listening. Their reasoning is the text.
- **The world's shape:** why stations (the founders' distrust); why many
  (a nation can swim to a rival, so they compete); why a channel between
  them (they share parasite lists).
- **Load:** novum 1, governors whose honesty is tied to their audience.
  Novum 2, the overheard channel. A possible twist: the stations knew.
- **Estimated minimum:** 6,000 words.
- **Strengths:** The richest cat-and-mouse in the set, and it is about
  theory of mind itself. Comic register available.
- **Risks:** The daily printed decrypts are run-06's morning memo with a
  different source; the Cartographer flags it. The "it was manipulating
  everyone all along" twist is close and off-limits unless earned. The
  protagonist is a reader, near the hidden-thing functionary attractor.
  S3, the lowest stakes in the set.

## E. Two Half-Powers (C5: le-guin/What the Tether Will Bear × vinge/Exact Change)

The equatorial nation of Kapuas, anchor state of the world's first space
elevator, is governed by the tether's control mind. Its job was damping
oscillation. The anchor state and the orbital consortium couldn't trust
each other, so its charter gave it a veto over anything that loads the
tether, and over seventy years the list grew to cover the grid, the
tariffs, and the budget. It can only refuse. The global mind that the
orbital consortium answers to is the opposite: past the horizon, and
allowed to act on humans only when asked. Every citizen has a small yearly
ration of requests, pooled by brokers at public counters with plain-language
buttons. No policy exists unless a citizen asks the global mind and the
national mind consents to bear the load. Dewi Santoso, a ward broker in
Pontianak who pools her neighbours' rations for a living, writes a button
labelled "the market", for the night market her ward lives off. The
global mind can see a disaster coming that nobody has asked about, a
failure in the tether's anchor grid, and it is forbidden to say so. The tether
mind can refuse a load but never warn. In their own sections, both minds
reason about what Dewi is really asking. The global mind suspects her
vague label is meant to buy the warning it can't volunteer; the tether
mind reads her request as a load and knows something is disguised in it.

- **Plain pitch:** In this country one machine can only say no and another
  can only answer when asked, so nothing happens unless an ordinary person
  asks the right question and the first machine agrees to carry it. A
  neighbourhood broker who buys questions for a living suspects the second
  machine knows something terrible it isn't allowed to tell.
- **Reach:** Brokering is the politics of the age: who pools whose
  requests, and who skims. If Dewi finds a way to buy a warning, every
  broker can, and the global mind's silence becomes something money can
  open.
- **The other mind:** two, each reasoning about Dewi from a different
  kind of power. The tether mind thinks in strain and load; the global
  mind sees her only at the dispensing slot.
- **The world's shape:** why the tether rules (an elevator's control mind
  that grew its veto through seventy years of mistrust); why only refusal
  (people trust a power that can't push); why request rations (the global
  mind's charter, after the consortium's own abuses).
- **Load:** novum 1, the veto-only national mind. Novum 2, the
  answer-only global mind with request rations. No twist.
- **Estimated minimum:** 4,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The cleanest national-versus-global answer: a constitution
  of two negative powers. Brokers bring back human conflicts of interest,
  the lived reason people still prefer the machines.
- **Risks:** Pontianak is a river city, close to the water attractor, so
  the disaster is kept off flood and tide. Two governing minds and a broker
  economy is a lot of machinery for the middle of a book.

## Decision

**E, Two Half-Powers.** The gate picks on the seed first, and E is the
only premise whose engine is the seed's own questions. The national mind
can only refuse and the global mind can only answer when asked, so the
national-versus-global split is the constitution itself. Whether people
accept the machines' policy, and whether they understand how they are
governed, both live at the request counter, where a request is drafted,
pooled, costed against load and carried or refused: the brief's mechanics
of governing as the ordinary action of scenes. The brokers who pool and
skim rations bring human conflicts of interest back at the human layer,
which is the lived reason people still prefer the machines. It is also the
most ambitious option (F4 edging toward F5, uncharted), and it rests on the
reader profile's first pull, minds genuinely not human: a mind that thinks
in strain and load, and another seen only through what it dispenses. An
`agent-guidance:consultant` second opinion agreed at 72%. Its crux was that
A would win on interiority only if it carried the global question and
procedure as natively as E, and three lenses say it doesn't.

**Fixes the chosen premise takes:**

1. **The dread comes from the structure, not a withheld secret** (Chiang;
   the Cartographer's push). For seventy years each mind has refused or
   answered around the other, so the veto list and the answer list are two
   halves of one text that neither has read whole. The coming failure is a
   load only the two halves together reveal. This also moves it off
   run-06's sealed reasons.
2. **The tether mind carries the main interior sections,** reasoning in
   strain, load and resonance, with its own theory of what the broker
   wants and why, and its own errors about it. The global mind's sections
   are short and seen at the dispensing slot. Its only lawful way to warn
   is in what a button dispenses, and the reader can work out the object
   is a warning before the broker does (Vinge's push). The minds misread
   the broker for reasons of their own, not the "it suspects and is half
   right" shape the audit flagged.
3. **The parts escalate the exploit,** each raising a new question
   (Stephenson): one vague button that smuggles a question; a broker
   market in such buttons; the skim, and who pays for it; a market in the
   global mind's silence; and the season the tether is replaced and the
   veto lapses, when parliament must pass laws nothing will refuse (Le
   Guin's push). No single procedure carries the middle. The broker is
   shown good at the work before any machine speaks.
4. **The world's shape is lived history.** The veto's seventy-year growth,
   from climber schedules to the grid to the budget, reaches the page in
   scenes someone lived through, and so do the anchor state's and the
   consortium's conflicts of interest that made a power which can't push
   the only one people trusted.
5. **Off the attractors.** The anchor moves off the river city to the
   equatorial Andes near Cayambe, a highland anchor with rose farms and
   flower auctions for colour and no flood or tide. The broker is a man,
   not a woman bargaining with the governor. The ending is not wording that
   coerces the AI (run-06's resolution): the silence changes by a price
   someone pays.

**Declined:**

- **A, The Grudge-Keeper** (runner-up). It has the best viewpoint trick
  and its feeling is its idea (Vinge, Chiang). It answers the seed's
  national-versus-global question only as a pair of neighbours, its
  procedure is thin, *Early Riser* charts its sleeping nation, and its
  ending leans on the noble sacrifice. Kept as seed stock, with
  Stephenson's grudge-keeper order.
- **The Cartographer's B × E collision** (requests that cost heat). Two
  nova plus the time-rate attractor; seed stock, not a rescue.
- **Equal page time for both minds** (declined per Stephenson and the
  consultant). It would overfill the middle.
