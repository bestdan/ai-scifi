## The one thing

Close the Vô Zé gap on 7 Oct. He is in the tower and hears his own Virgin sour, and the next three pages show nothing of what he knows. The relay is now fair: `high ••• · virgin • · high •••` gives exactly one sour stroke, and "a sour high's a low" turns it into the valley's `high ••• · low • · high •••`. The reader holding the rule gets there, and the cold reader says "I had to work for it. Once I had that, it landed." That is the intended pleasure. But the old ringer who coined the rule, and is the only person in the story who could correct it, lets it ride without a word on the page. One shrug or one look down from the tower fixes it. Without it, the reader has to guess whether Vô Zé is complicit, deaf or proud.

## Notes

1. **7 Oct, Vô Zé (structural, small).** See above. Also "Zé's ringing it, uai" shows the town can't imagine he'd ring a lie. A line from him would use that.
2. **26 Sep, the miscut (structural, small).** The hum was "eight cents sharp" and is never mentioned again. A reader who follows the rules asks two things. Is the hum still sharp? And why not tune the whole bell by cutting elsewhere, if the strike note can only fall? One clause from Ceiça would settle both: the strike note sets the pitch, the hum is a matter for the old ears, and she won't chase a ruined partial. Without it the "recast or hang it" choice has a visible hole.
3. **26 Sep, who lets a novice cut (line-level).** Ceiça gives a first-ever cut on the feast bell, with no practice piece and no recast margin. "Your grandfather made mine" explains the tradition, but a clever character would have him cut scrap first. One line, such as "Practise on the scrap tin first," refused or skipped, would stop it reading as plot need.
4. **16 Sep, bells and numbers (line-level).** The first reader lost track of "the Virgin, the small one and the low one." A single sentence naming the three at the table, in order of pitch, would help. The numbers (400, 390, 412) are carried by Lia's laugh and don't need more.
5. **The vigil mark (line-level).** The first reader "didn't know what it was for." `high • · low • ...` is never glossed on the 19th or the 28th, and what he reads in it is the delay branch. This is fair as subtext, but add three words to "the vigil. The doors stay open all night": for example "a bell for waiting."
6. **Pregar (line-level).** The first reader was never sure whether it was "an insult or a crime." Rafaela defines it on the 19th and says it to the form on the 28th, and Ceiça's "where I can see your hands" gets the reader to the right reading. Leave it.
7. **Rafaela's age (line-level, check).** She is 24. She "sat in the assembly" in 2049 as an open child of about 14, then set at 16. The dates work, but only if the reader does the sums. Say "I was fourteen" once and the Annex's history becomes checkable.

## What to protect

- "Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back." and "You can only take away. Where. That's the whole trade." These are the two rules the cut turns on, and both are planted before the climax.
- The planted feast pattern at the table ("three on the small one, one on the Virgin, three on the small one") and "A crack's only a buzz, nobody reads a buzz." The ring on 7 Oct obeys them.
- Lia's correction to four hundred and twelve, and Vô Zé looking at the ceiling "as if the number were written there in a hand he didn't trust." It shows the open's gift and sets up "pitch before number."
- The 2 Oct denial: "Zero chance... Uai, I don't," followed by "Dadá," said kindly. This is fair play for the drift rule, and the cold reader read the whole change from it.
- "It's a good bell, uai. It's a bit low. So are lots of people."
- "Then you'll stand here all week," she said, "where I can see your hands," and moved over.
- The last line. It is concrete and unresolved, and the reader needs no verdict.

## Blind spot disclosure

I weight rule consistency over feeling. The cold reader's best moments (Tonho's unplayed message, the cello, the accordion) are all outside my lens, and I would not trade any of them for tighter acoustics. I can't judge the Portuguese texture, and I can't judge whether Davi's "choice" is meaningful or merely drift. That is a Le Guin and Chiang question. My notes 2, 3, 5 and 7 are about explicit logic, and each can be dropped if the other lenses say the scene is better a little loose.
