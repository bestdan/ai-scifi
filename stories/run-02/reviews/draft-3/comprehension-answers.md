# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A teenager, Davi, in a Brazilian bell-founding town, has to decide whether to take a drug that permanently fixes his brain so he can inherit his mother's foundry licence, or stay "open" for another year. Open people pick up the voice and habits of whoever they're near, and he chooses to cure beside his mother at the foundry instead of going off with his friends. He ruins his first bell cut, and the town misreads the flat bell as a death sign.

2. If he sets, he gets the seat and the licence and his mother steps down with honour, but his hands then learn the trade slowly, like an adult's, and he may never be as good as she is. If he waits a year, his mother keeps the licence past the feast and the town calls her "agarrada" (a parent who won't let go). He also risks drifting into his friends' shape and losing the trade. If he fails, the family loses the foundry, or its future. There are only three founders left in Minas.

3. The law (the Lei da Entrega) was set up because treated people stay open to whoever spoke last. Councils were being swayed, and untreated parents were hanging on to their offices and getting taken advantage of. So there is a fixed day, a rite, and a "closed" heir. The reason for the treatment itself I'm less sure of. I think it was to keep children able to learn fast, and the cost is that the adult brain can't be reopened.

4. Late on, I learned the town reads a sour high bell as a death sign, and that his cut made the new Virgin sound exactly like that. That recast the flat-bell scene. I also learned, when Vô Zé says "Dadá", that Davi already talks like his mother, and he can't hear it. That changed how I saw his "choice" and the fountain scenes, where he was being swept along. The reveal about Tonho's daughter's voice message also made the cost of being open feel real.

5. At the feast the new bell rang fifteen cents flat, and the town passed on "someone in the house is going" down the valley. Ceiça let them think it was her giving up the seat. He received the licence and the vote token, and she became his apprentice. The next morning she slowed his hand on the board, and he heard the flat note before counting. He had taken the tablets, cured beside her, and sounded like her. The flat bell was the one he cut wrong, so it comes back as his own work.

6. Characters:
- Davi (Dadá): the teenage heir, good with models, bad with hands.
- Ceiça (Conceição): his mother, the bell founder, who is quiet and funny.
- Vô Zé: his grandfather, the tower ringer, 78, who keeps miscounting.
- Lia: his nine-year-old cousin who has learned the whole bell code and charges him a coin for advice.
- Rafaela Brum: the twenty-four-year-old setting counsellor, who plays cello badly and set early beside her boyfriend.
- Seu Tonho: the butcher, untreated, the one "agarrado", whose daughter Kátia went off and won't be played.
- The pack: Davi's friends at the fountain.
- Kátia: Tonho's daughter, who is open and drifts from city to city.
- The ringer at Pedra Branca: relays the bell message.

7. Unsure about:
- How the treatment really works. I'm not sure about the "first week of life" treatment, and not sure whether "open" people are actually harmed.
- Whether Davi truly chose, or just ended up beside his mother.
- Whether Ceiça knew the bell would be misread and let it stand on purpose.
- What the vigil notation was for, and why it mattered when he looked at it at the clinic.
- Whether the town will ever find out the bell was flat.
