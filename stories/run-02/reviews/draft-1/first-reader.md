# First Reader report: "Strike Note", draft 1

## Where I got lost

- **The opening paragraphs (16 September).** Strickle, loam, core, false bell, profile, "hair fat at the shoulder". I got the gist, that she is shaping clay for a bell, but I was nodding along to words I didn't have. I kept going because the mother felt real.
- **"Bricked. Zero chance. Fully bricked."** I had no idea what it meant until Lia said "Take the seat. Zero chance otherwise. Otherwise it's bricked." Even then I only half-got it as teenage slang. Then "Advice is one real" and the coin: I worked out Lia is selling advice, but it took a beat.
- **The first bell sequence ("low ••• · high •••• · low •").** The explanation came right after, so I followed it. But the "one low stroke in the middle of a feast means somebody is going" rule was dropped inside Vô Zé's talk about beans and I almost skimmed past it.
- **The words "open", "set", "agarrada", "cata-vento", "brabo", "firma".** For about the first page I didn't understand who was what. It cleared up during the clinic reading, but I was unsure through the first two scenes why "the open" were treated as a separate kind of people.
- **The Annex reading (19 September).** I read the legal text mostly for the feel of it. I understood the point from Rafaela's plain version afterwards: treated people don't drift, untreated ones soak up whoever they stand near, and the pill locks you. I'm not sure I'd have missed anything by skipping the long clause.
- **Why the seat can't just wait.** It took me a while to see the town's pressure on a parent who holds on. The butcher scene explained it.
- **The 2 October exchange with Vô Zé.** Davi's speech there ("Zero chance. Sound like me. Always have. Uai, I don't...") confused me for a moment. Then I got it: he is insisting he sounds like himself while using his mother's word and the camp's word in the same breath. I think I got it, but I felt the confusion first.
- **The 7 October relay.** The code is carried down the valley, and I wasn't sure for a sentence or two whether the Pedra Branca ringer was repeating the mistake or correcting it. It became clear.

## Where I got bored

- **The long fountain paragraph on 22 September.** One enormous run of teenage planning talk. I understood it was meant to feel like being swept along, and it did, but I skimmed the middle (the lab, Christmas, why Christmas).
- **The Annex reading, the second half.** Clauses Two and Three. I was waiting for her to stop reading. She did, and I was glad.
- **The first half-page of the 16 September scene.** I was waiting for a person to speak to another person. Once Davi said "Clean profile" and she asked "You want it?" I was in.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **"Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back."** Landed early and stayed with me.
- **Rafaela stopping the reading, initialling both pages, writing "read in full."** Funny and sad at once.
- **The cello missing the same high note, and stopping when he knocks.** I thought about it for the rest of the story. Her not saying how it's going, and "He had heard how it was going."
- **Seu Tonho and the unplayed voice message: "Then I'd know what she sounds like now."** This was the best thing in the story for me. It made the stakes feel human, not procedural.
- **The 22 September paragraph where he cannot find the sentence where the decision was made.** "He could have reproduced every sentence, and none of the sentences was the one where it happened." I felt it in my stomach.
- **The mother's accordion.** "I never knew it was a price." Warm and not showy.
- **Davi's reread of his own notebook line before taking the pill.** The reasoning there ran on a long sentence and I believed him. "It was just a thing you knew, like the loam."
- **"Then you'll stand here all week where I can see your hands," and moved over.** I loved her not making a speech.
- **Vô Zé: "Dadá," he said, kindly.** That one got me, because the grandfather knows and Davi doesn't.
- **The flat bell sending a "someone is going" message down the whole valley, and Ceiça's "Me, out of that chair. This one's got cinnamon in."** The best turn in the story. A joke and a grief at once, and the canjica pots on the step are lovely.
- **Seu Tonho watching with the phone, "and didn't play anything."**
- **The pack on 7 October: one word in three, laughing late.** It echoed the untreated boy at the fountain earlier, and I saw the loop close without being told.
- **The last paragraph, her hand over his, slowing it.**

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Ceiça first, then Davi. Ceiça I was on side with from her first line of dialogue; she says almost nothing about her feelings and I believed all of it. Davi I cared about from the clinic scene on; before that he was a bit insufferable (the "obviously", the model that tells him the strike note), and I think that was meant. I also cared about Seu Tonho and Rafaela, more than I expected to. Lia was a pleasure but I didn't feel anything for her.

Yes, I'd have kept reading past page 2. Not at the very start, where I was holding on for the mother. The clinic scene is where I knew I was staying.

## The question I'd still be thinking about the next day

Whether Davi chose, or just drifted toward whoever he stood beside last. Rafaela asks it of herself ("I chose whatever he'd said last"), and then the story answers it by having him choose the loam board on purpose, and I still wasn't sure that was a free choice and not the mother's pull. Then I'd wonder what he sounds like now, and whether he'd know.
