## The one thing

Keep the ending's ambiguity where it is, and cut the one paragraph that
settles it for the reader. The 28 September notebook paragraph ("a hand learns
what it's standing beside, and he'd always known that") has Davi explain his
own drift in the register of the sheet. That is the charted beat: a character
reasons his way to the right choice and then does it. The uncharted part is
that nobody can tell whether he chose. Let the reader keep that.

## Notes

1. **Novum, structural:** nearest I know of for elective neuro-modification is
   Egan's "Reasons to Be Cheerful" (1997) and Chiang's "Liking What You See"
   (2002), both high confidence. Neither makes adulthood a dose with a
   calendar, and neither ties it to an inherited trade and a vote. Real
   distance. The closest structural neighbour remains *King Lear* (the
   abdication). The story is edge-of-map leaning uncharted.
2. **The bell code, structural:** a signal that spreads by relay and is
   misread is old. Sayers's *The Nine Tailors* (1934) is the nearest bell
   story I know (a mystery, not SF; high on the title and author). The
   beacon relay in *The Return of the King* (Tolkien) is the nearest chain
   of towers. What is uncharted is that the misreading is true to the
   grammar, and the mother lets it stand because it is half true. Protect
   it, and don't let anyone explain the joke.
3. **Stock beat, 7 October:** "Dadá," he said, kindly, is the best line. But
   "Zero chance. Sound like me. Always have. Uai, I don't" is the
   drift-shown-by-contradiction beat that every unreliable-self story
   reaches for. The line works because it is mixed, camp and mother in one
   breath. Keep the mixing, but cut the following clause, "I've always
   talked like this, the same as I always talked at that table with the
   beans". It repeats the point.
4. **Stock beat, line-level:** the wise old ringer with the failing memory
   (Vô Zé and the count, "four hundred and twelve") is a familiar figure.
   Lia correcting him is the twist on it, and it earns its place.
5. **Drift toward the nearest neighbour, structural:** the mother's accordion
   speech and "I never knew it was a price" tilt toward the "Gift of the
   Magi" shape the stage 3 critique warned of, a hidden cost revealed as
   love. It stays clear because her cost is old and she names it aloud
   once. Don't add a second sacrifice.
6. **Not charted but flagged, line-level:** "bricked", "zero chance",
   "one real" and "ceiling" are the open's slang. The reader's note that it
   took a beat is right. These are invented as the world's slang, so keep
   them, but "zero chance" appears in the adult mother's mouth by the end,
   which is the point. Check that it isn't also drifting into the narration.
7. **Tone charter:** no stock dystopia. The Lei da Entrega is a believable
   law with a cost, and not a menace. Good.
