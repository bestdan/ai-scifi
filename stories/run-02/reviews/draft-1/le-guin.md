## The one thing

The narration is supposed to drift and then settle into her register, and
the page doesn't do it. Davi's speech drifts ("ceiling", "bricked", the
tangled 2 October denial), but the narration stays one even voice from 16
September to 8 October. So the reader never hears the fixing, and the
choice-or-drift question rests on the First Reader's puzzling over it
rather than on an ear. Let the narration take the camp's clip in the pack
scenes and her long sentences ending on the thing in her hands from 28
September on. (Structural: it decides what the ending means.)

## Notes

1. **28 September, the notebook reread:** "a hand learns what it's standing
   beside, and he'd always known that..." is Davi's reasoning stated whole,
   in a long tidy sentence. The First Reader believed it, which is the
   danger: it explains the choice. Make it more hers, or cut one clause and
   let the board do it. (Line-level.)
2. **Who pays, the children:** Tonho, Rafaela and the untreated boy at the
   fountain are paid in full. But no one asks what the newborns were given
   in place of a vote. Not a scene; one adult, once, near the Annex or
   Vô Zé's table. Lia copying "in his exact rasp" nearly does it. (Structural,
   small.)
3. **2 October, Davi's denial:** the First Reader got lost here. The
   run-on is the right idea but "Zero chance. Sound like me. Always have.
   Uai, I don't," is three voices at once and reads as an error. Mark the
   borrowings with fewer words. (Line-level.)
4. **22 September fountain paragraph:** one enormous block. It earns the
   swept-along feel, but the middle skims. Cut a third. (Line-level.)
5. **8 October:** "and he counted" ends on an action the reader can't
   verify, and the vigil note stays unpaid-off. A lyric glance at which
   note he hears would close it. (Line-level.)
6. **Rafaela's Annex reading:** good as a broken rule, but the second half
   is read for the ledger. Let her stop one clause earlier. (Line-level.)

## What to protect

- "Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back."
- Seu Tonho's unplayed voice message: "Then I'd know what she sounds like
  now." And the phone held and "didn't play anything."
- "Somebody is going, uai. Me, out of that chair. This one's got cinnamon
  in."
- Rafaela stopping the reading and writing *read in full*; the cello that
  misses the same note and stops when he knocks.
- "Then you'll stand here all week where I can see your hands," and moved
  over.
- "Pitch before number." The code is the one thing in the story that
  belongs to him, and it is.
- Vô Zé: "Dadá," he said, kindly.
- The Entrega words: "I hand you the trade. From today I'm your
  apprentice. Teach me."

## Blind spot disclosure

I weigh society and the cost to the minor people above pace, so I may
overvalue the Tonho and Rafaela threads and underweight the first page's
jargon load, which the First Reader found heavy. I don't judge the metallurgy
or the neuroscience, and I'm slow to flag a gentle ending as too gentle. Let
Banks and Watts argue that the last scene wants more teeth.
