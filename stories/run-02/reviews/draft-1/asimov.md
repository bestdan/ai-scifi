## The one thing

Make the flat-bell misreading obey the code. The rules are in the story: "Pitch before number", and "one low stroke dropped in the middle of a feast" means someone is going. At the feast, no low stroke is rung. All nine strokes are the new high bell, 15 cents flat. Yet the Pedra Branca ringer relays `high ••• · low • · high •••` "with the low stroke where he'd heard it". He heard none. A reader who has the rule cannot make the town's error come out of it. The climax needs a rule the town can misapply, and this one is not it.

## Notes

1. **7 October, the relay (structural).** Fifteen cents flat is a tuning fault. It is not "low" in the code's sense, which is the old low bell against the high bell. The new bell was meant to sit "a clean fifth over the old low bell", and flat it is still a fifth. Plant a second clause in scene 1: a stroke that sits sour against the low bell is the sign. Then the town's error is a fair, checkable misreading of a stated rule. Vô Zé's table talk is the place for it.
2. **Timeline of the bell (structural).** Ceiça says "It goes up Thursday", which is 27 September. The Rosário bell tolls deaths, and the shoemaker "had been dying since June". The flat bell would ring for ten days before the feast. The town would have had its first flat-bell reading then, and Vô Zé would know it by the feast. On the page it is first heard "in the square" on the 7th. Either hang it on the 6th, or bind the clapper until the feast, and say so once.
3. **Cracked bell precedent (structural).** The old high bell "had rung like a frying pan since" July, and nobody read that as a death sign. Why does a flat bell trigger the reading when a cracked one didn't? Say the town reads pitch, not timbre, and fix note 1 with that.
4. **26 September, the cut (structural).** Ceiça, a master with a church's money paid in March, lets a 16-year-old make the final irreversible cut. She has just watched his hand tear the waist ("Bricked"), and she says "Uai. Then do it." The plot needs her to be careless. Give her a reason on the page: a deliberate lesson on a test cut, or her hands busy at the pour. Or let the cut be a choice she regrets aloud. As written, a reader will count this against her.
5. **Why not hold still while open (structural).** The reader can see why setting is rational: on 27 September Davi is carried into "Ribeiro's in" without deciding. But no one says outright why he can't stay open and stand at the board for a year. State it in one line from Rafaela or from Davi's own reasoning: the pull goes to whoever speaks last.
6. **16 September, opening (line-level).** "strickle", "false bell", "profile", "core" arrive before anyone speaks. The first reader waited "for a person to speak to another person". Open on "Clean profile" and fold the setup in.
7. **22 September, fountain paragraph (line-level).** The first reader skimmed the middle. The run-on is meant to swamp the reader, and it does. Trim to four or five beats and keep "ceiling" and Christmas.
8. **Cure date (line-level).** The sheet says the nets harden "over the six or seven days following the final tablet." The sixth is certification, the seventh the rite. Say "six days" in Rafaela's mouth, or give her a line that the sixth is the earliest she can sign.
9. **Lia's "Advice is one real" (line-level).** The first reader needed a beat. A reader who knows the economy ("nobody trusts advice") will see it, so this may just work.

## What to protect

- "Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back." It is the rule, a plant and a moral in nine words.
- "You can only take away. Where. That's the whole trade."
- Rafaela's "I chose whatever he'd said last", and the pledges folder "this thick": the Why row, played as an anecdote.
- Davi's 2 October denial in his mother's cadence, which is fair play for the drift rule.
- "I'm not taking the year." and "Then you'll stand here all week, where I can see your hands," and moved over.
- "Me, out of that chair. This one's got cinnamon in." Fix the code beneath it, but keep the turn.

## Blind spot disclosure

I read for rule consistency and fair play, so I may over-weight the bell-code logic against a reader who enjoys the misreading as a joke. I can't judge the ending's weight or the Portuguese texture. The sibling-level humour in Ceiça's silence works for me only if the code is tight. Le Guin and Chiang should push back on any fix that makes the town less foolish.
