# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A young man, Davi, in a Minas Gerais bell-founding town must choose whether to take a pill that permanently fixes his brain, and with it the sound of his voice and the way his hands learn, so he can inherit his mother's foundry licence on feast day, or stay "open" for a year and go off with his friends. He ruins a bell by cutting it flat, takes the pill, and cures standing beside his mother at the foundry board, so he comes out sounding like her and with the trade.

2. He stands to gain the licence, the seat and the family trade, and a fixed adult self. He stands to lose the open years: the city, the pack of friends, and the fast talk he shares with them. If he stays open, his hands learn at a child's speed, his mother has to hold the licence another year and be called "agarrada" by the town, and the family risks losing the trade. If he sets badly, say beside the wrong people, he is stuck that way for life. The drift is the danger: he will sound like whoever he stands beside that week and won't notice.

3. The law came after years when open people in councils kept changing their minds to suit whoever spoke to them last (the weathervane years, the pledges taken back). Parents who held on were also taken advantage of. So power passes on a fixed day by rite, and only to someone certified "closed". The Annex also says the mechanism of the second effect is not well understood. I only half-know why they made it a ritual and not a choice. I took it as a way to make the handover dignified and the decision binding.

4. Late on I learned that the flat bell reads as "someone in the house is going" to the whole valley, so the town grieves for Ceiça's stepping down, not for a death. That reframed the earlier lessons about the ringers' code and made Davi's mistake into a public sign. I also saw late, with Vô Zé's "Dadá", that Davi already sounds like his mother and the camp and can't hear it, which made me doubt how free his earlier certainty was.

5. On 7 October the new bell, 15 cents flat, rings at the feast and the square hears a death sign. The sign is relayed tower to tower down the valley. Ceiça says nothing and jokes that she is the one going, out of the chair. Davi receives the licence and the brass token at the Entrega, and she says the apprentice words. On 8 October they work side by side, her hand over his, and the Rosário rings six with the bell a shade low. It happened because he chose to set, and the bell was flat because he cut low near the lip and nobody puts metal back.

6. 
- Davi (Dadá) Ribeiro: the protagonist, the heir, good with models and drawings, with hands that haven't learned the work yet.
- Ceiça (Conceição Aparecida Ribeiro): his mother, the founder, a bell-founder of real skill, laconic.
- Vô Zé: his grandfather, 78, rings the Rosário bells, keeps the code.
- Lia: nine-year-old cousin, sells advice for one real, repeats the grandfather and counts the deaths he rang.
- Rafaela Brum: the setting counsellor and câmara member, 24, plays cello, set late, certifies him.
- Seu Tonho: the untreated butcher, "agarrado", holds his licence because his daughter Kátia is a cata-vento; hasn't played her message.
- Kátia: Tonho's daughter, open, drifts from city to city; never seen.
- The pack: Davi's open friends from the state school, who plan Christmas in Belo Horizonte.
- The shoemaker on Rua da Cadeia: the man who died on the first day.

7. 
- Whether Davi's choice was free or was already the mother's pull, since he had begun to talk like her and hum her forró.

- What happens to the open generally, whether they stay open for good and what "ceiling" means as slang.
- Whether the new bell stays at the church flat, and what the town finally thinks.
- Whether his mother's accordion story was meant to push him or only to tell him.
- What the 8 October "counted" is counting.
