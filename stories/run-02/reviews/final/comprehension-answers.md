# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A teenage boy from a family of bell founders has to choose between taking a tablet that fixes his brain for good, so he can inherit the family licence, and staying open a year to learn the trade slowly with his mother. He takes the tablet, makes a cut that leaves the new church bell flat, and ends up inheriting the licence and a bell that the valley mistakes for a death announcement.

2. He stands to gain the foundry's licence and vote, and hands that learn the trade. If he sets now, he also gives up the pack of friends and their plans for Belo Horizonte. If he stays open, he keeps the friends and his mother has to hold the licence another year and be called "agarrada". If he sets, there is no way back. I don't think failing meant disaster. Failing mostly meant the town talking, a lost year, or a flat bell.

3. The town made this arrangement after the "weathervane years", when untreated parents had held offices too long and open young council members changed their votes to suit whoever spoke last. So seats now pass on a fixed day, only to heirs certified "closed". I was less sure why the treatment keeps children open at all. I took it to be a deliberate trade, because open children learn skills like hands and accents fast.

4. Late on, I learned that the cut was Davi's. Heir's last cut on the feast bell, and he made it wrong. The ringing code then turns the flatness into the message "someone in the house is going", which I'd been told early on. I also saw the repeated "I can't find the sentence where it was decided" as being about him being open, and I only gradually connected it to the fountain scenes. Another late discovery: the mother is quietly keeping the false alarm going, since she is the one "going" out of the chair.

5. At the feast the new bell's Virgin stroke sounded fifteen cents low, and the old ringers took it as a low stroke inside a feast. The news spread down the valley as a death omen, and Ceiça said nothing and accepted the soup. Then Davi received the licence and token at the Entrega, and she called herself his apprentice. The next morning the Virgin sounds flat again and he hears it before he counts. It happened because he took the tablets, his hand slipped at the lathe, and the feast date wouldn't move.

6. Characters:
- Davi (also "Dadá"): the boy, sixteen, heir, who cuts the bell wrong and sets.
- Ceiça (Conceição Aparecida Ribeiro): his mother, the bell founder, who offers him a year of her time.
- Vô Zé: his grandfather, seventy-eight, the Rosário bell ringer.
- Lia: his nine-year-old cousin, sharp, learns the bell code at once.
- Rafaela Brum: the town setting counsellor, twenty-four, who plays the cello and set at sixteen beside a boyfriend.
- Seu Tonho: the butcher, untreated, "agarrado", with a daughter Kátia who is a wind-follower and left a message he hasn't played.
- The pack: Davi's open friends at the fountain.

7. I'm unsure about:
- Whether Davi chose freely, or whether the pack's pull and his sense of the feast date decided it for him.
- Whether the tablets really made him sound like his mother, or just stood next to her.
- Whether Ceiça knew the bell was flat on purpose, or let it go.
- What "Max" was.
- Exactly how the hum and the strike note differ in what people hear.
- Whether Tonho plays the message.
