## The one thing

Make the 7 October ring produce the relay by the stated rule. At the table: "a high bell that sits sour against the low one, that's a low, to an old ear. And one low stroke dropped in the middle of a feast means somebody in that house is going." At the feast the code is `high ••• · high ••• · high •••`, and all nine strokes are the new bell, equally sour. By the rule that is nine lows, not "one low stroke dropped in the middle". Yet Pedra Branca relays `high ••• · low • · high •••`. A reader holding the rule cannot reach the town's error. The fix is mechanical. Either the feast pattern includes strokes on the old bell, so that only some strokes sour against it. Or the townspeople register only the first stroke ("You hear the low one first"), and the narration says they relay one low and then count the rest as highs from habit. Draft 1's problem is half-fixed: the rule exists, but the event doesn't follow it.

## Notes

1. **26 Sep and 16 Sep, the hands arithmetic (structural).** The rule is "a child's hands learn a cut in a bell or two", and Ceiça learned on "forty-some bells". Davi has had "his hands on it a bell or two a season" for sixteen years. By the story's own numbers he should be close to her already. Say plainly that nobody lets a child onto the cutter, because metal can't be put back, and that the loam is all he's had. Then this is his first real cut, and "the heir makes the last cut" becomes the first cut too.
2. **7 Oct (structural).** Vô Zé is in the tower and hears his own bell sour. He and Ceiça let the town misread for the whole day. That is believable for Ceiça ("didn't explain"). One line from Vô Zé, even a shrug, would show he knows and is letting it ride.
3. **26 Sep, "Then that's a bell you won't make" (line-level).** The first reader read this as an answer about Christmas. Its meaning, Davi's ten years, is only inferred. Put "ten years" beside it in her mouth or his.
4. **7 Oct, the Congado sentence (line-level).** One sentence runs the whole procession and delays the ring. Cut to the Moçambique, the queen, and the clapper.
5. **Pregar (line-level).** Defined on the 19th, used on the 28th, nine days later. A short echo in between would help the cold reader.

## What to protect

- "Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back."
- "You can only take away. Where. That's the whole trade."
- "A crack's only a buzz, nobody reads a buzz." It is the right plant, but the ring must obey it.
- Lia's correction to four hundred and twelve: it shows the open's gift and sets up the rule that numbers follow pitch.
- "It's a good bell, uai. It's a bit low. So are lots of people."
- "Whose board?" "Ours." And "Pregar", said to the form.
- "Me, out of that chair. This one's got cinnamon in."
- The 2 Oct denial: "Zero chance... Uai, I don't." It is fair play for the drift rule.
- The last line, which shows "pitch before number" turned into a habit.

## Blind spot disclosure

I weight rule consistency over feeling. The misreading may work for a reader as a joke even if the code doesn't derive it, and I can't judge that. I can't judge the Portuguese texture or the ending's weight. If Le Guin or Chiang say the flat-bell scene is better a little wrong, weigh that against my note, but I would fix the nine-strokes logic regardless.
