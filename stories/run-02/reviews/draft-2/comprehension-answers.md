# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A teenage boy in a Minas Gerais bell-founding family has to decide whether to take a pill course that "sets" his brain, so he can inherit his mother's foundry licence and town vote on feast day, or stay "open" another year and keep learning the craft by hand. He stays open and works at the board beside his mother, and is set that way. The town misreads the slightly flat bell he cut as a death omen, and he ends up as the licence holder with his mother as his apprentice.

2. If he sets now, he gets the seat, the vote and the licence right away, but his hands will learn slowly, like his mother's with the accordion and Rafaela's with the cello, maybe ten years to be good. If he stays open, he can learn the trade quickly, at a child's speed, but his mother has to hold the licence past the feast and be called agarrada, and his speech and views will drift to whoever he is near, including the city friends at Christmas. If he fails, he becomes a competent-in-theory founder who cannot make the bells with his hands, and the family trade and licence suffer.

3. The law (the Entrega) exists because during the "weathervane years" the open, who shift opinion to the last speaker, made public office unstable, and old officeholders who weren't given a dignified way out stayed too long and were manipulated. So seats pass on a fixed day, by rite, only to someone who is set. The preamble was read aloud so the heir understands the trade-offs. The treatment at birth keeps people open; the pills close them. The Annex admits the mechanism isn't well understood.

4. Late in the story I realised the choice might not be a choice in the way he thought: the "stay near the person" rule means he is shaped by whoever he stands beside in the curing week. Also, his mother had offered the year but it cost her, and the flat bell I'd thought was just a mistake becomes a death omen for the town. Also learning that the open don't hear their own drift (Vô Zé on the 2nd).

5. He took the pills on the 28th, 29th and 30th while working beside his mother at the board, so he sounded like her afterwards, and Rafaela signed him off on the sixth. At the feast the flat bell was rung and heard as a low stroke in a feast, meaning someone in the house is dying. The news passed down the valley, and his mother let people believe it, joking that it was her leaving the chair. He received the licence and token, and she said she was his apprentice. The next morning she put her hand over his and slowed the board, and he heard the low in the bell before counting.

6. Davi (Dadá): the protagonist, seventeen or so, model-and-app minded, camp slang.
   Ceiça (Conceição Aparecida Ribeiro): his mother, bell founder, ninth-year trained, standing down.
   Vô Zé: his grandfather, seventy-eight, rings the Rosário tower.
   Lia: nine-year-old cousin, open, learned the bell code quickly, charges "advice is one real".
   Rafaela Brum: town setting counsellor and câmara member, twenty-four, cellist, set at twenty.
   Seu Tonho: the butcher, untreated, holds a licence he can't hand on, daughter Kátia is a wanderer.
   Kátia: Tonho's daughter, open, left a voice message he hasn't played.
   The pack: Davi's open school friends at the fountain.
