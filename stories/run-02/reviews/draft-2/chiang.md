## The one thing
Trust the drift. The form now shows what the premise says, so the text should stop also saying it. Cut the second, explicit statement of the question on 2 October (see note 1). Let the first one, at the fountain, stand alone and let the reader recognise the repeat.

## Notes

1. **2 Oct, "tried to find the place where it had been decided... none of the sentences was the one where it happened" (structural):** This repeats the 22 Sep sentence almost word for word, and the plan asked for exactly that. But by now the narration has been in Ceiça's cadence for four scenes, and the reader has already heard the drift. The repeat narrates the effect the form has already produced. Keep a shorter version: "He tried to find the place where it had been decided, and couldn't." Drop the second sentence. The first reader was moved by it in both places, so I would not cut it entirely.
2. **7 Oct, the Congado sentence (line-level):** One sentence of about 150 words, in her cadence, and the first reader waited for the bell. The length is the form, but the bell is where it should end, not where it continues. Break it once before "Vô Zé was up in the tower".
3. **8 Oct, last line (structural):** Pitch-before-number is now shown, but in scene 1 Davi did it the same way ("Low first: a death"). So the line reads as habit, not change. The change could be what he hears: the flat low, which he made, as a plain low, where scene 1 had him naming the buzz as only a buzz. Make one concrete clause of that.
4. **22 Sep, "A model on a cutter, his model..." (line-level):** This paragraph anticipates Ceiça's "A machine bell's a machine bell" on 26 Sep, and "Nobody answered" is a zinger. It is the one scene-sized element I would cut. The machine objection is better in her mouth.
5. **19 Sep, Rafaela's "I chose whatever he'd said last" (line-level):** I still think it states the theme. I accept the plan's decision to keep it. The cold reader's confusion over whether she was admitting a mistake or boasting suggests the line could be flatter: drop "I was sixteen and open," and let her say the second half alone.
6. **28 Sep notebook paragraph:** Now plainly hers, which is what I asked. No further change.

## Blind spot disclosure
I over-weight the form and under-weight momentum. My note 1 asks for less explicitness on a point that the Understood axes may need: the earlier gate failed on the Ending row, and the echo may be what makes it pass. Trust the grader over me there.
