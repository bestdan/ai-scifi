# First Reader report, draft 2

## Where I got lost

- The opening paragraphs (16 Sep, "Clean profile... Hair fat at the shoulder"). I didn't know what was being talked about until the long paragraph about the board explained it. I kept going because the plank and the curl of loam were vivid. Still, the first two lines were noise to me.
- The bell-code lines (the dots in the quoted blocks). I followed that low means a death and high means a feast, but the first one ("Four high: of the Rosário brotherhood") took me a moment to work out. After the table scene I was fine.
- "Pregar" on the 19th. I got that it meant standing next to someone on purpose to fix them in your shape. It then came up again on the 28th, and I had to remember.
- The 22 Sep fountain paragraph ran by fast. I understood that he was swept in and the decision never happened anywhere. The words "that's ceiling" meant nothing to me, though I got that it was slang.
- "Advice is one real": I took it as Lia charging a coin. It was charming, but it took me a sentence.
- Rafaela's "I chose whatever he'd said last." I was a little unsure whether she was admitting a mistake or boasting.
- "A bell won't be made" and "Then that's a bell you won't make" (26 Sep). I read it as her reply about Christmas, and I didn't see right away that she meant the ten years of him not being good yet.

## Where I got bored

- The long legal reading on the 19th (the numbered clauses). It's long, but I got through it because Rafaela interrupted it with her own remarks and "That's the honest sentence." Clause One in particular I skimmed. I was waiting for her to get to "Things not on the sheet", and that part woke me up.
- The Congado procession in one huge sentence on 7 Oct. Lovely list, but I was waiting for the moment the bell rang.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- Ceiça packing the tear in the loam with two fingers: "Loam you can put back. Nobody puts metal back."
- Lia, nine, correcting her grandfather to four hundred and twelve, and Vô Zé looking at the ceiling "as if the number were written there in a hand he didn't trust."
- Seu Tonho's phone: "I haven't played it. Then I'd know what she sounds like now." And again at the end, "held his phone, and didn't play anything." That one got me.
- "It's a good bell, uai. It's a bit low. So are lots of people." I laughed and then it hurt.
- The accordion story. Her saying "I never knew it was a price."
- The chair scene: "Whose board?" "Ours." And Rafaela saying "Pregar" to the form.
- Him sliding into his mother's way of talking without hearing it, and Vô Zé gently saying "Dadá" and nothing more. When he says "Uai, I don't," I smiled.
- The flat bell being heard as a death in the house and carried down the whole valley, with her answering "Somebody is going, uai. Me, out of that chair. This one's got cinnamon in." That was my favourite moment.
- The last line, hearing the low before he'd counted.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared most about Ceiça, then Davi, and a surprising amount about Seu Tonho and Rafaela, each of whom got only a few minutes. Yes, I'd have kept reading well past page 2. I'd have been wobbly on the first half page, and the table scene is where I was hooked. By the clinic scene I was in.

One thing I felt: I wasn't sure he'd chosen it himself, and the story seems to know that. The line about looking for the place where it was decided got me, in both places it appears.

## The question I'd still be thinking about the next day

Was it really his choice to set at the board with his mother, or did he just do what the last person near him wanted, the same as the pack? Nothing in the story says the two are different, and I couldn't tell if he could.

A smaller one: what did Kátia's voice message say?
