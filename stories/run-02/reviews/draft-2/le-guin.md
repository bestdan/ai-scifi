## The one thing

The drift now works on the ear, and that was my round-1 demand. The remaining
risk is that the story's question ("was it his choice?") is answered by the
reader's ear and not by the page. Leave it there. The change that matters is
to protect it. Don't let any later pass add a line, in Davi's head or anyone's
mouth, that says which it was. The First Reader's final question is the story
working. (Structural: no change, only restraint.)

## Notes

1. **7 October, the Congado sentence:** one sentence, about 130 words, and the
   First Reader waited it out. It is good ethnography ("the gungas on their
   ankles, all those tin rattles chattering at once in step"), but it delays the
   bell. Cut the parasols and the old queen's rosary, or break it at the
   Moçambique. (Line-level.)
2. **28 September, the notebook paragraph:** "ten years is ten years whichever
   end of it you start from" is still the one clause that sounds like Davi's own
   arithmetic. It is the only reasoning here that is his. Keep it, because the
   story needs one thread of his, but put it last, so her phrases carry the
   weight and his is the thing under them. (Line-level.)
3. **8 October, hand over hand:** "put her hand over his on the board" pays off
   "an inch off, not touching". It is also the tidiest gesture in the story, and
   the third curl landing on his boots. Let the third repetition go; the
   apprentice line and the hand are enough. (Line-level.)
4. **Kátia's voice message:** the First Reader asked what it said. That is a
   good open question, not a defect. Do not answer it.
5. **The open children:** Lia gets the code in two trips, and Vô Zé gets "Nobody
   asked any of you". It is small and enough. I'd still like Lia to be wrong
   about something outside the code, so the gift reads as a trade and not a
   perk. One clause. (Line-level.)

No structural problem remains from my lens.

## Blind spot disclosure

I'm drawn to society and to the minor people, and I read Tonho and Rafaela
generously. I'm slow to hear pace problems, and I may be over-forgiving of the
long set pieces. I don't judge the bell acoustics. I may also be too content
with a gentle ending. Let the Watts and Banks lenses argue for teeth.
