# Stage 2 audit: run-02

Cartographer. 27 cards from nine lenses. Yield is judged at 5,500 words, the
midpoint of the brief's range.

## The seed itself

The bare seed is **charted**, as the brief expects. Children much smarter
than the adults who raise them have a long shelf:

- *The Hampdenshire Wonder* (J. D. Beresford, 1911), confidence high;
- "Mimsy Were the Borogoves" (Lewis Padgett, i.e. Kuttner and Moore, 1943),
  confidence high;
- *Odd John* (Olaf Stapledon, 1935), confidence high;
- *Children of the Atom* (Wilmar H. Shiras, 1953 fix-up of "In Hiding", 1948),
  confidence high;
- *The Midwich Cuckoos* (John Wyndham, 1957), confidence high;
- *Beggars in Spain* (Nancy Kress, novella 1991, novel 1993) and *Beggars and
  Choosers* (1994), confidence high;
- "The Evolution of Human Science" (Ted Chiang, *Nature*, 2000), confidence
  high;
- *Klara and the Sun* (Kazuo Ishiguro, 2021), with its "lifted" children,
  confidence high.

The **handover** direction has its own near neighbour: *Supernova Era* (Liu
Cixin, Chinese 2003, English 2019), in which every adult dies within a year
and the children inherit the world after a crash "Great Education"
(confidence high). It's a catastrophe, not a handover between living
generations, so it differs in kind from every card here. The brief waives
novelty for the seed, so the Novelty scores below are for each card's angle.

**A correction for the lenses.** Four cards cite Chiang's "Catching Crumbs
from the Table" and "The Evolution of Human Science" as if they were two
works. They are the same story: it ran in *Nature* on 1 June 2000 as
"Catching Crumbs from the Table" and was retitled for *Stories of Your Life
and Others* (2002). It is an editorial with no characters, and it is the
inverse case (ordinary humans interpreting metahuman work), so it is the
nearest neighbour for the whole explainer cluster below, but no closer than
"theme".

Kress is cited on 11 of 27 cards. Most of those citations are correct and not
close: Kress's axis is economic and adversarial. I don't repeat her below
except where she really is the nearest.

## Convergence

The lenses worked blind. They converged hard. Ten clusters, from strongest to
weakest:

1. **The explainer institution** (8 cards). Chiang Fourth Draft, Chiang
   Countersign, Egan Median Reader Clause, Banks Reasons Given, Asimov
   Assessor Rule, Asimov Explain It Back, Vinge Plain Reader, and Le Guin
   Owing Year. Six lenses invented a rule that the treated must explain, or
   be checked, at the untreated level. Three of them (Egan, Banks, Vinge) are
   the same statute: a law is valid only once an untreated reader signs it.
   The territory is open in published fiction (the nearest is Chiang's 2000
   editorial, the inverse), but the clustering is an **attractor in the
   model**. It's the first clever move once the seed meets institutions.
   Next run: let one lens take it and steer the rest away.
2. **Water infrastructure** (8 cards). Lem's ditch board, Banks's flood
   barriers and coastal retreat, Asimov's water authority and harbour
   authority, Rajaniemi's dike-reeve, and Vinge's tidal barrier. Ferries
   appear three times (Le Guin, Asimov, Rajaniemi), and Le Guin adds a
   boatyard. That's 12 of 27 cards at the shoreline. This is an
   **attractor**, not charted ground: water boards are the model's safe
   choice of an institution that is consequential, local, and not
   dystopian. Next run should ban it outright.
3. **Names.** "Ines" is the protagonist of four cards (Egan, Banks, Asimov,
   Vinge). Tomasz and Tomas appear in two (Chiang, Banks); Ilse and Iiris,
   and Marit and Maren, are near-twins. Settings run Turku, Zeeland,
   Lerwick, a Welsh valleys town, Niagara, Mindelo: a North Atlantic,
   Northern European register. **Attractor**, pure and simple, and a
   drafter has to rename from the world, as style-guide rule 7 requires.
4. **Teen girl at a choice to tell or not tell** (21 of 27 protagonists are
   girls or women; about 8 cards end on "expose it or let it run").
   **Attractor.** It's a sensible shape for the brief, but the uniformity is
   a model habit.
5. **The old maths teacher** (3 cards: Asimov, Rajaniemi, Vinge), and
   **grandmothers as interpreter or refuge** (6 cards: Chiang Unread Room,
   Banks Hinge, Lem Ditch Board, Egan Median Reader, Watts Keeper, Le Guin
   Keepers). The brief named teachers, so the first is half-prompted. The
   grandmothers are an **attractor**: the untreated elder as a warm
   repository.
6. **Elder knowledge that intelligence can't reach** (5 cards: Egan Forty
   Winters, Lem Ditch Board, Le Guin Stepping-Down, Asimov Assessor Rule,
   Watts Keeper). **Charted in nonfiction** (Scott, Lansing, Chesterton) and
   open in fiction. It's also the model's way of consoling the elders, which
   risks the Panglossian failure the charter names. It's useful only where
   the elders' knowledge can also be wrong (see the Forty Winters push).
7. **Critical periods kept open** (2 cards: Watts Open Window and Rajaniemi
   Setting). Same mechanism, same papers, same shape of choice. Uncharted in
   fiction as far as I can find; the convergence comes from real
   neuroscience being the obvious mechanism for "learns much faster". A
   **mechanism attractor**, but a good one. Pick one card, not both.
8. **The gap as a chosen variable** (3 cards: Egan Gap Dial, Banks Hinge,
   Vinge Version Day). **Partly charted** (Kress, *Gattaca*, Ishiguro). The
   ban (Banks) is the least charted of the three.
9. **Opacity as a gift** (3 cards: Chiang Unread Room, Banks Hinge,
   Rajaniemi Lagman). A parent or child protected by not being seen
   through. Uncharted, and the strongest convergence that isn't a habit.
10. **Orchards and harvests** (3 cards: Chiang's cider orchard, Watts's cider
    orchard, Egan's icewine vineyard). **Attractor.**

Two titles collide too: Le Guin's "The Keepers" and Watts's "The Keeper".

**Verdict.** Almost all of the convergence is in the model, not in the
literature. The ideas cluster on territory that is thin in published SF, but
the settings, names and institutions cluster on the model's defaults, and
the next run should steer those away.
