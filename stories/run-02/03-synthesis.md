# Stage 3 synthesis: run-02

Four premises from the stage 2 shortlist. Every one keeps the brief's fixed
seed: a treatment given at birth that adds about 60 IQ points and much
faster learning. The audit's attractor list applies to all four: no water
boards, harbours, ferries or orchards, no protagonist named Ines, and no
North Atlantic default setting. Settings below are placeholders chosen to
stay off that list; stage 4 fixes them.

## A. The Setting Day

*Collision 1: le-guin/The Stepping-Down × watts/The Open Window,
rajaniemi/Setting. Audit 36/40.*

The treatment works by keeping the brain's critical periods open, so the
treated never settle into fixed adults: they learn anything in weeks, and
their tastes, accents and loyalties drift with whoever they stand near.
Adulthood has become a dose you choose, which sets the brain like plaster.
Towns have built a rite around it. In a treated child's sixteenth year, on
a fixed day, the untreated parent steps down: hands over the household
seat, the trade licence and the council vote, and becomes the child's
junior. The seat goes only to someone who has set, because nobody wants a
council that drifts. A girl is three weeks from her rite in her mother's
trade, a hand craft (say, a bell foundry or a stone-carving yard) where she
can model everything and her hands still can't do it. Only an unset brain
can still train her hands at a child's speed. Set on the day, and she takes
a seat she can't fill, with hands that will learn at an adult's pace from
then on. Delay, and the town reads it as her mother refusing to step down.
**Push past the map:** her mother has been set all her life without ever
having chosen it, and when asked which to do, her answer is that she never
knew it was a price. A friend her age sets early, without telling her, so
that she won't have to choose between him and the world.

- **Strengths:** the coming of age is literal and chosen, which fits the
  brief exactly; the handover is between living people on a named day; the
  cost runs both ways and lives in the body.
- **Risks:** two nova that must arrive together without an info-dump; the
  "60 IQ points from plasticity" link is a stretch the audit flagged; the
  friend subplot may not fit.
- **Load:** novum 1, open plasticity plus the setting dose. Novum 2, the
  stepping-down rite, which the first forces. No twist.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## B. The Regent

*Collision 2: rajaniemi/The Minority of the Dike-Reeve × lem/Minutes of the
Saddle Creek Ditch Board × egan/Forty Winters. Audit 34/40.*

The law won't let a fourteen-year-old hold public office, so treated heirs
to posts get regents: untreated adults who hold the seat until the heir
comes of age, with one power only, to delay a signature. A treated girl of
sixteen is heir to the chair of a mountain avalanche commission (moved off
the audit's water attractor), and her regent is her old teacher. She can
prove the new closure plan is better on every variable she models. He
stalls on the strength of forty winters of samples and an old commission
rule, kept on the books with "reason unknown; retain". Thinking speeds up
with the treatment, but winters don't come faster, and the elders hold
more samples. To overrule him she has to take the seat early. **Push past
the map:** both of them are partly wrong. The climate has moved, so his
samples are stale, and her model is overfitted to too few winters. The old
rule protects something that neither of their reasons covers.

- **Strengths:** the strongest rigor on the list; a clean, novum-driven
  choice; the regent gives the elders a real role without sentiment.
- **Risks:** three nova in a short story; the "elder wisdom wins" shape
  risks consoling the untreated (the audit's Panglossian warning); avalanche
  science needs research.
- **Load:** novum 1, regency of the treated. Novum 2, the samples-versus-
  inference split. Twist: the old rule's real reason.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words.

## C. Wearing Lead

*Collision 3: rajaniemi/The Lagman × chiang/The Unread Room. Audit 34/40.*

One generation in, treated parents can read their treated children almost
completely: tells, motives, lies, all at once. The only place left to be
partly misunderstood is with an untreated adult. A treated girl of fifteen
has one: her mother, who is untreated, who misreads her warmly, and whose
kitchen is the one room where she has been able to become someone without
being watched doing it. Her treated father sees through her; her mother
never has. Then she catches it: the answers that arrive too evenly, the
missing honest mistakes. Her mother was in the early trial and has been
faking slowness ("wearing lead") for fifteen years, not for any office or
advantage, only so that her daughter would have someone who didn't
understand her. **Push past the map:** nobody else would care, so there is
nothing to report. The girl's choice is whether to say she knows, which
ends the gift, or to start wearing lead herself so that her mother can keep
giving it.

- **Strengths:** the most intimate premise; the twist grows out of the
  novum; the ending choice is itself a coming of age, the child taking up
  the parent's role.
- **Risks:** "smart enough to read anyone" is asserted rather than argued;
  the handover the brief asks for is present only as a private, inverted
  one; close to "Harrison Bergeron" in image.
- **Load:** novum 1, transparency among the treated (opacity as a refuge).
  Twist: the mother is treated.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## D. Plain Account

*Collision 4: banks/Minding × chiang/Fourth Draft. Audit 32/40.*

Treated teenagers must certify that untreated relatives consent to the
decisions they can't follow, by writing a "plain account" the relative can
restate and a treated assessor finds complete. A girl's coming-of-age exam
is to write one for her father, a decision about a surgery. Meanwhile she
and her friends quietly "mind" their parents, fixing their work at night so
they keep feeling competent. Across four drafts of the account, she finds
her father has been correcting her drafts in a hand she can read: he has
been minding her too. **Push past the map:** his corrections are right and
hers were wrong.

- **Strengths:** a natural document form; two loving deceptions meet at a
  consent form; low cast.
- **Risks:** sits in the audit's strongest model attractor (the explainer
  institution, eight cards); two nova plus a twist; the minding practice
  strains belief.
- **Load:** novum 1, the plain-account law. Novum 2, minding. Twist: the
  father's counter-minding.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words.
