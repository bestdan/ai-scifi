# Stage 5 critique: Chiang lens

Blind spots: I am weak on pace and conflict, and I tend to prefer the measured option. Read my cuts against Asimov's and Banks's.

The question the outline is about: if a mind that drifts chooses the person it will be fixed as, whose choice is it? The form (narration drifting, then fixing) fits that question well. The notes below are about where the outline doesn't yet cash it in.

1. **[must-fix] The climax is summarised, not staged.** Scene 5 spans nine days in 1,100 words, and "the narration does not give a reason" is its only dramatic event. The idea's real climax is the instant the drift stops, and the reader should hear it stop. Fix: dramatise two beats at full length. First, the 28th, where he reads his own note and takes the tablet. Second, the denial to Vô Zé, with the narration's last drifting sentence ending mid-register on "uai". Compress the rest of the week to a paragraph. Cut about 300 words from scenes 3 and 6 to pay for it.

2. **[must-fix] Make the bell code a visible formal device.** The bible's "one register that never drifts" is invisible to a reader unless it is on the page. Fix: render bell-code messages as strike counts in a fixed typographic form, such as numerals with rests. Use the same form for the death at noon in scene 1, his note in scene 2 and the town's reading in scene 6. Then the reader can check the note against the tablet and check the town's misreading against the actual flat strike. The reader holds the one thing Davi's drift can't touch, and that makes the ending fair.

3. **[should] Scene 3 is the unnecessary scene.** It carries three jobs: the pack, Seu Tonho and the machine thought. Seu Tonho can work in one image at the Entrega, his door and the unplayed message. The machine thought is already answered in scene 4. Keep only the pack's overlapping decision, about 400 words, and put it in scene 1's lunchtime or as a call arriving in scene 4. The pack's later messages in scene 5 need that one demonstration and no more.

4. **[should] The ending should unsettle the question, not answer it tenderly.** "He reads them in the voice that has always been his" is too neat. The outline says the voice is hers after the cure, so the sentence resolves the open question by assertion. Her hand slowing his is a good gesture, but it affirms inheritance. Fix: end on the hand-over-hand with no claim about whose voice it is. Let the reader notice that the register is hers and that the bell code still reads in his. Alternatively, have him hear the flat bell as flat, correctly, when everyone else hears a message. That one fact is his, and it costs nothing to state.

5. **[should] Scenes 1 and 2 introduce more than they anchor.** Scene 1 brings Davi, Ceiça, Vô Zé, Lia, the Entrega, the bell and "the open". Scene 2 adds Rafaela, firma, the nets, the pull, cata-vento and the 28th. Fix: in scene 1 keep Lia as "his cousin" (no name) until she copies him, and drop Entrega until Vô Zé's kitchen line. Move *cata-vento* to scene 3 via Kátia. The consent sheet then does only the nets, the pull and "none yet".

The cost is paid in metal in scene 4 (the flat cut), which is good and needs no restatement. Don't add narration that tells us he lost the pack; let the unreadable messages show it.
