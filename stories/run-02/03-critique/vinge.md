# Stage 3 critique: Vinge lens

**Blind spots.** I like plain prose less than I should, and I push futurity
even where a story's power is its human scale. I can't judge whether a kitchen
scene lands emotionally. Chiang and Banks should override me on that, and on
whether C's feeling is earned.

## A. The Setting Day

**Futurity:** F3. Adulthood becomes a dose, and "elder" means whoever has
set. The rite itself (a retiring parent, a seat, a licence) is Lear and
*inkyo* in a new coat, and the bell foundry and town council are the familiar
part. **Viewpoint:** this is the only premise with a real trick. A drifting
mind is writeable only from inside, and the girl is three weeks from fixing
hers. If the narration drifts with whoever she stands near, the reader
watches an unfixed mind work. **Weak link:** the 60-point claim. The story
fails if the treated are merely "flexible". **Push:** let the prose itself
drift, in dialect, vocabulary and what it notices, and let the council's
fear of a drifting member be the strange institution, in place of the
parental step-down.

## B. The Regent

**Futurity:** F2. Regency, a commission and an old teacher are all present
tense with better arithmetic. **Viewpoint:** it holds, but nothing in it
needs the treatment. A clever girl against an experienced man is the shape of
a hundred stories. The treated mind appears only as "she can prove it". **Push:**
the network. She isn't alone. A dozen treated heirs on other commissions run
the same models on the same few winters and agree with her, and their
agreement is correlated error. Her consensus is the overfit. That scales the
premise up from one girl and gives the elders something no sentiment can.
Without it this is the least futurist card.

## C. Wearing Lead

**Futurity:** F3. Mutual near-transparency among the treated changes what
privacy, parent and lie mean, and it is the best posthuman effect on the
list, because it is shown by what the father doesn't say. **Viewpoint:**
holds. We stand with the one being read, so the superior reading is never
narrated from inside. **Too familiar:** the kitchen, the fifteen-year-old and
the warm mother. "Harrison Bergeron" is a real overlap. **Weak link:** "reads
almost completely" is asserted. **Push:** show a treated household's
conventions for living with being read. They would have manners that work as
encryption, such as stock gestures, deliberate noise and rehearsed
sincerity. Then the mother's lead is a cipher the girl has to break, using
tools no ordinary adult would have.

## D. Plain Account

**Futurity:** F2. A consent form, a surgery and a father are familiar, and the
"minding" practice reads as a present-day teenager fixing a parent's pride.
**Viewpoint:** the plain account is a lossy compression of a treated mind
for an untreated reader, which is a good device. The reader sees the
reduction, and the narration can show what gets lost. But this is the
audit's strongest attractor, with eight cards sharing it. **Push:** drop
minding. Let the four drafts degrade visibly, each plainer and each losing a
fact the surgery depends on, so the reader finds the gap before she does.

## Ranking

1. **A.** It is the only premise that finds a way around the wall and not
   merely a smarter person inside a familiar room.
2. **C.** It has the most original effect of intelligence, and the viewpoint
   is correctly placed.
3. **B.** The correlated-error push could lift it, but as written it is F2.
4. **D.** It sits in the attractor cluster, and the novum is a procedure.

**Why A should win.** The brief's whole subject is a generation that
outruns the adults, and A is the premise that makes the outrunning visible in
the form. A mind that stays open is a mind that can't be summed up, so the
story can't stand outside it and describe it. It has to be lived through a
voice that changes, and that is the trick Vinge-style stories need. Its
estimated minimum of 4,500 words sits well inside the 4,000 to 7,000 range
and leaves room for the drift to be earned. The risks are real. The two nova
must arrive together without a lecture, and the plasticity-to-IQ link has to
be argued by what the girl can do, never by a speech. The friend who sets
early is the best human beat, and it costs a second character, so keep the
cast small. The mother's "I never knew it was a price" is a good line to
build toward, since the one adult who can't be treated is the one who can
say what setting cost.
