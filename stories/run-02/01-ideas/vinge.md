# Idea cards: vinge

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front.** I push futurity even when a
story's power is in its human scale, and this brief asks for a coming-of-age
story, which is human scale by definition. My instincts run toward
technothriller plotting, plain prose and characters who serve as functions.
I won't pretend to cover warmth or interiority. Le Guin and Chiang should
push back on all three cards for that. I've also leaned towards F3 and F4
while the brief's floor is F2, so check whether the reach costs the story
its intimacy.

## Card 1: The Plain Reader

- **Novum:** Once the treated cohort drafts the province's law, no rule takes force until a sworn untreated reader, a plainer, signs that they understand it. Being slow is now a credentialed public office.
- **Futurity:** F3. "Elder" turns from authority into audience: the old are the people the young have to earn.
- **How it works:** Treated drafters wrote correct rules that the untreated majority couldn't follow, so nobody obeyed them. The fix is a veto by comprehension.
- **First-order consequence:** Untreated adults get paid, respected work as plainers.
- **Second-order consequence:** Treated drafters learn to write downward, which turns out to be the hardest skill they have.
- **Third-order consequence:** The office has a death date. When the last untreated reader dies, nobody is left to be slow for.
- **The story:** Ines, 16, is a treated apprentice drafter. Her plainer is her old maths teacher. He can't follow her tidal-barrier rule, and the clause he stumbles on protects a fishing village. She can simplify the rule and lose the protection, or invoke the override her cohort keeps wanting to use, which would quietly end his office.
- **Why this lens:** Which mind is just slow enough to be the narrator? Here the in-world answer is the trick that makes the story writeable: the story is told in the register Ines uses for a plainer, so the text itself is a plain reading.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "Catching Crumbs from the Table", which is the reverse case (humans interpreting metahuman science). Kress's *Beggars in Spain*, but only for the resentment between the treated and the untreated. Plain-language law movements. I know of no slow-reader veto.

## Card 2: Version Day

- **Novum:** The treated generation's first major product is a better treatment, so each cohort designs the one that will surpass it, and the interval between handovers keeps shrinking. A generation stops being defined by age and starts being defined by version, and siblings end up in different generations.
- **Futurity:** F3, verging on F4. On a single family tree you can see the slope toward an event horizon.
- **How it works:** V1 came twenty years ago. The V1 cohort designed V2, which has been given for the last seven years. V3, designed jointly by V1 and V2, is up for county consent. If the interval keeps shrinking, around V6 the gap closes within one childhood.
- **First-order consequence:** Families hold three versions under one roof.
- **Second-order consequence:** Your elders can be your younger siblings: a 12-year-old V2 reviews his 19-year-old sister's work.
- **Third-order consequence:** Each cohort gets twenty, then ten, then five years at the top before it becomes the slow ones. Every young adult has to learn the thing their untreated parents learned.
- **The story:** Tamsin, 19, V1, sits on the consent board. Her mother is untreated and her brother is V2. The V3 vote decides whether her unborn niece outruns her within a decade. Coming of age means giving the gift her mother gave her, at a faster rate.
- **Why this lens:** Recursive self-improvement running through births. The treated minds are shown only through what each version does to the viewpoint.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress's *Beggars and Choosers* (the Supers outclass the Sleepless). Stross's *Accelerando* (each Macx generation more posthuman). The bare idea of a successor overtaking its makers is charted. What I haven't seen is an iterated, voted ladder inside one household.

## Card 3: Raft

- **Novum:** The treated children learn faster than any teacher can supply, so they learn from each other, in rafts of five sharing one continuous notebook, the slate. By adolescence the raft is the unit of competence, and it is hired, licensed and contracted whole.
- **Futurity:** F4. The working minds of the handover aren't single.
- **How it works:** The mechanism is social and runs through a tool, with no telepathy. The slate holds each raftmate's half-thoughts, and one member's half-step finishes another's. Five fast learners pooling their notes outrun any adult.
- **First-order consequence:** Single-mind institutions such as one-person contracts and solo licences buckle under rafts.
- **Second-order consequence:** One raft replaces a whole adult team. The parents still work alone, and their children find this endearing and slightly sad.
- **Third-order consequence:** Losing a raftmate becomes a new category of grief, with no name in the parents' language.
- **The story:** Ama, 17, is in a raft hired to take over regional rail dispatch from her father's team. One raftmate gets a solo place abroad. If he goes, the contract fails and the remaining four become a lesser mind. The raft contract lets them hold him. Ama has to decide whether to vote to keep him, while watching her father hand his desk to the raft and let his own child go.
- **Why this lens:** What's the smallest unit of mind? A pack mind built from the seed itself, the way the Tines in *A Fire Upon the Deep* are.
- **Suspected prior art:** Sturgeon's *More Than Human* (a gestalt through psi). Vinge's own Tines (biological). Leckie's ancillaries. This one is cultural and comes out of the seed. Closest risk: the gestalt plot of "will the group let one go".
