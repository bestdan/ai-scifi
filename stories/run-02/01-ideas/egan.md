# Idea cards: egan

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front:** I let a derivation outrank the
scene, I underplay emotion or deliver it flattened, and my cards drift towards
lectures. Each card below has a human choice at its end, but the First Reader
and Chiang should check whether that choice carries feeling or is just the
last line of a proof. I also lean on mechanism, so a card can be rigorous and
still not be warm enough for a coming-of-age story.

Method: in each card I hold the seed fixed, pick one parameter the seed
quietly leaves alone, and follow that parameter until it reaches a person.

## Card 1: Forty Winters

- **Novum:** The treatment speeds up thinking, but it can't make the world
  produce data any faster. Treated children take over every field limited by
  thinking within a decade. Fields limited by the world's own clock (frost,
  vintages, droughts, how a child grows) stay with untreated elders, who
  simply hold more samples.
- **Futurity:** F2. Society sorts its work into two classes, and that sorting
  decides the handover.
- **How it works:** A skill is learned from inferences or from samples. The
  treatment multiplies the first and does nothing for the second. A
  fourteen-year-old has seen fourteen winters, however fast she thinks.
- **First-order consequence:** Law, code and design change hands almost at
  once.
- **Second-order consequence:** Whatever is limited by samples (orchards,
  fisheries, midwifery) becomes the elders' last protected ground, which
  people call "slow trades".
- **Third-order consequence:** Treated teenagers learn the one humility their
  intelligence can't buy. A brilliant model fitted to too few winters is
  overfitted, and they know the statistics of that well enough to feel it.
- **The story:** Nell, fifteen and treated, is apprenticed at a Niagara
  icewine vineyard to Doug, untreated and sixty-one. On a borderline frost
  night her model says pick now, and his forty vintages say wait. He lets her
  make the call, which is how he hands the vineyard over.
- **Why this lens:** It separates computation from data and derives the
  border between them.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress, *Beggars in Spain* (an enhanced cohort
  displacing the old). That book is about resentment, not a split between
  thinking and sampling. I don't know of a close match.

## Card 2: The Median Reader Clause

- **Novum:** A law is valid only if a randomly drawn untreated citizen can
  read it and correctly predict its effects within one hour. The treated
  generation inherits a legislature whose bandwidth is pinned to the minds it
  is replacing.
- **Futurity:** F2. Lawmaking reorganizes around a verification bound.
- **How it works:** Speech runs at roughly forty bits a second whatever the
  speaker's IQ. The clause turns that into a constraint on proofs: every
  statute must come with an argument short enough for a slow verifier to
  check.
- **First-order consequence:** Treated drafters must compress what they mean
  into forms an untreated reader can verify.
- **Second-order consequence:** "Downwriting" becomes the most prestigious
  craft, and the constraint turns out to cut away clever, fragile law.
- **Third-order consequence:** The clause has a sunset tied to the untreated
  share of the electorate, which falls below half next year. The cohort has
  to decide whether it still wants to be checked by someone slower.
- **The story:** Ines, seventeen, is a junior downwriter. Her untreated
  grandmother is a paid verifier. Ines has come to believe that the clause
  made her think better and not merely slower. Her cohort is voting to let it
  lapse, and her own draft of the repeal fails her grandmother's hour.
- **Why this lens:** Reasoning treated as a moral stance, with a complexity
  bound treated as a physical constant.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress again, for the politics. Chiang's
  "Understand" for private compression. Neither builds the verification
  bound into law.

## Card 3: The Gap Dial

- **Novum:** The treatment is graded. The first treated cohort are now
  parents, and they must choose how much smarter than themselves their own
  children will be. A gap that once happened to them becomes a setting they
  pick.
- **Futurity:** F3. Parenthood becomes choosing how fast you become obsolete.
- **How it works:** The dose-response curve is known and roughly additive up
  to about +60 over the parents, with returns falling after that. A zero gap
  is possible, and so is repeating their parents' gap or exceeding it.
- **First-order consequence:** Every pregnancy carries a parameter, and
  couples argue about it with real data.
- **Second-order consequence:** Only the first treated generation knows what
  being raised by slower minds was like from the inside. Their memories of it
  become the evidence the decision rests on.
- **Third-order consequence:** Families split into "level" lineages, where
  parents can teach, and "stepped" lineages, where children outrun their
  parents by design. Kinship starts to be described in units of gap.
- **The story:** Teodor, twenty-nine and treated, was raised by an untreated
  mother he outran at seven. He is the one arguing for a gap. His reason is
  not ambition: loving someone he could see all the way through taught him
  something he wants his child to learn. His wife wants zero. The coming of
  age is his own, and it ends with the night he tells his mother which way
  they chose.
- **Why this lens:** It follows the premise one more step. The seed's gap
  becomes a free variable.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress, *Beggars and Choosers*; Huxley's castes.
  Neither has parents choosing the gap above themselves.
