## Comprehension items (structural)

1. **Premise (partial).** The reader had "take a pill ... or stay 'open'
   for a year and go off with his friends", but not that staying open means
   learning *her trade* at a child's speed. Make the open year's purpose
   unmistakable: at the board, eight bells with a child's hands. Put it in
   Davi's thought on the clinic steps (19 Sep) in plain words, and again in
   Ceiça's offer.
2. **Stakes (partial).** The reader missed the cost of setting: hands at an
   adult's pace, maybe ten years to match her. Make it land before the
   climax, in a picture as well as a number: Rafaela's cello, missing its
   note after four years, is what his cutter will be. Let Davi connect the
   two in one line on 26 Sep (the ten years) and hold the number "ten
   years" in his head on 28 Sep.
3. **Ending (partial).** The reader missed three things.
   - **He declined her offer.** Make the line say what he is declining:
     "I'm not taking your year."
   - **He can't tell whether he chose.** Make this explicit once, after the
     cure. Echo the protected fountain line: he tries to find the place
     where it was decided and can't, and none of the sentences is the one
     where it happened.
   - **She is formally his junior when she slows his hand.** Say it on
     8 Oct: she stands at the board as his apprentice.

## Consensus

4. **The narration must drift, then fix.** Raised by Chiang, Le Guin and
   the Cartographer, with Watts in part. The register of the *narration*,
   not only Davi's speech, takes on the nearest person:
   - the pack scenes (22 and 27 Sep) in the camp's clipped run-on;
   - the clinic with Rafaela's dry short sentences and a "brabo" in the
     narration;
   - from 28 Sep, the narration in Ceiça's long sentences that end on the
     thing in her hands, and from the cure on it stays there to the end.
   No marker flags it. The bell-code passages stay in one fixed register
   throughout.
5. **The 28 September notebook paragraph explains the choice.** Raised by
   Chiang, Le Guin and the Cartographer. Cut the tidy reasoning ("a hand
   learns what it's standing beside, and he'd always known that"). Keep its
   long run in her cadence (Watts protects it). The reasons it gives should
   be plainly hers, her phrases from 26 Sep ("the feast doesn't move", "you
   hang what you've got"), with none that sounds like his own argument. The
   reader should hear whose they are without being told.
6. **2 October denial.** Raised by Le Guin, Chiang, Watts and the
   Cartographer, and it confused the First Reader. Two registers at most:
   one camp word, then her "uai" and her cadence. Cut the "I've always
   talked like this ... with the beans" clause.
7. **22 September fountain paragraph.** Raised by Asimov, Chiang and Le
   Guin. Cut by a third. Keep "that's actually ceiling", the moment he
   hears himself say it, and Christmas.
8. **The last line.** Raised by Chiang and Le Guin. "And he counted" must
   be legible. Vô Zé's rule is "pitch before number". Make the final beat
   show which he does first now, in one concrete clause, and leave what it
   means unsaid.

## Adopted single-lens notes

9. **The misreading must obey the code** (Asimov, structural). Plant a
   second clause in scene 1 at the table: a high stroke that sits sour
   against the low bell is heard *as* a low stroke ("a sour high's a low,
   to an old ear"). A crack is only a buzz, and nobody reads timbre, which
   answers why the cracked bell was never read. The relay on 7 Oct then
   follows from a stated rule.
10. **The bell's timeline** (Asimov). The new bell is hung on Thursday with
    its clapper tied, by custom, until the feast, and say so once. It is
    first heard at the Congado.
11. **Why Ceiça lets him cut** (Asimov). Give it a reason on the page: by
    custom the heir makes the last cut on the bell for his own feast. She
    hands him the cutter because of the custom, not carelessly.
12. **Why the open year isn't free** (Watts, Asimov). One line each. Why
    his open hands haven't learned: practice hours, a bell or two a season
    at the board, the rest at school and camps. Why he can't just stay open
    at the board: the pull goes to whoever speaks last, and in a year the
    pack and the city will have spoken last. Ceiça's "Then that's a bell
    you won't make" already half does it.
13. **Ceiça as the last speaker** (Watts). Rafaela notices. When he says he
    will cure "At the board", she says one word, *pregar*, or asks whose
    board, and writes it down. Nobody answers it. Ceiça herself stays
    unreadable.
14. **The children who had no vote** (Le Guin). One adult line, once, near
    the Annex or at Vô Zé's table: nobody asked the babies.
15. **One open virtue worth envying** (Watts). Lia shows the open's gift
    once: at the table she has picked up the whole bell code, or Vô Zé's
    rasp and his rope rhythm, in a week. Keep it to two sentences.
16. **Opening jargon** (Asimov, the First Reader). Open on a person
    speaking ("Clean profile") and fold the strickle, core and false bell
    in after.
17. **The Annex** (Le Guin, the First Reader). Rafaela stops one clause
    earlier. She says the nets cure in "about six days", so the sixth is
    the earliest she can sign (Asimov).
18. **Trim the 7 October pack crossing** (Chiang wanted it cut). Keep two
    or three sentences: the word he has never heard, and laughing late.
    Cut the rest.

## Conflicts

  theme stated. Asimov protects it. **Decision: keep.** The cold reader's
  uncertainty about whether Davi chose is the effect the story wants, and
  this line plants the question without answering his.
- **The 7 October pack crossing.** Chiang says cut; the drafter added it to
  show the cost. **Decision: trim** (item 18).
