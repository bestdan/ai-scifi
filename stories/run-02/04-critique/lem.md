# Stage 4 critique: Lem lens

Blind spots: I favour essay over scene, distrust the reader-pleasing ending,
and can leave characters as ciphers. Weigh my notes against Le Guin on
Ceição and Rafaela, and let the First Reader rule on warmth.

1. **[must-fix] The open are a human in costume.** The bible says they
   "find almost everything obvious" and "see the structure of anything in
   minutes." That is a clever teenager. Sixty points should leave something
   the adults cannot follow, and the draft needs one such thing that stays
   opaque. Give the school pack a way of talking or deciding that Davi
   cannot translate for his mother, or for us, and that he himself only half
   follows when he is away from them. Never explain it. The pack's slang
   dying by Christmas is the right hint: let the reader see it is not only
   slang.

2. **[must-fix] The ending resolves too much, and the irony is left on the
   table.** Davi "sets in her voice" by standing beside his mother all
   week, then the narration goes quiet and she covers his hand. That is a
   reconciliation with a bow on it. The bible's own premise holds the real
   joke: an open mind cannot know that "I will stand next to her all week"
   is his choice rather than the pull of the last person he stood near.
   Whom was he last near? Make the decision visibly traceable to a
   conversation (Rafaela, or the camp friends) and do not tell us which.
   Then the settled narration is not proof of a self found. It is the
   narration that happened to be nearest.

3. **[should] The ledger's Why and Mechanism rows invite a lecture.** The
   one-time explanation is defensible, but Rafaela "recites the reason the
   law makes her give" is a scientist explaining to a boy who already
   knows. Make it a document instead: the state's consent sheet, drafted in
   2052 bureaucratese and 2052 slang, which Rafaela reads aloud flat and
   then abandons. The mechanism and the reason stay explicit, and the
   stale sheet is the comedy. What stays unexplained is what the nets do
   beyond the cited effects. Say plainly that the counsellors do not know.

4. **[should] Puncture the guild's vanity.** The cast treats the hand as
   sacred, and the story never asks whether it is. Davi spent ten days at a
   robotics camp and can model every partial to the cent. A cutter driven
   by his model would likely tune the bell truer than either of them. Do
   not answer that. Let Davi think it once and let nobody around him reply.
   Ceição's "you can only take away" then lands as a craft truth and as a
   small human absurdity.

5. **[could] Comedy of the rite.** The material is funny and unused: the
   town's sinos que falam announcing the Entrega, Seu Tonho the segurador
   whose good cuts nobody buys, the open kids mimicking Rafaela's 2049
   "brabo" behind her back. Keep the butcher sad and a little ridiculous at
   once. Do not let the square become a lesson about holdovers.

6. **[could] Let the flat bell be misread.** "Only he and she can hear"
   is a sentimental private signal. Better if the town hears the flat bell
   and decodes it, as it decodes everything, as a message: a death, a
   dispute. The town reads it wrongly and confidently. Mother and son both
   know it is only a bad cut.

**Ledger check:** every row can be explicit on the page without explaining
the alien away, provided note 1 is honoured. The rows cover the human
mechanism. They say nothing about what the open mind is like, and they
should keep saying nothing.
