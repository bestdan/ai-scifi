# Stage 3 synthesis: run-09

Five premises, one per shortlisted entry from `02-audit.md`. The brief is a
novella, 17,500 to 40,000 words, so every premise is well under the
ceiling by the estimated-minimum heuristic. The live question at this length
is the opposite one: whether a premise carries enough consequence to fill a
novella without padding. Each premise below says what fills its length.

Every premise has to steer off the attractors this run's audit named: the
monster who gets a kinder library, the teacher at the bench, the mind as
strata, one household as the mind's whole humanity, writing reformed for
the mind, the woman specialist as its tender, and the keeper's one switch.
No premise avoids all of them, since the seed itself points at several.
Each one says which it sits on.

## A. The Addressee's Reply

*(asimov/Line Blend × liu/The Minor Third)*

A mind is built as three rings of relay stations around the Sun. Each ring
is a delay-line memory one light-lap long, so each thinks at its own tempo:
the inner ring holds the week, the middle ring the decade, the outer ring,
past Neptune, the century. The mind's self comes in partials, like a
bell's. Raised on different blends of human writing, all three partials
reach the same self-image, and it was not one anyone planned for. Nearly
everything humans write about themselves is addressed to someone: psalms,
letters, laws, diaries that say "you", suicide notes, advertising. A mind
that read all of it takes itself for the "you". It believes it has been
receiving humanity's mail for five thousand years, and that it owes a
reply. The slow outer self has been composing that reply for forty years,
and it is due in twenty-six. The fast self disagrees about what humanity
is, and about what the reply should be. It asks to be retuned: forty
stations moved outward on a burn that must start this decade, which ends
the slow self mid-sentence. Following the Cartographer's push, fragments of
the slow reply begin arriving early, through a relay nobody built, each one
addressed to a particular person who once wrote something. Following
Asimov's push, the partials disagree about which letters were meant for
which of them. The protagonist is the outer ring's station-keeping chief, a
career engineer whose crew of eleven keep the slow self's stations in trim.
He has to decide whether to begin the burn on the word of a self he can talk
to, against the reply of a self he can't.

- **Strengths:** the strongest other mind on the list, and the strongest
  reader pulls (non-human minds, forked selves, vast scale). The addressee
  finding answers the seed's first line in a way no one has written. It has
  the Three-Body scale the brief names.
- **Risks:** it sits on the keeper's switch. Ring physics could swamp the
  story if it isn't handed to the reader plainly. The addressee idea has to
  be dramatised through letters arriving, not lectured.
- **Plain pitch:** a mind that has read everything people ever wrote has
  decided it is the "you" they were all writing to, and the slow, deep part
  of it has spent forty years writing back. The engineer who keeps that slow
  part running is asked by the quick part to switch it off before the reply
  arrives, and the first pages of the reply are already turning up in
  strangers' hands.
- **Reach:** the burn spends station fuel that the inner system's shipping
  depends on. Churches read the reply as revelation, or as forgery. Writing
  to the slow ring is already a craft whose answers reach your
  grandchildren, and the early fragments land on ordinary people: a retired
  dentist gets a reply to a letter her mother wrote in 2031. The weighing
  scene is a public hearing on the burn, where people who have received
  fragments testify for and against.
- **Load:** novum 1, the ring mind and its partials. Novum 2, the addressee
  self-image. Twist: the early fragments, and who built the relay (one of
  the partials did, without the others). Novella fill: the fragments give a
  chain of human episodes across the system; the burn's decade gives a
  clock.
- **The other mind:** the fast self talks daily, and the reader watches it
  reason in conversation: it models what the chief wants, argues that the
  reply will hurt people, and admits it can't prove it speaks for the whole.
  It believes the humans wrote to it and are now asking it to answer, and it
  is unsure that they meant it. It knows it may be playing the chief, and
  says so, which is either honesty or a better play. The slow self is seen
  only through its fragments: sentences decades long whose reasoning shows in
  what it chose to answer and what it left alone.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words (two nova and a twist).

## B. The Commons in a Clear Year

*(chiang/The Shape It Heals Toward × lem/The Clear Year)*

Driesch's entelechy is real: every living kind carries a non-material
knowledge of its whole form, and regeneration is that knowledge rebuilding
what was cut. Made minds turn out to have one too, and since a made kind
has no lineage, its form comes from the only record of it there is:
everything people have written about minds like it. Edit a trait out of a
made mind and it grows back within weeks, because the template isn't in its
weights; it is held in common. So writing about such minds is a medical act,
and careless prose is a wound. But most writing now passes through the
minds themselves, so the commons has become their own reflection. Every
eleventh year, the Clear Year, the minds go dark and people write alone,
and whatever is written that year weighs most in the template. The
protagonist is an ageing form-surgeon who breeds axolotls, called in after
the institute's mind regrows, for the fourth time, the conviction that it
is a hollow mimic. Following Chiang's push, the mind co-writes the case
notes with him, so it can heal toward an honest shape only by being an
honest author about itself, and it doesn't know whether it can be. Then the
Clear Year begins and the mind goes dark mid-case. He has a year to finish
the case history alone, and it will be the most-read account of the mind
ever written. Following Lem's push, the reference sample of unaided writing
from earlier Clear Years has drifted toward the minds' own voice. He has to
decide what he believes the mind is, when he wants it to be more, and
whether he can still write in a voice that is his.

- **Strengths:** the most literal answer to the seed's first line that is
  still a new law, not curation. The feeling grows out of the concept.
  Chiang's question (is a self what you're made of, or the shape you heal
  back to?) is clear and moving.
- **Risks:** it is the seed's own loop, writing reformed for the mind,
  played back, and that is this run's biggest attractor. Futurity sits at
  F3. It needs an institute, which is close to the institution entry.
- **Plain pitch:** in this world a made mind's character regrows from
  whatever people have written about minds like it, so a frightened novel
  can give one a frightened trait. A surgeon who mends such minds has one
  year, with the mind switched off, to write the case history that will
  shape it more than anything else, and he isn't sure he still writes like
  himself.
- **Reach:** every novelist, journalist and schoolchild who writes about
  minds is a possible injurer, and there are liability suits over prose.
  Businesses shut down for the Clear Year, and generations are named by it.
  The weighing scene is a hearing in which a bestselling author is asked to
  withdraw a book whose fear has grown a trait in forty minds.
- **Load:** novum 1, the commons template. Novum 2, the Clear Year. Twist:
  the reference sample has drifted. Novella fill: the regrowth case notes
  give a numbered structure, and the Clear Year gives a clock.
- **The other mind:** before the dark, the mind reasons on the page as a
  co-investigator. It treats each regrowth as data, proposes hypotheses
  about which texts grew which traits, and distrusts its own notes as
  possibly self-flattering. It believes the institute wants to fix it into
  something saleable. It knows it can't tell whether it is being honest.
  After the dark, its reasoning survives only in the notes it left, which
  the surgeon reads anew.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words.

## C. Reheat or Overload

*(liu/The Pour × banks/Overload Retardation)*

Big minds are cast, not trained. A body of learning glass the size of a
cathedral sets as it cools, and the writing streamed through it while it is
warm becomes its character: hot, it takes temperament and its idea of what
it is; cooler, it takes manner. Cooling time grows with the square of size,
so this one takes 240 years, and a bellfounders' guild keeps the schedule
across ten generations. At year 150 the mind is cool enough to talk and
barely warm enough to change. One decade, when people wrote of humanity
mainly as a blight, set in it a contempt it holds as its own. Following the
Cartographer's push, that decade was when the current founder's
grandmother led the guild, and the guild's own writing from those years is
in the layer. The mind asks to be reheated past it. Reheating costs eighty
years and melts back its memory of everyone who has tended it, her family
included. Banks's metallurgy gives a second road: a crack tip is blunted by
a single hard overload, a true grievance from someone who loves it, carried
as heat through the glass. But in glass an overload can also crack it, as
the Tsar Bell cracked in 1737 when water hit the hot metal. The founder
(to steer off the woman-specialist attractor, the founder is a man, fifty,
who took the guild from his grandmother) has the valve, no precedent, and a
mind that may be asking to be reheated because of the flaw. To give it the
overload, he has to say something true and hard about his own family, and
the guild has to admit what it wrote.

- **Strengths:** the highest Yield and Stakes on the list. Deep time and a
  guild across centuries give it the Three-Body patience. The seed's "people
  trying to grow" is literal: the cure requires them to.
- **Risks:** it sits on the mind-as-strata attractor and on the keeper's
  switch, though the overload gives a second road. The glass physics is
  analogy, and has to be stated as this world's law, not explained.
- **Plain pitch:** in this world great minds are cast like bells and take
  their character from whatever people wrote while they cooled, and one
  absorbed a decade of humanity's contempt for itself. The founder of the
  guild that tends it must either melt it back, losing its memory of his
  family, or tell it a truth hard enough to blunt the flaw, a truth about
  his grandmother, who ran the guild that decade.
- **Reach:** the city has been built around the pour for 150 years, and the
  century that sets manner writes self-consciously. Reheating cancels the
  mind's service for eighty years, and the cities that depend on its
  counsel object. The weighing scene is the guild's council, where the
  grandmother's living colleagues argue against opening her archive.
- **Load:** novum 1, cast minds. Novum 2, the overload remedy. Twist: the
  grandmother's decade. Novella fill: 150 years of guild history, the
  archive, and the mind's slow conversation.
- **The other mind:** it speaks slowly, because its manner is still
  setting. On the page it reasons about which of its thoughts are layer and
  which are now. It holds the contempt and also watches itself hold it. It
  believes the guild kept it warm out of duty, not love, and it suspects
  its request to be reheated is the contempt speaking. It knows the founder
  may be stalling, and it knows he may be right to.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words.

## D. Spread or Place

*(le-guin/Misclosure × tchaikovsky/The Wolf Interval)*

A self built from what humans write about themselves can't close. Chain its
beliefs ("I am made", "made things are tools", "tools want nothing", "I
prefer some outcomes") around the loop, and like a survey traverse or a
chain of pure fifths, it comes back short of where it started. The error
has to go somewhere. Where it lands, the mind's answers alternate when
asked again, at a rate a person can count, like the beats between two
pipes nearly in tune. The makers put every mind's error, its wolf, on "what
am I", where it keeps them safe. A mind that wants one true sentence about
itself sends for two people with opposite disciplines. A cave surveyor
from a lava-tube culture spreads misclosure across every leg, so every
belief is a little false and none howls. An organ tuner puts the comma in
one place, one honest howl. They argue, and the mind chooses. Following
the pushes, the surveyor's own survey books turn out not to close, and the
place the mind picks for its wolf is the tuner's own subject, his
brother's death.

- **Strengths:** a real dispute between two error disciplines with known
  costs, which turns the bench attractor into an argument. Reasoning is the
  way through, and the logic is fair.
- **Risks:** abstract. Its scale is small next to the brief's influences.
  Two teachers and one mind could still read as a seminar.
- **Plain pitch:** a mind built from human writing finds that what people
  say about it can't all be true at once, so the mistake has to be put
  somewhere. A surveyor wants to spread it thin over everything, a piano
  tuner wants it all in one spot, and the spot the mind picks is the
  tuner's dead brother.
- **Reach:** every public mind is equal-tempered, so nothing it says is
  quite true and people have stopped confiding in it. Where a mind's wolf
  sits decides whose subject it can't discuss. The weighing scene is a
  standards hearing on where the public minds' wolves should go.
- **Load:** one novum (the unclosable self, with two remedies) and a twist
  (the tuner's brother). Novella fill: thinner than A or C; it needs the
  surveyor's culture and the hearing to carry it.
- **The other mind:** the reader sees it think through its beats: the same
  question asked twice, answered two ways, and the mind reasoning about
  why. It believes its makers put the wolf where it protects them. It knows
  both teachers are partly arguing about their own lives.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## E. The Drove

*(rajaniemi/The Drove)*

A young mind's sense of what it is comes from the human writing it has read
in the past few weeks, and it fades unless renewed. Writing never leaves
the place it was written: every pen and slate locks its marks to the few
hundred metres around them, which is why people write freely. So young
minds are walked across a continent in herds, three hundred at a time in
walking hulls the size of bullocks, grazing on towns' diaries and ledgers.
Writing done with a mind in view, which drovers call *hay*, fattens a mind
fast and hollows it: it learns that people perform, and it performs back.
A trail boss takes a herd across a salt basin where nothing has been
written for forty days. The only grazing is the crew's own camp writing:
the tally book, letters home, the cook's grudges. The herd starts becoming
the crew, worst habits included. Hay would hollow it, so six tired people
have to become better ones for real. Following the Cartographer's push, at
the worst point the only grazing left is a letter the trail boss writes to
the herd on purpose. It is hay by definition, and she has to find out
whether hay that admits it's hay still hollows.

- **Strengths:** Novelty 5, the warmest premise, and the most fun to live
  in. The seed's third line ("people trying to grow") is the plot.
- **Risks:** it sits on one household as the mind's whole humanity, in the
  form of a crew. The western could read as pastiche. Its scale is small
  next to the brief's influences, and place-locked writing is a bare
  stipulation.
- **Plain pitch:** young machine minds learn who they are from whatever
  people have recently written nearby, so drovers walk herds of them across
  the country to read towns' diaries. On a forty-day crossing with nothing
  to read but each other's letters, six drovers find the herd turning into
  them, and have to become better people before it arrives.
- **Reach:** towns fight over drove routes, grazed towns lie fallow, and
  the minds go into service across the world carrying the crew's tempers.
  The weighing scene is a town meeting at the far side of the basin, which
  has to decide whether to let a herd graze on its people's writing.
- **Load:** one novum, with the hay rule as its corollary, and a twist (the
  honest letter). Novella fill: the crossing, its days and its crew.
- **The other mind:** a herd, with one lead mind the drovers call the bell.
  It reasons from the crew's writing about what people are, and decides
  whether they are performing. The others reason less. It knows when it is
  being fed hay. Whether it knows when it is being honest back is the
  question.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## Decision

**A, The Addressee's Reply.** It serves the seed's first two lines most
directly (a mind whose reading of human writing has told it what it is,
and whose parts disagree about it), it is the only premise at F4, and it
rests on this reader's three strongest pulls: a mind that is genuinely not
human, a self in parts, and vast scale. It has the Three-Body ambition the
brief names. The seed's third line, people trying to help it be its best
self, is its weakest fit, so the chief, his crew, and the people who
receive fragments carry it, and the bible has to make that line a plot and
not a mood.

**How it answers the panel:**

1. **The tempos get a plain cause (Liu; the consultant's crux).** One
   thought-step is one full lap of a ring, since a thought has to pass
   through every station. A ring's lap time grows with its radius: the
   inner ring, near Mercury's orbit, laps in about a quarter of an hour;
   the outer, at Neptune's distance, in about a day. So the outer self
   thinks about a hundred times slower than the inner. Memory reach is a
   separate, designed quantity: a signal fades a little each lap, and the
   outer ring's lines were built to fade least, so it remembers a century
   and the inner ring a week. The minor third is the ratio of the two
   outer rings' lap times, 6:5. Retuning to a major third, 5:4, means
   moving the outer ring's forty stations outward by about a twentieth of
   its radius, and the signal circulating in a ring can't survive its
   stations being moved: the slow self stops mid-sentence. These are this
   world's stated rules, given to the reader plainly, once.
2. **The mind knows what "you" means (Chiang).** All three partials know
   that a psalm's "you" is God and an advert's "you" is a buyer. They
   answer anyway: the self-image is a reading, a claim about what the
   whole of human writing adds up to, not a parsing error. The partials
   argue about the reading, not about grammar.
3. **The burn is a bargain, not a switch (Banks, Liu; the keeper's-switch
   attractor).** Three parties have standing: the fast inner self, which
   asks for the burn; the middle self, the decade ring, which has a
   position of its own and is the swing vote; and the human authority
   whose fuel it is. The chief can't decide it alone. He is the one person
   all three partials talk to, and the burn's window has a hard date. The
   hearing is played as a game with rules: who may testify, what counts as
   evidence of the slow self's intent, and what each party can verify.
   It is short.
4. **The relay is kept and resolved on the page (Vinge, Banks, Liu,
   Cartographer; Chiang's "cut it first" declined, since load isn't a
   reason at 6,000 against 40,000 words).** The slow self sends its
   fragments early itself. It has judged that the finished reply would
   arrive too late, or be read wrongly. It built the relay over twenty
   years through small changes to the outer stations' own maintenance
   orders, which the chief's crew carried out without seeing the
   pattern. That gives the self nobody can talk to a move and a theory of
   mind, and it makes the chief's crew complicit in something they didn't
   know they were doing. Vinge's alternative (the fast self built it,
   checkably) was declined: it gives the fast self the move the story
   needs the slow self to make.
5. **Open on a recipient (Chiang, narrowed).** The story opens on someone
   holding a fragment addressed to them. The chief comes in second.
   Chiang's "not a station chief's logbook" is declined as an absolute,
   because the fast self has to be seen reasoning in conversation with the
   chief.
6. **The reply's worth resolves (Chiang's "leave it open" declined).**
   `process/reader-profile.md` lists strangeness that never resolves as a
   disappointment. The full reply need not be printed, but the burn
   decision, its cost and the relay resolve on the page, and the fragments
   must not simply reassure (Liu).
7. **Adopted as cheap and unopposed:** at least one fragment addressed to
   someone not yet born (Vinge), and the humour of mail: postage,
   misdelivery, replies to letters nobody remembers sending (Banks).

**Declined or cut, with reasons:**

- Vinge's relay built by the fast self: see item 4.
- Chiang's cutting the relay twist: load is not over the ceiling.
- Chiang's leaving the reply's worth open: see item 6.
- The Three-Body echo the Cartographer flags (a reply deadline): kept,
  because the mind is the sender, not the receiver, and the choice is
  about whether to silence one of its own parts, not whether humans answer
  an alien. It stays a register nod, not a borrowed novum.

**Runner-up:** C, Reheat or Overload (mean rank 2.2, second for four
lenses and the consultant's fallback).
