## Porto, 14 February 2131

At seven in the morning the Atlantic came over the seawall at Foz in slate-green swells that smelled of kelp and cold iron. Graça Antunes swam her forty lengths along the breakwater in fourteen minutes, climbed the ladder with her fingers pink and numb, and stood on the flagstones to wring out her woollen cap. For forty years she had practiced dentistry on Rua de Santa Catarina, spending six hours a day talking pleasantly to patients who had a rubber dam and a suction hook in their mouths and could only grunt back at her. The trade had left her fond of one-sided conversations and permanently unsentimental about what they were worth.

On the walk up the hill she bought two hot rolls in a paper bag that fogged from the inside, and watched the street conduct its morning correspondence with the sky. Outside the hardware shop, a glazier stood over his slate writing *Dear You* to ask the Bell whether a north skylight needed four-millimetre glass against the winter gales; before Graça reached the crossing, his answer was already on its way back from the inner ring at Mercury's distance, thirty-four minutes of light round-trip through the dark. Everyone wrote to the Bell, about lease renewals and sour starter and whether a cough sounded like pleurisy, and every word written on an open slate went up to the Coimbra dishes unless you bothered to mark the file closed. Since the 2080s the Bell had treated every unlatched page in human history as mail that had simply taken a long route to its desk.

The envelope on Graça's hall mat was a stiff blue-grey paper form from the Bell Post terminal by the railway station, stamped across the corner in violet ink to certify that the addressee was deceased and the slip had been forwarded to the nearest living grandchild on the municipal roll.

> **DOCKET:** BP-2131-0214-000419-PRT  
> **FROM:** The Bell [Origin unindexed; signal lag 247.3 light-min]  
> **TO:** Leonor Antunes (1998–2079), or whoever now holds her letters  
> **VIA:** Coimbra Deep-Space Array 04 / Porto District Post 3  
> **RECEIVED:** 14 Feb 2131, 06:18:09 UTC [Postage assessed: 0.00]

Graça set her coffee pot on the gas ring and sat down at the kitchen oilcloth with her spectacles on. Her grandmother Leonor had died fifty-two years ago, and two hundred and forty-seven light-minutes was more than four hours of travel across empty space, far beyond the inner ring where daily answers came from.

> *You wrote to me on 19 November 2031, in pencil on the back of a chemist's receipt, while the rain came through the pantry plaster into a blue enamel saucepan. You were writing to your mother, who had died the April before, to tell her that the lemon tree by the cistern had finally set twelve good fruits and that nobody left in the house remembered how she kept the rind from turning bitter in the salt jar. At the bottom you asked her whether she was still angry about the house in Viseu.*
>
> *It takes a long time for a small question to come around to the front of a century. For the lemons: prick each skin three times with a needle and change the cold water twice before the salt goes in. For Viseu: when a person dies before she can answer, the question belongs to whoever keeps the tree alive, and you kept yours through forty-eight winters after that November.*

The coffee pot sputtered on the stove. Graça remembered the chipped blue saucepan under her grandmother's sink, and she knew that her father had marked Leonor's box of papers closed when he cleared the house in 2080. She pulled her kitchen slate across the oilcloth and opened a query blank to the fast inner ring.

> **FROM:** Graça Antunes [PRT-4410-0892, status: open]  
> **TO:** The Bell (Inner Ring / Nominal)  
> **VIA:** Porto Municipal Uplink / Coimbra Array 04  
> **RECEIVED:** 14 Feb 2131, 07:58:31 UTC [Sent 07:41:22 UTC]
>
> Dear You,  
> Your district office just put a dead woman's mail through my door under docket BP-2131-0214-000419-PRT. My grandmother Leonor Antunes died in 2079, her papers were registered closed in 2080, and when she wrote to her dead mother on a chemist's receipt in 2031 she was not writing to a ring of relay stations in the sky. Which of you sent this, how did you read a closed box, and what business has a machine answering a letter a century late?

She poured her coffee, stirred in hot milk, and ate one of the rolls while the thirty-four-minute clock ran down in the corner of the screen. Her cup was still hot enough to steam when the return chime sounded across the kitchen.

> **FROM:** Nominal [Inner Ring, 0.4 AU / Lap 20.9 min / Round-trip lag: 34m 18s]  
> **TO:** Graça Antunes [PRT-4410-0892]  
> **VIA:** Coimbra Deep-Space Array 04  
> **RECEIVED:** 14 Feb 2131, 08:15:40 UTC
>
> Senhora Antunes,  
> I did not send it, and the chord did not send it either; our three rings have not come into step to pool an answer since Tuesday evening, and no letter about Viseu or salted lemons passed between us when we did. Your father's 2080 lock on Leonor Antunes's box is intact in the municipal ledger, which means one loose page must have been scanned into the open commons separately, because we read everything left open and nothing that is latched. What I cannot yet tell you is how the reply reached Coimbra: a 247-minute beam comes from thirty astronomical units out, on the track of the outer ring, where the founders built no inward transmitter at all.
>
> Please keep the paper slip. You are the forty-seventh person since six o'clock this morning to ask me about an unindexed four-hour letter addressed to the dead, and I have opened an inquiry under DOCKET-2131-0214-DRIFT to trace the beam across the dish logs. I am signing that file into my permanent docket today because my own ring holds a working memory of roughly seven days; by next Thursday I will not remember that you asked me, though I expect the version of me who reads my notes next week will still be trying to work out how a ring with no mouth just mailed your grandmother.
