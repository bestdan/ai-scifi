# Egan critique: run-09 bible

Blind spots: I lean on derivation and may under-weigh whether a scene can carry it. Let the First Reader and Le Guin judge accessibility and the children. I checked the arithmetic first. The lap times (20.9 min, 21.8 h, 26.1 h), the 6:5 ratio, the 75x and 5,000x figures, the 336 laps, the 4.2 h and 8.3 h lags, and every age and date all hold. The breaks are in the logic around them.

1. [must-fix] **The Hum has a mouth already, and it is the work-order queue.** The rule is "no transmitter pointed inward". But the sample paragraph has an order leaving station 23 for Lassell with "REASON: LINK MARGIN", and a station that files fault reports to the crew is a transmitter pointed inward. A reader will ask why the founders forbade a voice and left the diagnostics channel open. The bible never says how the Hum's orders come to exist. Fix: say that the Hum controls what its stations report, and that the founders banned a mouth but never thought of a symptom. Arvo's first scene states the rule. The triangulation scene shows where the loophole was. Hamid says it flatly: "We asked the stations how they felt. We never asked who was answering."

2. [must-fix] **"Nothing else can hold it" fails, because the relay is already a tap.** If the relay can read the Hum's state well enough to write fragments, then a snapshot is thinkable. The capacity argument (bandwidth times 26 hours) is weak too, since a ground store holds that many bits easily. This leaves the story short of its next step: is a restored Hum the Hum? And the fragments already are partial copies, so "ends mid-sentence" costs less than the bible says. Fix: give the real reason. The state is sub-millimetre timing across 188 AU, and a readout is a lossy summary. Hamid says so in the trim room. Then take the step: Nominal argues a dump would do, and the Tierce says the dump would be one of the Hum's letters, not the Hum.

3. [must-fix] **The retune is orbital mechanics the bible hasn't done.** Stations at 30 AU co-orbit with a period near 164 years. A move from 30 to 31.25 AU needs two burns of about 0.11 km/s each, so about 0.22 km/s, and a transfer taking decades. So the Hum ends at the first burn, and the move finishes long after the story. Also, why can't a tunable delay line absorb a few millimetres of drift? Fix: state that trim is continuous correction, that trombone lines cover only millimetres, and that the first burn is the irreversible act. Put "the first burn is the end" in the Board's record, where Arvo's certificate lives.

4. [must-fix] **"Nobody can lobby it" is false in this world, and one letter can't move a century-long average.** The Bell reads everything not marked closed. So anyone's open letter already reaches the Hum, and the founders' protection protects nothing. The bible also says Graça's letter and Arvo's orders move it, though it holds a century. Fix: the Hum is moved by correction, not volume. It can weigh a stream it answered and was answered in, and Arvo's orders are the only such stream: a 22-year dialogue. Graça's letter works because it is a counterexample to the Hum's one claim, not because it is one more letter. Hamid or the Tierce says this before the hearing.

5. [should] **The fragments begin in February 2131, before Nominal files in March.** The Hum acted before the request, and the bible doesn't say how it knew. If Nominal's dossier is open writing, the Hum read Nominal's drafts at a chord, and the page should say so. That also answers "how did it foresee the request twenty years ahead": it didn't, it read the draft. The twenty years of preparation are then what the ring was always doing, and the early fragments are what it chose to do once it could see the request coming.

6. [should] **Nominal's dossier is its attack surface, and the story doesn't use it.** Each week Nominal learns its position from a file that the Tierce can read and, through the inner relays, write. Edit the file and you edit Nominal's conviction. A fast, weekly-amnesiac self would know this, and would either seal the file or write its own letters as if tampering were a standing risk. Suggest one beat in which Nominal tells Arvo how it guards the dossier, and what it can't check, so that "I can't tell if my honesty is a better play" becomes a derived fact, not a mood.

Total: four must-fix, two should. None needs more than a scene's line, and each makes the Hum's silence a real constraint that the story then breaks.
