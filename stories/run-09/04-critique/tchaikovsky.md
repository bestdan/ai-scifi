# Stage 4 critique: Tchaikovsky lens (the Naturalist)

Blind spots: I trust that a mind can be reached, so I may be too easy on a
Hum that ends up persuaded. I don't judge the physics (Egan), whether the
Hum has anyone home (Watts), or whether it should resolve at all (Lem).

1. **[must-fix] The Hum's body has no senses and no output organ on the page.**
   The bible says what it holds (timing, 100 years) but not what it feels. It
   "has no mouth", yet it writes fragments, picks addressees and routes them to
   Porto. The relay's mechanism is never stated: what the nine stations'
   "relay hardware" does, how a lap of timing becomes a letter, and why
   maintenance orders are the channel and not something simpler. A reader will
   ask "why not just ask the Tierce to pass it on?", and the answer (the
   chord is five laps of pooling, and a fragment needs the Hum alone, or the
   Tierce would censor it) is not in the bible. Give the relay a plain
   mechanism and the reason for it, stated in the triangulation scene. Give
   the Hum a first sense: it feels the ring's length the way we feel
   posture, so a swapped aperture is a limb moved. Then the interludes can
   be written from the body.

2. **[must-fix] The Hum's foresight is circular, and that is its best mistake.**
   The relay was built from 2109 because the Hum "foresaw" Nominal's request.
   But the fragments began in February 2131 and Nominal filed in March, in
   response. The Hum's plan caused the event it feared. Nominal forgets
   weekly and can't be forecast from a century's average, so the Hum read
   it as a trend. Make that the Hum's error, and let the Tierce or Nominal
   say so at the hearing. Both sides are right about something, and the
   reader holds both: the Hum was right that a request would come, and wrong
   that it was fixed.

3. **[must-fix] The Tierce is a judge in a costume, and Nominal is a very good
   chatbot.** The Tierce is "courteous and exact" and weighs evidence; those are
   a jury's traits, not a ten-year, 22-hour mind's. Nominal's "I can't tell
   whether my honesty is honesty" is the stock self-doubting-AI beat. Derive
   each from its tempo. The Tierce remembers Arvo's decade, so its
   judgements should be personal and grudging, tied to Arvo's last ten
   years, and it should be wrong in ways a ten-year memory is wrong
   (everything before 2121 looks like legend). Nominal, new each week, should
   be unfair to its own past self: it argues against Monday's letter as
   against a stranger.

4. **[should] The three selves share one corpus. Why do they differ in values?**
   Tempo explains style and what each can't think. It doesn't explain why
   Nominal wants the Hum silenced. Add the in-world cause: what a week of
   memory makes a self fear (a slow thing nobody can check) and what a century
   makes it fear (a quick thing that never learns). Put it in Nominal's
   letter, in its own terms.

5. **[should] The Hum's change of mind is a persuasion arc, and the ending is close to
   mutual understanding.** Human letters move it, then it concedes "a reader
   may say what a reader can say". What does it lose? Name a cost it pays that
   it can't be repaid, such as the Reply it composed for 40 years, never read,
   with the unsent pages. Give it one thing it keeps wrongly: it still
   counts Arvo's orders as the only letters in its feed that were meant for it.
   Leave one misreading uncorrected on the page.

6. **[could] The two-levels scene exists only in outline.** Arvo's work order
   reads as an order; the Hum's reading is a letter. Stage one scene, an order
   in two columns, Arvo's version and the Hum's, before the triangulation, so
   the reader holds both. A second: the Desmond fragment seen at Nominal's pace
   (within the hour), the Tierce's (two days) and the Hum's (months later,
   still replying to the hearing's first question). The mail humour is
   fond; keep it.
