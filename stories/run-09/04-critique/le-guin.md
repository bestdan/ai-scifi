# Le Guin lens: bible critique (run-09)

Blind spots: I read for pacing too slowly, I don't audit the physics (Egan
and Watts do), and I can lean toward instruction. Discount any note below
that asks the story to stop and teach.

1. **[must-fix] "Nominal" is a word the crew says every shift.** The bell
   term is right for the culture, but this story is full of technicians, and
   "nominal" is what a technician says when a station is fine. A reader meets
   "Nominal was nominal" and stops. The glossary's Job test passes on the
   culture and fails on the page. Suggestion: add a bible rule that no
   character uses the adjective (the crew say "green", "in trim", "clean"),
   and have Graça's first scene show a person use the name as a name, with
   the capital, in a sentence where it can't be the adjective.

2. **[must-fix] The 2031 letter can't have been "marked closed".** The
   glossary's clear-at-first-use line says Graça finds her grandmother's other
   letters were closed and this one wasn't. But the Bell's corpus dates from
   2077, and closing is a convention of a world that has the Bell. Someone
   else did it, or didn't. Suggestion: make that someone a living person. Her
   father, clearing the house in the 2080s, closed the box of letters and
   missed one. Graça's search then runs through a family (who kept what, who
   decided for the dead), and the consent question gets a kitchen table.

3. **[must-fix] The history lives in an argument and a plaque.** Nominal
   telling Arvo the Quick Years is advocacy; a class at a plaque is a
   field-trip. Nobody in Lassell remembers it. Someone should: a founder's
   child born around 2046 was six at the Vault Vote and is about 85 now.
   Suggestion: make her Nikolasi's mother or a neighbour of Arvo's, an old
   woman who is still on the school rota, and let her be the one who answers
   Mele about the seed vault, badly and with a grievance. That is also your
   old person, who the cast currently lacks.

4. **[should] Mele and the lag.** World item 1 says Nominal answers within
   the hour, but Triton is 4.2 hours from the inner system, so a Lassell
   child waits about eight and a half. That is a gift. Lassell's children
   write to the Bell the evening before homework is due, and the answer
   arrives mid-lesson, too good, which the teacher suspects as cheating.
   Mele acts nine when she hides the reply rather than asks about it. Don't
   have her say "will it mark it"; have her cover the screen.

5. **[should] Nikolasi and Graça are close to functions.** Nikolasi finds the
   pattern and delivers the list; Graça opens, vanishes, returns to testify.
   Give Nikolasi something he wants that is not the plot (a daughter who is
   suddenly afraid of the Bell is a start), and give Graça a middle: the
   search for the letter. Also use the taboo you built. Liv's letters to the
   Bell are open, so Nominal has read them. One scene where it chooses to
   say it will not read them aloud, and Arvo sees that it could, gives the
   Liv thread a pressure and makes Nominal's restraint a character act, not
   a play.

6. **[should] Who pays is Desmond, and he is a plot instrument.** His son
   reads the answer first, and the story needs the son as a person (name,
   job, why he is angry at his father and not at the Hum). Other payers are
   named only in the ledger: the old who planned their dying around 2157,
   Lassell's crew, the dead who never consented. Let one old person in the
   Porto Reply Office queue lose the date in a scene, so the cost of the
   ruling lands on someone we've met at a counter.

Names and glossary: the names are plausible and respectful, with Mele,
Nikolasi and Graça working best. Tierce, the Hum, lap and the chord have
Jobs. "Closed" and "trim" are plain words and need no glossary entry.
