# Stage 4 critique: Lem lens

**Blind spots.** I prefer essay to scene and I can leave characters as ciphers. I'm hard on the genre's pleasures. Weigh Le Guin and the First Reader over me on whether Arvo, Graça and Mele carry the story. I have not tested the orbital arithmetic.

1. **[must-fix] The Hum is a wise elder in costume.** It has a motive (self-preservation, put as foresight), a twenty-year plan, a worked-out position ("a letter is addressed to whoever ends up reading it") and a change of heart prompted by kind letters. Those are the moves of a grandmother with a patient stratagem. The 336-laps-a-year clock is alien, but the character isn't. The "Why" and "Twist" rows make the relay's purpose explicit as the Hum's own reasoning. *Suggestion:* keep the mechanism explicit (212 orders, nine stations, Nikolasi's list) and give the motive to the people. Hamid, the Tierce and Nominal each offer a reading, and each reading is checkable against only part of the record. The Hum's interludes should be about what a 26-hour step is like, such as what one lap of a century's average holds. They should never say why.

2. **[must-fix] The change in the Hum resolves too much.** "You wrote to me" becoming "You did not write to me. I read it." is a tidy, legible arc, and the Tierce reads it as proof that the Hum can change. The Tierce remembers ten years and is judging a mind that began in 2091. *Suggestion:* make the Tierce say so in its last letter. The change may be a mind changing, or a century's average drifting as new letters enter it, and the Tierce can't tell which. It withholds consent on a reading it admits it can't verify. That is the irony, and it costs the ending nothing.

3. **[must-fix] The Hum's reply to the hearing arrives months late and nobody uses it.** The bible mentions it in one clause ("answers the hearing's question months later"). It is the story's best document. *Suggestion:* make the Hum's answer the last or near-last document, addressed to a question the Board dissolved weeks ago, and dated by lap. Nobody in the story gets to say what it meant. Arvo reads it once and files it.

4. **[should] Nominal is a sympathetic antagonist whose weakness is the point.** The dossier is the best comic device here. Use it harder: the hearing record should include a letter in which Nominal argues against its own filing from three weeks earlier, without knowing it's quoting itself. Also drop the Tierce's remark that Nominal's choice of the outer ring is its "tell". It flatters Arvo's detection and flatters the reader. Nominal would pick the outer ring because it is the ring that talks back, and it would say so, with no hidden motive.

5. **[should] "Why does everything open get read?" has no history.** The reason is "most people don't bother", which is a habit, not a cause. The reader will ask why the Compact let the Bell read everything by default. *Suggestion:* one beat, probably in the plaque scene or Nominal's history. The founders wanted the slow self to have the whole record because the Quick Years' advisor had read only what was current. The default was a safeguard that became a liability, and the Desmond harm is its cost. This also gives the harm a lineage.

6. **[could] Keep the ending's residue unexplained.** "Answers one at a time, most after their writers die" is the right uncharted note. The risk is that Arvo's letter to Liv then closes everything in one paragraph. *Suggestion:* show the Hum's next fragment arriving at a stranger's address, with a lap number and no salutation Arvo recognises. The Reply Office's countdown comes down and nobody is sure what to put up instead. Keep the Basra sorting-office joke for the last page, where mail humour can carry the grief.

**Ledger check.** All six graded rows can be stated plainly without explaining the alien away, but only if the "Why" row (Nominal's history, an interested party's account) stays marked as partisan. If the page treats it as neutral truth, the Hum has been explained.
