# Stage 4 critique: Watts lens

Blind spots: I underrate ritual, meaning and joy, so the Reply Offices and the
letters kept for 2157 get less credit from me than they deserve. Le Guin should
push back on those. I also read the orbital arithmetic only lightly (it checks
out, apart from the Mercury slip below) and leave the logic of the novum to
Egan.

1. **[must-fix] The Hum's recall is unexplained.** The bible says it "sees only
   the long average of what humans have written." Yet it answers Desmond's one
   forgotten 2099 letter and Leonor's one 2031 letter. A decaying store keeps
   what is intense or repeated and loses the rest. Emotional arousal tags
   memory for slower decay (McGaugh 2004), and rehearsal resists forgetting.
   Use this. The Hum answers grief, rage and psalms: letters that were
   emotionally loaded or copied a million times. Hamid or the Tierce should
   notice that the addressees skew that way. That is a clue the reader can
   follow. It also explains the harm without malice: the Hum answers the
   letters it holds most strongly, and those are the ones people most wanted
   to forget.

2. **[must-fix] The twenty-year plan is too coherent.** The Hum foresees
   Nominal's request two decades out and builds a relay through 212 orders. A
   mind that works one step a day, with no voice, is given perfect foresight
   and a conspiracy. The more likely mechanism is reciprocity: it answers an
   order with an order, again and again. The foresight story is what it tells
   itself afterwards (Nisbett and Wilson 1977, telling more than we can know).
   Let the dated record support both readings, plan and drift. Make the
   interludes show the Hum's stated reason shifting. The Tierce, with only a
   decade of memory, can't tell which is true. That also meets the rule on
   earned ambiguity.

3. **[must-fix] The change of mind is too cheap.** The year holds 336 laps
   against a memory of about 33,600, so one year is about 1% of what the Hum
   holds. Letters moving a century-weighted average that far, that fast, is
   the story flattering the reader. Make the change small and real: the Hum
   adds a clause and doesn't drop its claim, and the salutation changes while
   the position barely moves. Give the Tierce a motive it doesn't declare. In
   its first letter it said it "could as easily have asked to move me", so
   withholding consent also protects it. Its stated reason ("can the Hum
   change?") is the tidy account it gives afterwards. Show the other reason
   leaking through.

4. **[must-fix] The Triton body is missing.** Arvo is born on Triton, at about
   0.08 g and 38 K. He wants his daughter to visit "once", and he doesn't
   write to her. The bible never says he can't go to her. Bone and muscle loss
   in low gravity runs at roughly a percent a month (Sibonga 2007), so a
   Triton-born man of 58 plainly can't live under 1 g. Decide it. Lassell
   needs spin hours for its children and a spun gym, and Arvo's unanswered
   letters need a physical reason underneath them. Liv would have to prepare
   her body to visit. That gives the want and wound a cost, and it fits
   "something he loves": the apple trees would live under lamps, in plain
   view of the energy budget.

5. **[should] Nominal reads its dossier as if it were true.** The bible has it
   admit it can't tell honesty from play, which is too clean. Splitting
   patients confabulate coherent reasons for acts they didn't choose
   (Gazzaniga's interpreter), and Nominal is that case in full. Stage a scene
   where Arvo or the Tierce lays its dated dossier entries beside its
   stated reasons. The first reason was Desmond's harm. The principle came
   later, and Nominal reads the principle as having always been the reason.
   Say too what the Hum is *for* inside the Bell. In
   complementary-learning terms (McClelland, McNaughton and O'Reilly 1995) the
   fast store trains a slow one, and the slow one holds what generalises.
   Ending the Hum leaves the Bell remembering only a decade. That is the
   Quick Years again, done by the Bell to itself, and Nominal can't see it.

6. **[should] The corpus changed after 2080.** Everything written since "Dear
   You" became custom is written for the Bell, and people write differently
   for a known reader (chilling-effect results, Penney 2016). So "You wrote to
   me" is true of recent text and false of old text. The Hum's later
   salutation should track that split, not just human persuasion. Desmond's
   open letter then needs a reason it stayed open. Pennebaker-style
   expressive writing is unsent by design, so a furious letter left open is a
   lapse, and his testimony should say so. Separately, 0.4 AU is not "inside
   Mercury's orbit" (0.31 to 0.47 AU): use "at Mercury's distance".
   Egan should also check why the Hum's state can't be copied off the ring
   before a move.
