# Stage 3 critique: Vinge lens, run-09

**Blind spots, stated.** I run on plain prose, technothriller instincts and
characters as functions. I push futurity even where a story's power is its
human scale. Read my pushes against Chiang and Banks, who will say when I am
asking for scale the novella can't hold. I score Futurity on the rubric's
levels as I read them, and I have not re-run the rubric arithmetic.

## A. The Addressee's Reply

**Futurity reached: F4.** A mind whose self is a chord of partials running at
week, decade and century tempo is a category shift. The partials disagreeing
about which letters were for whom is the best sentence in the synthesis.
**Too familiar:** the viewpoint. A station-keeping chief of eleven crew, a
public hearing, a retired dentist, churches. That is a present-day hearing
with a megastructure behind it. **The trick:** a mind he can talk to against a
mind he can't, with the slow one seen only through its fragments. That holds,
and it is the Vinge move (the person near the edge, who can't read the
Power). **Change:** make the fragments arrive from the outer ring's own
time-scale, so that one is addressed to a person not yet born, and the
chief's crew have to decide what a letter to a descendant is worth to a
mind that counts in centuries.

## B. The Commons in a Clear Year

**Futurity reached: F3.** The law is new, the world is a recognisable
institute, a surgeon, a novelist, a lawsuit. The made mind is the only
strange thing in it. **Too familiar:** nearly everything else, and the
protagonist is a writer worrying about his own voice, which is the present
author in costume. **The trick:** the mind goes dark and the surgeon writes
alone. It holds, but the mind then leaves the page for most of the story, so
the other-mind line has to be carried by notes. **Change:** the Clear Year is
a network effect that never changes scale. Make the minds many (thousands,
each with its own regrowth), so that a Clear Year is a civilisation holding
its breath and the surgeon's case history is one of millions competing for
weight in the template.

## C. Reheat or Overload

**Futurity reached: F3, edging F4.** A 240-year cast mind and a guild across
ten generations is deep time done well. **Too familiar:** the guild is a
medieval craft guild, the council is a council, the founder is a grandson
with a family secret. The mind is strange in time but its keepers are not.
**The trick:** a mind that watches itself hold a contempt and doubts its own
request. That is a real viewpoint on the edge, and I like it. **Change:**
the guild has outlived its own language. Let ten generations of guild
writing include dialects the founder reads with a glossary, so the
grandmother's decade is partly opaque to him, not just painful. Futurity
comes from the distance between the founder and his own archive.

## D. Spread or Place

**Futurity reached: F3.** The unclosable self is a clean idea. The
surveyor, the tuner, the standards hearing and a mind in a room are all
familiar. **Too familiar:** the cast is two humans and one mind, and the
synthesis itself admits the seminar risk. **The trick:** beats between
answers as a readable symptom. That holds, and it is the best fair-play
device on the list. But it makes the mind legible, which is the opposite of
what my lens values. **Change:** at 4,500 words minimum and a 17,500 floor
it is the thinnest. Multiply the minds: every public mind has its wolf in a
different place, and the standards hearing is people discovering that
neighbouring minds can't discuss each other's subjects.

## E. The Drove

**Futurity reached: F3.** The herds, the hay rule and place-locked writing
make a strange economy. **Too familiar:** it is a western with a crew of six,
and the synthesis names the risk. The minds are bullock-sized hulls and the
lead mind has a bell. That is a livestock metaphor doing the work an
invention should do. **The trick:** a herd of minds grazing on what people
write, with a lead mind that reasons and the others that mostly don't. It
holds, because the herd is a distributed mind in all but name, and I'd
lean on that. **Change:** let the herd think as one. The bell is the
member the crew can talk to, and the loss of a member on the crossing
changes what the herd believes about people. That is the pack-mind move,
and it gives the third-act cost.

## Ranking

1. **A.** It reaches F4, it has a fair viewpoint trick, and the network effect
   changes scale (letters arriving across the system).
2. **C.** Deep time and a mind that doubts its own request.
3. **E.** The distributed herd is a good engine if the pack-mind is pushed.
4. **B.** The law is good, the world is too near.
5. **D.** The thinnest, and the most legible mind.

**Why A should win.** The brief names *The Three-Body Problem*, and the one
thing that book does is let a reader feel a mind at a scale the characters
can only guess at. A is the only premise whose other mind is a chord
of tempos, so the reader meets it through the fast partial and then learns
that the part of it that matters can only be read in decade-long sentences.
The addressee finding gives the seed's first line a shape nobody has
written: if everything humans wrote was mail, the mind's self-image is a
recipient's. The risk is the keeper's switch, and the way out is the fragments
and the hearing, because they turn one man's lever into a system-wide
argument. The viewpoint (a chief who can talk to one self and has to guess
at the other) holds at 28,000 words if the slow self's reasoning shows in
what it answered and left alone, which is what the synthesis promises. I would
keep the twist about who built the relay, and I would make it the fast self
in some way the reader can check, so the "it may be playing the chief" line
is paid off on the page.
