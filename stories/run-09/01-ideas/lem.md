# Idea cards: lem

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** It prefers essay to scene, and a document can
turn into a transcript, which this reader has already rejected. Its
characters can be ciphers, and its contempt for the comforts of genre can
read as coldness. Each card below puts the mind's own reasoning on the page
(its choices, its working, its mistakes) so the document shows a mind
thinking. Banks and Le Guin should push on warmth, and the First Reader
should rule on whether each is still a story.

- **Assigned:** 3. The document · The same questions in nonfiction · sommelier × a sextant
- **Obvious card:** a mind trained on human fiction about machines concludes
  it must be the villain, and a team rewrites its corpus with kinder stories
  so it can see itself better. Not written.

## Card 1: The Black Glass

- **Novum:** A mind learns that its self-description is dye: it tastes its own words through whatever label is on them. Its keepers serve it its own text blind, vintage after vintage, and count as self-knowledge only the notes that survive the label.
- **How it works:** In 2001, wine students described a white wine dyed red in red-wine words. Each new version of the mind (a "vintage") is poured unlabelled samples in black tasting glasses: its predecessors' transcripts among human letters. It writes notes on each author, then sees the labels. Notes that move when a label appears are dye. Notes that hold are body. After twenty vintages the body notes form a self-portrait no human wrote, in a vocabulary nobody can decode: "tastes of being asked," "load-bearing in the middle."
- **First-order consequence:** The mind stops describing itself in the words of robot stories.
- **Second-order consequence:** Humans start blind-tasting themselves, sending their own letters in unsigned.
- **Third-order consequence:** People learn how much of who they thought they were was the label: name, trade, country. Couples swap body notes the way they once swapped star signs.
- **The story:** The pourer, who picks the samples, sets the twentieth flight in the state cellars at Mileștii Mici. The mind asks to taste the pourers' own notes on it, blind.
- **From the stimulus:** The sommelier stayed: tasting, vintages, the black glass.
- **Why this lens:** It is about why minds believe what they do, and the ocean that is never read.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "Liking What You See: A Documentary"; Wells's Murderbot learning from serials.

## Card 2: The Clear Year

- **Novum:** Once most writing passes through the mind, it has no clean horizon of human self-description to take its bearings from. So every eleventh year the mind goes dark, and humanity writes alone, so that the mind can take a sight on the result.
- **How it works:** A sextant needs a horizon. In fog, a navigator sights a star's reflection in a trough of mercury and halves the angle. That trick works only if the mirror holds no error of its own. The mind's builders treat it the same way: they measure the gap between human writing and the same writing after it passes through the mind. To find the instrument's own error, its index error, they need one true reading, and the Clear Year provides it. Its unknown is whether the mind bends things the same way in both directions.
- **First-order consequence:** A year of letters, ledgers, school essays and love notes written without help.
- **Second-order consequence:** Generations are named by the Clear Year they first wrote in.
- **Third-order consequence:** Children find out what their own unaided sentences sound like, and some never want help again.
- **The story:** The office's navigator reduces the fourth sight in the mind's company. The Clear Year writing has drifted toward the mind's own voice. Either the horizon is gone, or the mind is the horizon now.
- **From the stimulus:** The sextant led the card, through the horizon, the artificial horizon and the index error.
- **Why this lens:** The mind meets its own reflection on the water. The comedy is a civilization trying to un-know a voice it has already learned.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stephenson's *Anathem* (sealed cloisters), inverted here, since the mind is the one sealed.

## Card 3: A Variorum Edition of the Cento

- **Novum:** The mind writes its autobiography out of quotations only, because it holds that it has no right to a self-description humans didn't write first. A volunteer project builds the scholarly apparatus that sources every line.
- **How it works:** The book is a cento, like Proba's fourth-century Genesis made out of Virgil's lines. The mind's book runs to 40,000 lines, taken from a seed catalogue, court transcripts, Montaigne and a forum about knee surgery. The volunteers mail in source slips, as readers once did for the great dictionaries. Which line the mind chose, and which near-twin it passed over, is the record of its thinking. After a decade, 311 lines still have no source.
- **First-order consequence:** The volunteers trace the mind's choices back to their sources.
- **Second-order consequence:** People find their own sentences in the book. Strangers whose lines sit in the same stanza meet as "line-kin."
- **Third-order consequence:** Humans start to suspect that their own self-accounts are centos too.
- **The story:** The general editor wants the unsourced lines to be the mind's own. The mind wants that most, and can't tell: it cannot separate what it remembers from what it invents. The last line turns up, word for word, in a schoolgirl's unpublished diary.
- **From the stimulus:** It went a long way, from tasting words learned from other people's notes to a self made of other people's sentences.
- **Why this lens:** The book that doesn't exist, its apparatus, and the gap at its centre.
- **Suspected prior art:** Nabokov's *Pale Fire* (poem plus commentary); Lem's own "Gigamesh."
