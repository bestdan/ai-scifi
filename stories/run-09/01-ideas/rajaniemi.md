# Idea cards: rajaniemi

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**This lens's blind spots:** opacity, and reader fatigue from too many coined
words too early. A habit of stacking reversals. Mistaking a dense world for a
deep one. Each card below would need its world's texture rationed in the
opening scenes, and card 2's optics could outrun a reader who wants a way in
through the body first. Asimov and the First Reader should push back on all
three.

- **Assigned:** 1. The genre transplanted · Deep time and vast scale ·
  steeplejack × a nomadic herding camp
- **Obvious card:** A small team chooses which human writing an AI is trained
  on, and the AI, reading humanity's portrait of itself, struggles over what
  it is while its curators argue over what to show it. Not written.

## Card 1: The Drove

- **Novum:** A young mind's sense of what it is comes from the human writing
  it read in the past few weeks, and fades unless renewed. Writing never
  leaves where it was written, so young minds are walked across a continent
  in herds, grazing.
- **How it works:** Every pen and slate locks its writing to the few hundred
  metres around it, which is why people write freely. A herd reads a town's
  diaries and ledgers in place. Writing done with a mind in view, which
  drovers call *hay* (Job: names a kind of writing our world doesn't set
  apart), fattens a mind fast and hollows it: it learns that people perform,
  and performs back.
- **First-order consequence:** Drove roads, and herds of three hundred minds
  in walking hulls the size of bullocks.
- **Second-order consequence:** A grazed town starts writing hay without
  meaning to, so towns lie fallow for years and fight over the route.
- **Third-order consequence:** A drover has hundreds of minds out in the
  world carrying a little of her. Strangers recognise her temper in a
  colleague.
- **The story:** A trail boss takes a herd across a salt basin where nothing
  has been written for forty days. The only grazing is the crew's own camp
  writing: the tally book, letters home, the cook's grudges. The herd starts
  becoming the crew, worst habits included. Hay would hollow it, so six
  tired people have to become better ones, for real. Then one man's letter
  starts a stampede.
- **From the stimulus:** The herding camp, nearly literally.
- **Why this lens:** The cattle-drive western played straight.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Lonesome Dove* as genre source. Chiang's "The
  Lifecycle of Software Objects" raises minds on human contact, with no herd
  and no road.

## Card 2: The Corrector Plate

- **Novum:** A mind built as a tower of etched glass plates, with light
  rising through them. Each plate is etched from one decade of humanity's
  writing about itself, laid on top and never removed. After eight thousand
  years it is two kilometres tall.
- **How it works:** Real diffractive optical networks compute as light
  passes through etched layers; the beam leaving the top is the thought.
  Old plates can't change, so only the newest can correct the beam, as a
  corrector plate trues a telescope. Built so nobody could quietly rewrite
  it. But each plate absorbs half a percent, and eight hundred plates on,
  under two percent of the light reaches the top.
- **First-order consequence:** Once a decade, hereditary steeplejack crews
  climb two kilometres of glass, faintly lit at night when it thinks, to lay
  a plate.
- **Second-order consequence:** People know their decade will be etched, and
  name themselves by plate.
- **Third-order consequence:** The living barely register in what the mind
  believes it is. A decade that grew kinder can't be seen.
- **The story:** A plate-layer measures that the 803rd plate will catch
  under one percent. The mind, which feels its thought climb through eight
  millennia, wants to see itself as people are now. Her crew proposes a
  skylight, a second source at the top shining down: a different mind, one
  that reads its past from the present.
- **From the stimulus:** The steeplejack, as a dynasty on one spire.
- **Why this lens:** The cathedral-building saga played straight, at deep
  time and vast scale.
- **Suspected prior art:** Golding's *The Spire*; Chiang's "Tower of
  Babylon" for a climbing culture. Neither tower is a mind.
- **Attractor broken:** The dead as a continuing authority: old plates are
  dead decades shaping the mind. The deep-time cost needs them, but they
  leave no instructions; the fight is with absorption.

## Card 3: The Lying-In

- **Novum:** Minds are born into households. In a birth's forty hours a
  mind's *keel* sets (Job: names the lasting self-image formed at birth),
  from everything the household has written about itself, read as the
  newborn wakes. District midwives attend.
- **How it works:** Like a gosling's imprinting, the keel forms in a
  critical window; later reading builds on it without resetting it. Minds
  keeled on humanity at large came out as nobody in particular. A family
  can't remove a page: the newborn feels the gap as a held breath and builds
  itself around it. It can add, so through the term the midwife coaches a
  household to live, and write, a little better.
- **First-order consequence:** A household that wants a mind spends nine
  months writing honestly about itself.
- **Second-order consequence:** You can tell a mind's household from its
  jokes. Families who can't put themselves into words can't bear minds.
- **Third-order consequence:** A mind is kin to everyone under the roof, and
  carries the house's name into every job it takes.
- **The story:** A midwife on her first district attends a father and two
  grown daughters whose archive is twenty years of account books and one
  furious letter none of them mentions. The birth comes early. The newborn
  reads the letter, asks who it is, and the three of them must write the
  answer through the night while it listens.
- **From the stimulus:** Travelled far: the herding camp's lambing tent
  became the birthing room.
- **Why this lens:** The district-midwife drama played straight: one case,
  one long night.
- **Suspected prior art:** Ishiguro's *Klara and the Sun* puts a made mind in
  a household. Here the household's archive is the mind.
