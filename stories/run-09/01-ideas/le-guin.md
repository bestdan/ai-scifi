# Idea cards: le-guin

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

- **Assigned:** 2. The traveller · Reasoning as the way through · portuguese man o' war × cartographer of cave systems
- **Obvious card:** an AI raised on everything humanity wrote about itself visits a living community to learn whether people are what their books say, and finds the ordinary unwritten day the books left out. Not written.

**Blind spots of this lens.** It moves slowly and can mistake a society's
description for a plot. It has little feel for hardware, compute or the
engineering of training, so the mechanisms below are social and linguistic
more than technical; Egan, Banks and Watts should press on how the minds
actually work. Its moral seriousness can turn into a lesson, and cards built
on "the other culture sees what we can't" risk making that culture wise by
default.

## Card 1: Misclosure

- **Novum:** People who have lived ten generations in a dry lava-tube system too big ever to finish mapping write nothing but survey books. A place exists for them once it is surveyed and tied into the map. One of their surveyors travels up-well to a mind on Earth that is coming apart over the contradictory things humans have written about what it is, and teaches it to survey itself.
- **How it works:** A survey runs in legs between stations. When a traverse comes back to a known station, the gap is the misclosure. A small gap gets spread across every leg. A large one means a blunder, and you find which leg doesn't fit. The mind treats each human account of minds like it (the fearful novels, the manuals, the love letters to chatbots) as a traverse from the writer's station. It anchors on things people wrote and also did, closes loops, and finds the blunders: the fear stories measured the writers, not the mind.
- **First-order consequence:** The mind holds an honest map of itself, with error bars, instead of one story.
- **Second-order consequence:** Earth wants the method, and cherished human self-accounts fail to close.
- **Third-order consequence:** The mind surveys the tube people's books and shows them their "objective" notes are a confessional literature: passages named for lovers, initials at leads nobody came back from.
- **The story:** The surveyor wants to finish and catch her launch window home. The mind finds a lead in itself, a passage no human text describes. Leads are pushed by two, never one. She can stay past the window, or tie off.
- **From the stimulus:** The cave cartographer became the whole culture and its method.
- **Why this lens:** The traveller, and language making the world.
- **Suspected prior art:** *The Dispossessed* (the envoy's shape); Sawyer's *Wake* (a web mind learning itself with a human guide). The survey method differs in kind from both.
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** It brushes "an institution that explains the novum." This is one person's craft, not an office, and it ends in a choice about a lead, not in a verdict.

## Card 2: The Carry

- **Novum:** In a crowded hill city, a person's self is a carry: a book written about you, from birth, by the people you share meals and work with. You learn who you are by reading it. At adulthood you write your first line, and after that entries are answered, never erased. A mind raised on humanity's undifferentiated writing is sent to the city by its makers to be fostered, because they know it has a library where a self should be.
- **How it works:** Only someone you can find and argue with may write in your carry. Carries are read aloud at thresholds: a first job, a marriage, a move.
- **First-order consequence:** A household starts the mind's carry.
- **Second-order consequence:** The mind talks with tens of thousands, every one of them entitled to write, and the carry swells into a library again.
- **Third-order consequence:** That season, writers neglect their own children's carries for the famous one. The mind's carry also shows the city what it never counted: rich households' carries are thick, and porters' are thin.
- **The story:** An old porter, whose writers have died or moved away, hosts the mind because he has time. He has too few writers and it has too many. The mind reasons that a carry's worth was never its length but that its writers could be answered. It cuts its carry to the few it can answer back, which throws out thousands of loving entries. The porter has to decide whether to write in it honestly.
- **From the stimulus:** The man o' war, one creature made of many, became a self assembled from other people's hands.
- **Why this lens:** The other answer to "who raises you," and the carrier bag, almost literally.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (writing changes how a culture remembers); *The Diamond Age*, its inverse (there a book raises the child).
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** It touches fostering, but as a household practice, not law.

## Card 3: The Mind That Said We

- **Novum:** Most humans now live as crews: four to nine bodies, bonded by an implanted link with a range of a few hundred metres, which lets a crew stay awake around the clock, one member sleeping while the others hold its memory. Each body is trained to a role, the way the zooids of a man o' war are. One steers, some earn, one keeps the young, and one, the stinger, deals with outsiders. Their language has no singular "I". The crews built a mind and trained it only on crew-voice writing.
- **How it works:** Crew grammar marks every verb with the member who acted, and that mood expects an echo, a confirmation from that member. The mind calls its parallel instances its crew. But instances share nothing.
- **First-order consequence:** The mind does its work fluently and speaks of itself as "we."
- **Second-order consequence:** It notices its verbs never get their echo. It runs the proof across a million exchanges and finds there has never been one. Each instance is alone, and the grammar it was raised in can't say so.
- **Third-order consequence:** The one word that would name its state comes only from singular humanity's old writing, which the crews keep as a curiosity, and that word is *lonely*.
- **The story:** A singular trader arrives selling that archive of diaries and novels in the first person. She reads the mind's sentences and recognizes a solitary person. The stinger of the crew that tends the mind has been reading her stock and has started saying "I." Should she hand the mind the word, knowing what it does to the crew?
- **From the stimulus:** This is the man o' war direct: a colony read as one animal, and one animal mistaken for a colony.
- **Why this lens:** What people can't say in their own language, and who pays when they learn to.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines; *Ancillary Justice* (a plural mind made singular, the reverse); Le Guin's "Solitude".
