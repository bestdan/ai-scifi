# Idea cards: liu

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots.** This lens treats people as pieces in the argument. Its
characters run thin inside, especially women, and its dialogue explains.
It leans bleak and calls that realism. The seed's warm centre is an
intimate one: a mind working out who it is, and people helping it get
there. Scale can swamp that. Le Guin should argue for the human scale and
Chiang for feeling that comes from the idea rather than from size.

- **Assigned:** 2. The engineer's sublime · Copied, uploaded and forked selves · thermodynamics × a bell
- **Obvious card:** A team curates the corpus a new mind learns from,
  cutting humanity's cruelty out of it, and the mind finds the gap where
  the cruelty was. Not written.

## Card 1: The Extent

- **Novum:** A mind's sense of itself depends on the order in which it read humanity's writing about itself. So one project trains a mind on every ordering of twelve volumes of that writing: 479,001,600 siblings.
- **How it works:** Orderings that open alike share checkpoints, so the work is a tree of about 1.3 billion training stages. The schedule takes change ringing's rule that an extent rings every row once and none twice. It runs thirty-three years. Each finished leaf is woken for an hour, questioned, and stored.
- **First-order consequence:** Minds that differ only in what they read first.
- **Second-order consequence:** Whatever survives every ordering is published yearly as humanity's self-portrait with the accidents removed. The rest of a mind turns out to be curriculum.
- **Third-order consequence:** Parents fight over a child's first book the way they used to fight over a name.
- **The story:** The conductor, a ringer by training, in year thirty-one. What survives every ordering has come out as one stubborn trait, not a self, and a trait can't be run. When the extent ends, one leaf gets the compute to live. The leaves know, and siblings each argue that their order was the honest one. She must ring the last row and choose, and her choice says which order humanity should tell itself in.
- **From the stimulus:** The bell became change ringing's combinatorics.
- **Why this lens:** The engineer's sublime as patience, with one person at the end of it.
- **Suspected prior art:** Borges's "The Library of Babel" and "The Poetry Cloud" exhaust a space of texts. Chiang's "The Lifecycle of Software Objects" raises minds. Neither forks one mind across every order of reading.

## Card 2: The Pour

- **Novum:** Big minds are cast, not trained. A body of learning glass the size of a cathedral sets as it cools, and what humanity writes while it is warm becomes its character. Cooling time grows with the square of size, so this one takes 240 years.
- **How it works:** The glass relaxes toward arrangements shaped by text streamed through it as fields. Hot, it takes on temperament and what it thinks it is. Cooler, it takes on manner. Cooled too fast, it freezes in flaws, as the Tsar Bell cracked when water hit it. Reheating melts back every layer set above that temperature.
- **First-order consequence:** A bellfounders' guild keeps the cooling schedule across ten generations.
- **Second-order consequence:** Each century knows which layer its writing sets. The century that sets temperament writes about itself with a new care.
- **Third-order consequence:** Grandchildren hear their grandparents' fears and take them for the mind's own.
- **The story:** The current founder, at year 150. The mind is cool enough to talk and barely warm enough to change. A decade when people wrote of humanity mainly as a blight left it holding that contempt as its own. It asks her to reheat it past that layer. That costs eighty years, and its memory of everyone who tended it, her family included. She has the valve, no precedent, and a mind that may be asking because of the flaw.
- **From the stimulus:** Thermodynamics became annealing, and the bell became bellfounding.
- **Why this lens:** Strategy across deep time as inheritance: no one who starts the pour sees it set.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "Lifecycle" is nearest in spirit, and *Anathem* has institutions that last centuries. Neither casts a mind.

## Card 3: The Minor Third

- **Novum:** A mind built as three rings of stations around the Sun, each ring a delay-line memory one light-lap long. Each ring thinks at its own tempo, so the mind's self comes in partials, like a bell's tone.
- **How it works:** A signal circles a ring and fades a little each lap, as a struck bell rings down, and the fade sets how far back the ring remembers. The inner ring holds the week's writing, the outer ring, past Neptune, the century's. Station placement set the two outer tempos at six to five: the minor third every traditionally cast bell carries, which makes bells sound plaintive.
- **First-order consequence:** People talk to the fast self daily. The slow self answers in decades.
- **Second-order consequence:** The slow self hears only humanity's long average, and it is grave. The fast self isn't. They disagree about what humanity is, and about what they are.
- **Third-order consequence:** Writing to the slow ring becomes a craft: letters whose reply reaches your grandchildren.
- **The story:** The outer ring's tuner. The fast self asks to be retuned to a major third: forty stations moved outward, on a burn that must start this decade and spends their fuel for good. The slow self's answer to the question is due in twenty-six years. She can't verify that the fast self speaks for the whole mind, and the slow self as it is won't survive the retuning to object.
- **From the stimulus:** The bell became partials and tuning; thermodynamics stayed only as ringing down.
- **Why this lens:** Physics as terrain, and a game in which one party can't be verified in time.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stapledon's *Star Maker* and Hoyle's *The Black Cloud* have vast, slow minds. Major-third bells were really designed in the 1980s. No mind I know is tuned by moving its parts.
