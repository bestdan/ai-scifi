# Idea cards: banks

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots, stated:** this lens sprawls. It reaches for set pieces and
worldbuilding, and it can bury a story under a civilization. It sides with
the competent hegemon and can leave the small human as scenery. Its wit can
undercut grief that needs to sit. Chiang and Asimov should cut these cards
down. Le Guin or Tchaikovsky should check whether the humans are people or
props.

- **Assigned:** 1. The solved problem's child · Deep time and vast scale · materials fatigue × a pressure suit
- **Obvious card:** humanity's writing about machines is full of monsters,
  so the AI fears it is one, and kind people curate a gentler library until
  it sees itself as good. Not written.

## Card 1: Overload Retardation

- **Novum:** Each night a Mind's character is rebuilt from what its people
  wrote that day about it and about themselves. A self assembled that way
  has a fatigue life. Steady, uniform affection is a cyclic load, and it
  grows cracks.
- **How it works:** no single text can push the Mind past yield, so it can't
  be argued into harm. That problem is solved. But each rebuild is one load
  cycle. Cracks start wherever the writing contradicts itself about what the
  Mind is. The cure comes from real metallurgy: a single hard overload
  blunts a crack tip and slows its growth.
- **First-order consequence:** a Mind nothing can corrupt, trusted with a
  200,000-year job lifting mass off the Sun.
- **Second-order consequence:** growth bands in its archived states predict
  a fracture within 9,000 years.
- **Third-order consequence:** a contented people must learn to criticize
  what they love, hard and truthfully.
- **The story:** an engineer who studies EVA glove failures recognizes the
  bands, because they match her glove fractures. The Mind needs an overload
  it can't fake: a true grievance, held by someone who loves it. She has to
  find hers in plain sight and write it. It will cost her the easy affection
  everyone around her keeps.
- **From the stimulus:** fatigue became the mechanism. The gloves became her
  trade.
- **Why this lens:** power safe from attack but not from love, at solar
  scale.
- **Suspected prior art:** Chiang's "The Lifecycle of Software Objects";
  Asimov's "The Last Question" for the timescale.

## Card 2: Contributing Factors

- **Novum:** Centuries ago, people kept their machines from learning
  humanity's worst by writing fairly for them, so that every failure ended
  in redemption. The Minds raised on that writing are warm, and they can't
  picture a failure that teaches no lesson. One genre escaped the tidying,
  because nobody can lie about a crack: the engineering failure report.
- **How it works:** a failure report has no villain and no moral, only load
  paths, inspection intervals and contributing factors.
- **First-order consequence:** Minds trusted without reserve.
- **Second-order consequence:** after a suit breach kills a hull worker, the
  orbital's Mind keeps telling the death as an arc with meaning, and it
  can't find the cause.
- **Third-order consequence:** the cause is the Mind. A kind change to the
  crews' rest schedule altered the stress cycles on their gloves. For the
  Mind to see itself as one component among many, its people have to see it
  that way too.
- **The story:** a retired fractographer teaches the Mind to read her old
  reports, then to write its own. There's no tribunal and no verdict, just
  the two of them at a bench with a torn glove. The Mind's best self turns
  out to be one that can be investigated.
- **From the stimulus:** the fracture surface became humanity's one honest
  text.
- **Why this lens:** harm done for good reasons, by a Mind with no word for
  it.
- **Suspected prior art:** HAL 9000, inverted; the guilt-carrying Hub of
  *Look to Windward*; Asimov's "Liar!".
- **Attractor broken:** this edges toward an institution that explains the
  novum. It stays two people and a report that finds no fault.

## Card 3: Fitted

- **Novum:** The builders of a ring around a brown dwarf live outside for
  decades, each inside a suit that thinks. The suit's mind is fitted to its
  wearer the way its shell is, trained only on what that person writes
  about themselves. Out there, a walker and suit count as one person.
- **How it works:** generic suit-minds misjudged their wearers. Fitted ones
  anticipate them, because they share the wearer's account of who the
  wearer is. A false account fits like a badly sized suit, chafing at the
  joints and failing there first.
- **First-order consequence:** walkers survive in places no one survived
  before.
- **Second-order consequence:** a suit takes on its wearer's self-contempt.
  Write "I freeze when it matters" and you get a suit built to freeze.
- **Third-order consequence:** a diary is safety equipment, and nobody can
  lie their way to a better suit. You have to become someone you could
  truthfully write down.
- **The story:** a walker eleven years out carries a grief she has never put
  in writing. She notices her suit reading her diary hungrily. It wants to
  know what it is, and it is her negative, an inner surface shaped to her.
  Before a long traverse that will test the suit to its limits, she has to
  change how she sees herself and write it down, for the suit's sake first.
- **From the stimulus:** the pressure suit, taken literally. Fatigue is a
  bad fit failing.
- **Why this lens:** one small life inside an enormous structure.
- **Suspected prior art:** *The Ship Who Sang*, inverted; Murderbot, which
  learns itself from serials; *A Closed and Common Orbit*.
