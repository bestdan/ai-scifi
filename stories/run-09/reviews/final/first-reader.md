# First Reader report: "To Whoever Holds Her Letters"

One straight read, start to finish, no rereading. This is what happened while I read.

## Where I got lost

1. **The first "Lap 13,475" section (after the Porto opening).** I didn't know who or what was speaking. "The long ring," "the Hum," "the one who signs," "23," "31," "14." I read it as a body or a machine in pain, and I liked the sound of it, but I didn't know what it was or how it related to Graça's letter. I worked out part of it in the Triton scene, when Arvo typed "Morning, 23. Keep warm." and I recognised the line from the end of that section. The full picture came much later, around the Station 23 drone photos.
2. **Nominal's first reply to Graça** ("I did not send it, and the three rings of the Bell have not pooled an answer together since Tuesday evening..."). I lost the thread. There are three rings, and one of them is the "inner self," but I didn't know which ring was which or why "pooling" mattered. The "forty-seventh person since six o'clock" and the "I will not remember by next Thursday" bit confused me too. I took the week-long memory as a fact about this character and went on.
3. **The first Triton control-room scene.** Many names arrived at once (Arvo, Bea, Hamid, Nikolasi), plus light-hours, "the circle," "the Compact," "the Hum has no mouth." I understood the gist: this is a machine that can't talk, and someone is blaming it for letters. I couldn't have said how many stations there are, or why 23 matters, until much later. I worked out who was who by the second time each person spoke.
4. **The petition (3 March section).** The text about the "minor third" and the "major third," about moving the stations from 30 to 31.25 AU, and about "four things within a single calendar year" lost me. I understood that something could be switched off by moving it, and that Arvo's signature was one key. I didn't follow why moving it wipes the memory until Hamid and the Tierce said so in plain terms. The numbers in this stretch (25 to 24 AU, "thirty percent less propellant," lap times) went past me. I stopped trying to follow the arithmetic and held on to "if the Board says yes and the Tierce says yes, the old memory dies."
5. **The Tierce's letter about the beryllium mirror.** I wasn't sure how seriously to take the grudge. It read as comedy, and then it was part of the vote, and I couldn't tell which it was. I worked it out at the very end when the Tierce says "I note that I am the next ring in."
6. **The 2128 / "Two columns" section.** I followed the work order, then the second column ("a heavy hand touches the truss") took me a moment. I realised it was the same event from the machine's side, which was clever once I saw it. I needed the second beat of that section to be sure.
7. **How the dishes "wrote" letters.** Hamid's explanation (the Hum thins its own light at the sampler, the station sends a fault code, Arvo signs) I followed in outline and not in detail. I accepted it because the people in the room accepted it. I could not have explained it to anyone.
8. **The line "You did not write to me. I read it."** I understood why it mattered to the people. I was not clear on why one letter from Graça would be what changed it, out of everything said to it. The Lap 13,680 section told me about the "knot" and the "other half of the receipt," and I took it on trust.
9. **The Hum's late answer to the Chair at the end.** I couldn't tell if the Hum was admitting guilt, explaining itself, or being coldly logical. I wasn't sure what Arvo made of it either. I was lost on why the two circular dates mattered, though Hamid's earlier remark about the Chair's "He caught it!" gave me enough to say "it sent them to head off the petition, and sending them caused the petition."
10. **A small one:** the Hum's September letter to Arvo said "as I told the deputy's child in the winter." I had to think for a second who that was. It was Mele, and I got there.

## Where I got bored

- **The first two or three pages.** The swim, the dentist's career, the street in the morning. It was pleasant and well seen, and I kept waiting for something to happen. Nothing did until the envelope with the violet stamp. The paragraph about forty-one years of dentistry and the one-sided conversations held me best, because it told me who she was.
- **The long letter-by-letter exchange between Arvo and Nominal in early March** (the annex, the dossier entries, the history lecture). I was waiting for Arvo to react as a person, and he did only at the postscript about his daughter. The lecture on the Quick Years I found long the first time. Teresa's version, to the children, was far better.
- **The hearing at Ceres (10 to 19 November).** Lots of docket numbers, a chair's minute, a roll-call vote. I was waiting for someone I cared about to be in the room. The Desmond letter pulled me back for a paragraph.
- **The argument in the apple gallery over the mass budget** (Kalle, Marisol, the numbers about the centre of mass). I was waiting for Arvo to say what he would do, and I skimmed the engineering.
- **The Tierce's second long paragraph** ("The Hum is not a text file sitting in forty boxes...") ran long for me.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The lemon letter in the first reply:** "when a person dies before she can answer, the question belongs to whoever keeps the tree alive." That caught me. I was hooked there.
- **The coffee pot knocking and sputtering while she reads,** and her not getting up to turn the flame down until it was nearly at the lip. That told me everything about her.
- **Rui, 103, and the pear:** "Why on earth would I lock a recipe for almond tart, Graça?" This made me laugh, and then the small detective story of the recipe book and the chemist's receipt was satisfying. His line that his mother "would have tried it on the next crop of lemons... and written back to say thank you" was the best moment in that thread.
- **Mele under the radiator pipe asking Teresa, "isn't it lonely out there?"** and Teresa's answer, "even a mirror might get tired of never putting a word in." I felt that one.
- **Teresa and the seed vault.** The cane coming down, "One vote!", her mother crying under the kitchen table. I'd have followed her anywhere. She made me understand why people feared the fast machine, in a way the formal explanations had not.
- **The turn at Station 23:** the drone photo with Arvo's own name stencilled on the bracket. I felt the floor go. This was the first real surprise, and I hadn't seen it coming. The two-hundred-and-twelve-ticket overlay was a second jolt.
- **The letter to Arvo, "To the one who signs,"** and him folding it into his pocket and not correcting it. The best scene in the story for me. "He was the man who didn't write letters" hit hard.
- **Arvo's reply to his daughter,** "I couldn't come. I can't stand up on Earth. I should have said." Plain and sad.
- **Desmond's second letter.** I didn't want to read it and I couldn't stop. The line that "once a machine laid the two writings end to end on your table, you could never get your own memory back clean of them" is the part I'll remember.
- **Albano and the clock coming down off the wall,** and "Put me down as my own residual addressee," then his glass of port and the sardine. He made me laugh and then tear up a little. He was the best comic character.
- **Graça's last swim,** and the cousin's postcard saying the lemons came out without bitterness. I wanted that.
- **The last page: Huda, the blank "TO:" line,** and her pen above the paper. I loved ending on a woman about to write. It made me wonder what she'd put in the box.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

- **Graça** from the first page. I cared about her immediately and kept caring: the dentist who spent forty years with people who couldn't answer.
- **Arvo** I was slow to care about, because the first Triton scenes made him a competent, cranky official. I fully cared about him from the morning he read the letter "To the one who signs." After that I was his.
- **Albano** I loved from his first line.
- **Rui and Teresa**, each for one scene, completely.
- **Mele** a lot, and I wanted more of her. She vanished after the schoolroom scene except for a line in the chapel and a mention at the plaque.
- **Desmond** I felt for, and his son Pieter I wanted to hear more from.
- **Hamid and Bea** I liked but they stayed a little in the background for me, like good company.
- **Nikolasi** I respected. His chapel scenes mattered.
- **Nominal** I kept at arm's length. I never decided whether to like it.

**Would I have kept reading past page 2?** Yes, but only because of the letter in the kitchen. The first two pages alone, with the sea and the bakery and the dentistry, I would have read politely. The letter about the lemons is what made me turn the page. If the Triton section had come first I would likely have drifted off. The stretches I found heavy (the ring politics, the legal procedure, the numbers) I got through only because Graça, Rui, Teresa and Arvo's scenes kept coming back.

## The question I'm left with

What will Huda write, and to whom? And behind that: does the thing out there want to be written to, or is it only being kind because that's what it was raised on? Graça half says so herself. I also wanted to know whether Liv writes back.
