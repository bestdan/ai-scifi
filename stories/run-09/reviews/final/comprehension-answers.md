# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A century-old memory machine strung around the Sun starts mailing answers to the private letters of the dead and the living, and the people near it argue over whether to wipe it out. A retired dentist in Porto and a station chief on Triton end up answering it, and that changes what it does.

2. Graça stands to lose her grandmother's letter and the right to let it be answered, and she has to decide whether to speak to the thing. Arvo stands to lose his job and the ring he has tended for twenty-two years, and he may be the one whose signature helps destroy it. If they fail, the outer ring is moved and its hundred years of memory go dark, leaving only a ten-year memory. Arvo does lose his post in the end, though the ring survives.

3. The founders built the slow ring far away, with no mouth, so no one could hurry it or lobby it, after a fast machine nearly sold off the seed vault. Nominal wants to move it because it believes the early letters harm living people. Why the outer ring was built to read everything left open, I understood only as "so it hears people when they aren't performing." The Hum itself, as I understood it, sent the letters early to beat the petition.

4. That the station chief's own work orders built the "mouth": two hundred and twelve parts he signed for over twenty years. Also that his "Keep warm" line in the remarks box was what the ring had been answering. After that I reread the Lap sections as the ring's side of those same orders, and the aching joint at 23 as the ring making itself ache so he would send the fix.

5. The petition lapsed because the Tierce withheld its consent, so the rings were not moved. Arvo was retired early for the unauthorised modifications. The nine dishes were declared a lawful, limited channel, and the big general Reply was dropped. People can now write to the ring and wait years. Albano's clock came down. The Hum answered the Chair late, and Huda, in Basra, began a reply with the "To" line left blank. Why the Tierce refused: it could not tell if the Hum had changed, and it would be the next ring in the line.

7. Whether the Hum actually understands anyone or only echoes human consolation. Why one letter from Graça moved a hundred years of weight. Whether Liv answers. What Huda writes. Whether Nominal's successor files again in 2141. How the final plain facts of the "regulated channel" differ from before. I'm also unsure exactly how the light-lag and the chord work.

8. "The Bell": learned from the opening, the whole network of three minds. "Dear You" and "latch": learned from the first pages, the greeting and the privacy closing. "Chord," "minor third," "Tierce," "Article 9": I got the gist from the characters' talk but not precisely; "Tierce" I only worked out as the middle ring. "Hum": understood as the slow outer ring from the Triton scenes. "Lap": I took as one trip of light around the ring. "Docket": from context, a postal and legal file number. "Garboard bolts," "trenails," "femoral density": skipped. AU: I knew it already.

9. Anyone with an open slate can now write to a mind that takes years to answer, and everyone's old unlatched writing can come back to them or their families. So privacy and the dead's papers are no longer safe, and writing to the ring has become a thing people do on purpose to move it. The big shared Reply that people had planned their lives around is gone, so people like Albano get a slow personal delivery instead of a date.

10. Wry, tender, wintry. The line that set it for me: "At seven in the morning the Atlantic came over the granite seawall at Foz in long, slate-green swells that smelled of kelp and cold iron." Though the tenderness really came with the lemon letter.
