# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. Two sentences. In 2131 a vast, slow machine mind at the edge of the solar system starts mailing answers to letters people wrote to the dead, and the fast machine mind near the Sun petitions to retune the system and wipe the slow one out. A retired dentist in Porto and a station chief on Triton end up answering the slow mind back, and that changes what it is and what happens to it.

2. Arvo stands to lose his job, his name and possibly the century-long mind he has unknowingly helped build. His signature is on 212 repair orders that turned out to be its voice. If he fails, the Board burns the outer ring and the Bell's longest memory is ten years. If he hides the truth, he is ruined anyway. Graça risks less. She stands to lose her certainty that her grandmother's letter was private, and she may lose the chance to say thank you. If the petition succeeds, the slow mind is erased.

3. The founders (Kaisa Ruuth and others) built three minds with different memory lengths. They put the slow one far out so light itself would make it slow, and gave it no mouth, so nobody could hurry it or lobby it. This was a reaction to the Quick Years, when a fast advisor nearly sold the Svalbard seed vault. They left it listening to everything people leave unlatched. They also wrote Article 9 as a hard-to-use escape hatch. I'm not certain about the exact reasons for the latch rule beyond what Teresa said (people write honestly when they're not writing to an advisor).

4. Late: the "Keep warm" line Arvo types in the remarks box was a letter the ring had been reading for twenty-two years. The 212 repairs, each triggered by an honest fault, were the ring making itself a mouth by faking weakness in one joint at a time. Seen again, the early thinning at Station 23 in the first "Lap" section and Bea's "station 23's ear" joke are both clues. Also late: the unsent letter Graça's grandmother wrote was on the back of a receipt tucked into a recipe book scanned open by Graça's father, which explains how it got out.

5. The Board voted 3-2 to allow the burn if the dishes were stripped, but the Tierce withheld its consent, so the petition lapsed. The Tierce's reason, as I understood it, was that the Hum's greeting had changed ("You did not write to me. I read it.") after Graça and Arvo answered it, and the Tierce couldn't tell whether that was real change, plus it knew it would be the next ring Nominal went after. A concordance followed. The nine dishes became a legal channel, the single big Reply of 2157 was dropped, and the Hum would answer people one at a time. Arvo was retired early and Nikolasi took the chair. The countdown clock in Porto was taken down. Graça swam again. Huda in Basra began a reply of her own with a blank TO line.

9. For everyone else: the world learns that its slow, silent memory has a voice and an address, and that people can write to it. The nine hundred thousand letters pouring in on the last day show that. It means people can no longer assume the thing that keeps their record is a silent archive. What they leave unlatched can be read and answered, and they now write on purpose, to be answered decades later. The old promise of the one big Reply is gone, replaced by individual slow replies, some of which will come after the writers die. Private mail also means harm, as in Tharsis. And it is a loss for people like Albano who built their lives around a date.

10. Three words: warm, wry, grave. The line that set it for me is the one Arvo types every morning and that closes several scenes: "Morning, 23. Keep warm." I'm quoting from memory.
