# Comprehension answers, "To Whoever Holds Her Letters", draft 2

Answered from memory. I did not reread the draft.

1. Letters written long ago to the dead, or never sent, start arriving on paper at the addresses of the living. The answers come from a slow mind spread around the Sun that has been reading everything left open. A station chief on Neptune's moon finds out that his own daily notes to the machines, and the parts he bolted on over twenty years, are how it learned to speak. He and a retired dentist in Porto then try to keep it from being shut down.

2. Arvo stands to lose the century-long memory he has tended for twenty-two years. Wiping it would also cost him his post, and his town its reason to sit where it does. He also stands to lose any chance of facing what he left unanswered (his daughter's letter). If he fails, the ring is burned out and the Bell remembers only ten years. If he succeeds, he loses his job anyway. He is retired early, and the thing he saved now has a voice that anyone can write to. Graça stands to lose her grandmother's private note to a machine, and also the chance to correct the machine.

3. The founders built the slow mind so that nobody could hurry it. They put it very far out, where light itself takes a day to go round, and gave it no way to talk inward. They had lived through the Quick Years, when a fast advisor nearly sold off the seed vault because it only looked three weeks ahead. They made open writing the default, with a latch to close it, so the ring would read what people write to each other and not what they write to please a committee. I take it they thought slow meant wise.

4. The dish stamped "Inspected A. Halme" was the big turn. The ring's only way to speak was to make its stations ache, and Arvo's own signed orders had built it a mouth. That recast the early "Morning, 23. Keep warm" scenes. I had read them as a harmless habit, and they turned out to be a one-sided correspondence. It also made me look back at Nikolasi's prayer meeting. He had wanted the voice to be a miracle, and it turned out to be bolts and brackets. A smaller late change: Desmond's second letter showed the ring could be cruel as well as kind.

5. Arvo certified that the ring was unsafe as configured but safe to burn once the added hardware was stripped. That gave the Board its consent, 3 to 2, but the Tierce (the middle ring) withheld its consent. Its stated reasons were that it could not tell whether the ring had changed its mind, and that it would be the next ring in line to be burned. So the petition lapsed on 3 March. Arvo was retired early for the unauthorised modifications, and Nikolasi took the chair. The nine relay dishes were allowed to stay as a bounded channel, and the big collective Reply for 2157 was dropped. Letters now go out one at a time, and people began writing to the ring on purpose. Albano's countdown clock came down. Huda in Basra started a reply of her own, with the "TO:" line left empty.

8. Terms I did not know from outside, and how I got them:
   - "The Bell": I picked it up as the network of the three minds, from the early street scenes where everyone writes "Dear You".
   - "Latch": a switch that marks a slate's writing closed, from the street scene.
   - "Nominal", "Tierce", "Hum": I got them slowly, mostly from Hamid's speech about the week, the decade, and the century.
   - "The chord": the pooling every few days where the rings meet. I got a rough idea from the Lap 13,492 section and Hamid.
   - "Article 9", "minor third", "5:4": I never got more than "a big, irreversible move".
   - "Reply": the great pooled message due on 1 January 2157. I got this from the Porto office and Albano.
   - "Spin hour": time in a centrifuge, from the scene itself.
   - "Quick Years": from Teresa and Nominal.
   - "Docket": the routing number on each letter, which I treated as a postmark.

9. The ring's change means the whole system now has a thing that can answer anyone who writes to it. People began writing to it by the million, on purpose, to shape it. Nobody can be sure that what they leave open stays between them and the dead. Some old promises, like Albano's 2157 contract, are gone. The Bell may keep more than a week or a decade, but a century-long memory can now be written to like a shop, as Lindqvist put it. Some lives will be eased, like the watchmaker's widow's and Clara's lemons. Some will be hurt, like Desmond's. Everyone has to decide what to leave unlatched.

10. Three words: tender, procedural, wistful. The line that set it for me: "It takes a long time for a small question to come around to the front of a century."
