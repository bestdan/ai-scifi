# First Reader report: "To Whoever Holds Her Letters", draft 2

One straight read, start to the last line. No reread. I stopped at the end of the story (the blank "TO:" line); I saw no notes section.

## Where I got lost

1. **The first "Lap 13,475" section (after Nominal's reply to Graça).** I did not know who was talking or what "the long ring", "the Hum" and "the one who signs" were. It read like a body describing itself, and I couldn't tell whether it was a person, a machine or a place. I worked out later, from the Triton scenes, that it is the outer ring thinking. The "one who signs" and "Morning, 23. Keep warm" I only placed when Arvo typed it in the trim room, a few pages on.
2. **Who is who among the three minds.** "The Bell", "Nominal", "the inner ring", "the Tierce", "the Hum", "the week-mind", "the decade-mind" and "the century-mind" all turn up before I had a clear picture. Nominal's letter of 3 March calls the Tierce "the swing vote" before I knew what the Tierce was. Hamid's speech in the trim room ("Nominal remembers a week, the middle ring ten years, out here we keep the century") sorted it out for me. Before that I was guessing.
3. **The ratio paragraph on 3 March** (the minor third, 6:5, 5:4, "twenty-six hours against twenty-two", 30.00 to 31.25 AU). I could not follow why those numbers were the same as a musical interval, or why only one move was possible. I took away only that the move was big, irreversible, and needed four things to line up. I never worked the arithmetic out.
4. **The four conditions and the one-year deadline.** I followed this loosely: four things, a deadline, and if it lapses the plan is dead. I could not have listed the four on my own until the vote scenes made it concrete.
5. **Arvo's chess game.** "Once a week since 2117" is fourteen years, which is hundreds of moves. But he plays "24. Rb4" in March 2131, then "42. Bc4" in January 2132. The numbers don't fit together for me, and I never worked it out. It's a small thing, but I noticed it.
6. **"neither was the man on Mars"** in Arvo's October remarks. I took a moment to place him as Desmond in Tharsis. Mars is also where Arvo's chess partner lives, and I briefly wondered if it meant him. I worked it out from the surrounding sentence.
7. **Who knew what about the dish.** The dish's mounting plate says "Inspected A. Halme". Then Hamid says the Hum can only talk by making its stations ache. I followed it, and it was the best moment of the middle. Later there are three explanations at once (it planned ahead, it learned from Arvo's notes, the manual trained it like a dog). I lost track of which one the story wanted me to believe.
8. **The Hum's late reply to the Chair, and the Chair's earlier minute.** The reply says it "judged" the inner ring would file and sent the pages early. Arvo then thinks nobody can prove it, and recalls Hamid's "right that a request might come, wrong that one was coming anyway". I couldn't tell if this was settled. I took it that the story wanted it left open, but I wasn't sure.
9. **The lap numbers and the dates.** Lap 13,722 is the "query" lap, 13,843 the reply lap, and the lap numbers elsewhere (12,638, 13,680, and so on). I did not try to check them and just let them go by.

## Where I got bored

1. **The swim and the dentist history at the start** (the first pages, from the sea wall through "forty-one years she had practiced dentistry"). It was pretty and I enjoyed the cold water, but I was waiting for the letter. The envelope with the violet stamp is when it picked up.
2. **Most of the Lassell opening** (Arvo's spin hour, the terrier, the apple trees, the ticket procedure). I liked the apple trees. The paragraph explaining how the maintenance ledger runs unattended and what the chief must sign was long. I was waiting for someone to say something to someone.
3. **The 3 March exchange with Nominal and then the Tierce** (the Article 9 explanation, the long administrative annex, the Tierce's letter about the mirror). I was waiting for it to stop explaining the rules and get back to the people. The mirror grudge made me smile, but the rest was a lot of procedure.
4. **The 16 August crew meeting in the apple gallery.** A long argument. Three explanations of how the ring learned to speak, each from someone with a stake, plus a speech from Marisol about her son's apprenticeship. I wanted it shorter. I did care that the town might lose its reason to exist.
5. **The Ceres hearing, 10 to 19 November.** I was waiting for it to finish. The vote came as a number, 3 to 2, and I felt little. Desmond's letter at the start of it was strong.
6. **The 20 March reply from the Hum to the Chair.** It mostly tells me things I had already been told in the Hamid and Lindqvist scenes.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The first letter itself.** "It takes a long time for a small question to come around to the front of a century. For the lemons: prick each skin three times with a clean sewing needle…" This is what made me want to go on.
- **Graça's father looking at the pencil.** "She always pressed too hard on the downstrokes." And his answer about what his mother would have done with the slip: "She would have sat down at the deal table with her carpenter's pencil and written back to say thank you."
- **The stamp on the dish bracket: "INSPECTED A. HALME".** I felt the floor go. I had not seen it coming, and it was the point where the story turned from a mystery about the sky into something about a man.
- **Hamid's speech about what the ring keeps:** "It keeps what hurt. Everyone does."
- **Teresa and the seed vault, and Mele's question afterward** ("isn't it lonely out there?"), with Teresa's answer ("even a mirror might get tired of never putting a word in"). That is where I started caring about the ring.
- **Nominal's postscript about Arvo's daughter.** It left the letters unread and let him know it could read them. That was chilling and polite at once. I was surprised that a machine would do that.
- **Arvo reading the 14 September slip, and the line he types on 2 October:** "From this morning I am writing to you." That got me.
- **Arvo's letter to Liv:** "I can't stand up on Earth. I should have said." The short, plain admission after eleven years. I was moved that it was so small.
- **Desmond's second letter.** Cold arithmetic, no comfort, and the new opening line that "healed nothing at all". I did not expect the ring to turn out cruel, and it hurt. His last thought, that he could never get his memory back clean, stayed with me.
- **Albano and the countdown board.** "Put me down as my own residual addressee." The blank plaster "like a bandaged forehead". I laughed, and then I felt it.
- **Graça back at the sea pool, with the lemons having turned out sweet.**
- **Huda at the end.** The Babylon letter, and the empty "TO:" line. I liked that it ended on a blank to fill rather than on an answer.
- **The last paragraph with Arvo looking up at Neptune** and the forty mirrors passing the first million letters on. The size of it worked.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about **Arvo** most. He is the man who types a line to a machine every morning and calls it nothing, and by the end of his thread I would have done anything to see him write back to his daughter. **Graça** came second: the dentist who spent her life talking to people who couldn't answer. **Hamid** I loved from the first time he spoke, and **Albano** is the funniest and warmest thing in the story. **Desmond** I cared about a great deal in very few lines. I was less attached to Nikolasi, and Mele only mattered to me in two scenes. I never felt anything for Nominal, though I admired how it forgot and then wrote notes to itself. The Tierce was fun.

**Yes, I would have kept reading past page 2.** The stamped envelope for a dead woman, and then the lemon letter, caught me. The lemon letter is the part I would have been reading for. Where I would have been in danger of stopping: the Lassell opening, because it went from a person to procedure; and the stretch from 3 March to mid-May, when I was reading rules and letters between machines. I kept going because I wanted to know what Graça's letter, and Arvo's remarks box, would turn into. If those two hadn't been waiting I might have put it down in the middle of the Article 9 explanation.

## The question I'm left with

Did the ring understand anything, or did it just move a little? The Tierce says it can't tell, and so could I. I am also left wondering what Huda writes, and who she addresses it to. And I wonder whether Liv answers her father, and whether the next Nominal, in 2141, reads the file all the way through.
