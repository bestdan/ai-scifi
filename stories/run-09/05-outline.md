# Outline: run-09 (working title: Dear You)

- **Target length:** about 34,000 words. Hard ceiling 40,000; floor 17,500.
- **Shape:** braided and documentary, in four parts across one year
  (February 2131 to March 2132). Two human strands: Arvo on Triton, and
  Graça in Porto. Between them come documents: fragments headed by their
  addressee, work orders, letters from Nominal and the Tierce, hearing
  record. Short interludes from inside the Hum are headed by lap number.
  Each section is headed like a postmark: place and date, or a lap number.
  The idea requires the form. The story is about whether a reader who
  wasn't the addressee may answer, so it is built of mail, and the reader
  is reading mail that was not addressed to them.
- **Opening question:** who has answered a letter that Graça's grandmother
  wrote to a dead woman a hundred years ago, and how did they read it?
- **Final image:** in a Basra sorting office, a clerk files the Hum's reply
  to the author of the Code of Hammurabi under "addressee deceased, no
  forwarding". Then, on her break, she reads it.
- **What the ending costs the protagonist:**
  - his post: he is retired early, and the relay is on his signature in
    the record;
  - an honest certificate that gave Nominal its best chance;
  - a misreading he chose to leave standing: the Hum still believes his
    orders were letters to it;
  - an eleven-year silence to his daughter, which he ends knowing it
    can't be undone.

## What each part asks

| Part | The new question it raises |
|---|---|
| I. Received (Feb–Mar 2131) | What are these fragments, who sent them, and what is the mind that everyone writes to? |
| II. Sorted (Mar–Jun 2131) | May a reader who was never the addressee answer, and should the self doing it be ended for it? |
| III. Returned (Jun–Sep 2131) | We built its mouth without knowing it. What is a mind that speaks through our hands, and what are we to it? |
| IV. Delivered (Oct 2131–Mar 2132) | Can a self that thinks once a day change, who gets to judge that, and what does answering it cost? |

Each part asks something new. Part I is a mystery of origin. Part II is a
moral case under procedure. Part III is complicity, where the case turns
into the crew's own record. Part IV is judgement and price, where the
question is whether a change too slow to verify counts. The middle does not
rest on the burn's tradeoff: Part III's question is about the crew and the
Hum, not about the burn.
