# Stage 5 critique: Chiang lens

Blind spots: I under-weight momentum. Scenes 7 to 12 are low-conflict, and I may call that fine when it drags. Banks and Asimov should judge pace and clue fairness.

The parts do each raise a new question. Part IV's "can it change" is the nearest to a repeat of Part II's "should it end", but the new evidence (the counterexample, the answered orders) makes it a different question. I'm not tagging it.

1. [must-fix] **Graça's answer happens offstage, so the idea has no cost on her side.** Scene 11 ends with her decision, scene 18 receives "she wasn't writing to you", and between them Graça has no scene. The idea is that answering a reader who wasn't the addressee costs something. Her cost is exposing Leonor's private 2031 letter, and the family argument at the kitchen table doesn't settle it. Add her letter to the Hum as a document in Part III, in her own words. It should quote Leonor, and she should hesitate over whether to. Then scene 20's testimony has a price behind it.

2. [must-fix] **Scene 21 is overloaded and states the cost.** The Tierce's letter, the lapse, the ruling, the retirement, Nominal's 2141 refiling and the flood all fit in 1,500 words. Arvo's retirement and his signature on the record are listed as a result, not enacted. Give the Tierce's letter its own scene. Put the ruling and Arvo's retirement in a second, short scene where he clears his desk or signs off. That spares scene 12, whose weight can be moved. Let the stripped relay be the first thing he does after the ruling, so that his certificate costs him on the page.

3. [should] **Scene 12 is the unnecessary one.** Nominal's weekly amnesia is already planted in 7, and Liv's wound is planted in 7 and paid in 23. Fold "it will not read Liv's letters to him" into 7, put the file line there too, and cut 12. Part II then moves from 11 straight to Hamid's timing, which shortens the middle.

4. [should] **The form states the idea but doesn't enact it.** The story says it is mail not addressed to the reader. A light device: give each document a routing line (received, forwarded by, lag), and let the line change when the reader gets one. Early fragments carry "Delivered to: [addressee]". The fragment to Arvo (17) carries his name. The last document, in Basra, carries "addressee deceased, no forwarding" with nothing under it, so the reader is the one who holds it. That would carry the idea without a speech.

5. [should] **Scene 24's ending answers too neatly.** The clerk reading on her break answers "may a non-addressee read?" with yes. But the story's question is about *answering*, and reading was never in doubt. Have her read it and then do something small that leaves answering open: tuck a note with it, or put it back and start a card for the Basra office that nobody will see. Keep the Hum's late answer and its mistake ahead of her, so the last image sits under the circularity, not under reassurance.

Also, scene 3 introduces Arvo, Lassell, Hamid, Bea, the Tierce, trim and the chord in 2,000 words, which is more names than it anchors. Hold the Tierce and the chord until scene 5 or 7, and keep Bea's "station 23's ear" joke as the one plant.
