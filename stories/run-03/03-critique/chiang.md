# Chiang lens: stage 3 critique, run-03

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens is low on action and plot momentum and can
over-prize the measured and the polished. It leans toward premises that are
one clean question. It is the wrong lens to judge crisis pacing (A) or
whether a village vote has comic and social texture (B). Banks and Vinge
should push back there.

## A. The Witness

**Question:** what is authority worth when it rests on understanding nothing?
The law trusts the father because he cannot follow her, and so cannot be
captured by her. That is a real question about testimony, and it is the
premise's best idea. **Feeling from concept:** partly. The father's perjury
grows from the witness law. The grid crisis does not grow from anything; it
is a ticking clock bolted on. **Form:** a hearing could carry it, with her
three-moves-ahead model of his lie in brackets. Plain third-person
crisis-thriller wastes the idea. **Likely failure:** the load models and the
board politics eat the 4,000 words, and the father turns into the wise
elder the synthesis itself warns of. **First page:** yes. A fourteen-year-old
chairing a board while her father sits silent is immediately about love and
embarrassment.

## B. The Mouth

**Question:** who owns an idea once someone else has made it believable?
Joon's plan is true, and Mi-rae's jokes make it trusted. Is the truth the
farmers accept still his? **Feeling from concept:** yes, strongly. Her
additions, "our mother used to say", are both the theft and the gift, and
that doubleness is the story. **Form:** alternate Joon's plan as written
with Mi-rae's speech as spoken, so the reader sees what the mouth added.
**Likely failure:** the vote decides it, and the wrong ending is Joon speaking
and the farmers cheering or failing; either answers too neatly. The Korean
terms also need Job-test discipline. **First page:** yes, the pair carries
it. One unnecessary scene: any explanation of the legal-head law.

## C. Foreseeable

**Question:** is a person who can be predicted nine times in ten
responsible for what she does? And the inversion that makes it humane: her
mother wants to be blamed, because blame is the proof she chose.
**Feeling from concept:** the best in the set. The mother's refusal of
excuse is the concept's emotional edge, not an addition. **Form:** the
prediction log is the form: bracketed forecasts of what her mother will say,
scored right or wrong, with the court's request arriving late as the same
document read the other way. **Likely failure:** the foreseeability hearing
turns into procedure, and the twist (the log shows Tamsin predicted the
shortcut) lands as a gotcha instead of a cost. Put the log's one hit in
domestic scenes first. 4,500 words is honest and fits. **First page:** yes, a
girl guessing her mother's next sentence at breakfast and being right.

## D. The Long Minority

**Question:** what does a rushed childhood cost, and who decides? Honest
and three-generational, but it carries two nova and a reveal, and the
interesting grief, the mother's unspoken years, is explained rather than
produced by the concept. **Form:** the logbook as documents could work.
**Likely failure:** at 6,000 words the legal machinery crowds out the
mother-daughter scene it exists for. It also delays the seed by law and leans
toward the elder-knowledge cluster, which the brief's seed note disfavours.
**First page:** weak; a petition is not a person.

## Ranking

1. **C, Foreseeable.** The idea produces the feeling, the form is native, and the seed holds on every page.
2. **B, The Mouth.** A real concept-born ache, but the vote invites a neat ending.
3. **A, The Witness.** Best mechanism; the plot is borrowed, not derived.
4. **D, The Long Minority.** Over-loaded, and softens the gap.

## Why C should win

Everything in Foreseeable follows from one fact: a child watches one person
for fifteen years and can finish her sentences. The seed's gap does not need
demonstrating by a competition or a crisis; it is in how the daughter knows
her mother better than her mother knows herself, and in what that knowledge
makes unbearable. The mother's request to be blamed is a thought experiment
about free will that a reader can feel in the kitchen, with no seminar. The
twist is the novum's own consequence, so it costs the daughter something she
cannot take back: to excuse her mother she must confess she saw it coming.
Guard it against two failures: keep the court to a few pages, and let the
ending leave open whether Tamsin's prediction of her mother was an insight
or a kind of contempt. Do not let the mother turn out to have predicted
Tamsin back; that would invert the seed.
