# Vinge lens: stage 3 critique, run-03

**Blind spots, stated:** I push futurity even where a story's power is human
scale, I read characters as functions, and I have no ear for plain prose. Where
I mark something "too familiar", Chiang and Banks may rightly say the
familiarity is what carries the feeling. The brief's floor is F2, so I judge
reach against F2 to F3 and don't ask for F4.

## A. The Witness

**Futurity:** F2, brushing F3. Parent becomes a sedating presence for a junior
superior, which is a real shift. **Too familiar:** the setting. A utility board,
a heat wave and a line-worker father are a present-day grid crisis with a
teenager in the chair. The "leave" motion is a standard fitness-for-office
hearing. **Viewpoint trick:** it holds, and it is the best in the run. She sees
three moves ahead of her father and the reader gets the lie before he tells it,
so her intelligence shows through prediction, not assertion. The danger is the
card's own: if his instinct to lie turns out to be right, he has out-judged her
and the seed inverts. **Push further:** make the law more alien. Board seats are
held in cohort pairs, and the witness is chosen by the council from strangers,
because a parent's bias is known. Then the father being the witness is an
exception she negotiated, and the lie becomes a betrayal of procedure as well
as a kindness.

## B. The Mouth

**Futurity:** F3. "Elder" is a legal status that follows mind, and a trade
exists to launder children's ideas through adult voices. That changes what
parent, child and elder mean. **Too familiar:** hill terraces, a cooperative
vote and a sister who raised him. This is a village-novel setting, and the
treated are visible only as a boy. Nothing shows what the cohort is doing
elsewhere. **Viewpoint trick:** it holds, and it is the cleanest network-effect
idea on the table: a bias in human learning routes knowledge through costume.
The risk is Mi-rae's additions, the jokes and "our mother used to say". If
those are why farmers comply, then the adult's craft carries the harvest.
**Push further:** show the mouth trade at scale. Mi-rae is one of thousands,
with a guild, rates and a rank, and Joon learns she is a mid-tier mouth who has
turned down better clients to stay with him. The sister's value is then legible
as a market, and she is not just warm.

## C. Foreseeable

**Futurity:** F3, and the only premise whose novum changes what responsibility
between kin is. **Too familiar:** a scaffolding foreman, an injured worker and a
court hearing. The legal machinery is Earth's, with one new form added. The
"nine in ten" accuracy is also modest. A mind 60 points up would model its
mother better than that. **Viewpoint trick:** it holds, because the log is the
trick. Bracketed predictions let the reader watch the gap in the text. The
twist, that she predicted the shortcut, is native to the novum. **Push further:**
raise the accuracy, and have courts rely on that. Her mother learns the log
holds predictions about her own future choices, still unspent, and the court
wants those too. The question stops being about one accident and becomes whether
anyone may be handed her own forecast.

## D. The Long Minority

**Futurity:** F3, nominally F4 in structure: three generations, two invented
institutions, and a ruling class that was itself rushed. Of the four, this is
the one with a recognizable society beyond the family. **Too familiar:** the
rail tunnel and the ministry petition are procedural furniture. The
grandmother's logbook is the elder-memory trope the audit flagged. **Viewpoint
trick:** it wobbles. The handover is delayed by law, so the gap has to be shown
by Oriel out-designing professionals on paper, and two institutions have to be
set up in 6,000 words. The audit's best push is the right one: the harm fell on
the untreated parents the cohort outranked. **Push further:** cut the
residential school. Keep the long minority and the buried harm, and let the
untreated grandparents' demotion be the whole reveal.

## Ranking

1. **C. Foreseeable.** Highest futurity, the gap is the novum, and the log puts
   the gap on the page.
2. **B. The Mouth.** The best institution, needing one move to scale.
3. **A. The Witness.** The best viewpoint mechanics, but the worldbuilding is the
   most present-day.
4. **D. The Long Minority.** The widest society, but it delays the seed and is
   overloaded.

**Why C should win.** The brief wants the treated to replace the adults they
outthink, and wants the story to show that outthinking, not report it. C is the
only premise where outthinking is what the story is made of. Tamsin does not
win an argument; she knows her mother's words before they are said, and the
form lets the reader see it. Every other premise has to stage a scene to show
the gap, and C builds it into the sentence. The twist also costs nothing
extra, because the log belongs to the novum. The warning from the other lenses
will be the courtroom register. The cure is to keep the court offstage and the
kitchen on: the story happens at the table where her mother asks her not to
file. If the panel prefers an F2 safety margin, B is the one to pick, and
should carry the guild push.
