# Le Guin lens: stage 4 critique of the bible, run-03

**Blind spots, stated.** I read for society, people and who pays. I'm weak on the arithmetic and the legal logic (leave those to Egan) and on pace. I may ask for more gathering than a 4,000 to 7,000 word story can hold. Weigh my asks against Banks and Egan.

1. **[must-fix] Nobody is raising or being raised, and nobody is old.**
   "Who raises the children" is answered in one line (untreated parents,
   outgrown at ten). The cast has a mother, a 54-year-old and children. There
   is no father (the "Cornish family on her father's side" is a name with no
   man behind it). There is no grandparent, no teacher, no school. Where did
   Tamsin and Kofi go to school, and who taught them for the two years before
   they outran the teacher? Suggestion: one father line (gone, dead, on a
   rig, whichever), and one scene detail of the old: Gaz's wife at the
   hospital with the leeks, or Lorraine's own mother. Put the school in a
   sentence: the primary teacher who kept order and learned the sums from the
   class.

2. **[must-fix] Kofi is the one who pays, and the bible never lets us see it.**
   Tamsin's statement moves blame off the firm and, by the Why row, the
   hearing exists to move it "onto Kofi's schedule and the firm's insurer".
   A thirteen-year-old who keeps pigeons and "doesn't know how to stand on a
   site" is the person the ending puts under the knife, and he has no scene
   after the collapse. Give him one: Kofi on the roof of the pigeon loft, or
   at the hospital, asking Tamsin whether the schedule was wrong. His cost is
   the third kind of "being held responsible" the ladder names. Without
   him the ending is a mother-daughter transaction and the world cancels out.

3. **[must-fix] The names belong to the wrong decade.** The bible says
   Lorraine is "a common English name for her generation". She was born in
   2019. Lorraine and Gary peaked half a century earlier. Tamsin (2044) and
   Kofi are plausible. Fix it with a reason in the page ("named for her
   gran") or choose names of a 2019 Sheffield birth for Lorraine and a 2005
   birth for Gaz, as the guide's "names come from the world" rule asks. Keep
   Gaz if he gets the nickname-since-school line; Lorraine needs the gran.

4. **[should] Lorraine drifts toward spokeswoman.** "Don't you dare say I
   couldn't help it" and the "held to account like a grown woman" want in the
   Premise row are the theme spoken aloud, and the guide bans nobody
   delivering the theme. Lorraine's love outside the plot (darts at the Fat
   Cat) is listed and has no scene. Suggestion: let her say it sideways, at
   the board, about a dart ("that one was mine, don't tell me it drifted"),
   and cut the explicit sentence or give it to someone who is lying.

5. **[should] Priya is the exposition function.** She is the one who tells
   Tamsin the law, the reason for it and the cost of "limited" (rows Premise
   and Why). That is the scientist who explains to the less informed.
   Tamsin, a checker at the office, knows the law better than her boss.
   Suggestion: let Priya be wrong or evasive about it (her flaw is that rules
   settle what's right), and put the plain explanation in Tamsin's narration
   as she fills in the form.

6. **[could] The structure is all dilemma, and the bible has the pieces of a
   gathering.** Dinner, darts, the viaduct, pigeons and leeks are a carrier
   bag the plot only borrows from. Suggestion: order the scenes so the
   Saturday at the Fat Cat sits before the hearing, with Tamsin's brackets on
   a game she can't win, so the reader loves the people being fined before
   the fine arrives. The ending's miss (Mum on the finished scaffold) then
   pays for a whole evening of watching her, not a sentence.

**Glossary.** The four terms pass the Job test. "Limited" is the strongest,
since the law's own cruelty is in the word, and Mum repeating it carries a
culture. "Statement" and "ties" are plain English with a technical sense and
need no more. No term is decoration.
