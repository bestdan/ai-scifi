# Watts critique: run-03 bible (Foreseeable)

**Blind spots, stated:** I underrate ritual, warmth and joy as forces, and I
push toward the cold conclusion. Let Le Guin pull back on the kitchen scenes.
I haven't checked the legal framing; that's Egan's and Lem's ground.

1. **[must-fix] The log's grading is the hole, and it's the best material in
   the bible.** Tamsin scores her own predictions, so "Tick" and "Miss" are
   hers to call. Hindsight bias is among the most replicated findings in the
   field (Fischhoff 1975): people misremember what they predicted once the
   outcome is known. A court would ask who timestamped the 3 June entry and
   whether it was edited. A treated mind that is good at models is not
   immune to motivated scoring. Suggestion: one early scene where Tamsin
   scores a borderline prediction generously, and the court scene where the
   other side asks about exactly that. The twist then rests on whether she
   trusts her own record.

2. **[must-fix] The ending flinches by treating a miss as proof of choice.**
   Mum surprises her; Tamsin logs "Miss" and is glad. But under the novum's
   own logic a miss is the model's ten percent: noise, or a gap in the data.
   It is not evidence of agency. Wegner and the Libet-style readiness-potential
   work say the feeling of choosing and the fact of causation come apart in
   anyone. Don't let the surprise vindicate Lorraine. Let Tamsin be unable to
   say whether it was a choice or an error bar, and glad anyway. That is the
   more unsettling truth and still a warm ending.

3. **[must-fix] The unpursued logic: Tamsin has chosen silence for years.**
   If she forecasts nine in ten and keeps the house rule, her log across six
   years must hold earlier foreseen corner-cuts she let pass. The court
   reading the whole log finds a pattern, not one lapse. Responsibility
   shifts toward the predictor, which is the real inversion of "Mum as
   weather." Put two or three smaller foreseen-and-unspoken entries in the
   log, planted in scene 1 or 2, so the hand-over costs Tamsin more than one
   week's guilt.

4. **[should] "Nine in ten" needs a denominator.** Habit is very predictable:
   Song et al. (Science, 2010) found about 93% predictability in human
   location. But that is dominated by routine. The tea being stewed is cheap.
   The shortcut is a rare, pressured choice, exactly where accuracy
   falls. A reader with a statistics degree will ask. Let Tamsin say that
   most of her hits are trivial and the number drops where it matters, and
   that she called the shortcut anyway. Also note Mum knows about the log,
   so her behaviour is already shaped by being watched (reactance); one line
   will cover it.

5. **[should] "A mind that forgets nothing" is wrong.** Memory is
   reconstructive for everyone, and faster learning is not photographic
   recall. Say "compresses a person into a model" instead. Also the rule
   "treated people are hard for each other to predict" has no stated
   selective or mechanistic reason; if habit modelling works, it works on
   peers. Either give a reason (they know they are modelled and vary on
   purpose) or cut it. Related: Kofi, a modeller of people, would build slack
   into a late-running crew's schedule. Rule 1 (no close data on that crew)
   covers him; state it where he first appears.

6. **[could] Priya's "rules settle the right thing" is a tidy foil.** A real
   sixteen-year-old boss, adolescent in impulse control even if treated, is
   more likely to be socially anxious about outranking former teachers than
   rigid. Let her bend a rule once, which the bible already gives her a
   reason to want avoided.
