# Egan lens: stage 4 critique of the bible

Blind spots: I read for logic and arithmetic, so I underweight the warmth of the kitchen scenes and may push the story toward a lecture. Let the First Reader and Chiang overrule me where a derivation would outrank a scene.

1. **[must-fix] The dates and ages don't agree.** Tuesday 3 June, Friday 6 June and Monday 9 June fit only 2059 (or 2025, 2031 and so on). In 2059, Mum, "born in 2019" and "forty-one", would be 39 or 40. In 2060, 3 June is a Thursday. Tamsin at fifteen in June 2059 was born between June 2043 and June 2044, but "everyone born after 2044 is treated". Pick one year and set the arithmetic as a traced row. Suggestion: set it in 2060, move the weekdays (3 June is Thursday, so shift to Thursday 5, Sunday 8, Wednesday 11 June, or pick other dates), make Mum born in 2019 and Tamsin born in 2045. Also make the years of the job fit the age: "foreman for twenty-two years" means foreman at nineteen, so say "scaffolder for twenty-two years".

2. **[must-fix] The firm's lawyer wants the wrong thing.** The Why row says the firm's lawyer asks for the statement because it "would move the blame onto Kofi's schedule and the firm's insurer". But Stakes says clearing Mum means the firm pays. The firm wants Mum guilty. The party who wants her cleared is Gaz's side, since the firm pays him. The party who wants the log is the court. Fix: the court asks, Gaz's lawyer wants the statement, and the firm's lawyer opposes it. Then scene 7 has real friction, and Gaz's loyalty to Mum is tested.

3. **[must-fix] A mind this fast would not just stay silent.** The house rule says a spoken prediction changes what Mum does. A treated fifteen-year-old who models Mum to 90% knows which question ("is Kofi's date still Monday?") would stop the shortcut without ever "telling a prediction". She could also tell Priya or Kofi. The story must say why she didn't, or the court and any reader will find the gap before Tamsin does. Best fix is to make it the point. In scene 6, she finds she had many ways to move Mum and used none. That is her real fault, and it is the "predicting is not understanding" error the bible names. It also makes her statement more than a confession.

4. **[must-fix] "Could have chosen otherwise" is falsified by the log itself.** The log has misses (one in ten), so a statement that Mum "couldn't have chosen otherwise" is not an honest reading of it. The court asking for the log is then not a trap; the dilemma was already half-answered. Take the next step. The misses are the evidence of Mum's agency, and the entry for 3 June needs a confidence figure beside it ("70%"), so the reader can see a 70% forecast is not a certainty. Put the log's overall hit rate and its misses into the ruling. The judge can say a person who surprises one time in ten is a person who chooses.

5. **[should] The premise of who signs off needs a reason.** In a world where treated teens run schedules, designs and checks, why does an untreated foreman still sign off the ties? One plain line answers it: law needs a signer who can be held responsible, and the law won't accept a person who can't be blamed, so the signature is the last job that belongs to the untreated. This also sets up the "limited" finding as the end of that last job. Add it to world-list item 4.

6. **[should] The new-ness of the law and the ladder are too settled.** The oldest treated child is about sixteen, so the whole treated workforce is five cohorts, and "courts began asking" can have precedent for about three years. Say so in a line ("the third year anyone had asked"). The punishment of Tamsin ("a term off checking for not reporting a risk she foresaw") makes a rule that treated children must report their forecasts about family. That contradicts the taboo on saying predictions aloud and would touch every treated household. The judge should reject that rule or the bible should name it as a failure to act on a plan she checked, not on a forecast about Mum. Million-copy question: every treated child now holds a log that could be demanded. One line in scene 7 can show the other parents' fear.
