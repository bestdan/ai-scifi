# First Reader report: draft 4

## Where I got lost

- Paragraph 3 ("Everyone born since 2043 is treated..."). I took "treated" and the injection in, but it arrived as a block of explanation before I had met anyone. I understood it. I just felt told.
- The stairwell paragraph ("The landings were cast into the concrete core at one end..."). I followed the shrinking concrete once I read it slowly. I never worked out why she is so pleased until later, when the story says it is a find. Worked out from Priya's "Good. Nice."
- The statement of the law, around "The form took four minutes." I followed the first two rules fine. The "catch" paragraph took me two goes in my head to see why clearing her also ends her signing. Worked out from the next paragraph, "They'd be one thing."
- "None of this falls on the treated: we rarely sign anything before sixteen, and nobody can predict us..." I didn't see why it was there. I never worked it out.
- The Tuesday entry. It is in the pad paragraph as "one entry in particular that I didn't write on the pad", and I didn't know which entry or why it was dangerous. I guessed it was about the ties, and that was confirmed at the reread. The scene of the night walk and "the Tuesday entry was going to be read out" confirmed it.
- Early on, the predicted-and-scored brackets confused me for a few lines. I worked out the system from the "That's my score" paragraph.
- Priya's "The office reports at fifty... You had it at eighty." I got it, but only after a beat. It is where the story's two rules meet.
- The judge's reasoning. I wasn't sure why she ruled "could" when the lawyer's own cross-examination made the log look unreliable. Then Tamsin says the judge was right for a thin reason, and that cleared it up a bit. I'm still not sure I trust it.

## Where I got bored

- The start. The first page and a half is the office, the desks, the treatment, the stairwell fault and the viaduct. I was waiting for a person. Priya and the cricket woke it up a little. It really started for me at "Mum got in at half six with brick dust in her eyebrows."
- The statement-of-law section is long and flat. I was waiting for her to decide.
- The first half of the hearing, the lawyer going through the log page by page. It went on a bit, though the "mark your own homework" line held me.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- "This tea's stewed," Mum said, and drank it. Then "Hit. 4,388 of 4,871." Funny, and it told me what the log is.
- Mum scoring Tamsin back and always giving herself the point.
- "She said that was what her dad used to say about fish."
- Kyle in the hospital with the leeks: "I'm telling her to tell you."
- "Kyle has pins in his leg because I didn't want a row at dinner." This is the line that hit hardest. I hadn't seen it coming and it turned everything over.
- The darts. She goes for the showing-off shot and points the dart at Tamsin: "Don't tell me it drifted." Delightful.
- The judge: "She never wrote any of it down, which I've always considered the kindest thing she did."
- Priya with the lanyard and the biscuit: "You'll have to let me have that one."
- The old maths teacher: "Took me till Thursday. Good bearing, though."
- Mum reading the whole log: "I'm very boring, aren't I."
- The ending on the scaffold, "Don't—" and then she stops. The refusal to score it. I was moved. The dip in every brick from the maker's hand also got me.

## Who I cared about, and would I have kept reading past page 2

Mum, first and most. Then Tamsin, once she did the wrong thing and said so plainly. Kyle and Priya next.

Past page 2: yes, but only just. I'd have kept going because of the first line about finding the fault and the cricket joke. The pull really came when Mum walked in. If the office section had run any longer I might have drifted.

## The question I'm left with

When Mum stopped at "Don't—", was she choosing? And a bigger one underneath: did Tamsin file "could" because it was true, or because it was the one answer that would also punish her?
