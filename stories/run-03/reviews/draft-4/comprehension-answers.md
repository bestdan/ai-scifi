# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A fifteen-year-old, Tamsin, has kept a log since she was nine of what her mother will do, and it is right most of the time. When her mother's scaffold accident puts her before a court, Tamsin has to say in writing whether her mother could have chosen otherwise, and she has to hand over the log. She says "could", because she had predicted the mistake and said nothing, and she wants the blame to be real.

2. Tamsin stands to lose her mother's trust and her mother's trade. If she writes "couldn't", Mum is cleared but recorded as limited and can never sign for a scaffold again. If she writes "could", Mum is fined and suspended and keeps her signature. If she wrote nothing, the court could order the log anyway. Her own risk is that the log shows she knew and kept quiet. If she failed, Mum would lose her whole career, or Tamsin would lose her mother and her own standing. In the end she lost a term off checking and a day and a half of Mum's silence.

3. The law came about because the treated took over schedules and designs, and the untreated got blamed for failing at systems they couldn't follow. The courts meant it as mercy, so they ask the person who knows the worker best, usually a treated child, whether the worker could have chosen otherwise. A "couldn't" clears the worker and shifts blame to the firm, but the court then treats the worker as limited. I'm not sure the makers saw that catch coming. The office's rule that any safety risk you know of must be reported was set up so that checkers act on what they know.

4. Late on I learned that Tamsin had predicted the one-set-of-ties mistake at eighty per cent. She wrote it the Tuesday evening, before it happened, and didn't ask "Both sets?" because she didn't want a row. That changed the dinner scene, where she kept "another question about Friday". I also learned that her fix for the stairwell was good but her old teacher had found it too, only two days later. It changed how I saw the second read as a kindly leftover job.

5. At the end Tamsin goes up the viaduct scaffold with Mum. She writes a prediction that Mum will tell her not to look down, and Mum says "Don't—" and stops. Tamsin can't tell if it was habit or choice, so she can't score it. She puts the phone away without writing anything. Before that, the judge found Mum responsible and not limited, with a fine of three thousand pounds and six months' suspension. Tamsin was put on a term of records work in the basement for not reporting the risk. The reasons: her log's misses showed Mum did otherwise about three times in ten on the starred entries, and the judge treated that as evidence.
