## The one thing
Keep the novelty where it is (the daughter's private forecast log becoming the court's evidence of a mother's will) and cut the two places where the draft drifts back to the map: the judge as untreated elder-sage, and the mother-reads-the-log catharsis. The distance is real; the drift is in the last quarter.

## Notes

1. **Prior art, overall (structural: none).** Nearest I know of: "What's Expected of Us" (Ted Chiang, Nature, 2005), confidence high, for free will under prediction, but it is a theme, not this setup. Also near: "The Minority Report" (P. K. Dick, 1956); Strawson, "Freedom and Resentment" (1962), the "weather" objective attitude, which the line "I'd been treating her like weather. You don't argue with weather" states almost by name. The hit/miss tally resembles forecaster calibration scoring; I know of no fiction that makes it a child's ledger on a parent. Confidence medium. Status: uncharted. The twist (the log shows she foresaw the shortcut and kept the house rule) I know no prior use of.
2. **The judge, scene 7 (structural, drift).** "My own mother could do this to me... She never wrote any of it down, which I've always considered the kindest thing she did." The reader loved it, and that is the warning: it is the elder-wisdom attractor (the untreated old woman who knows better), and the judge then does the thematic arithmetic aloud ("four hundred and ninety-one times it shows her doing something other than what was expected"). Push: keep the mother-line, drop the speech about what the count means; let Tamsin's "thin reason" paragraph be the only reading.
3. **Mum reads the log, "I'm very boring, aren't I" (structural, mild).** Warm and funny, but it is the reconciliation-by-laughter stock beat, and "There's no rule in our house now. There's nothing left for it to cover" closes the arc before the viaduct does. My stage 3 push was that Mum has been reading the log for years. Here she reads it once, afterwards. Consider letting one entry surprise Tamsin about what Mum already knew.
4. **Courtroom (line-level).** Kyle's lawyer: "Is that a woman choosing? Or is that the weather?" says the theme in the lawyer's mouth, a legal-thriller beat I flagged in stage 3. Tamsin's "It's both" is good; the question could be less tidy.
5. **Stock beats, small (line-level).** Priya's biscuit and "You'll have to let me have that one" is a gentle consolation callback; cricket-wrong-about-judges is a clean run, fine. The Wicker glass block, the viaduct as beloved object: charted comfort, not a problem.
6. **Cluster 9 (teen girl, tell-or-don't).** Mostly escaped: the choice is not whether to tell but whether to file, and the mother asks for the harder judgement. Protect that.

## What to protect
- The self-scored log and its generous hit: "I gave it to myself. Hit. 4,402 of 4,888." and the lawyer's "you mark it kindly when it suits you". This takes the novum off the map.
- "She scores me back sometimes, and she has never once got it right, and she always gives herself the point."
- The law as consequence: "If you couldn't choose this time, it can't trust you to choose next time", and the ruling "right for a thin reason".
- The unscored last prediction and "I couldn't tell you which of the two I'm glad of."
