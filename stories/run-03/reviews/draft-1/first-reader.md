# First Reader report: draft 1 ("Weather Report")

## Where I got lost

1. **Opening, paragraphs 2-3 ("Everyone born since 2043 is treated").** I didn't know what "treated" meant and nothing said. I guessed it was some enhancement that makes kids good at planning. I kept going on that guess. It was roughly confirmed a few pages on (the log, the court), but never spelled out. I never learned what the treatment is.
2. **The bracketed lines and "Hit. 4,388 of 4,871."** The first one ("She'll say the tea's stewed... Ninety-five.") stopped me. I assumed these were the narrator's guesses with a percentage, and that "Hit" meant she was right. I worked it out when the log was named two paragraphs later. For a few lines I wasn't sure whether the brackets were in her head or on paper.
3. **"Hit. 4,388 of 4,871" vs "Miss. 4,388 of 4,872".** I worked out that the first number is hits and the second is total. It took me a moment. I didn't try to check the sums.
4. **The Kofi/ties conversation at dinner.** I followed the gist (a scaffold, a rush, a thirteen-year-old planner). I wasn't sure what "ties" were until Mum explained them. That explanation was clear enough.
5. **The "limited" law paragraph (the long one after the form arrives).** I followed it, but it is a lot of rules at once. I had to hold "cleared, but limited" against "found negligent, but keeps her signature". I was confused for a moment about why being cleared was the bad outcome. It cleared up in the next few lines.
6. **"Eighty per cent" and "the eighty" at the hearing.** For a second I wasn't sure which entry was meant. It was the Tuesday one.
7. **Midway, the "Tamsin" mention in the notes.** That is outside the story, so ignore it. Inside the story I never saw the narrator's first name. The lawyer calls her "Miss Pike". It didn't bother me.

## Where I got bored

- **The opening page, from "There are forty desks" through the stairwell paragraph and Priya and the cricket.** I was waiting for something to happen to a person. The stairwell fault is explained carefully, but I had no stake in it. The cricket jokes are pleasant but I was waiting. It picks up at Mum coming home.
- **The paragraph about the bus driver's son and the boy on her floor.** It felt like information delivered, and the story stopped for it.
- **The hearing, the stretch where the lawyer goes through the log page by page.** Some of it was gripping, but the middle of it ran a bit long for me.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **"She got a pencil out of the drawer and turned the gas bill over and said, 'Draw it.'"** and the line about her dad and fish. Warm and funny, and it made Mum real.
- **"She scores me back sometimes, and she has never once got it right, and she always gives herself the point."** I laughed.
- **Mum fishing the letter out of the bin, reading the date, putting it back.** "Second of July. Just so I know." This got me.
- **"I was glad of the rule for the first time in five years, and I knew while I was being glad that it was the wrong thing to be glad of."** A sharp, honest moment.
- **The darts: "That one was mine. Don't tell me it drifted."** Delightful.
- **Kyle in hospital talking about the leeks, and later "She taught me to tie a tube at the top of a chimney".** Lovely.
- **The Tuesday entry shown in full, and the realisation "I'd been treating her like weather. You don't argue with weather. You write it down."** The surprise that she had predicted the shortcut and said nothing landed hard. I had half suspected it from "I'll come to it", but seeing it was still a punch.
- **The judge: "My own mother could do this to me... She never wrote any of it down, which I've always considered the kindest thing she did."** I loved this.
- **Mum reading the whole log: "I'm very boring, aren't I," and laughing into the oilcloth.** Moving and funny.
- **The ending on the viaduct: "Don't—" and she stops.** And the narrator saying she can't tell which of the two she's glad of. This is the best part. It left me with a lump.

## Who I cared about, and would I have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Mum most, then the narrator, then Kyle. I cared about Mum from the first dinner scene on. The narrator I came to care about as she carried the secret.

Past page 2: yes, but only just. The first page and a half is the weakest. I was reading politely, and the stairwell and the office didn't grab me. What kept me was the line "I wrote an entry in my log about Friday. I'll come to it", which made me want to know. Once Mum walks in with brick dust in her eyebrows I was in for good.

## The question I'm left with

At the end: did Mum stop herself on purpose, or did the wind take the word? The narrator says she can't tell, and neither can I. I'm also left wondering whether the narrator will ever tell Mum what she was glad of.
