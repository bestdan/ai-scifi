## The one thing

Make the decision to hand over the log a forced move, or make it visibly a choice. The cost the story charges (the public log, the term off checking, the end of the house rule) rests on it. At the moment Tamsin's own text undercuts it. The letter asks for "any written predictions supporting it", and a statement that Mum *could* have chosen otherwise has no predictions supporting it. Scene 5 says "Either way, the letter wanted the log", but the letter never says that. A reader who thinks like Tamsin will find an exit she doesn't use. State the rule on the page. For example, the court demands the whole log whichever way the child answers, so it can test her word.

## Notes

1. **Scene 5 (the pad) and scene 7.** This is the fair-play hole above. It also changes the ending's meaning. If attaching the log was optional, "I filed ... and attached the log, all of it" is a confession. If it was compelled, it is a sacrifice she didn't choose. Pick one and say which. (structural)
2. **Scene 2 against scene 6.** At dinner Tamsin asks "What do the ties do?", "Can't Kofi sign it off later?" and "So what will you do?" She is already asking questions that evening, and the scene is good for it. Then scene 6 says "I never thought of either" and "I'd stopped thinking of her as someone you can say things to." The reader can answer the question before she does, which is the fair-play pleasure I like. But the line overclaims, and the dinner contradicts it. Have her notice that she asked three questions and not the fourth. (line-level)
3. **Where the plot needs her stupid.** A fifteen-year-old who finds a 2090 landing crack in a minute scores a half-set of ties at height at 80% and doesn't weigh the harm. The log records likelihood only. One sentence in scene 6, such as "I'd put the shortcut at eighty and never wrote down what the shortcut could cost", turns the stupidity into a rule of the log. It is cheap and it keeps her clever. (line-level)
4. **Scene 7, "On the entries the witness marked as important".** Nothing earlier says she marks entries by importance. The "nearer seven" in scene 2 is her estimate, not a column in the log. The judge's decisive statistic rests on an unplanted fact. Plant one line in scene 2 or 3, for example a star on the entries that matter. (structural, small)
5. **Checkers' duty to report (corridor, scene 7).** "Checkers have to report any safety risk they know of" first appears here, as the penalty. It was never planted. Put it in scene 1 beside the end desks. (line-level)
6. **Scene 4, the form against the letter.** A form arrives in Tamsin's work inbox on Monday and "the letter" arrives on Friday. Both are the statement request. Say once that the form is the notice and the letter the summons, or cut one. (line-level)
7. **"Treated" and the tally.** The first reader never learned what the treatment is. Scene 1 should say in a clause that it is given before birth and makes children learn several times faster. The first bracket-and-"Hit. 4,388 of 4,871" also needs half a line saying hits over total. (line-level)
8. **The scaffold law.** A foreman who signed a sheet she knew was incomplete reads, to a logician, as plainly choosing. The reader may wonder why anyone expected "couldn't". The firm's lawyer says this, but the 'mercy' standard needs one sentence on what would count as couldn't, such as schedule pressure the worker can't refuse. (structural, small)

## What to protect

- The 3 June entry beside the shed-roof and darts-drive entries. The pattern of silence is the clean puzzle.
- "I had been treating her like weather. You don't argue with weather. You write it down." It states the novum's cost in plain words.
- The judge's counting and "four hundred and ninety-one times", and Tamsin's admission that the ruling is right for a thin reason. The law's non sequitur is left unrepaired, which is correct.
- "Draw it" with the gas bill.
- Mum fishing the letter out of the bin: "Second of July. Just so I know."
- The tally arithmetic: 4,922 minus 4,431 is 491. It checks, and I checked it.

## Blind spot disclosure

I treat the log as a ruleset and the ending as an edge case, so I may be over-pressing the disclosure mechanics while the first reader, who was moved, didn't care. I can't judge Mum's warmth or the viaduct. I also want the final "Don't—" to be solvable, and it is deliberately not. Le Guin and Lem should overrule me if my notes 2 and 3 make Tamsin more rational at the cost of the shame that carries the story.
