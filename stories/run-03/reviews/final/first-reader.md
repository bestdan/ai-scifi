# First Reader report: Weather Report (final.md)

## Where I got lost

- Paragraph 3 ("Everyone born since 2043 is treated..."). I took in the injection and the "cleverer than parents" idea fine, but it arrived as a block of explanation before I had a reason to care. I understood it, but I was reading it as homework.
- "Concrete goes on shrinking... steel doesn't" paragraph (the stairwell). I followed it because it was explained step by step. The line "I can set that out now in a paragraph" told me the narrator was explaining it to me, which helped.
- The Kyle's-lawyer exchange at the hearing. At first I didn't see why Kyle's lawyer wanted Mum cleared and then attacked Tamsin's log. I worked it out a couple of lines later from "that put the fault on the firm. That made him my problem." Then it made sense: he wanted "couldn't", so he was undermining her "could".
- The law section ("The treated took over the schedules..."). I was lost for a moment on which answer was worse for Mum: "couldn't" clears her but ends her signing, "could" fines her. I worked it out from the paragraph that says the two are "one thing", and again from the pad scene later.
- The nine-lines statement said "predicted her for six" years, and elsewhere the log "since I was nine". I did the sum (15 minus 9 is 6) and it was fine.
- Mum's early line "Draw it." and the "Miss" for the pasta entry: I wasn't sure at first why that was a Miss. Then I saw she did ask what's for pudding... actually she didn't; she drew. I worked it out: she did something other than the prediction.
- "The office reports at fifty... You had it at eighty." I got there by connecting it to Priya's first-rule speech earlier.
- "She'll put it in the bin" and then she fished it out: I read that as Hit, and the scoring held. No problem.

## Where I got bored

- The first page and a half, the office and the explanation of the treatment (paragraphs 2 to 4, the forty desks, the teachers on the end desks with biscuits). I was waiting for something to happen. The stairwell fault was interesting in a quiet way, but I didn't yet know what the story was about.
- The Priya cricket jokes ran a little long for me on the second time. They were warm, but I wanted to get back to Mum.
- The law explanation in short paragraphs was a little dry, though I wanted to know it.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- "This tea's stewed," Mum said, and drank it. / Hit. The log as a joke about tea, and then the numbers going up. I laughed.
- Mum taking the last garlic bread and scoring herself the point on a prediction I'd never made: "she has never once got it right, and she always gives herself the point."
- The rule in the house, and Mum turning down the foreman's job at Hollis's "to show me she could."
- Kyle and the leeks: "Tell Frey the leeks are fine." / "I'm here." / "I'm telling her to tell you." Lovely.
- Kofi in his school blazer holding the pigeon feed he forgot to put down. "I don't know you at all. I've known you a year." That one got me.
- The moment Mum nearly asked whether I'd seen it coming, then asked for toast. And "glad of the rule... the wrong thing to be glad of."
- The darts: she goes for the showing-off shot and points the dart at me: "Don't tell me it drifted." I was delighted.
- "The log has a number for how likely a thing is. It has nowhere to put how bad." That one stopped me.
- The judge: "She never wrote any of it down, which I've always considered the kindest thing she did."
- Priya: "I didn't see it coming. You'll have to let me have that one."
- The ending on the scaffold: "Don't—" and then nothing, and Tamsin not being able to score it, and not writing anything. This moved me most.
- The brick with the dip where the maker's hand pressed it, "It isn't on the drawings."

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Mum most, then Tamsin, then Kyle and Kofi. Priya I liked a lot. I would have kept reading past page 2, but only just: the opening was calm and a bit explanatory, and what hooked me was the first scene with Mum and the tea, which comes shortly after the break. If the story had stayed in the office I might have drifted off. Once the log appeared I was in.

## The question I'm left with

When Mum stopped at "Don't—", was it habit or choice? And a bigger one: did Tamsin do the right thing by filing, or did she just do the thing that cost her the most? I also wondered whether Mum forgave her, since the salt scene says she didn't speak for a day and a half and then it was dropped. The "I'm very boring, aren't I" line felt like the answer, but I wasn't sure.
