# Ledger rows

| Row | The fact |
|---|---|
| Premise | Tamsin, treated, can predict her untreated mother about nine times in ten. After a scaffold her mother signed for collapses, Tamsin must decide whether to tell the court her mother couldn't have chosen otherwise, which would clear her mother but mark her as unable to choose, when her mother wants to be blamed. |
| Stakes | If Tamsin files a statement that her mother couldn't have chosen otherwise, Mum is cleared and the firm takes the blame, but Mum is recorded as limited and loses the right to sign for scaffolds, her trade of twenty-two years. If Tamsin doesn't, Mum faces a finding of negligence: a fine and six months off work. Filing means handing the court her log. |
| Why | After treated planners took over the schedules and designs, untreated workers kept being blamed for failing at systems they couldn't follow. From 2056 the courts, wanting mercy and wanting the best evidence of a person's mind, began asking that person's treated relative, who can predict them. But the law reasons that if a person couldn't have chosen otherwise, she can't be trusted to choose again, so "limited" bars her from safety work. |
| Twist | Tamsin's log shows that on Tuesday 3 June, at dinner, she predicted Mum would fix only one set of ties on the top two levels to meet Kofi's date, at eighty per cent. She kept the house rule and said nothing, as she had before, though she could have asked a question or had Kofi move the date. The statement means handing the log over and admitting she saw it coming. |
| Ending | Tamsin files a statement saying her mother could have chosen otherwise, and hands over the whole log, her own prediction included. In court Kyle's lawyer makes her admit she scored her own log, and the judge counts the misses as evidence that Freya chooses. Freya is found responsible: fined and suspended for six months, but not limited, so she keeps the right to sign. Tamsin is taken off checking for a term for not reporting a safety risk she foresaw. In the last scene, up the viaduct scaffold, Tamsin predicts Mum will tell her not to look down; Mum starts to say it and stops. Tamsin can't tell whether that was habit or a choice, and is glad anyway; she doesn't score it. |
