# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A fifteen-year-old girl, Tamsin, who has been logging predictions of her untreated mother's behaviour since she was nine, has to write a court statement on whether her mother could have chosen otherwise when a scaffold she signed off failed and injured a colleague. She files that her mother could have, with the whole log attached, and her mother is held responsible but keeps her right to sign.

2. Tamsin stands to lose her mother's career or her mother's trust. Saying "couldn't" clears her mother but records her as limited and bars her from ever signing for safety work again. Saying "could" means a fine and a suspension but she keeps her signature. Tamsin also risks her own standing: her log shows she predicted the mistake and said nothing, which costs her a term of demotion to the records room.

3. The courts introduced the rule from 2056 because the untreated were being blamed for failing at systems the treated designed. They meant it as mercy: ask the person who knows the worker's mind best. It backfires, because a "couldn't" gets the worker cleared but permanently labelled limited. I don't think anyone designed the trap on purpose; it came from trying to be fair.

4. Late on, I learned the Tuesday entry: Tamsin had predicted, at eighty, that her mother would fix only one set of ties, and had not asked the next question. That recoloured the earlier dinner scene where she said "So what will you do?" and Mum said "Get it done Friday". I'd read it as casual. Also the law section recoloured the bus-driver boy's story, which I'd heard as a passing anecdote.

5. At the end, Mum is fined three thousand pounds, suspended for six months, and not limited. Tamsin is moved to the records basement for a term because she knew of a safety risk and did not report it. Mum reads the whole log and calls herself boring. They climb the viaduct scaffold; Mum begins "Don't—" and stops, and Tamsin can't tell if it was habit or choice, so she can't score it. She puts her phone away without writing anything. The reason is that Tamsin stopped trying to turn her mother into a number.
