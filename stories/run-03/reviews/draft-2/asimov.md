## The one thing

Settle what the court does with the child's word. Scene 2 and scene 4 say the court "takes the child at her word": a "no" clears the worker, and the boy's father was cleared on it. At the hearing the judge instead counts the misses herself and rules on that, "Both are true, and it is still the evidence I have." Both can't be the rule. A reader who has the scene 4 steps will ask why the cross-examination of Tamsin matters, and why her "couldn't" would not simply have ended the matter. If the judge decides on the evidence, Tamsin's statement is only one input, and the dilemma loses its force.

## Notes

1. **Scene 4 ("the law takes the child at her word") against scene 7 (the judge's count).** This is the structural edge case. One repair keeps everything else: the court accepts a "no" because a "no" protects the worker, and tests a "yes" against the log. Say that in one line in scene 4, or have the judge say it in scene 7. Then her filing "could" is a choice the court is free to audit, and the miss count is the audit. (structural)
2. **Scene 5, "or wrote nothing".** The form says the log must come "if one exists". A logician sees the exit: she could delete it. She doesn't, and that is the point, but nobody says so. One clause in scene 5 closes it, for example that she never considered it. (line-level)
3. **Scenes 1 and 2, "I'll come to it" and "that I'll come back to".** The first reader got wary of the narrator and couldn't tell if "the entry about Friday" and the ties entry were the same. Call it "the Friday entry" both times. (line-level)
4. **Corridor, Priya.** The charge is that she "had known of one, in writing, at eighty per cent". Scene 1's rule says "any safety risk she knows of", and an 80% forecast is not knowing. The story has just argued that a prediction is not a fact, so a line from Priya ("I argued that eighty counts as knowing") would make the penalty a deliberate stretch, not a slip. (line-level)

## What to protect

- The tally, which checks: 4,922 minus 4,431 is 491, and the darts sequence 4,430 of 4,920, then 4,430 of 4,921, then 4,431 of 4,922 holds.
- "The log has a number for how likely a thing is. It has nowhere to put how bad." This is the rule of the novum, stated plainly.
- "I had asked." with "Both sets?". The reader can answer before she does.
- Concrete rule-demonstrations: "Somewhere around 2090 the fifth-floor landing would crack along its steel end", and the teacher's yellow note, "LANDING BEARING, QUERY?".
- "The judge was right for a thin reason, and I knew it sitting there." Leave the law unrepaired.

## Blind spot disclosure

I read the story as a ruleset, so I may be pressing note 1 harder than the reader needs. The first reader followed the law and wasn't lost on it. I can't judge Mum's warmth, the pub, or the viaduct, and I want "Don't—" to be solvable when it is deliberately not. Le Guin and Chiang should overrule me on any fix that makes Tamsin more tidy.
