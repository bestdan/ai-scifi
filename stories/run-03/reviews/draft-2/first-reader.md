# First Reader report: draft 2 ("Weather Report")

## Where I got lost

- **Paragraph 3 (the injection).** A lot of explanation at once: the injection, the birth year, school ending at eleven, and the rule about reporting risks. I followed it, but I was being told things, not shown them. I worked it out by reading on.
- **"I'll come to it" (end of the first scene) and "that I'll come back to" (second scene).** I didn't know what Friday or the entry was. I understood these were being held back from me, and that made me wary of the narrator. Cleared up much later, at the Tuesday entry.
- **The Tuesday and Friday dates.** The first scene says the stairwell was found on Tuesday 3 June, and the log entry "about Friday" was written that Tuesday. I wasn't sure at first whether it was the same entry as the ties. I worked it out when the ties entry came up late on.
- **The law paragraphs after the form arrives.** The steps were short and I followed them. The line "Clearing her and recording her as someone who can't choose were the same tick in the same box" took me a moment. I got it from the next paragraph.
- **The scaffold ties.** I had to take on trust that one set of ties was a real danger and not a technicality. Mum's explanation helped.
- **The darts.** The explanations were enough. I wasn't lost.

## Where I got bored

- **The first scene's middle (Priya and the cricket, then the maths teacher's yellow note).** Pleasant, but I was waiting for the mother or the problem to show up. The cricket joke came back later and paid off a little.
- **The long hearing, in the stretch where the lawyer goes through the log.** I was waiting for the ruling. It picked up with the judge's remark about her own mother.
- **The records-room exchange with the old teacher.** Short, so I wasn't bored, but I wasn't gripped either.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **"This tea's stewed," Mum said, and drank it. / Hit.** The log as a joke about tea, and then it turning into the thing that matters.
- **The garlic bread: "You're thinking I'll have the last bit of garlic bread. Hit."** Mum scoring her daughter and always giving herself the point. Warm and funny.
- **Mum: "That one was mine. Don't tell me it drifted."** at the darts, and the miss scored against Tamsin. I loved it.
- **"The log has a number for how likely a thing is. It has nowhere to put how bad."**
- **"I had asked. 'So what will you do?'"** I was surprised that she had asked and still stopped short of the next question.
- **Bev's hug, and Kyle with the leeks.** "Tell Frey the leeks are fine."
- **The judge: "She never wrote any of it down, which I've always considered the kindest thing she did."** This landed hard.
- **Mum reading the whole log: "I'm very boring, aren't I."**
- **The ending on the viaduct.** "Don't—" and the unscored guess. I was moved. I'd have wanted a firmer answer, but the not-knowing worked.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Mum most, then Tamsin. Mum comes alive in the kitchen scene, and I'd have kept reading for her. Kyle and the maths teacher I cared about a little. I'd have kept reading past page 2. The first page was the weakest part for me. The tea and the log hooked me, and the form with the mother's name on it made me need to know what she'd do.

## The question I'm left with

On the viaduct, did Mum stop because the wind took the word, or because she chose to stop? And is a person who can be predicted at ninety percent still choosing? The story leaves that open, and I wanted it open.
