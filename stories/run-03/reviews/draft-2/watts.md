## The one thing
Plant the observer effect on the fatal prediction itself. The story knows that Mum defies the log: she turned down Hollis's "to show me she could", and she takes the showing-off shot "Don't tell me it drifted". At the Tuesday dinner she says "Don't do that face, Tamsin. I can see you doing your weather." So Mum saw the prediction on Tamsin's face three days before she fixed one set of ties. Either that nudged her toward the shortcut, as the Hollis story says it would, or she did it in spite of being read. Both readings change what "could have chosen otherwise" means, and the court never hears either. One sentence in the scene 6 reread would do it: Tamsin notices she is the only reader of Mum who was in the room. Without it the story's tidiest causal chain has a hole its own premise should catch.

## Notes

1. **Line-level. Scene 7, the judge's count:** the misses are treated as samples of freedom. The log has a selection problem that scene 2 plants ("I only log what I can score afterwards"), and Kyle's lawyer names it. The misses also include Mum performing unpredictability for a daughter she knows is watching (garlic bread, the treble twenty). Tamsin's "thin reason" paragraph covers only misses-as-model-error. It never says hits don't show she was unfree. One clause, and cut "It could be the one time in ten I hadn't got enough of her yet", which half-explains the ruling's weakness.
2. **Line-level. Scene 7, Mum's testimony:** "The second set were in the van. I could see them from the top." Mum wants the blame, so her account of her own choosing is the least neutral evidence in the room. Tamsin knows this. Let her notice it, briefly.
3. **Line-level. Scene 1, the stairwell:** unfixed from my draft 1 note. Differential shortening is real, but a seven-storey block is a weak case and "for decades" overstates it. Shrinkage is mostly early. Make the block taller, or name creep. Keep the bearing.
4. **Line-level. Scene 3, the fall:** "A level on a scaffold is two metres" is right and the injury fits. No change needed.
5. **Line-level. The tally:** I checked it. 4,431 + 491 = 4,922, and 4,431/4,922 is 90.0 per cent. Nothing to fix.

## Blind spot disclosure
I push toward mechanism and hunt for hidden causes, so the observer-effect note may over-read what is a warm, deliberately light touch. The judge, the maths teacher and Bev may be doing work I undervalue. I did not check the legal procedure, and I have no standing on rhythm. Le Guin and Banks may rightly defend the viaduct and the biscuit as the story's meaning. I am not an engineer, so note 3 is a structural-mechanics guess.
