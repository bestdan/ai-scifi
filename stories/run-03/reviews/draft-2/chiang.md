## The one thing
The form is right: a log of bracketed predictions, scored Hit or Miss, so the reader watches freedom appear only as error. Draft 2 has the best proof of that in the story, "That one was mine. Don't tell me it drifted", and then the story explains it. Cut the narrator's glosses that tell the reader what the log means, and let the entries argue. The idea should arrive as the tally, not as commentary.

## Notes
1. **"I'll come to it" / "that I'll come back to" (scenes 1, 2).** Structural. The retrospective narrator ("I can set that out now in a paragraph") breaks the log's live present, and the cold reader said it made her "wary of the narrator". The revision plan kept this on purpose. I still think the Tuesday entry, entered in order and unremarked, would hurt more. If it stays, one mention is enough. Drop the scene 1 line, since the later one does the work.
2. **"He'd done the thing I do, which is to think a person is a set of numbers you haven't got yet." (Kofi)** Line-level. Kofi's "I don't know you at all" already shows it, and the narration then explains it. Cut the sentence.
3. **"The judge was right for a thin reason... It could be the one time in ten I hadn't got enough of her yet."** Line-level. This is the philosophical core, but "It doesn't prove she chose anything" is as far as it should go aloud. Consider ending the paragraph there.
4. **Gran's phone call (scene 7).** Line-level. It is the one unnecessary beat: it turns nothing and was kept as a checklist item. Cut it. The Kyle visit carries the pressure and can open the scene.
5. **"I'd been treating her like weather." / "the wrong thing to be glad of."** Line-level. Two confessions of the same fault. Keep the second, which is dramatised, and let the scene 6 version stop at "I didn't put it."
6. **First Reader's "I'd have wanted a firmer answer."** I disagree. The ending asks the question without answering it, and that is the point of the form.
