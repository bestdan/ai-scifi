# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. Tamsin, a fifteen-year-old who can predict her mother almost perfectly and keeps a scored log of it, is asked by a court whether her mother, a scaffolder, could have chosen otherwise when a scaffold failed and a colleague was hurt. She tells the truth, that her mother could have, and hands over the log, though a "couldn't" would have cleared her mother and cost her her career.

2. Tamsin stands to lose her mother's trust and her own standing as a checker, and she risks her mother being fined and suspended. If she said "couldn't", her mother would be cleared but permanently barred from signing for scaffolds. If she said "could" or said nothing, her mother would be fined and suspended but keep her trade. Filing also meant handing over the log, with the Tuesday entry showing she had seen the danger coming and said nothing.

3. As I understood it, the courts thought it unfair that untreated workers took the blame for systems they couldn't follow. So they asked the person who knew the worker best, usually a treated child, and called it mercy and evidence. The "limited" rule follows from taking the child at her word. I don't think the story says why the law was made to hurt the people it protects. It reads as an unintended consequence.

4. The big late reveal was the Tuesday log entry: she had predicted at eighty percent that her mother would fix only one set of ties, and she never asked "Both sets?" because asking would have felt like telling. That changed the earlier scenes. The "I'll come to it" hints, the rule against telling Mum predictions, and her relief at the rule all looked different. The rule hadn't protected her mother. It had let Tamsin avoid acting.

5. The judge found Mum responsible: a three thousand pound fine, six months' suspension, not limited. Tamsin was moved for a term to records in the basement because she had known of a risk and not reported it. Mum read the whole log. At the viaduct scaffold Tamsin predicted Mum would tell her not to look down, and Mum began "Don't—" and stopped. Tamsin doesn't know whether the wind cut her off or Mum chose to stop. She didn't score it.
