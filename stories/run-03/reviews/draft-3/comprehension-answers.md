# Comprehension answers (from memory)

1. Tamsin, a treated fifteen-year-old, has kept a log of predictions about her untreated mother for years. When her mother's shortcut on a scaffold gets a man hurt, Tamsin has to decide whether to tell a court her mother could have chosen otherwise, and the log she filed is what decides the case.

2. Tamsin stands to lose her mother's trade and standing, or to lose her own honesty. If she says "couldn't", her mother is cleared but recorded as limited and can never sign for a scaffold again. If she says "could", her mother is fined and suspended but keeps her signature. If she writes nothing, the court can order the log anyway. Tamsin herself risks being punished for having known, and she is: she gets a term in the records basement. She also risks her mother's trust, and her mother stops speaking to her for a day and a half.

3. As I understood it, the treated took over designs and schedules while the untreated did the work, so the untreated got blamed for systems they couldn't follow. The courts called that unfair and began asking the person who knew the worker best. They called it mercy and evidence. The catch, that a "couldn't" marks you as limited, seems like something they didn't intend or didn't mind. I'm not sure the story says which.

4. The Tuesday entry. I had no idea Tamsin had predicted the ties shortcut three days before it happened, at eighty per cent. Until then the log looked like a charming game about tea. After it, the rule that she never tells her mother a prediction looked like something that let her stay quiet. The "Both sets?" question she didn't ask also changed how I saw the earlier dinner scene.

5. The court ruled that Mum could have chosen otherwise: a fine of three thousand pounds, six months suspended, and not limited. The judge went by the log's numbers, though she noted Tamsin had marked it herself. Priya told Tamsin she was moved to records for a term, because she'd known of a risk in writing and hadn't reported it. Later Mum read the whole log. At the viaduct scaffold Tamsin predicted "Don't—" and Mum began it and stopped. Tamsin can't tell whether Mum stopped on her own or because of the log, and left it unscored.

6. Characters:
- Tamsin: narrator, fifteen, treated, checks building plans, keeps the log.
- Freya "Frey" Pike: her mum, untreated scaffolder and foreman, suspended after the accident.
- Priya Shah: sixteen, Tamsin's section boss, anxious, wrong about cricket.
- Kofi Mensah: thirteen, the firm's planner, keeps pigeons, set the one-day schedule.
- Kyle Whitlam: Mum's long-time crew member, fell and broke his leg, grows leeks.
- Bev: Kyle's wife.
- Tamsin's old maths teacher: does the second read, checks her work with a ruler and yellow notes. I don't remember his name, if he had one.
- The judge: untreated woman with half-moon glasses, retiring.
- Kyle's lawyer: clicks a pen, wanted Mum cleared.
- The firm's lawyer: from Leeds, wore a cardigan.
- Dad: left when Tamsin was six, lives in Cornwall, sends a puffin card.
- Gran: rings on Sunday.
- The boy on Tamsin's floor and his bus-driving dad: example of someone cleared and then limited.
