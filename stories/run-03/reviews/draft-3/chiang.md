## The one thing
The step from the Tuesday entry to the verdict is right: the idea becomes a tally. The weak stretch is the "Here it is in plain words, a step at a time" passage. The first reader called it "homework", and I agree. The log is the story's form, so let the law arrive in its terms where it can: a short run of entries scored Hit/Miss, or the boy's father's case told as one scored incident, so the court's reasoning shows up as a consequence. Keep the plain chain the gate needs. Cut the throat-clearing around it ("Here it is in plain words", "It took me four minutes, and the law behind it took me the rest of the morning"), and the treated-exemption paragraph can shrink to one sentence.

## Notes
1. **Hearing, Kyle's lawyer: "Is that a woman choosing? Or is that the weather?"** Line-level. This is the only place a character says the theme aloud, and the title already carries it. End on "At eighty per cent. And you're right about her nine times in ten." and let him let it sit.
2. **Scene 6, "I can set that out now in a paragraph" (scene 1), and "I'll never know which."** Structural, minor. The retrospective narrator is now the only break in the log's live present. One occurrence is enough, and scene 1's is the one that does less work.
3. **Ending: "I was glad anyway. I still am, and I couldn't tell you which of the two I'm glad of."** Line-level. "I've got no number for that. I could put her at fifty, and I'd be guessing" already closes the question. The gladness is a second ending to a question the form has answered. Stop at the fifty.
4. **Midnight at the viaduct (scene 6).** Line-level. "Up close the arches are taller than they look... full of shadow. I stood under it a long time" is mood, and it also pre-announces her decision ("I knew which word I was going to write"). The viaduct pays off better in the climb. Cut to two sentences.
5. **The unnecessary scene.** Sunday's Gran call is one line and harmless. If anything goes, it is the second pass of Priya's cricket gag at lunch. The reader found it "slightly longer than I needed".
6. **First Reader's "why did she file?"** I would not fix this. Tamsin's three motives (true, lesser harm, punishment) are the question the story should leave open. The ending deepens it without answering it.

## Blind spot disclosure
I cut for thematic economy and may undervalue what the comprehension gate needs said aloud. Where my note on the law passage conflicts with a Why or Premise row, the gate wins. I am low on plot momentum, so I have not judged the pace of the hearing against the cold reader's boredom with the opening.
