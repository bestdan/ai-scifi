## The one thing

Say why the child's word beats the worker's own. Mum tells the judge "The second set were in the van. I could see them from the top", and she says "I want it to be my fault". Under the rule scene 4 gives, a Tamsin "couldn't" is taken unchecked and clears her anyway, over Mum's confession. That is the engine of the whole dilemma, but the page never says it. A reader who is thinking along will ask why Mum's "I chose" doesn't settle the matter. One clause in scene 4 fixes it: the court puts the child's answer above the worker's, because the worker is the one person it does not trust to judge her own mind. It is also the best statement of the novum's cruelty.

## Notes

1. **Scene 4, "The court takes a 'couldn't' without checking it".** Same issue, from the other side. The form also demands the log "if one exists", so a "couldn't" is not quite taken unchecked. Say that the log goes in either way, and that the court checks it only against a "could". (structural)
2. **Scene 8, "The second read had been stopped the week of the hearing".** Nothing says why, and scene 2's rule is that the person who signs must be an adult who can be blamed. If the two untreated readers are in the basement, who signs the plans? Either give the reason in a clause (the office moved the signing to Priya at sixteen) or cut the stoppage. A rule is dropped without notice. (structural, small)
3. **Scene 1, dates.** The first reader could not place the year. Every date in the story depends on it. "That June" could carry "of 2059" once. (line-level)
4. **Scene 8, "put the phone back in my pocket".** The log has never been on a phone. Scene 2 has her writing it at the table and "opened the log under the table". One early word, "on my phone", plants it and removes the reader's stumble at the ending. (line-level)
5. **Scene 7, the judge's count.** The arithmetic holds: 4,922 minus 4,431 is 491. The starred "three times in ten" matches the seven-in-ten claim. The first reader still doubted it. A clerk's repeat of the figure would settle it, but this is optional. (line-level)

I checked the weekdays against 2059 (3 June Tuesday, 20 June Friday, 4 July Friday, 27 July Sunday) and they all hold. The tally never contradicts itself. The Tuesday entry is scored only on the ninth, as "an entry from the week before", so the unscored-until-resolved rule is kept.

## What to protect

- "The log has a number for how likely a thing is. It has nowhere to put how bad."
- "I had asked." with "Both sets?". A reader can answer before she does, and the plant is fair.
- "The judge was right for a thin reason, and I knew it sitting there. A miss in my log only proves my model of my mother was wrong that time." Leave the law unrepaired.
- The ties explanation at dinner: "One set holds it on a still day. It won't hold it in a blow."
- "Clearing her and recording her as limited wouldn't be two things the court did. They'd be one thing."
- "Priya: 'The office reports at fifty, Tamsin. You had it at eighty.'" This turns the earlier rule into a plain edge case.

## Blind spot disclosure

I read the story as a ruleset, so I press on mechanics where the reader wants Mum. The first reader followed the law, and the "reads like homework" complaint is hers, not mine. Chiang and Le Guin should overrule any fix of mine that makes the law-passage longer. I can't judge the viaduct or the ambiguity of "Don't—", and I want that one left unsolved.
