# Idea cards: egan

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** I let a derivation outrank the scene, and the
emotion can arrive flattened behind it. My cards lean toward lecture: the
drafter will need to bury the arithmetic in domestic detail. Accessibility
suffers, so the First Reader and Chiang should push on whether a cold reader
feels these. One more: the fixed seed keeps the main minds human, so this lens
reaches F3 here and no further; I have not faked F4.

## Card 1: The Long Plots

- **Novum:** Treated minds learn fast, but elapsed time can't be compressed.
  The one expertise the handover can't take is having watched something for
  forty years, so the untreated hold it.
- **Futurity:** F3. "Elder" stops meaning wiser and comes to mean the holder of
  duration, a different kind of kin.
- **How it works:** Learning speed scales with how much data there is to learn
  from. Text, simulation and lab work all compress. But a tree's growth, a
  bridge's fatigue, a marriage, a cohort's ageing all take the years they take.
- **First-order consequence:** By sixteen the treated have caught up on every
  field whose evidence is already written down.
- **Second-order consequence:** Their work stalls at the longitudinal data.
  Decades-long records, and the people who kept them, turn scarce.
- **Third-order consequence:** Untreated sixty-year-olds are courted, not
  pensioned off. A whole culture learns to respect patience it can't feel.
- **The story:** Iris is fifteen and treated, and she has just inherited an
  untreated woman's forty-year forest plot. The plot also logs the treated
  cohort's own health, hers included. Rivals want to model the remaining
  decades and publish now. She can model it well. She has to choose whether to
  wait.
- **Why this lens:** I took the speed of thought as the variable and looked for
  the one quantity it can't touch.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress, *Beggars in Spain* (enhanced children as
  successors); Keyes, *Flowers for Algernon*. Neither makes duration the
  scarce good.

## Card 2: The Plateau Cohort

- **Novum:** The treatment improved every year until it plateaued at +60. So a
  generation raised to be overtaken by the next cohort is now the first that
  won't be.
- **Futurity:** F3. Career, seniority and "elder" are redefined twice in one
  lifetime.
- **How it works:** Protocol refinements added about four points a year over
  fifteen cohorts, then the gains stopped at a biological ceiling. Each cohort
  outclassed the one before it, so a person's relative peak came early.
- **First-order consequence:** Leadership goes to the youngest competent
  people. A typical tenure lasts about six years before someone sharper
  arrives.
- **Second-order consequence:** Institutions build in handover by design.
  Being surpassed is the culture's central rite, with its own etiquette.
- **Third-order consequence:** At the plateau the rite breaks. Nobody under
  sixty knows how to hold a job for forty years.
- **The story:** Teodora is seventeen and from the plateau cohort, and she runs
  a river authority. Her mentor is nineteen, from the cohort just before hers,
  and has trained her all along to replace him. Now Teodora learns that no one
  is coming to replace her. The only people who know how to stay are her
  untreated grandparents, whom she was raised to think of as a stage before
  the beginning.
- **Why this lens:** I followed the rollout curve to its end and asked what
  its end does.
- **Suspected prior art:** Kress, *Beggars* trilogy, has a discrete
  Superbright successor generation. This card is close enough to watch, but it
  differs in kind: here the novum is a continuous gradient and its stopping.

## Card 3: Majority

- **Novum:** Cognitive maturity comes at nine. Puberty still comes at thirteen.
  The treated hit adolescence while already holding office, so their untreated
  parents' one remaining expertise is having gone through it.
- **Futurity:** F3. Parenthood changes: the parent guides a child through
  adolescence while the child is the parent's superior.
- **How it works:** The treatment speeds up how the brain wires itself for
  learning, but not the hormones. Law grants "majority" by examination rather
  than by age, so a treated child is legally an adult years before the body
  is.
- **First-order consequence:** Nine-to-twelve-year-olds fill civic posts.
- **Second-order consequence:** Puberty then hits sitting officials. Statutory
  adolescence leave appears, and so does a politics of who gets to invoke it
  against whom.
- **Third-order consequence:** Untreated adults are re-valued as people who
  have been through adolescence and come out the other side. Fathering becomes
  a kind of advisory job.
- **The story:** Nadia is fourteen and chairs the regional grid board. Her
  untreated father is a line worker who works under her authority. A rival
  moves to place her on adolescence leave during a crisis. Her father knows
  she is compromised and can say so. Nadia must decide whether to put herself
  on leave and hand the crisis to the rival.
- **Why this lens:** I decoupled two clocks that everyone treats as one clock.
- **Suspected prior art:** Card, *Ender's Game* (child commanders); Kress
  again. Neither treats puberty arriving in office as the mechanism.
