## A. The Unthinking

Nearest prior art: *Memento* (Nolan, 2000, film), confidence high. A man acts on
conclusions his past self left, without the reasons. Unchanged from the audit.
Also near: "Exhalation" (Chiang, 2008). The audit's note stands: I found no
fiction built on Bennett-style uncomputation of reasons, and the search this
round found only the physics (Landauer 1961, Bennett 1973), with no story. I
hold that at confidence medium-low, because it is the kind of idea an Egan or
Stross short story could already contain.

What the synthesis added: the son lending his own heat, and the cost to him of
keeping what he'd budgeted to forget, including this night. That is the audit's
own push. It adds no new prior art I can name. "A child must pay to know why a
parent decided" has no work I know of. The closest folk analogue is a parent
whose reasons are sealed, which is the "trust me" scene and not a novum.

New concern: the heat-lending turns the choice into a sacrifice-for-knowledge
trade, and that shape is charted at the level of plot (pay a price to learn a
hard truth). The thermodynamic setting keeps it off the map, but the plot
skeleton is stock.

Revised N: **4** (was 5). The mechanism is still off the map. I'm docking a
point because the audit's 5 rested on a low-confidence absence claim, and the
plot underneath is the familiar mother, son, one night, farewell.
Status: uncharted (confidence medium-low).

Attractor check: it is the core of **cluster 3a**, conclusions without reasons.
Five lenses independently produced "a belief whose origin can't be audited".
That is the model's pull and not the map's, and A is its purest instance. Cluster
7 (F3's four nouns) misses A, since A's F4 category is "the self as budget".
Cluster 5 (certifier) doesn't apply. It also hits the stage 2 stock skeleton
(a bereavement, a ritual, a quiet functionary), though the physics works against
that. Senna is on the edge of the Nordic register (cluster 8), a weak hit.
Verdict: the novum is distinctive. The convergence on "unexplained certainty" is
an attractor, and A's novelty rests on the mechanism and not on that image.

## B. The Keeper's Successor

Nearest prior art: the Swordholder in *Death's End* (Liu Cixin, 2010; English
2016), confidence high. Also near: Wallfacers in *The Dark Forest* (Liu, 2008;
English 2015); "Rejoined", *Star Trek: Deep Space Nine* (1995, TV); *Dr.
Strangelove* (1964, film). All from the audit.

New prior art the combination and the twist bring in:

- **Precedent autonomy, Ulysses contracts and the Dworkin/Dresser debate**
  (nonfiction: Ronald Dworkin, *Life's Dominion*, 1993, from memory; Rebecca
  Dresser's replies; confirmed by search this round). This is the sharpest
  neighbour. The whole question of whether a past self may bind a changed
  self (Dresser: "a new person") is the argument B stages, and "Ulysses bound
  to the mast" is its emblem. Confidence high that it overlaps the *question*.
  It isn't fiction, but the Cartographer's honesty rule counts the idea as
  charted.
- ***Flowers for Algernon*** (Daniel Keyes, 1959 story, 1966 novel), confidence
  high. A man writes progress reports to a future self whose capacities have
  changed, and what the earlier self leaves for the later one is the book's
  engine. A letter to a changed successor is not new.
- **"Mr. Hyde's Letter"** (Brad Berens, microfiction, Substack; found by
  search, not read by me beyond the summary). A personality changed by
  medication, and the duty of the new self to the old. Low-weight prior art, but
  it is the same letter-across-a-personality-change premise.
- Stevenson's *Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* (1886), where letters pass
  between versions of one man. Distant.
- *Memento* (2000, film), and Egan's "Learning to Be Me" (1990, from the audit).

Distance: B's own twist is the real novum. A clause that makes a letter to your
own successor count as *persuasion*, so that the former self isn't exempt, turns
the usual remedy (leave a note) into a violation. I know of no work that does
this. The task, "make the next Oyelaran someone who chooses rightly while
arguing for nothing", is a values-shaping problem under an anti-persuasion
clause, closer to the Dworkin/Dresser problem than to any story. I found nothing
that matches it. I rate the clause as the novelty and the premise around it as
charted. Confidence medium. I searched for the clause directly and found
nothing, but a search turning up nothing is weak evidence.

Revised N: **4** (unchanged). The combination was already 4 on the audit's
estimate. The clause holds that score, and the new prior art (Dworkin/Dresser,
*Algernon*) stops it going up.
Status: edge-of-map.

Attractor check: hits **cluster 3** (self discontinuous with its past) and
**cluster 4** (consent, commitment, thresholds), and borders on cluster 5
because Oyelaran's job is a functionary's, a person whose craft is being
unreadable. Cluster 3a is not hit: the issue here is what to value, not an
unexplained certainty. Nordic register: "Oyelaran" is not in it, which is a
point for B. So this is two clusters, but they are the audit's two big
"mechanism" clusters. The risk is being the second-most-typical story the model
tells: someone makes a promise across a change of self.

## C. The One Day

Nearest prior art: *Ubik* (Dick, 1969), confidence medium (the audit left the
"each waking uses up remaining time" detail unconfirmed, and I didn't confirm it
this round either). Also near: *Before I Go to Sleep* (S. J. Watson, 2011), which
the combination drops as a direct match. New prior art the combination brings
in, all from memory:

- ***Good Bye, Lenin!*** (Wolfgang Becker, 2003, film), confidence high. A son
  curates what a woken mother is told about the years she missed, and what he
  leaves out is the story. This is the nearest neighbour for the "someone has to
  tell him the years in a day, and what she leaves out decides it" engine. The
  audit missed it, and it is nearer than *Before I Go to Sleep*.
- **Rip Van Winkle** (Washington Irving, 1819) and *The Sleeper Awakes* (H. G.
  Wells, 1910; serialized 1899 as *When the Sleeper Wakes*), confidence high.
  Someone woken after years has to be told what happened. Waking to a changed
  world is on the charted-territory list.
- *Awakenings* (Sacks, 1973; film 1990), as in the audit: a single costly waking.
- The "wake me when..." request, a promise to be woken at an event, which is a
  stock device I can't pin to one work.

What survives: a waking that happens once and kills; a language with no word for
"dead" (in the Wintering card, not in this synthesis); a telling that decides how
the listener dies. The old audit scored the combination N4 because it removed
Spring Telling's direct match, but it traded that for *Good Bye, Lenin!* and the
whole Rip Van Winkle tradition.

Revised N: **3** (was est. 4).
Status: edge-of-map, close to charted.

Attractor check: the worst of the four, and the synthesis admits it. It sits in
**cluster 1** (the dead as continuing participants: the dying kept at the table,
inheritance through the dead via the boundary), **cluster 6** (the pastoral
valley, the boundary dispute, engineered torpor shared with Spring Telling),
and **cluster 8** (Marrit, in the register the audit named). "A wedding, a
grandfather and a boundary" is also the stock quiet bereavement-and-ritual
skeleton from the audit's third force. Three clusters out of eight. I'd treat
this as the model's default story with a good mechanism.

## D. Release

Nearest prior art: *Coco* (Pixar, 2017, film), confidence medium, as in the
audit. New prior art the twist brings in. A dead person asking to be released
is among the most charted things in fiction, so I list by family and not
exhaustively.

- **The unquiet dead who ask to be laid to rest.** Ghost stories and exorcism,
  *Hamlet* (Shakespeare, c. 1600), *Beloved* (Toni Morrison, 1987), confidence
  high. This is folk-level charted ground.
- **Uploaded or revived dead who want out.** *San Junipero* (*Black Mirror*, 2016,
  TV), confidence high that it concerns the choice to pass over from a hosted
  afterlife. The *Quantum Thief* trilogy (Rajaniemi, 2010 on), from memory, with
  gogols (copies of the dead, kept in servitude) that want to be ended. *Surface
  Detail* (Banks, 2010), from the audit. *Bridesicle* (Will McIntosh, 2009 story;
  the search returned it with a revival-ethics page, so I'm citing the title and
  author with confidence medium, and the year from memory). Ian McDonald's
  *Terminal Cafe* (1994, from the search), with the revived dead.
- **A Strange Horizons story** the search surfaced (URL slug "haja-hoje", page
  title "Size"; I did not read it, and I can't confirm its author or year). One
  snippet has revived people asking the narrator to stop the revival. Treat it
  as a possible neighbour at confidence low.
- Egan's *Permutation City* (1994, from memory), where a copy of a dead man
  has the right to be left alone.

What survives: the *mechanism* (k-of-n secret sharing of a person, with
quorum-by-geography so that emigration breaks it) and the *polarity* (leaving is
mercy, and the loving keepers are the antagonists). The audit's "I found no
fiction built on threshold secret-sharing of a person" still stands (low
confidence). But the twist itself, the dead asking to go, is the part with the
most prior art, and the mercy-versus-grief shape is *San Junipero*'s.

Revised N: **3** (was 4).
Status: edge-of-map. The mechanism keeps it off "charted". Take the twist away
and nothing remains but *Coco*.

Attractor check: **cluster 1** (the dead, literally; the "dead as continuing
participants" cluster, and D makes the dead the premise), **cluster 4** (k-of-n
is one of the three cards "literally built on a quorum"), and **cluster 8**
(Ilmi, Marjatta, pominki). Cluster 2 is not hit. The audit's own Caveat said
this card sits inside the dead-and-inheritance attractor, and the twist does not
leave it. It makes the card sit deeper, because "the dead speak and are heard" is
the attractor's centre. The Risks section already names the six-keeper cast.

## Ranking on novelty alone

1. **A. The Unthinking (N4).** The mechanism has no neighbour I can name; the
   prior art is a plot skeleton, not the premise.
2. **B. The Keeper's Successor (N4).** The clause is new; the surrounding
   question is argued in nonfiction (Dworkin/Dresser) and in *Algernon*. I rank
   it second because A's novum is wholly off the map, while B's is one clause on
   mapped ground.
3. **D. Release (N3).** The k-of-n mechanism is uncharted, but the twist is
   among the most charted in the genre.
4. **C. The One Day (N3).** It collects the most prior art (*Good Bye, Lenin!*,
   Rip Van Winkle, *Ubik*, *Awakenings*) and sits in three attractor clusters.

**Where I'd be wrong.** A vs B is a one-point call; a reader who weights B's clause
more heavily could reverse them. D vs C is close, too. I put D above C because
D has an uncharted mechanism and C does not. If the gate cares about attractor
risk as well as novelty, A drops nowhere (its cluster is the one that matters
least to the *plot*), and C and D should be considered for replacement.

## Pushes (one per premise, for the panel, if wanted)

- A: let the lent heat run both ways. The son, not Senna, discovers the reasoning,
  and what he can't do is tell her.
- B: let the successor receive no letter but one clause-compliant gift that
  persuades anyway, such as a changed schedule or a book left on the desk.
- C: Marrit herself has been told, for eleven years, a version the grandfather
  built so she would stay; her telling is the correction.
- D: Marjatta asks to be kept, and Ilmi's leaving is the cruelty. That
  reverses the reversal and takes D off *San Junipero*'s path.
