# Stage 3 critique: the Banks lens

This lens is built from Iain M. Banks's published work and essays. It is a lens, not the author, and it does not speak in his voice.

**Blind spots, stated up front.** This lens rewards scale, set pieces and a benevolent hegemon's dirty work, and it tends to want more worldbuilding than 2,500 words can hold. Short stories aren't its natural form. It may over-credit premises with room for an extra civilization, and under-credit quiet, domestic ones. Discount any "needs more scale" note accordingly. Defer to the Chiang and Asimov lenses on economy.

---

## A. The Unthinking

**Friction.** Real and internal. Senna's earlier self did something to her without consent, in good faith, and cannot be cross-examined. Good: the mother is both the benevolent power and the victim. The son's demand for a reason is a fair fight, since he is owed the reason and she may not be able to give it.

**Dirty work.** Past-Senna did it. She signed off on a prohibition and erased her warrant for it. Present-Senna must either enforce an order she can't audit or violate her own best judgment. The son's heat is the second price: he pays in memory for her answer.

**Wit.** Thin as written. The humour lives in bureaucracy: a ledger that records only verdicts is a civilization of people who keep receipts but no reasons. Lean on the household's small economies (who spends what to forget a quarrel) and let the thermodynamics be petty.

**Scale and intimacy.** Scale is the habitat's thermal budget, felt through one night's arithmetic. It fits. The risk is Landauer lectures.

**Enjoyable to inhabit?** Mostly, but a heat-starved habitat leans cold. It needs warmth and comedy on the page, or it tips grim.

---

## B. The Keeper's Successor

**Friction.** The best of the four. The treaty's own clause traps her, and the trap is fair. She has to shape a stranger without arguing. That is manipulation ethics at its sharpest, and the story must decide whether grooming a successor's values counts as persuasion.

**Dirty work.** Two polities that can't be trusted have outsourced trust to one unpersuadable woman. They did the dirty work of making her the thing they can't be. She does the rest: engineering a future self she will never meet. Who signed off is clear, and the story can ask whether she should have.

**Wit.** Strong, and structural. A person whose craft is being unpersuadable, escrowed by minds that can't keep promises, is comedy in the treaty's own voice. Let the escrow agents be fussy, courteous and absurdly exact. Name the polities something Banks-adjacent in spirit, which means silly, though not borrowed.

**Scale and intimacy.** Best match. Posthuman polities loom outside the frame, and the only thing on stage is one slow mind and her choice. That is the Outside Context Problem seen from the slowest seat. The scale can be felt in a single detail: what the polities do instead of speaking.

**Enjoyable to inhabit?** Yes. A treaty-keeper's craft is a pleasure to watch. The risk is two nova in 2,500 words. Cut hard.

---

## C. The One Day

**Friction.** Real but familiar. Brothers wake a man to settle a boundary, and the granddaughter decides what he hears. What she tells him decides who he dies as. Good, though the moral choice is more private than political.

**Dirty work.** The brothers do it, rather bluntly, and Marrit is left cleaning up. The tendance of eleven years is its own unpaid labour. No institution signed off. It is a family arrangement, which is truer but smaller.

**Wit.** Dry and domestic: a place set at table for the torpid, the unwed granddaughter. Gentle comedy is available and the warmest of the four. Not much bite.

**Scale and intimacy.** All intimacy. The valley is the whole world. Nothing vast is felt, only stated, and by this lens' own test that is a gap, though possibly an acceptable one for the form.

**Enjoyable to inhabit?** The most. It is also the nearest to the model-attractor cluster the audit flagged (the dead, the valley, the boundary vote). Charming, and likely to read as already written.

---

## D. Release

**Friction.** The reversal is nice: mercy is departure and the keepers are the villains. But the keepers are good people being corrected by a dead woman, and the conflict resolves when she wakes and says so. That is conflict dissolved by someone being reasonable, which the tone charter warns against.

**Dirty work.** The keepers do the harm, kindly. Ilmi does the leaving. Marjatta gets to forgive everyone from beyond the grave. Nobody pays much.

**Wit.** The sirp mechanics could be funny, such as a quorum that has to be bribed with food, but six keepers can't all be characters in 2,500 words.

**Scale and intimacy.** The light-lagged relay gives scale: a daughter goes where conversation takes years. It could land with one concrete detail. The cast size crowds out the intimacy.

**Enjoyable to inhabit?** Pleasant, close to *Coco* in shape, and the ending is a little too kind.

---

## Ranking

1. **B, The Keeper's Successor**
2. **A, The Unthinking**
3. **C, The One Day**
4. **D, Release**

**Why B should win.** B is the only premise where the novum itself forces a morally uncomfortable act and the story has both the scale and the joke. The treaty clause turns a letter, the kindest thing a person can do for her successor, into a violation, so the heroine's love for her future self is the crime. That is a moral problem with real teeth: she must decide whether to shape someone's values without ever arguing for them, and there are no stock villains in the way of it. The two posthuman polities supply the vastness. They are never seen from inside, only through the escrow's fastidious paperwork, which is where the wit lives. A holds the same shape of problem at lower comedic yield and with a cold setting. C is the one readers will enjoy most and remember least. D resolves its conflict by decree. B's risk is real (two nova, treaty mechanics crowding the person), but it is a risk of cutting, and cutting is a solved problem. The lenses that cut well should be given B. This lens knows its own tendency to add, and says so.
