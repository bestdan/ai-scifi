# Stage 3 critique: Vinge lens (calibration-01)

This critique uses the Singularitarian lens built from Vernor Vinge's fiction and essay. It is a lens, not the author, and it quotes no one. Its blind spots are plain prose, technothriller instincts, characters treated as functions, and a habit of pushing futurity even where a story's power is human-scale. It will tend to under-rate premise C for that reason. Le Guin and Chiang should push back on it.

## A. The Unthinking

**Futurity: F4.** The minds are software, run under a heat budget, and they discard their own reasons. That makes them discontinuous with their past selves. The viewpoint is a mind on our side of the horizon, only one physics-step away from us. The trick holds. The budget makes the unseen visible: a conclusion with no history is exactly "an effect without an interior".

**Still familiar:** a mother and a son at a launch window, who demand a reason and argue about trust. That is a present-day family quarrel in a thermodynamic costume, and it is the risk the premise itself names.

**Change:** let the ledger show that other citizens hold the same unreasoned conclusion about the boy. Several people unthought the same thing independently, and no one can ask why. Senna's private dilemma becomes a civic fact she can read but not explain, and the reader sees a mind-scale effect from the edge. The scene count and cast stay the same.

## B. The Keeper's Successor

**Futurity: F4, touching F5 at the edges.** The polities can rewrite their own values and are seen only through the clauses of their treaty. That is the best shape in the set: the posthumans are shown by their effects. The viewpoint is the slow mind, a baseline human whose craft is being unpersuadable, and it holds. Her rebase adds a second change of mind to the story, and the treaty clause that catches the letter is fair play.

**Still familiar:** the escrowed human as commitment device (the Swordholder), and the word "persuasion", which the story treats as a stable concept.

**Change:** the polities rewrote themselves between drafting and the date, so the clause was written by minds who no longer exist. What it means by "persuasion" has drifted, and Oyelaran must read it the way one reads an artifact from the past. This puts the unwriteable minds into a document and keeps the 2,500 words.

## C. The One Day

**Futurity: F3, no higher.** Death has become a household decision and the viewpoint is plainly human. The trick is a trick of feeling, not of perspective. Nothing here faces the horizon, which is what the lens finds thin, and what Le Guin and Chiang would defend as the point.

**Still familiar:** nearly everything. The valley, the grandfather, the wedding, and a boundary dispute settled by a dying elder are the audit's attractor cluster.

**Change:** in eleven years the valley's speech has moved. The word for "wintering" has shifted, and so has the arithmetic of who owns what. A day is too short to teach the grandfather the new language, so Marrit has to translate him into the present. He becomes a person left behind in realtime, and the telling becomes a problem of translation across a gap. That raises the story towards F3.5 without a new cast or a new setting.

## D. Release

**Futurity: F3 to F4.** A person split into threshold shares is not single and not continuous. That is F4 in structure, but the story treats it as a mourning custom and keeps the human register throughout. The viewpoint, Ilmi, is human and close to the reader. The trick holds, but it isn't aimed at anything strange.

**Still familiar:** the pominki gathering, the pull of leaving the place of the dead, and the *Coco* shape of "the dead live while remembered". Six keepers are also six functions.

**Change:** different quorums wake different Marjattas. Any five of eight shards give a person, but not the same person each time. Cut the cast to the quorum on the night. The keepers who refuse to let go then want a particular aunt, and Ilmi's departure removes more than one version of her.

## Ranking

1. **B, The Keeper's Successor**
2. **A, The Unthinking**
3. **D, Release**
4. **C, The One Day**

**Why B should win.** B is the only premise in which the main minds are beyond us and the story still works. The polities cannot be shown from inside, so they are shown by the one thing they need from a human: a promise someone else will keep. That is the Zones-of-Thought kind of workaround, a viewpoint trick that moves the story to the edge of the unknowable and keeps it writeable. The trick holds because Oyelaran is slow by design, and her craft is the story's mechanism rather than a character note. Her rebase makes the horizon personal as well: she is about to become a mind she cannot know, and the clause forbids her to write to it. The cost is two nova in a short length, and the fix above helps with that, because the changed polities are carried by the document and not by scenes. A is a close second and has the best physics. It risks being a human family drama inside a heat budget, whereas B puts the human on the far side of the problem. The lens is biased toward futurity, so the panel should weigh B's Yield against C's warmth before the gate.
