# Stage 5 critique: Chiang lens

This is a lens built from Chiang's published work and interviews. It is not Chiang and does not speak for him.

**Blind spots:** I undervalue momentum and conflict, and I can over-trust a measured, polished shape. Scenes 3 to 5 are quiet and talky, so Banks and Watts should judge whether the middle sags. I also favour structural fixes over incident, so weigh my cuts against pace.

**Short answers**

- *Does the erasing grow out of the idea?* Yes. A judge whose value is being unknown erases her own reasons so that her next arrives unpersuaded. The idea produces the act. The risk is staging, not concept (see note 2).
- *Unnecessary scene?* No scene is dead, but scenes 4 and 5 are in the wrong order, and scene 3 carries a load that belongs elsewhere.
- *Cost on the page?* Mostly. Bisi's cost is still stated rather than shown (note 3).
- *Does the ending deepen?* Yes, except for one authorial wink (note 5).

**Notes**

1. **[must-fix] Scenes 4 and 5 are in the wrong order.** In scene 4 she asks Pell "whether they knew", but she does not deduce the design until scene 5. She is asking before she has the suspicion. *Fix:* put the arithmetic first (new scene 4), including Bisi's thirty-two months and the eels, which she remembers rather than hears. Then Pell's ruling and his perfect non-answer follow as scene 5. The question now has weight, the non-answer lands on a live hypothesis, and the erasing follows straight from his ruling. This also lightens scene 3, which currently carries the letter, § 9, Bisi's offer, the thirty-two months and the eels in 450 words.

2. **[must-fix] Scene 6 has too many jobs.** It holds Ros's hesitation, the rival reading, the heron, Bisi's taboo-break and the first laugh in 500 words. The climax needs one centre. *Fix:* make it her laughing at his joke, the first sign that the self doing the erasing is leaving. Cut the either/or ("tells him a joke instead, or laughs"). Reduce Ros to one gesture, give Hanne's rival reading a single line, and keep the heron because it is the best beat. When Bisi asks what she thinks, she answers about something small (the heron), not about the stars or the polities.

3. **[should] Show Bisi's cost with an object.** "Any certainty that her next will want him" is stated in the outline, not dramatised. *Fix:* on the last night he cooks, and by now she cannot taste it. He knows she can't, and he serves it anyway. The taste thread from scene 1 pays off here, and nobody says a word about it.

4. **[could] Add a light formal device.** Head each scene with its day count, running down from nine, with gaps where the story skips. The last scene's heading is blank, a page with nothing on it. This enacts erasure and the interval without explaining either. Don't use the account integers for this, because they must stay unexplained.

5. **[could] Cut the wink in the final image.** "Perhaps not to someone who knew how to look" tilts the question toward her next's reading. *Fix:* give only the grooves in raking light, and then she squares the stack. The reader decides whether grooves count as argument, which is the question the story should end on.
