# Stage 5 critique: Banks lens

A lens built from Banks's published work, not the author, and not his voice.

**Blind spots:** I like scale, set pieces and jokes, and I'm not a natural short-story reader. Every fix below is a swap or a cut, never an addition. The target stays 2,500 words.

**Verdict:** the funnel is sound. The polities are known only through their prose, which is the right choice. But the scale is told, not felt, and the wit is thin outside Mr. Pell.

1. **[must-fix] Scale is stated, never seen.** Nothing in the outline makes the Disputed Volume or the polities physically present. Fix: in scene 2, Mina points at the Volume's star over the bakery roof. It is faint, and every adult in town has trained themselves not to look at it. Mina asks the forbidden question about that star specifically. A child's finger holds the whole stake. Pay for the ~60 words by cutting the bakery's counter business.

2. **[must-fix] The wit is Pell's alone, and the laugh in scene 6 is unearned.** She "laughs at his for the first time" only works if the reader has already seen a Bisi joke fail. Fix: put one bad Bisi joke in scene 1 (a boat pun, ~25 words), met with the keeper's blank face. Scene 6 then re-tells it, or its punchline, and it works. Also give the polities one funny line of account prose in scene 1, in place of the integer's flat introduction. The integer can still fall.

3. **[should] Reorder Pell's non-answer.** Her question "did they know?" lands before she has done the arithmetic, so his perfect non-answer answers nothing. Fix: keep the ruling in scene 4 (margins, the schedule definition). Cut her question there. Move it to the last 60 words of scene 5, as a note she writes and tears up, or as a line she plans to put to Pell and then doesn't. The dodge then confirms a hypothesis she holds, and the reader's arithmetic arrives first.

4. **[should] Complicity is missing, and the morality is too clean.** Nobody signed off on anything. The polities are blameless, because we are asked to forgive their size. Fix: in scene 5, she knows she noticed the odd tail at signing and let it go because she wanted the seat. That is her dirty work. It costs one sentence, and it undercuts her belief that she was chosen for her conscience.

5. **[could] Enjoyment.** Scenes 5 to 7 stack broken sleep, grief and erasure. Allowed, but the charter wants a world worth standing in. Fix: let the erasing night have food. Bisi cooks for the erasers, and Ros's bread is on the table. Hanne eats while she works. Trim scene 5 by 40 words to pay for it. Scene 7 stays at 150.

**Not asked but noted:** from below, we see no one who works for the escrow office except Pell. A boatman or clerk's aide would give a view from underneath, but it costs words. I'm not recommending it.
