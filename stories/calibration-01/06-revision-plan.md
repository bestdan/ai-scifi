The First Reader stayed with the story ("yes, but only just"). Their summary
covered the turn and the erasing but **not the treaty or the reveal**. The
"why" isn't landing for a reader outside the genre.

## Consensus: fix these

1. **Rebuild the 3 a.m. hypothesis paragraph** (Chiang, Asimov, Watts, First
   Reader). Cut "Anyone who wanted her judgment…" through "…very close to
   keeping faith." Replace it with three or four short, *concrete* sentences
   that a non-genre reader can't miss, for example: they hadn't wanted her
   judgment, they'd wanted whoever she would be on the day. Then add Watts's
   one cold sentence: every note she erases makes her next a little more like
   everyone, which is the one woman the Parties could hedge against. Keep it
   a hypothesis.
2. **Cut the sentimental tells** (Le Guin, Watts, Chiang).
   - "It was the same terrible joke. She could not stop."
   - "and they both heard it." Let the look carry it.
   - The final "She had always written lightly." and "the way she always
     had." (See conflict A for the new last line.)
3. **Cut Mina's "Why does everybody have to?" and Hanne's explanation**
   (Chiang, Cartographer, Le Guin). The bible's "what it's for" line goes.
   Hanne may keep "Otherwise we'd all be our grandparents forever" only if it
   comes out unprompted and is about something else, such as the seed bun.
   The drafter decides.
4. **Fix Pell's contradiction** (Asimov). "The person, I'm sorry to say, is
   you" contradicts the schedule. Make it say that the person on the date is
   her next, as exactly as Pell would say it. Also settle "pass intact"
   against erasing: intact means every page, and Pell can say so precisely.
5. **Continuity** (Asimov).
   - "when they came" → "when you signed".
   - "to the day" → "to the month".
   - Hanne has turned **three** times, not four.
6. **Too lucid for a mind mid-turn** (Watts). Replace "It read now like a
   note from a woman who enjoyed catching people out" with the failure itself:
   she can't find what was sly about the paragraph, or what had pleased her.
   Shorten the "She had wanted the seat" admission to one plain sentence.
7. **Length.** Bring it down to about 2,550 words. Take Asimov's cut list
   (`reviews/draft-1/asimov.md`) as the default, with the exceptions below.
   Keep the crying at the ramp, cut to one sentence, because Watts praised it
   as bodily. Keep Hanne's mushroom eraser, because it's a texture beat Le Guin
   wants more of.
8. **One-line zinger paragraph endings** (Cartographer counted 8 or 9). Keep
   at most three. Fold the others into their paragraphs.

## Adopted single-lens notes

9. **The town outside the plot** (Le Guin's one thing). Add two or three brief
   glimpses, paid for by the cuts: a keeper on the jetty who waves and says
   nothing about work, paper stacked in a neighbour's window, one word of
   Yoruba from Bisi (*ìyàwó mi*, "my wife", unglossed). Each glimpse is a
   clause, not a paragraph.
10. **Break up the triplet** "Hanne groaned. Ros put her face in her floury
   hands. Oyelaran waited." (Cartographer).

## Conflicts, resolved by the orchestrator as proxy

**A. The ending.** The Cartographer scored it 2 and called the raking-light
image a palimpsest cliché. Chiang, Le Guin and Watts each scored it 4.
- **Decision:** keep the grooves, with the majority and the bible's ending.
  Make the image belong to this story rather than to palimpsests in general
  by using the § 14 plant that Asimov flagged as unused. The Parties keep
  their old selves "retained unedited, sealed". Hers turns out to be in the
  grooves. Give this a few words at most: a closing phrase that borrows
  § 14's language, then stop. For example: *Retained, she thought. Unedited.*
  No explanation.
- **Declined:** the Cartographer's suggestion that the heron should survive.
  The heron's erasure is the beat Chiang named as the cost paid on the page,
  and letting it survive would break § 9's logic.

**B. How explicit the reveal should be.** Chiang wants the derivation cut,
and the First Reader missed the reveal.
- **Decision:** make it shorter *and* plainer (see 1). Concrete sentences are
  both less explanatory and more legible than the abstract ones.

## Declined

- **The Cartographer's "official certifying a reading" flag on Pell's
  ruling.** The ruling is load-bearing for the logic. It stays short and
  comic.
- **The Cartographer's Nordic-register flag on Mina, Hanne and Ros.** These
  names fit the bible's mixed lake town, and changing them now would cost more
  than it buys. Logged for calibration-02.
