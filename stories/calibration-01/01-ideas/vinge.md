# Idea cards: Vinge

Two cards, per the brief. Lens blind spots, stated rather than covered: these
cards push futurity hard and lean on mechanism, so their human scale and prose
register are thin. They need Le Guin and Chiang to test whether the people in
them are more than functions of the novum.

## Card 1: The Sitting

- **Novum:** Each midwinter a river town merges its two hundred residents into one mind for a single night, and that mind governs. By morning it has dispersed and left only its minutes and a certainty in everyone, which they can't explain.
- **Futurity:** F4. The town's real legislator isn't single or continuous. It exists one night a year.
- **How it works:** Neural lace meshes everyone present. The merged mind can only bind people who attended, so absence is the one veto an individual has.
- **First-order consequence:** Decisions are wiser than any resident could make, and nobody can say why.
- **Second-order consequence:** People love the Sitting as a kin they're part of. They name it, and they mourn the years it was lesser.
- **Third-order consequence:** Dissent turns strange. You're arguing against yourself in a larger form.
- **The story:** Rosalía, eighty-one, has sat sixty times. This year's minutes resolve that next winter the town will sit jointly with four valley towns. The town-mind she loves will be dissolved into something larger. Her absence would break the quorum. The minutes hold one line addressed to her, in her own handwriting.
- **Why this lens:** distributed minds, the smallest unit of mind, and the viewpoint trick. The narrator is the part looking at the whole, which is shown only through its minutes.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's own Tines (permanent packs), Baxter's *Coalescence*, Leckie's ancillaries. None of them is periodic, civic and voluntary.

## Card 2: The Keeper

- **Novum:** Posthuman polities can rewrite their own values, so none of them can credibly promise anything. To make a treaty, they hire baseline humans as commitment devices: people too slow to renegotiate, sealed off from persuasion.
- **Futurity:** F4. The main minds are posthuman and appear only through the clauses they bind themselves to.
- **How it works:** Each treaty is reduced to one choice the keeper must make on a fixed date. The parties are forbidden all contact with keepers. If either side is caught persuading a keeper, the treaty is void and that side pays the penalty.
- **First-order consequence:** Being unpersuadable becomes a paid craft and a lineage, practised in quiet towns without feeds.
- **Second-order consequence:** Every argument a keeper hears is suspect, including love.
- **Third-order consequence:** Keepers' children grow up knowing that whatever they say to their parent is screened as possible tampering.
- **The story:** Oyelaran has held a treaty for thirty-four years. Nine days before her date, her adult son comes home with a brilliant reason to choose the other way. She has to decide whether it's his own reason. If she listens, she betrays her craft. If she refuses, she betrays him.
- **Why this lens:** who owns the intelligence, Focus inverted, and the viewpoint deliberately being the slow mind.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stephenson's *Anathem* (cloistered avout) and Schelling's game theory. Neither uses humans as escrow for superminds.
