# Idea cards: Egan

Two cards, per the calibration-01 brief.

## Card 1: The Unthinking

- **Novum:** In a habitat starved of heat-rejection, the citizens run on
  reversible logic. Erasing a bit costs heat, while running a thought
  backwards costs nothing. So the custom is to *unthink*: you deliberate, copy
  out the conclusion, then run the reasoning back to nothing.
- **Futurity:** F4. The minds are software, and thermodynamics has rewritten
  what memory, reasons and accountability mean.
- **How it works:** Landauer: erasing one bit costs at least kT ln 2.
  Bennett: compute, copy the answer, then uncompute, and only the answer
  remains. Adiabatic logic dissipates in proportion to its speed, so fast
  thought costs heat too. The radiators set a fixed annual budget, which is
  split into per-citizen allowances.
- **First-order consequence:** Reasoning leaves no trace, and forgetting is
  rationed.
- **Second-order consequence:** Everyone holds conclusions without the
  reasons. Votes, vows and grudges all rest on grounds that were uncomputed.
  Urgent thinking draws on the shared budget, so it's metered and resented.
- **Third-order consequence:** Your past self becomes an authority you can't
  question. "Why do you believe that?" is a request to spend someone's heat.
  Keeping your reasons becomes a mark of rank, or of distrust.
- **The story:** A mother's ledger holds a conclusion she reached months ago:
  *don't let him board the sail.* Her son leaves tomorrow and demands the
  reason. Re-deriving it at a cheap pace takes three days. Doing it by
  morning means borrowing heat from neighbours who will have to keep
  memories they'd budgeted to lose. And her present self may not reach the
  same answer, or may find out why she unthought it.
- **Why this lens:** It treats physics as a setting. The derivation goes from
  one equation down to a family quarrel.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stross and Rajaniemi gesture at the thermodynamics
  of computation. *Eternal Sunshine* does selective forgetting. I know of
  nothing built on reasons as the thing that gets uncomputed.

## Card 2: Quorum

- **Novum:** Software citizens guard against radiation by running three
  replicas in lockstep across separate sites. A majority vote corrects every
  divergence, so a self is literally a two-of-three consensus.
- **Futurity:** F4. Each mind is plural, and it continues only by agreement.
- **How it works:** Triple modular redundancy. Bit-flips get voted out within
  microseconds. A replica cut off by an outage keeps running, but on
  reconnection the majority overwrites it, because the code can't tell
  experience from noise. Merging is impossible. Only one history survives the
  vote.
- **First-order consequence:** Cosmic rays can't kill you.
- **Second-order consequence:** Travel means moving all three replicas or
  going provisional. Law treats a lone replica's acts as unsigned drafts.
  People avoid doing anything important while out of quorum.
- **Third-order consequence:** The minority week becomes a cultural space for
  confession and deniable affairs, things meant not to survive. Refusing to
  rejoin, a *schism*, strips the other two of their redundancy.
- **The story:** A replica at a relay station loses its link for nine days.
  In that time she makes a promise to the stranded technician beside her.
  When the link comes back, she will be voted out of existence by herself.
  With forty hours left, she can record the promise for the other two, who
  will read it without having lived it. Or she can schism, and leave the two
  of them, who are also her, exposed.
- **Why this lens:** Identity under transformation. It asks who you are when
  you're outvoted by yourself.
- **Suspected prior art:** Brin's *Kiln People* has copies that merge daily.
  Fork-and-merge appears in *Diaspora*. Majority-vote erasure of a self
  feels unclaimed, but the Cartographer should check.

## Lens blind spots, stated

Both cards lead with the derivation. The emotional engine in each (the
mother's unthought reason, the replica's promise) is sketched rather than
felt, and the drafting risks lectures on Landauer and voting codes. The
First Reader and Chiang should test whether the human stakes carry the
scenes without the physics. The cast in both is small: two or three minds and
a few scenes.
