# Idea cards: Banks

Two cards, per the calibration-01 brief. Blind spots, stated up front: this lens
sprawls, and it tends to bury a short story under worldbuilding. Both cards are
cut to one setting, three people and a single decision, but the world behind each
one is larger than 2,500 words can hold. Chiang and Asimov should cut hard.

## Card 1: The Unreached

- **Novum:** A post-scarcity polity whose Minds model people so well that
  anything a Mind says decides the answer. It keeps a small caste, the
  Unreached, whom no Mind may model, predict or address directly. They are the
  only people whose consent counts as consent.
- **Futurity:** F3. Will and consent have stopped being properties of a person
  and become properties of being unmodelled.
- **How it works:** Each Unreached person's data is quarantined from birth, so
  the habitat Minds hold a legally enforced hole in their predictions. Any
  irreversible act at habitat scale needs one Unreached person's yes, and it
  has to reach them through a dull, deliberately dim courier drone.
- **First-order consequence:** Irreversible decisions get a human veto that
  nobody can manipulate.
- **Second-order consequence:** The ambient care everyone else gets never
  reaches the Unreached. Nobody anticipates their needs, and they fall ill
  without anyone noticing.
- **Third-order consequence:** They become the only people who can surprise a
  Mind. That makes them prized and lonely, and gossip about them turns into an
  art form.
- **The story:** Teodh is a middle-aged potter, and she is Unreached. She is
  asked to ratify the disassembly of a moon that holds a dead species' tombs.
  The habitat Mind badly wants her yes and doesn't know what she'll say. Its
  clumsy attempts not to influence her are the story's joke. Her own want is
  smaller and nearer: someone to notice her failing kidney.
- **Why this lens:** The most powerful entity in the story needs the one thing
  it can't take.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "Franchise," where a computer picks one
  voter. That case is the inverse of this one: there the voter is chosen
  because he can be modelled. Le Guin's "Omelas" is a distant relative.

## Card 2: The Ossuary Treaty

- **Novum:** Two post-scarcity civilizations keep the peace by swapping their
  dead. Every archived mind of the deceased runs on the other side's substrate,
  so a war would erase each side's ancestors.
- **Futurity:** F3. Death, kin and place have come apart: your grandmother is
  a citizen of the enemy's computers.
- **How it works:** The archives are kept in balance by count. Taking someone
  home requires a matched return, and both sides audit the archives
  continuously. The dead run at a different clock rate and in a different
  physics library, so over centuries they drift culturally toward their host.
- **First-order consequence:** Three hundred years of peace.
- **Second-order consequence:** The dead go native. They marry other dead and
  form lobbies, and some of them have stopped thinking of home as home.
- **Third-order consequence:** The living relate to the dead the way they
  would to diplomats, and grief turns into a matter of foreign policy.
- **The story:** Ilve is a junior envoy sent to persuade his long-dead
  grandmother to come home. He doesn't know that his government is quietly
  pulling back its dead ahead of a war it believes is justified: the other side
  is doing something his side judges an atrocity. The grandmother has learned
  to read the archive's patterns and guesses why he has come. She refuses,
  because her staying is what holds the treaty together. Ilve then has to
  choose between filing her refusal and forcing her return.
- **Why this lens:** Power that means well does dirty work, and scale meets
  intimacy, because a war between civilizations depends on one family's visit.
- **Suspected prior art:** Banks's *Surface Detail*, where hosted afterlives
  are a matter of war. Hostage exchange in Herbert's *Dune* and in history.
  Using the dead as collateral feels uncharted to me, but the Cartographer
  should check.
