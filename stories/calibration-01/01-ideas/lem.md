# Idea cards: Lem

Two cards, per the calibration-01 brief.

**Blind spots, stated up front.** This lens prefers essay to scene and lets
characters thin into ciphers; both cards need Le Guin or Banks to give the
people inner weather of their own. It also leans cold, so the tone charter's
comic and awestruck registers are the target here, not desolation. Whether
either card is still a story is the First Reader's call, not this lens's.

**Discarded before writing:** an undecodable signal (*His Master's Voice*), an
alien artifact zone (*Roadside Picnic*), and a self-building city that builds
rooms no one asked for (too close to Nihei's *BLAME!*).

## Card 1: Probate Weather

- **Novum:** On a terraformed world, the dead are lofted: rendered and seeded into the engineered airborne plankton that runs the climate. Each lofting adds a strain, and regional rainfall measurably shifts afterwards. Kinship and inheritance are now settled by sequencing the rain.
- **Futurity:** F3. Death, kin and land have merged into one meteorological category.
- **How it works:** Strains differ in ice-nucleation behaviour, so each one nudges where clouds break. The causation is real but chaotic. No strain intends anything, but the law treats rainfall as testimony.
- **First-order consequence:** You are kin to whoever your grandfather rains on.
- **Second-order consequence:** Probate courts with rain gauges. Families buy upwind plots and court the dying of rival households.
- **Third-order consequence:** People read the weather the way earlier ages read entrails, and grieve a drought as abandonment.
- **The story:** A probate assessor certifies that a widow's late husband now falls on a neighbour's orchard, which makes the neighbour her heir. Then she finds the strain has hybridized past the resolution the law assumes. Does she certify noise as kinship, knowing the whole parish runs on it?
- **Why this lens:** A non-mind evolved process that people insist on reading as intention. Never explained: why the rain goes where it goes.
- **Suspected prior art:** Sky burial and rain-ancestor folklore, though not SF. Butler's *Xenogenesis* for gene-mediated kin, but the Oankali are minds. No close match known.

## Card 2: The Tracer's File on Mr. Ostrowiec's Conviction

- **Novum:** A gut consortium, introduced generations ago as an antidepressant, has evolved strains that carry dispositions. Character now spreads through shared meals. People catch convictions, tastes and tempers, and tracers establish their provenance.
- **Futurity:** F3. The self is a contagion record; "whose is this?" is asked of every opinion.
- **How it works:** Neuroactive metabolites signal along the vagus nerve. Strains move by shared food. Tracers can map transmission chains but can never predict what a strain will produce in a new host. The same strain gives one woman a taste for pears and another a vocation.
- **First-order consequence:** Dietary hygiene. "Clean-tablers" guard their characters; feast-towns merge theirs on purpose.
- **Second-order consequence:** Opinions carry pedigrees, and a borrowed conviction is held to be worth less than a homegrown one.
- **Third-order consequence:** No one is sure that anything they love is their own, and most stop caring.
- **The story:** Told as the tracer's case file: interview transcripts, a transmission diagram, redactions. A parish councillor has caught an unshakeable conviction that the village must build a bridge over a dry valley. He refuses scouring, which would purge the conviction along with unknown other traits, but he demands to know its source. The chain loops back through the tracer's own kitchen.
- **Why this lens:** Satire of rational self-possession, plus the document form whose gaps carry the story. Never explained: what any strain actually is.
- **Suspected prior art:** Heinlein's *The Puppet Masters* and parasite fiction (but those parasites are minds, and the stories are dystopian); Chiang's "Liking What You See" for engineered disposition. Moderate distance.
