# Idea cards: Rajaniemi (the Immersionist)

Two cards, per the calibration-01 brief. Blind spots stated up front: this lens
over-coins and front-loads density, and both cards lean on a vocabulary that
could drown a 2,500-word reader. Asimov and the First Reader should test
whether each term earns its place; the genre spine is there to carry the
reader through.

## Card 1: Juuri

- **Novum:** Kinship runs through engineered gut flora that keep a family's
  history as CRISPR spacer records. You inherit your ancestors by colonization,
  and an antibiotic course wipes out your lineage.
- **Futurity:** F3. Kin and death are redefined: a mother is whoever seeded
  you, and the dead go on living as a readable archive inside the living.
- **How it works:** Real molecular recorders (Cas1–Cas2 spacer acquisition)
  log metabolic events (fevers, famines, grief cortisol) into a heritable array.
  Strain-level tracing already follows mother-to-infant transfer.
- **Coinages:** *juuri* (Finnish for both "root" and "sourdough starter":
  lineage began as a kitchen practice among women); *rivi*, one readable line of
  an ancestor's year; *nukadoko*, the household bran crock that holds a backup
  culture (a Japanese tradition of keeping lineage outside the body); *kvass-kin*,
  in-laws by flora merger (a rival, Russian-descended fermentation tradition).
- **First-order consequence:** Families read their dead the way others read
  diaries.
- **Second-order consequence:** Marriage becomes a strain merger, and the
  dominant strain overwrites the weaker one.
- **Third-order consequence:** Infection is a moral crisis. Hospitals treat
  your body, but they also triage your ancestors.
- **The story:** This is a marriage plot played straight, on a deadline. Saara's
  nukadoko soured, which leaves her as the last carrier of her juuri. She has an
  infection that is turning septic, and she has a few days to choose. She can
  take broad-spectrum antibiotics, live, and end her line. She can gamble on a
  slow phage cocktail. Or she can accept the suitor she refused and become
  kvass-kin, letting his family's strain carry her grandmother in rewritten form.
- **Why this lens:** It builds a culture outward from one piece of everyday
  infrastructure, a funeral rite that lives in the gut, with real biology under
  it.
- **Suspected prior art:** Slonczewski's *Brain Plague* and Bear's *Blood Music*
  both have intelligent microbes. Here the flora is an archive, not a mind, so it
  differs in kind. I'd judge it moderately close.

## Card 2: The Pominki Quorum

- **Novum:** The dead are stored by threshold secret-sharing. Each of n friends
  carries a *sirp*, and the person wakes only when k of them gather. Any k−1
  shares hold no information about her at all.
- **Futurity:** F3–F4. Death becomes a matter of how many people still show up,
  and the dead are discontinuous minds.
- **How it works:** Shamir sharing is information-theoretic, so a share below
  the threshold reveals literally nothing. Proactive refresh means a quorum must
  periodically re-randomize the shares, or leakage accumulates.
- **Coinages:** *sirp* (clipped from Finnish *sirpale*, "shard"); *pominki*, the
  Russian wake-meal, now the waking itself; *relève*, the refresh day (a French
  military "changing of the guard"); *en* (Japanese 縁), the bond of keeping,
  which is heritable.
- **First-order consequence:** Friendship carries a duty of attendance.
- **Second-order consequence:** Children inherit sirps of people they never
  met, and estrangement has a body count.
- **Third-order consequence:** Leaving town becomes an act of killing, so
  emigration requires releases, and mobility is rationed by en.
- **The story:** This is a reunion piece in the *Big Chill* mould, set over one
  evening. Ilmi inherited her mother's sirp of Aunt Marjatta. The threshold is
  five of eight, and six keepers come. Ilmi wants to emigrate down a light-lagged
  relay. She can stay and keep, or go and leave the others one keeper short at
  every future pominki. Marjatta could free her by re-sharing, but re-sharing
  needs a stranger.
- **Why this lens:** Privacy as felt cryptography, and the funeral rite as
  everyday infrastructure.
- **Suspected prior art:** Brin's *Kiln People*, Stross's *Accelerando* and
  Leckie's distributed minds. None of them makes a self out of a threshold of
  other people, and I know of no close match.
