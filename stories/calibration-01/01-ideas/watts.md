# Idea cards: Watts

Two cards, as the brief asks. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots, stated up front.** This lens leans toward bleakness and toward
characters who serve as case studies. It also underrates ritual, meaning and
joy. Both cards put a ritual at the centre and keep the worlds livable, but
Le Guin and Banks should check whether the people read as people.

## Card 1: Second Litter

- **Novum:** Adults can reopen the childhood window for kin detection and,
  after weeks of shared living, come out as siblings: neurologically
  committed to each other's welfare and sexually averse to each other. Kin
  becomes something you can make.
- **Futurity:** F3. Kin stops being something you are born into and becomes
  something you are made into.
- **How it works:** Humans detect siblings by cue, not by relatedness:
  years of co-residence and seeing your mother care for an infant
  (Lieberman, Tooby & Cosmides 2007, *Nature*; the kibbutz data behind the
  Westermarck effect). MDMA and psychedelics reopen a social-reward critical
  period in adult mice (Nardou et al. 2019 and 2023, *Nature*). Combine the
  two and the cue system tags whoever you live with.
- **First-order consequence:** Chosen siblings, sworn into kin by a course
  of sessions.
- **Second-order consequence:** Marriage splits in two. A committed couple
  "sibs" to secure loyalty, and desire moves outside the bond.
- **Third-order consequence:** Desire becomes the one bond that can't be
  made to last, so it is reserved for strangers. People who never sibbed are
  pitied for having nobody bound to them.
- **The story:** Tamsin, married eleven years, feels her desire for her
  husband Iolo fading. Iolo books them a sibbing course: he would rather
  have her bound to him by instinct than lose her. She has to decide whether
  she wants love she can't withdraw, at the cost of the love she chose.
- **Why this lens:** Kin altruism is set off by cues, and nothing in
  evolution guards those cues against being exploited.
- **Suspected prior art:** Butler's *Xenogenesis* (the Oankali bond humans
  chemically); Le Guin's "Mountain Ways" (built kinship rules); *Brave New
  World*. None of them induces sibling-hood and its incest aversion.

## Card 2: The Spring Telling

- **Novum:** A settlement goes into engineered torpor every winter. In
  torpor the brain sheds dendritic spines and regrows them on waking, so
  everyone comes back slightly rewired. Partners rebuild each other by
  telling the year aloud.
- **Futurity:** F3. Memory and continuity of self get renewed once a year,
  and what you are renewed from is somebody else's account of you.
- **How it works:** Synthetic torpor has been induced in mice through
  hypothalamic Q neurons (Takahashi et al. 2020; Hrvatin et al. 2020, both
  in *Nature*). During torpor, hibernators' hippocampal dendrites retract
  and then regrow within hours of waking (Popov & Bocharova 1992;
  von der Ohe 2006). Tau is reversibly hyperphosphorylated over the same
  cycle (Arendt 2003). Ground squirrels lose some learned tasks over winter
  (Millesi 2001), while results for spatial memory are mixed.
- **First-order consequence:** Winter costs a quarter of the life support.
- **Second-order consequence:** What gets rehearsed in the first spring
  days survives, so the community holds tellings.
- **Third-order consequence:** Whoever tells your year shapes who you wake
  up as, and the role of teller becomes a form of power and of love.
- **The story:** Edda wakes from her fourth winter. As her partner Rasmus
  tells her the year, his version leaves out the autumn when she meant to
  leave him. Fragments of that autumn come back to her that don't fit his
  account. She has to decide which self to rehearse into permanence.
- **Why this lens:** The self is a story, and here that is literally true,
  because the story is rebuilt from tissue every spring.
- **Suspected prior art:** Cold-sleep tropes; *Eternal Sunshine*; Le Guin's
  seasonal Gethen. None of them has annual regrowth of synapses.
