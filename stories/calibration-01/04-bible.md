# Story bible: The Keeper's Margins (working title)

## Premise

Two posthuman polities can rewrite their own values, so neither can make a
credible promise. They have escrowed their peace in Oyelaran. She is a
baseline human, and her craft is being unpersuadable. For twenty-two years
she has read their quarterly accounts of themselves. On a fixed date she must
judge whether each one kept faith, and anyone caught arguing to her voids the
treaty. Nine days before that date, she begins her turn: the season every
adult passes through, which keeps the memories but grows a new self with new
values. Whoever judges on the date will be someone else. She wants to leave
her successor what she has concluded, but the clause doesn't distinguish by
author. Her twenty-two years of pencilled margin notes are argument too. Then
she reads the date again and understands it was set to fall just after her
turn. The polities never wanted her judgment. They wanted a judge nobody
could know in advance, so the only way to be found faithful was to have been
faithful. Over the last nights, with her husband and then her neighbours, she
erases every note she ever made.

## The novum

There are two, and only one of them gets explained.

### The turn (familiar to the characters, never explained)

- **What it is:** an engineered return of childhood plasticity, roughly every
  twenty-five years. Memory is intact, but temperament, taste and values
  re-form over about a week. Legally the person continues, but every vow
  lapses unless the new self ratifies it within a year.
- **What it costs:** the self you are now ends. People call this ordinary,
  in the way people call menopause or adolescence ordinary.
- **Prodrome:** taste goes first. Familiar foods turn flat, then strange. A
  date can be forecast to within a few days, and the town's doctor does it
  with a tongue swab and a chart.
- **Rules and edge cases:** the turn can't be postponed. The new self
  remembers the old one the way you remember a vivid novel: the events are
  vivid, but the feelings are not your own. This is Oyelaran's third turn, and
  she remembers the woman she was before the last one.

### The keeper's escrow (explained only through the treaty text and the clerk)

- **The polities:** the **Standing Revision** and the **Settled Question**.
  They are vast, they edit their own values, and they are never seen. They
  appear only as the authors of quarterly accounts, and as the drafters of a
  treaty whose prose is courteous and exact.
- **The treaty:** both polities refrain from the Disputed Volume, a quiet
  star system both want, for the term. On the date of determination the
  Keeper rules, for each polity, whether it *kept faith*. A polity found
  faithless forfeits the Volume to the other. The standard is the Keeper's
  conscience and nothing else.
- **The clause (§ 9):** "No party, agent or person shall present to the Keeper
  argument bearing on the Determination." If either side is caught, the
  treaty is void and the offender forfeits. The clause does not distinguish
  by author.
- **The paper town:** keepers live in Sallow Reach, a town with no feeds,
  where every input arrives on paper through the escrow office. That way,
  nothing can reach a keeper sideways.
- **The term:** twenty-two years, seven months and nine days. Its tail is odd,
  and Oyelaran noticed it at signing and let it go. **This is the fair-play
  clue.** Her second turn came about thirty-two months before the signing (Bisi
  mentions this in one line: "you'd been you less than three years when
  they came"). Turns run about twenty-five years apart, with a known
  spread. The term ends exactly nine days past the late edge of that
  spread. That the forecast lands where it does is evidence, not proof.
- **Why the polities did it** (this is the game theory, and it has to hold):
  a judge whose conscience is known for decades can be gamed. A polity can
  shape its conduct to look faithful *to her* without being faithful. A
  successor's conscience, unknowable until the day, can't be gamed. The only
  strategy that survives it is real compliance. Oyelaran's twenty-two years
  were a living, unbribable seat, and her conscience was never what the
  treaty was built on.
- **Edge cases the story must close:**
  - *Can she just write to her successor?* No. § 9 covers it.
  - *Can she leave her notes?* No. They are argument. The clerk confirms it,
    reading the clause in his fussiest manner.
  - *Can she destroy the reports?* No. They are treaty property, and the
    archive passes intact "to the Keeper on the date". Erasure is the only
    route.
  - *Graphite leaves grooves.* Over the years she wrote lightly, out of the
    keeper's habit of not committing too hard. Some grooves will remain.
    That's the story's last ambiguity (see the outline).

## The consequence ladder

1. **First order:** two minds that can't promise can still make a peace,
   because a slow human holds it.
2. **Second order:** trustworthiness becomes a paid craft. There is a town of
   keepers, an escrow office and a paper economy, and keepers are cloistered
   from argument, including the arguments of the people they love.
3. **Third order:** the polities' real innovation is the *replaceable* judge.
   A keeper's long-cultivated wisdom is, in the treaty's eyes, a
   vulnerability. A human whose identity is her judgment finds out that her
   judgment was the risk.

## The society

- **Sallow Reach:** a lake town of keepers, retired keepers and the people who
  marry them. Mail comes by boat. The houses are full of paper. The town has
  a good bakery, because people who can't have feeds take bread seriously.
- **The economy:** the escrow office pays well. Keepers are respected and a
  little pitied, as monks are.
- **Kinship:** spouses ratify each other after every turn, or they don't.
  Re-courtship is a recognized and tender genre of behaviour. The town's word
  for a spouse who has stayed through two turns is *twice-met*.
- **Death:** ordinary.
- **Taboos:** asking a keeper what she thinks. Children learn early not to
  ask Auntie what she thinks about the stars.
- **An ordinary day:** Oyelaran reads accounts in the morning and gardens in
  the afternoon, and Bisi cooks. Keepers wave to each other and never discuss
  work.

## Minds

- **The polities:** never shown from inside. They are known only through
  their prose: polite, enormous, exact, and faintly funny in its exactness.
  The treaty reads like an apology written in advance by someone very
  intelligent.
- **Oyelaran, in her prodrome:** her sensory world drifts. Her tea tastes of
  nothing, and later of something she has no name for. She is acutely aware
  that her certainty about the polities is a property of a self that is
  ending.

## Characters

**Folasade Oyelaran.** About seventy-four, keeper for twenty-two years. The
office and the town call her Oyelaran, and Bisi calls her Sade. The town's
naming is mixed, its families having come from many places. Hers is Yoruba.
- **Wants:** her twenty-two years of attention to *count*. She has privately
  concluded that the Settled Question's accounts are too perfect, and that
  it kept the letter and not the faith.
- **Wrong about:** what she was for. She believes she was chosen for her
  conscience.
- **What the novum does to her:** it ends her, and it shows her that she was
  an interval.
- **Loves, outside the plot:** her pencils, kept sharp with a knife. Herons.
  Bisi's terrible jokes.

**Adebisi "Bisi" Lind.** About seventy-six, her husband. He was a
boat-builder and is now the town's best cook. He married the woman she was
before her second turn, and courted her successor, who is the woman she is
now, from scratch over a year, and won.
- **Wants:** to be ratified a third time. He's frightened of it, and he never
  says so directly.
- **Wrong about:** nothing much. He's the person in the story who already
  knows how to love in sequence.
- **What the novum does to him:** he is about to lose his wife for the second
  time and court a stranger for the second time. He treats it as weather.
- **Loves:** the lake, and feeding people.

**Mr. Pell**, clerk of the escrow office.
- **Wants:** to do the job perfectly. He loves the treaty's prose.
- **Function:** comic precision, and the person who confirms the trap. He
  may not discuss the Determination, and he's very good at not discussing it.
- **Loves:** the clauses, read aloud.

**The neighbours.** Other keepers and spouses. They arrive on the last
night with erasers. Nobody asks what the notes said, because that is the
taboo turned into kindness.

## Point of view and voice

- **Teller:** close third person on Oyelaran.
- **Person and tense:** third person, past tense.
- **Register:** dry, warm and exact. The humour comes from precision, and
  the prose is plain enough to see through, with treaty text quoted in its
  own register.
- **Why this teller:** she is the slow mind, and she's the one whose
  certainty is the thing being dissolved.
- **Sample paragraph:**

  > The tea tasted of nothing. Not weak, not stewed: nothing, the way a word
  > goes to nothing if you say it forty times. Oyelaran drank it anyway,
  > because Bisi was watching, and because twenty-two years of not reacting
  > to things had left her with a face that did very little on its own.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning | Where the term comes from |
|---|---|---|
| the turn / turning | periodic re-forming of the self | plain speech, like "the change" |
| twice-met | a spouse who has stayed through two turns | town usage |
| keeper | escrow human for a treaty | the trade's own word |
| the Determination | the keeper's ruling on the date | treaty text |
| the Disputed Volume | the star system at stake | treaty text, bureaucratic |
| Standing Revision / Settled Question | the two polities | their own chosen names, both ironic |
| accounts | the quarterly reports of conduct | escrow usage |

## Rules deliberately broken

- **"The scientist who explains to a less-informed colleague."** The clerk
  explains § 9 to a keeper who already knows it. The comedy is that he
  explains it anyway, and that she needs to hear it said aloud.


## Amendments from the stress test

These amendments are binding. Where one conflicts with anything earlier in
this bible, the amendment wins. Notes are tagged with their lens:
`04-critique/<lens>.md`.

### Logic (Egan, Watts, Lem)

1. **The date** is fixed as above (Egan must-fix 1). **The outline's Calendar section is the final authority on the arithmetic.** The reader should be
   able to do the arithmetic alongside her: quote the term, show the
   doctor's forecast, and give Bisi's thirty-two-months line.
2. **Memory versus paper** (Watts must-fix 1, Egan must-fix 2). Mr. Pell
   rules, in his fussiest manner, that marginalia are "the Keeper's own
   apparatus" while she holds the seat, and become *argument presented by a
   person* once the archive passes to the Keeper on the date. Memory is not
   presented. So the erasure isn't about secrecy: her successor will
   remember the conclusions, but remember them the way you remember an
   opinion from a novel. What gets removed is her *reasons*, in her
   handwriting, carrying her authority. The successor inherits the evidence
   (twenty-two years of accounts she has read and remembers), not the
   advocacy. **This also answers the question of why a human, and why not
   a random draw** (Egan should). A stranger would have to be briefed, and
   briefing is argument under § 9. A successor who has the memories has the
   evidence without the brief.
3. **Hedging, not gaming** (Egan must-fix 3). Oyelaran's conclusion is that
   the polities can't game the judge, only hedge against her, and hedging
   across everyone she might become is close enough to keeping faith. Bisi
   provides the evidence of divergence: "You came back from the last one
   liking eels."
4. **It stays a hypothesis** (Lem must-fix 2). Mr. Pell neither confirms nor
   denies. A rival reading is voiced, by Hanne: the polities simply missed
   the turn, being too large to notice a woman's tongue going flat. She
   erases under uncertainty.
5. **One unexplained posthuman fact** (Lem must-fix 1, Egan could). § 14 is
   quoted: *"Each Party warrants that the self that executes this
   instrument has been retained unedited, sealed, to be consulted solely in
   the event of the Party's dispute with itself."* Every account also opens
   with a single unexplained integer, and the integers fall over the years.
   She has never known what they count, and the story never says.
6. **"Keeper" is defined in the schedule** (Lem should) as *"the person
   occupying the seat on the date of Determination"*. She has read this line
   a hundred times, and Mr. Pell reads it aloud. It's the second half of the
   fair-play clue.

### People and town (Le Guin, Watts)

7. **Bisi has a flaw he acts on** (Le Guin must-fix 2, Watts should). He
   doesn't want her to turn, though he has never said so. When the clause
   stops the letter, he offers to do the arguing himself: *"I'll tell her.
   Every night, while I court her. That's courting, not arguing."* That
   would breach § 9, and he knows it. She refuses him, and the refusal hurts
   them both. He also breaks the taboo once and asks what she thinks.
8. **A child and a second elder** (Le Guin must-fix 1). Mina, the baker's
   daughter, about eight, asks the forbidden question at the counter:
   *"Auntie, what do you think about the stars?"* **Hanne Oduya** is in her
   eighties, a retired keeper whose treaty ended long ago and who has turned
   three times (corrected at stage 7: a 25-year cycle gives three by eighty). She's dry and unsentimental, and she's the one who voices the
   rival reading.
9. **The erasing night has friction** (Le Guin must-fix 3). Only three
   people help: Bisi, Hanne, and Mina's mother, Ros, the baker. They turn
   pages face-down so nobody reads them. Ros hesitates, because she doesn't
   want to know, and doesn't want to have been in the room. In the margin of
   the 2041 third-quarter account there's a heron sketch, and they argue
   over whether it counts as argument. (Bisi wants to keep it. Oyelaran
   says, "It says *look at herons*," and erases it.) The grooves remain
   visible in raking light.
10. **Lucidity** (Watts must-fix 2). She does not introspect on her values
    changing. She feels her certainty as fact. The change surfaces only as
    small failures: an old note of hers reads cold to her, a reason she
    would have dismissed in March suddenly seems obvious, and she finds one
    of Bisi's terrible jokes funny for the first time. The prodrome is messy:
    broken sleep, waking hungry at three in the morning, crying at the
    lake's smell for no reason.
11. **What the turn is for** (Watts should) gets one line: it was engineered
    so that long lives don't set like plaster. Hanne: "Otherwise we'd all be
    our grandparents forever."
12. **Coinages for the turn** (Le Guin should): *your next* is the successor
    self ("your next will like the bakery"), and *the one before* is the
    previous self. The town's word for a spouse who has stayed through two
    turns, *twice-met*, is unchanged.
13. **Names** (Le Guin could): Bisi Lind, Mr. Pell, Hanne Oduya, Ros, Mina.

### Ending (Lem must-fix 3, Watts could, Egan should)

14. The story **stops before the Determination**. We never learn the
    verdict, what her notes said, or whether the Settled Question was
    faithless. The last image is the grooves. Perfect erasure would make
    her successor more predictable, closer to the average of everyone she
    could become, and the story doesn't resolve that. Erasing is not
    rewarded.

### Declined

- Lem's suggestion to open on an account fragment, in full. A one-line integer
  from an account may open the story, but the outline decides.
- Watts's reconsolidation drift, beyond note 10. It's already carried by
  "an old note reads cold".
