# Stage 2 audit: calibration-01

The Cartographer's map of 18 cards from nine lenses. I checked the attributions
I wasn't sure of by web search (2026-10-05). Where I still couldn't confirm a
detail, the entry says so. "Nearest I know of" means only that: if I list no
prior art, I don't know of any, which isn't proof that none exists.

**How to read the scores.** N Novelty, F Futurity, R Rigor, Y Yield,
E Estrangement, S Stakes. Weighted = 2N + F + R + 2Y + E + S, out of 40. I
judged Yield at the brief's length of about 2,500 words: a card scores high
only if its choice can play out with two or three people across three or four
scenes. Each card's "Why" line gives one justification per axis, in the same
order as the scores.

**Summary.** 3 cards are uncharted, 14 are edge-of-map and 1 is charted. None
is `charted (tone)`. Not one card leans on a stock dystopia. The run's real
failure is the opposite one, covered under Convergence below.

## Asimov

### asimov/Meltwater Citizen
Nearest prior art: *Dune* (Frank Herbert, 1965, novel), confidence high on the thematic overlap. Fremen treat water as kinship and tribal wealth, but water is never a legal test of citizenship.
Also near: *The Water Knife* (Paolo Bacigalupi, 2015, novel), where water rights are basin politics; real isotope forensics (isoscapes), which is fact rather than fiction.
Distance: Large. Nobody I know of makes citizenship a metabolic state that turns over in a fortnight. The loophole (food is water) is a true Asimov-style rule failure, and it's built into the novum, not added on top.
Status: uncharted (confidence medium, because water-politics SF is a crowded shelf and I may be missing a short story)
Scores: N4 F3 R4 Y4 E4 S4 → weighted 31/40
Why: no close match, but Dune is a thematic ancestor · place-as-chemistry reaches F3 without going further · the isotope and turnover numbers are about right, and the melon loophole holds · assayer, old man and granddaughter make a clean triangle in one certification scene · it makes birthplace and address look arbitrary · the graves are specific and can't be got back.
To push past the map: The old man knows exactly what he's doing. The granddaughter, who wants the dam, is the one who has been shipping him the melons, because she has her own reason for wanting him to vote. Then the assayer isn't solving a puzzle but catching a family in a conspiracy that runs both ways.

To push past the map: Put the drama in the lexicon. Have the clerk try to write "repeal" in the 1990s cohort's vocabulary, which can't express the idea without giving away the date. The story becomes a translation problem in which every synonym she tries is a fact she's not allowed to leak.

## Convergence

The lenses were blind to each other, and they still produced one story many
times over. Below is every cluster I can find, with membership counts out of 18.

**1. The dead as continuing participants (6 core cards, 9 counting graves and
tombs).** Plain Wording Office (the dead vote), Ossuary Treaty (the dead as
hostages), Wintering (the near-dead kept at home), Probate Weather (the dead
as rain), Pominki Quorum (the dead held in friends), and Juuri (the dead as a
gut archive). The stakes of Meltwater Citizen (family graves to be drowned),
The Unreached (a dead species' tombs) and The Unthinking (a reason that has
been "unthought", that is, a dead self) bring it to nine. Inheritance settled
through the dead appears three times: Probate Weather, Wintering and Juuri.

**2. Microbes or body chemistry as kinship, belonging or character (3 core,
5 adjacent).** Taking Ground (gut flora decides kin), Juuri (gut flora carries
the ancestors) and Tracer's File (gut flora carries character). Probate Weather
uses engineered microbes to carry the dead. Meltwater Citizen has the same
shape with isotopes instead of microbes: your body's chemistry decides your
polity.

**3. Memory and self rebuilt by others, or a self discontinuous with its past
(6 cards).** Spring Telling (rebuilt by your partner's account), Ratification
(values re-formed, memory kept), Quorum (overwritten by your majority),
Unthinking (your past self's conclusions with the reasons gone), Sitting (a
certainty nobody can explain) and Foundling Thoughts (a belief without a
source).

**3a. Conclusions without reasons (a tight sub-cluster of 5).** Unthinking,
Sitting, Foundling, Tracer's File ("an unshakeable conviction" with no known
source) and Keeper ("is this his own reason?"). Five lenses independently
arrived at *a belief whose origin can't be audited* as the core uncanny object.

**4. Consent, commitment, votes and thresholds (8 cards).** Unreached
(consent), Keeper (commitment), Ratification (vows), Plain Wording (the vote),
Meltwater (the vote), Sitting (quorum, with absence as veto), Quorum
(two-of-three) and Pominki (k-of-n). Three cards are literally built on a
quorum: Egan's, Rajaniemi's and Vinge's.

Four more clusters the brief didn't name are, if anything, stronger evidence.

**5. The certifier's dilemma (7 cards, the same plot engine).** An official
reads a measurement and must decide whether to certify it truthfully. Meltwater
(assayer), Probate Weather (assessor), Taking Ground (an earth-reader who can
misread), Foundling (a tracer who can implant a false provenance), Tracer's
File (tracer), Plain Wording (a wording clerk) and Ossuary (an envoy who files
or forces). Two lenses invented the same job title, *tracer*, for someone who
establishes where a disposition came from.

**6. A pastoral small polity on a river (6 cards).** Watersheds, valleys, dams
and flooding: Meltwater (a dam drowns graves), Taking Ground (a meadow given to
the river), Probate Weather (rain on orchards), Sitting (a river town),
Tracer's File (a bridge over a dry valley) and Wintering (a boundary dispute).
Meltwater and Taking Ground both turn on a watershed vote to flood family land.
Wintering and Spring Telling both use engineered torpor.

**7. The rubric's own words, played back.** The F3 definition lists "self,
death, place, kin". The cards' Futurity lines echo it nearly word for word:
"Death, kin and land have merged into one meteorological category" (Lem),
"Kinship and place have merged into one category" (Le Guin), "Death, kin and
place have come apart" (Banks), "Kin and death are redefined" (Rajaniemi).
Nearly every card aims at those four nouns. Only Vinge's two F4 cards and Egan's Quorum reach for a different category, and even they land on "self".

**8. A shared name register.** Tuva, Maren, Edda, Rasmus, Hesket, Marrit,
Ilmi, Saara, Teodh, Ilve, Odile. The Nordic and Baltic pastiche is consistent
across lenses that have no reason to share it. It dodges the style guide's
banned names by moving one shelf over.

**Verdict: an attractor, not charted territory.** Part of the ground is
charted: the dead as participants (*Ubik*, *Surface Detail*, *Black Mirror*,
*Coco*) and readable ancestors (*Dune*). But charted territory doesn't explain
the tightness. Nine independent lenses converged on the same job title, the
same flooded-valley vote, the same quorum mechanism and the same four nouns from
the rubric. That points to the generating model and the prompt, not to the map.
Three forces seem to combine:

- **The rubric's F3 definition is being read as a menu.** Its example
  categories became the targets.
- **The tone charter pushes the model toward folk pastoral.** Banning dystopia
  and asking for "worlds you'd want to visit" produced a pastoral rather than a
  range of registers: almost no cities, ships, nonhuman viewpoints, comedy or
  action, and only Egan's two cards (a radiator-limited habitat and a relay station) set anywhere engineered.
- **The model's stock move for "humane SF" is a quiet functionary, a
  bereavement and a ritual.** Each lens's persona coloured the surface, but
  that skeleton underneath stayed the same.

**Steering for the next run:**

- Rewrite the F3 example list so it isn't four nouns. Use examples of shape
  instead, or rotate the categories per run.
- Ban the certifier or official-reading-a-measurement protagonist as a default,
  as a style-guide plot entry.
- Put death and inheritance on a "needs a reason" list unless the brief seeds
  them.
- Require each lens to name its setting's scale (household, city, polity,
  ship, deep time), and reject a set in which more than a third of the cards
  share one.
- Add the Nordic-pastiche register to the banned-names note.

The lenses that resisted the pull best were Egan and Vinge, and that's where
the top scores are. The two most persona-coloured lenses, Le Guin and Lem, were
the most captured: all four of their cards sit inside clusters 1, 2, 5 or 6.

## Collisions

Each entry gives the parts, the collision, an estimated score, and why it's
uncharted where its parts aren't.

1. **Vinge/The Keeper × Chiang/The Ratification.** Oyelaran's rebase falls
   before her treaty date. The commitment device is about to become a new
   person, and the treaty assumed her values were fixed. She must decide
   whether to bind her successor, and how. Estimated N4 F4 R4 Y5 E4 S5 →
   **35/40**. *Why uncharted:* the Swordholder is never replaced by her own
   successor self, and "Rejoined" has no third party relying on the old self's
   values. The combination asks whether a promise can be held in escrow across
   a change of person, which neither card asks alone.

2. **Le Guin/Wintering × Watts/The Spring Telling.** The winterer gets one day
   awake, and someone has to tell him eleven years in it. Marrit is the teller,
   and what she leaves out is who her grandfather dies as. Estimated N4 F3 R3
   Y5 E4 S5 → **33/40**. *Why uncharted:* *Before I Go to Sleep* has a telling
   that repeats every morning. A telling that happens once, can't be revised,
   and comes at the end of a life changes the stake from deception to
   authorship. It also removes Spring Telling's direct match.

3. **Egan/The Unthinking × Vinge/The Sitting.** The town-mind uncomputes its
   own deliberation to stay inside the heat budget. The minutes hold
   conclusions only, which is why everyone wakes with a certainty they can't
   explain. Rosalía wants the reasoning for dissolving the town-mind re-derived
   before she votes by attending, and re-deriving it costs the whole town's
   heat. Estimated N5 F4 R4 Y3 E5 S4 → **33/40** (it ties on score, but the
   cast is bigger and the Yield at 2,500 words is lower than either part
   alone). *Why uncharted:* it gives the Sitting a physical reason for its
   inexplicable certainty, and makes "why?" a civic expense rather than a
   household one.

4. **Chiang/Foundling Thoughts × Lem/The Tracer's File.** In a culture with a
   provenance sense, gut-borne convictions don't feel like foundlings. They
   feel self-originated, because the sense tags them at encoding. The one
   class of belief that is truly someone else's feels most like your own.
   Estimated N4 F3 R4 Y4 E5 S4 → **32/40**. *Why uncharted:* it gives
   Foundling the mechanism it lacks and turns Lem's contagion into an
   *Inception* that nobody intended. The two lenses invented the same
   profession, and this merger uses that convergence instead of hiding it.

5. **Watts/Second Litter × Chiang/The Ratification.** Maren feels nothing for
   Tuva after her rebase. Tuva won't court a stranger, so she proposes sibbing:
   a ratification by instinct that guarantees loyalty and rules out desire.
   Estimated N4 F3 R4 Y4 E4 S5 → **32/40**. *Why uncharted:* Ratification
   offers only "ratify or lapse". Adding a third option, a bond that is
   permanent but not marital, turns a yes-or-no choice into a question about
   which kind of love can survive a change of self.

6. **Asimov/Meltwater Citizen × Lem/Probate Weather.** Citizenship is read
   from the isotopes of your body water, and the lofted dead shift where the
   rain falls. Every death moves the electorate, and a family can vote by
   dying upwind. Estimated N5 F3 R3 Y3 E5 S4 → **31/40**. *Why uncharted:* each
   card makes belonging chemical, and together they make the dead into
   electoral geography. But it's two systems to explain in 2,500 words, so the
   Yield is low.
