## The one thing
Trim "A Party could write its accounts to suit the woman at this table. The only way to suit a stranger was to keep faith." Keep "Perhaps they had wanted whoever she would be on the day" and the cold sentence. The two cut sentences restate the first as a rule.

## Notes

1. **3 a.m. paragraph (line-level):** The rebuild works. It is half the length, it is hedged by "Perhaps", and it stays on the hypothesis. The reader can now miss neither the date nor the motive. What remains is a residue of derivation: the middle two sentences. The cold sentence is the best of them, but "the one woman the Parties could hedge against" is faintly opaque. The reader must work out that "everyone" is the mean, and that the average is who a Party can write to. That puzzle is a fair one and I would leave it. The doubt is also now properly held, so Hanne's "a number they liked the look of" opposes it rather than repeating it.

2. **Last line (line-level):** "*Retained,* she thought. *Unedited.*" does the plant's work. The treaty's sealed selves become her hand in the grooves, and the story declines to explain it. The cost is that it answers the question. The grooves say that something of her persists, and "Unedited" reads as a verdict. The image is still doing the work in the sentence before: "every stroke she had ever pressed there, shallow as the trail of a snail on a step." Consider whether the stack and "Retained" alone would leave the reader to supply the second word.

3. **Pell's "nine days to run" (line-level):** I suggested last round that this was redundant with the first heading. It is still there. Trim Pell, not "Nine".

4. **Pell's ruling (line-level):** "the person, I'm sorry to say, will be the one before" is correct but takes two readings. Make it as exact as his other lines.

5. **Day-count (structural, accepted):** The count still serves the term only. I no longer think it is a fault, because the final scene's "Two" followed by dawn carries the successor's arrival. No change.

No structural note stands.

## What to protect

- Pell's non-answer, unchanged.

- "Nobody can present you with your own spectacles."

## Blind spot disclosure

I favour the solved clue and distrust stated ideas, so I may be pressing on two sentences that a general reader needs. The First Reader missed the reveal last round, and I am inclined to leave that to the Line Editor. Watts's reading of the ending as cold may differ from my reading of it as faintly consoling.
