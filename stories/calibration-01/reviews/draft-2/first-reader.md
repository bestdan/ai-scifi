# First Reader report: Raking Light (draft 2)

## Where I got lost

- "the ninetieth and final accounts, one from each Party" and "the Disputed Volume" / "the Settled Question": I never learned who the Parties are, what they are disputing, or why a woman sits between them. I took it as background and kept going, but I held it loosely the whole way.
- "an integer. This one was 7... she had never learned what they counted." I couldn't tell if this mattered. I think the numbers are counting down to something, but nothing ever came back to them.
- The section headings "Nine", "Eight", "Eight", "Seven", "Seven", "Two". I guessed a countdown, but the jump to "Two" made me unsure what was running out. Days? The taste?
- "It tasted of nothing, the way a word goes to nothing". Only at "Started. About a week to settle, from when the taste went" did I work out that she is turning into someone else. "Your next will like the bakery" made it clear. Until then I thought she was ill.
- "'You'd been you thirty-two months when you signed'": I had to push to follow this. Bisi is married to a series of women who are all her, and he is courting the next one. I got there around "I'd be a husband", but it was slow.
- "No party, agent or person shall present to the Keeper argument bearing on the Determination": I followed the rule, but not why her own notes would count as argument until Pell explained.
- "'the person occupying the seat on the date of Determination.'" and Pell's "the date, the archive passes": I lost track of who inherits what.

## Where I got bored

- The paragraph on the Standing Revision's page nine ("had wanted the Disputed Volume on the eleventh, nineteenth..."). I was waiting for someone to be in a room with a feeling.
- Pell's gate speech at the start, and his longer speech about spectacles and "treaty property". I enjoyed his voice, but I was waiting for him to finish.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- "the way other men carry a full bowl".
- "Twenty-two years of reading things that wanted a reaction from her had left it with almost nothing to do on its own."
- The joke, "Nothing... It waved." The first time it was small and sweet. The second time, when she laughs "like a hiccup", I felt it hard.
- "Mina's ears went red. Oyelaran's face did nothing, which was the kindest thing it knew how to do".
- "'You came back from the last one liking eels.'" This is where the story landed for me.
- The heron: "It says *look at herons*", and "the legs first".
- Ros setting down her eraser and later picking it up again.
- "Sly, underlined once, lightly", and she can't remember why. That hurt.
- The ending: her handwriting still showing in the raking light, "shallow as the trail of a snail on a step". I was glad the story didn't explain it.

## What I think the story is about

A woman who serves as judge in a long dispute is about to turn into a different person, as every keeper does on a schedule, and she tries to leave her successor a warning about the case. She ends up rubbing out all her notes to obey the rules, and her husband and neighbours help her do it. They love the person she is, who will vanish. Her marks on the page are still faintly there.

## Who I cared about, and would I keep reading

Bisi, first, and Oyelaran, a close second. Ros and Hanne mattered in small ways. Yes, I would have kept reading past page 2. I was slightly wary at the start, with the treaty and the Parties, and what held me was Bisi and the joke.

## The question I'd still be thinking about

Did the Parties choose the term on purpose, so that she would turn at the worst moment? Pell wouldn't say, and neither would the story. I'd also wonder what the next woman will make of the white margin and the faint marks.
