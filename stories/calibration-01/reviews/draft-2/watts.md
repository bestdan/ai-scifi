## The one thing
Let her feel the cost of the cold sentence once. Pell has confirmed her notes become argument, so she erases. The line "a little more like everyone, which was the one woman the Parties could hedge against" has already told her the erasing makes her next hedgeable. She proceeds with no friction. The heron pays some of that, but a clause of her own hesitation would finish it.

## Notes
1. **The cold sentence (bites, line-level):** It now sits where it belongs, after the term arithmetic and before the "Sly" note, and "Perhaps" keeps it a hypothesis. The logic follows from her premise: erasing removes the variance that made her a particular woman. Naming the average as "the one woman" is exact. Keep it. The only gap is the one above: nothing in her answers it.

2. **Over-lucid lines (fixed, one residue, line-level):** The "Sly" paragraph now ends in failure ("could not find what had been sly in it, or what had pleased her so much"). That is the right register, and a biology reader would nod. "She had wanted the seat." is much better as one plain sentence, but it still reads as an audit that arrives conveniently. Try putting it where she is not doing sums, or making her half-wrong about why.

3. **The 3 a.m. reasoning (line-level):** "They had built the term around the latest she could possibly be" is still a clean deduction made mid-turn. It is plausible for a woman who has read the treaty forty times. Hanne's "I know" afterwards keeps it from being a revelation.

4. **Heron versus "Sly" (resolved):** Round 1's mismatch is gone. A note that reads cold (Sly) and one that reads compelling (*look at herons*) now sit in the same story, so the clause threatens for the right reason.

5. **Biology (passes):** Tea to tongue to card-tasting bread, with crying at the ramp and hunger at three, is consistent. Dr. Ferreira's swab against a colour chart is a nice dry touch.
