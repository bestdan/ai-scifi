## The one thing
Let the logic bite. The hedging argument ("Hedge widely enough and it came very close to keeping faith") is delivered as a comfort. Followed through, it says that erasing her reasons makes her next closer to the average of everyone she might become, which is exactly the self the Parties hedge against. The draft never raises this. Put one cold sentence of it in the 3 a.m. scene or the erasing night.

## Notes
1. **Prodrome (passes):** The body is right and specific. "It tasted of card." "she found she was crying. She stood by the boat ramp until it passed." The 3 a.m. eel-from-the-pot scene ("It tasted of something now. She had no word for it, and she ate half the pot") is the best thing in the draft. Smell-to-limbic crying and broken-sleep hunger are plausible neuroendocrine drift. Nothing here would make a biology reader roll their eyes.

2. **Introspection (mostly passes, two leaks):** Amendment 10 holds in the big moments. She never remarks on the joke, and "It was the same terrible joke. She could not stop" is exactly right. But "It read now like a note from a woman who enjoyed catching people out" is too well-formed. A real mind finds the old note faintly embarrassing, or inexplicable. Narrating the earlier self as a character type is the confabulation the next self would do later, with distance. Also "She had wanted the seat. She could admit that at three in the morning with eel on her breath" is a clean self-audit mid-turn. Cut to the bare fact, or make the admission wrong. Ref: Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson 2013 on how badly people forecast their own value change.

3. **Narration tells the laugh:** "and they both heard it." Cut. The laugh already landed.

4. **Flinch, the epiphany:** The trap is solved in two paragraphs ("They had built the term around the latest she could possibly be") and she moves on with no cost. Amendment 14's point, "she was an interval", is stated nowhere in her. She is wrong about what she was for; she should be wrong a little longer, or visibly pay for being right.

5. **Memory versus paper (convincing legally, thin dramatically):** Pell's ruling is good: "Memory is not presented. You may remember anything you like." But the draft's own evidence undercuts the motive. The "Sly" note "read now like a note from a woman who enjoyed catching people out", so it reads cold to her next. If old notes land cold, why do they threaten the clause? The story needs the opposite beat once: a note that reads *compelling* because it is in her hand. The heron ("It says *look at herons*") nearly does this; use that register.

6. **Ending:** The grooves are right. "She had always written lightly" is an explanation, and the image does not need it.

## What to protect
- Pell's spectacles and "the person, I'm sorry to say, is you."
- Pell's non-answer on whether they knew, and Hanne's rival reading met with "I know."
- The 3 a.m. scene's sensory specificity.
- The identical joke, with only the listener changed.
- The heron.

## Blind spot disclosure
This lens wants cognition to be messier and less articulate than the register of the story allows, and may be asking a warm, dry narrator to be colder than it should be. The tone charter favours the Banks/Le Guin register, so note 4 should be read as "cost, not misery."
