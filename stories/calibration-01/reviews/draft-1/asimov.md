## The one thing
Make Pell's ruling say plainly who the "Keeper" is on the date. The schedule definition and "the person, I'm sorry to say, is you" contradict each other, and the story's legal hinge rests on it.

## Notes

1. **Scene 5, Pell's ruling:** "'the person occupying the seat on the date of Determination.' ... becomes argument presented by a person, and the person, I'm sorry to say, is you." If the Keeper on the date is her next, the archive "passes" to someone else and the presenter is her old self. Pell calls it "you" either way. A reader cannot tell whether she or her next is the person. Add one clause fixing which self the treaty treats as the Keeper. Also, "the accounts ... pass intact" sits uneasily with erasing them in scene 6. Say that graphite is hers and paper is not.

2. **Scene 2, Hanne:** "had turned four times" at "in her eighties". A 25-year cycle gives three turns by 80. A careful reader will notice the cycle fails. Make it three.

3. **Arithmetic, scene 4 (verified):** 2y 8m + 22y 7m = 25y 3m, which is Hanne's 25 years plus "a season" if a season is a quarter. Pell's quarterly rounds make that inferable. The nine extra days leave the one-week settling a margin of two days. The sum is correct. Her tea went flat nine days out, exactly the latest edge, so the term was built to the latest possible turn.
   - "to the day" overclaims, since "season" is loose. Use "to the month".
   - Bisi's "when they came" in scene 1 has no antecedent. Say "when you signed", or the reader won't know it is the signing.
   - Fair play otherwise holds. All four inputs sit in scenes 1 and 2, so an attentive reader can beat her. Hanne's "season" and the 32 months are the two the reader must work for.

4. **Scene 4, the hedge paragraph:** "Hedge widely enough and it came very close to keeping faith." and "A Party could have written its accounts to her, to look faithful in the light she read by." The step from "latest turn" to "they hedge rather than game" is compressed into abstractions. State it as a rule: a date mid-self lets a Party game the known woman, and a date at the edge forces it to write for an unknown one.

5. **Scene 1:** "§ 14 ... the self that executes this instrument has been retained unedited" is a clue the story never uses. The Parties keep their old selves and she does not, which is the asymmetry the plot runs on. Use it in scene 4 or cut it.

6. **Scene 6:** Hanne's rival reading ("They picked a number they liked the look of.") is fair and clear. Keep it.

7. **Cuts, about 300 words:**
   - Scene 1 (about 75): "He said the term the way some people say a psalm, with the commas in."; "Somewhere in the margin of the third quarter's account there was a question mark in her hand, and nothing after it."; "which on the boat meant holding them on my knees"; "Behind him the lake was flat and grey as a spoon."
   - Scene 2 (about 40): the crying-by-the-ramp paragraph; the weed-and-outboard-fuel sentence.
   - Scene 3 (about 40): Bisi washing the bowl and the boat-shed planes sentence.
   - Scene 4 (about 70): "She ate cold eel stew ... half the pot"; the hungry-girl opening; "They had had twenty-two years to learn the woman ... what made her pencil move".
   - Scene 6 (about 60): Hanne's eraser "grey and soft as a mushroom" detail; "Erasing makes a dry, busy sound, like something small living in a wall."; "Hanne blew the crumbs off."

## What to protect
The scene-1 joke planted word for word in scene 6. Pell's "Nobody can present you with your own spectacles." The four inputs and the scene-4 sum on the envelope. The heron.

## Blind spot disclosure
This lens wants every fact solvable and treats the sum as the story. It undervalues the grooves and Ros's eraser, and it may overprice the legal clarity of scene 5 against the comic fog Pell is meant to make.
