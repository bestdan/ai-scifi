# First Reader report: Raking Light, draft 1

## Where I got lost

- "the term of 'twenty-two years, seven months and nine days' has nine days to run." I didn't know what a "term" was or what was ending. The headings (Nine, Eight, Seven) read as chapter names; I only guessed they were a countdown much later.
- "an integer. This one was 7. ... she had never learned what they counted." I couldn't tell what the numbers meant or whether I was supposed to care. Same for "the Disputed Volume", "the Settled Question", "the Standing Revision". These are names I never got a handle on.
- "had turned four times" (Hanne), and "Started." from the doctor after a tongue swab. It took me a good while to work out that people periodically become a new person, and that the taste going is the warning. I was working it out through the whole second scene.
- "Your next will like the bakery." Whose "next"? I understood by the letter scene, but not before.
- "§ 9 ... It did not say *except the Keeper*." I followed that there was a rule and a gap, but not what the gap would let her do, until the letter was torn up.
- The night-before arithmetic ("Thirty-two months since her last turn ... twenty-five years and three months"). I trusted it rather than followed it. "They had built the term around the latest she could possibly be" lost me. I didn't see why that mattered, and the paragraph after it about hedging across every woman she might turn out to be was hard going.
- "Hanne's 'too big to notice a woman's tongue going flat. They picked a number they liked the look of.'" I couldn't tell which of two readings she meant, or what the first one was.

## Where I got bored

- Pell's first speech at the gate ("received at the office at ten to six and carried here by me without once being set down ..."). Funny for a line, but I was waiting for something to happen.
- Pell's second visit, "While you hold the seat, Keeper ... your marginalia are the Keeper's own apparatus, like your spectacles." Long legal explaining, and I was waiting for her to just decide.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- The joke: "Nothing," said Bisi. "It waved." And then at the end, when she laughs, "far too loud for the kitchen ... She could not stop." Then "Bisi did not laugh ... and they both heard it." That was the best moment in the story for me.
- "carried the satchel level in front of him, the way other men carry a full bowl."
- The bakery heel of bread: "It tasted of card. Oyelaran said it was very good."
- "Mina's ears went red." and Ros saying only "Mina."
- The heron: "It says *look at herons*," and then erasing it "the legs first."
- Bisi's "I'd be a husband."
- "He did not argue, which was how she knew he had known the answer before he asked."
- The ending: her handwriting still standing in the blank margin, "She had always written lightly."

## What I think the story is about

A woman who is periodically replaced by a new version of herself spends her last nine days before the change erasing every note she has written, to keep the next her from being steered by this one. She loses her taste for food and her sense of humour as it nears, her husband tries to keep her with jokes and cooking, and she can't entirely wipe herself out.

## Who I cared about, and would I keep reading

I cared about Bisi most, and about Oyelaran once the husband and the stew arrived. Yes, I'd have kept reading past page 2, but only just: the first page is mostly strange names, and it was the tea going tasteless and the bakery scene that pulled me in.

## The question I'd still be thinking about

Whether the new woman will be Oyelaran at all, and whether Bisi loves the one who is leaving or will start over with whoever comes. Also whether the "too big to notice" explanation or the carefully timed term was the true one, and I don't think I'm told.
