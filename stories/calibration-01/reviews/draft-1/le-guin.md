## The one thing
Let Sallow Reach exist outside the plot. The bible's ordinary day (keepers waving, never discussing work, paper-filled houses) is almost absent. Put two or three glimpses in the margins of scenes 1, 2 and 4, paid for by cutting the surplus words from Pell's speeches.

## Notes

1. **Lived-in?** Partly. The domestic detail is excellent: "Bisi had put out the tea and the knife she kept for her pencils, because he knew the order of her mornings better than she did." "Ros cut the heel off the loaf for her to try, because she did that for everyone." But the town is three rooms. No other keepers, no paper on the shelves, one child.
2. **People or functions?** Bisi is a person: "He did not argue, which was how she knew he had known the answer before he asked", then the boat shed "where there had been no boat for six years". Hanne nearly is ("chewed it with great thoroughness, to show what a self that liked bread could do"), yet she carries the exposition: "Otherwise we'd all be our grandparents forever." Mina's "Why does everybody have to?" exists to cue that line. Ros is the best minor figure ("for a long time she did not pick it up again, and nobody asked her to") but we never see what she wants.
3. **Erasing night.** Mostly earned. Ros's hesitation, Bisi's hand over the heron and "It says *look at herons*" give it friction. The sentiment leaks in "It was the same terrible joke. She could not stop." and "and they both heard it". The repetition already did that work, so cut both.
4. **Cultural texture.** Specific and respectful. Folasade and Sade, Adebisi and Bisi, Oduya, efo riro, pounded yam, and feeding as Bisi's love all read as real. The mixed town shows only in surnames (Lind, Ferreira, Pell). Two gaps. The dishes are named exactly when she can't taste them: "She could not taste it. Everything tasted of the thing with no name." And nobody speaks a word of Yoruba; one endearment or proverb from Bisi would do it.
5. **Coinages.** They work because they go unglossed. "Your next will like the bakery" and "Here, twice-met" are inferable on first hearing. "So we don't set" is the best of them. The bare integer ("This one was 7.") implies a whole polity.
6. **Ending.** "In the raking light her hand stood up out of it anyway" is the image. "She had always written lightly." and "the way she always had" then add two "always" beats. Stop on the grooves.

## What to protect
The heron exchange. Hanne's bun. Ros's flat hands. The joke told twice, with only the listener changed.

## Blind spot disclosure
I want more town than 2,500 words can carry and underweight the game theory. If cuts are needed, the Egan and Lem lenses should say which glimpses go.
