# Stage 4 stress test: Lem lens (the Skeptic)

This is a critique through a lens built from Lem's preoccupations. It is not Lem's voice or opinion.

**Blind spots:** I distrust genre pleasures readers enjoy, I prefer essay to scene, I tolerate cipher characters, and I lean bleak. Treat my pushes toward opacity as a bid to be weighed by Banks, Le Guin and the First Reader, not a verdict. The tone charter holds. I aim for the comic and awestruck.

## Notes

1. **[must-fix] The polities are humans in a costume made of prose.** Their motive is a legible game-theory move: avoid a gameable judge. Their manner is a courteous bureaucrat's. "Faintly funny in its exactness" is a human wit. Nothing in the text resists Oyelaran. Suggestion: put one unexplained, non-human fact on the page and never resolve it. For example, § 14 fixes the lake's heron count at an exact figure, or one quarterly account carries a section whose purpose she never works out after twenty-two years. The polities must be seen to want things the story cannot name.

2. **[must-fix] Her realization flatters.** "They wanted an unknown judge" is a clean, solved motive, and a baseline human reads two posthuman minds in one evening from a date. Keep it a hypothesis, her best reading, held by a self that is ending. Give one rival reading: the forecast spread made the date a coincidence, or the date serves a purpose she can't see. The clerk, who may not discuss the Determination, neither confirms nor denies. She erases anyway, so the erasure is an act taken under uncertainty. That costs more than an erasure taken under proof.

3. **[must-fix] The resolution resolves too much.** The bible closes every edge case, and "the game theory has to hold" makes the mechanism fully explicit. Stop before the Determination. Never say what the successor rules, or what the notes said. Leave the graphite grooves as the sole open question. The last scene is the neighbours with their erasers, with no verdict anywhere.

4. **[should] Puncture the vanity with a clause, not a speech.** Her vanity is that she was chosen for her conscience. Let the treaty itself deflate it. The clerk reads aloud a schedule that lists "Keeper (replaceable, see § 3, § 9)" between the lake's boat-hire and its paper stock. He loves the clause and doesn't notice what it says about her. This is the comic centre, and the reader should see it before she does.

5. **[should] Let the document carry the reveal.** Don't narrate "she understood". Quote § 9 in full. Quote the term line "twenty-two years, four months and nine days". Give the forecast for her turn as a one-line chart entry from the doctor's swab chart. The reader does the arithmetic alongside her, which gives the fair-play clue some weight. Roughly 150 words of exhibit replace 300 of interior.

6. **[could] Open on an account fragment.** Start with a 60-word excerpt of a Settled Question quarterly, then cut to the tasteless tea. The prose register is then shown, not described, and the polities are met first as text. Keep it short, because I overrate documents and the story needs its scenes.

## Summary

The warm, domestic scenes with Bisi and the neighbours are the story's strength, and I'd leave them alone. My notes concern what is claimed about the polities, and how much the ending claims.
