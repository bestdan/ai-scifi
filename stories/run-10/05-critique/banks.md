# Stage 5 critique: Banks lens

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens favours sprawl, set pieces and worldbuilding. It is a poor judge of what a 5,400-word story can hold, so defer to Chiang and Asimov on cuts. Where I ask for more, something else must go.

1. **[must-fix] Scale is stated, not felt.** The only vastness is scene 6: "forty million earpieces hitch". That is a number. Scene 1 has "bless" "around the hall", which is hall-sized. Give one concrete moment where the cough leaves the building. For example, Sione steps outside after the scene 1 hitch and sees a hauler halt her sled on the open shelf, say "bless" to nobody, and start the sentence again. Or let a failed promise be a physical thing: a sled left stopped on the ice. The bible already holds the image (sample paragraph). Put it in the outline and anchor scene 6's number to it.

2. **[must-fix] The cost is first met at the twist, and it should arrive earlier.** Ressa is introduced in scene 5, so the people who pay are a name on the day the reader learns why. Reorder the information: let a failed contract surface sideways in scene 3. Sione hears a hauler and her buyer lose a promise in a corridor, and he reads it as a cheating delegate. Scene 5 then re-reads that scene, as *Use of Weapons* does. It costs about 100 words. It also gives the "cheat" theory a victim, and it makes the fair-play plant stronger than the register entries alone.

3. **[should] Wit thins where the story darkens.** Scenes 1 to 3 have Vell, Tikk, lu pulu and smugness. Scenes 4 and 5 are grief, a misflag and a log, and no joke is listed. A bleak middle with no wit reads as self-pitying. Fix: give ennu's annotations dry comedy in the entries at 4,520 and 4,602, written in its seat-terms. For example, it files Sione's weather report under "a promise about a place neither seat has stood in". Also keep Vell funny while he gives the Stakes line in scene 5.

4. **[should] Sione watches his own climax.** Scene 6 has him translate while two peoples offer to change the word for him. In scene 7, ennu decides and he closes promises. The cost is real, but his one act is bookkeeping. Give him a choice in scene 6 or 7 that he could get wrong: say yes to the trial and be talked out of it by ennu, or keep all forty open and learn what that costs. As written, "power that needs" is ennu's role, not his.

5. **[could] Who's missing: Earth.** Sione is the human vote that Vell wants, yet no human authority appears. Nobody asks who hired him, who pays, or what Earth wants from a swing vote. One line in scene 6 would do: a message from home that advises him, politely, to say yes. That gives the trial a dirty-work backer (power that means well) at no cost in scenes.

**What each part asks.** I checked the table. Parts I to IV each raise a new question, and I flag no repeated tradeoff. Part III is one scene and carries the twist, so scene 3's cheat-hunt must be sharp enough to hold Part II.

**Reordering.** Linear order suits first person and the form device. I would not braid the timelines. Only the reorder in note 2 is worth it.
