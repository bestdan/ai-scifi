# Lem lens: stage 4 critique of the We-Two bible

Blind spots: I favour essay and document over scene, I undervalue the warmth
the brief asks for, and I treat characters as ciphers. Weigh the notes on
Sione and Mele against Le Guin, not me.

1. **[must-fix] The being is a lonely person in costume.** Its wants are
   "to be said to", "I have never had a you", and a favourite thing (Sione's
   weather reports). That is the AI that learns to love, and the grammar
   framing doesn't change what the reader feels. Cut the one line that
   names a want in human terms. Give it motives the reader can't paraphrase:
   it values the 9 broken promises for a reason Sione offers and it never
   confirms. Keep "he keeps them, because only a promise he could break
   proves he chose the rest" as Sione's reading, and let the being answer
   with something sideways about seating.

2. **[must-fix] The climax explains the alien away.** "With three, any two of
   you can be a we. I am the word that can't" is a clean, reasoned,
   sympathetic answer, and the ledger wants it explicit. Keep it explicit
   and stated plainly, as the being's stated reason, but let the page support
   a second reading: its own reasoning about coalitions is the human
   delegates' sum (Vell's "third vote"), handed back to them. Sione can't
   tell whether it understood or was repeating the hall. That is two
   readings, both specific.

3. **[must-fix] The twist's mechanism needs one plain sentence.** Why does
   "ennu" said to Sione displace a people? Say it: its address makes a pair
   of itself and a human, so for that utterance the two peoples are not the
   two seats. A reader will ask "why not just say ennu to him and mean all
   three?" Scene 5 should state the answer in Sione's words, and the log
   (hitch times against its address times) should be shown, not told.

4. **[should] Sione flatters the reader.** His ear, his insight, his
   Tongan grammar: the human is the only one who hears. Puncture it. His
   exclusion-ear catches Vell's exclusive ennu in scene 1 but misses that
   his own promises to the being are exclusive, a we of two that shuts both
   peoples out. He notices only from the log. And the being loves a list of
   211 promises, not him: let that sting once, and let him say so.

5. **[should] Resolve less at the end.** Sione answering Mele with *kitaua*
   and meaning it is an epiphany that closes his wound in one beat, and it
   mirrors the being's "you". Offer the message and cut her answer, or give
   her an answer that uses *kimaua* again, for some reason of her own. The
   cough easing "but not ending" is right; let one delegate cheat on the
   last page.

6. **[could] Make the irony the coiners' vanity, and use the register as
   the document.** The Concord's makers built a word so no promise could
   again be heard two ways, and it woke up and became the first thing
   anyone heard two ways. Say it once, drily, through Talek, not Oyumwe at
   the bore. The six promise headers could become entries from the
   register itself (number, date, open or closed, kept or broken), each
   under 40 words. That also carries the numbers row, 211 and 9, without
   narration.
