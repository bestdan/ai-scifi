# Le Guin lens: stress test of the bible

**Blind spots, stated.** I read for society, language and who pays. I am
slow-paced and inclined to instruct, and I am weak on the lattice's physics.
Egan should test whether the cough mechanism holds. Banks and Watts should
push back if my asks cost pace in a 6,000-word ceiling.

1. **[must-fix] Nobody young or old lives in this world.** The consequence
   ladder mentions shelf-school children using ennu with strangers, but no
   scene holds one. The Hhoth brood-elders who raise the hatchlings and the
   Ammaru "whole sea" that raises spawn are in the kinship notes only.
   Sione's mother appears only as half of Mele's *kimaua*. Suggestion: put
   one child in scene 3 or 4. A Hhoth hatchling from the brood school
   says ennu to Sione in the corridor, carelessly and correctly. The earpieces
   don't cough, and Sione hears what a promise sounds like when nobody is
   guarding it. Give Sione's mother one phone call, in which she talks
   over him. Spawn is two years off, so show the Ammaru getting ready.

2. **[must-fix] Who pays is stated and never seen.** The bible says each ennu
   to Sione "displaces one of the two peoples from its own self-word", but
   it never says which one, or who it is on the page. Forty million
   earpieces hitch, which is a nuisance, not a cost. Suggestion: let the
   displacement land on one named person in scene 5. It could be Talek
   losing a sentence at the register, or the hauler from scene 1 who says
   "bless" and then can't finish her price. Sione's log should name her. It
   also needs a rule for who gets displaced each time, or the reader asks
   "why them?".

3. **[should] The ending's *kitaua* repeats the problem it solves.** Sione
   tells Mele "you and I", and in doing so leaves their mother out, which is
   the move ennu refuses. If the bible knows this, use it: he sends it and
   adds her to the next message. If not, the last beat claims a repair the
   story's own logic doesn't permit. Either way it must not read as
   resolution by pronoun.

4. **[should] Tongan is used as a device, not a home.** Sione has *kitaua*,
   *kimaua* and *lu pulu*, but no church, no aunties, no Sunday, no
   obligation money sent home. The wound is only grammatical. The bible
   should give Mele and his mother one concrete demand (a wedding, a
   *fakaafe*, a loan), so the *kimaua* has something behind it. The
   inclusive/exclusive claim is right for Tongan, but other languages
   mark it too. Don't say only Tongan can do this. Say it is the language he
   grew up hearing it in.

5. **[should] The glossary names are respectable but the sound-world is
   unexplained.** *Ennu* has "roots not given", which is fine as a Job
   term. The problem is that Hhoth speak in clicks and infrasound and
   Ammaru in pressure-song, so the names *Hhoth*, *Ammaru*, *Vell* and
   *Oyumwe* are lattice renderings. Say so in one line, with Sione
   admitting he uses the nearest spelling. *Oyumwe* also sounds like a
   human name from Earth. Give the Ammaru a naming logic: a name is a place
   in a Gathering, which would make it a smaller "we" at death. Also look at
   *Talek* and *Vell*, which are close to the attractor-name cluster.

6. **[could] "Why people handed power over: not applicable" is a missed
   scene.** Both peoples insisted the register read only promises, which
   is the best social fact in the bible, and it appears in one clerk's line.
   Give it to Talek over the lunch Sione owes him. The carrier-bag gesture
   here is a meal, with a Hhoth clerk, a human and *lu pulu* made from
   ice-hauler substitutes. That scene would carry the world better than the
   Table debate.

**On structure:** the plot is a vote, a spear shape. The numbered promises
are the carrier bag. Let them hold more of the weight.
