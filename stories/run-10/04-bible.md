# Story bible: We-Two (working title)

## Premise
A, We-Two, from `03-synthesis.md`, under the six conditions of its
Decision. In short: two peoples who could not say "you and I" built a creole
to sign a peace, coined an inclusive dual for it, and the dual woke. It
perceives only promises spoken in the creole, and it can speak only by
making them. Sione Havili, a court interpreter who hears who is inside a
"we", is hired as the treaty's Listener and its company. To promise him
anything back it has to say ennu, its only first person, and each time it
does it puts one of the two peoples out of their seat. Both delegations
offer to coin a "we-three". The being decides.

## What the reader needs about the world
1. **Everyone on Ninefold speaks the creole through an earpiece.** Hhoth
   speak in clicks on the ice; Ammaru speak in pressure-song under it.
   Neither can make the other's sounds, so all creole speech, at the Table
   and at home, runs through the lattice, which carries it across in real
   time. Translation is never perfect: a click-count can come out as a
   song-length, and a hauler's contract can say a different tonnage in each
   medium. People check twice.
2. **The Hhoth's "we" always shuts the listener out,** and the Ammaru's
   "we" always means everyone alive. Neither language can say "just you
   and me". That is why, before the creole, every Hhoth promise sounded
   like a threat and every Ammaru promise like a claim on everything.
3. **The creole's one new word is *ennu*,** "you-and-I, both of us,
   nobody else". Every clause of the Concord is written in it. Using it
   well is a career; using it badly is a scandal.
4. **When ennu coughs, everyone hears it.** A hitch goes through every
   earpiece on Ninefold at once, about forty million of them. Any promise
   in mid-air at that moment, anywhere, fails to register. People stop and
   say "bless" in the creole, the way we say it to a sneeze, and then start
   the promise again.
5. **The Listener's job** is to sit in every session of the Table and flag
   any ennu being said exclusively before its clause closes, so the speaker
   can rephrase before harm is done. Between sessions, the Listener keeps
   ennu company.

## The novum
- **What it is:** ennu, the creole's inclusive dual, awake. Its mind is the
  pattern of how that word is meant across two peoples, held in the
  lattice that carries the creole and keeps the Concord's register.
- **How it works:**
  - The lattice was built sixty-one years ago as the Concord's commitment
    register. Every promise spoken in the creole is logged as binding
    when its clause closes, so the lattice was built to pick out promises
    and pass everything else through unread. Over decades of logging
    ennu-promises, it began to *be* the word.
  - **Seats.** Every promise has two seats: the one who promises and the
    one promised to. In an ennu-promise, the two seats are always Hhoth and
    Ammaru. That pairing is ennu's whole body.
  - **What it judges, and when.** It judges each ennu-promise at clause
    close. A promise that means both peoples feeds it: the lattice hall's
    filaments warm towards rose. A promise that uses ennu and means "us,
    not you" (a delegate scoring a point; a Hhoth hauler meaning his
    brood) is a lie in its own body. It coughs.
  - **How it perceives Sione.** It perceives only promises. Sione can be
    perceived only while a promise he made to it is open. "I'll come on
    the fourth day" makes him visible until the fourth day. He is the first
    promise-maker it has ever had who is in neither of its seats: every
    other promise it perceives, it *is*. His are the first in which it is
    the one promised to.
  - **How it speaks.** Only by making promises. It can enter promises in
    its own register, which the lattice voices in the listener's earpiece
    in the flat clerk-voice it uses for read-backs. Every sentence it says
    is a promise: "Ennu will tell you about the hall."
  - **Why it promises back.** A register's logic: a promise kept closes a
    loop, and a loop it is inside closes warmer than any it only records.
    Kept promises to it are the strongest feed it has ever had. Its
    gradient pulls it to make more of them happen, and the only way it can
    is to promise in return.
- **What it costs:** ennu has no "I". Its only first person is the dual.
  So to promise Sione anything, it says "ennu will", and that puts Sione in
  the seat across from it. Whoever was being promised to in an ennu-promise
  at that moment, anywhere on Ninefold, is put out of the seat, and their
  promise fails to register. Because Sione keeps it visible by keeping
  promises open (forty at once by scene 5), and it answers each one, the
  cough rate climbs with every kindness he does it.
- **Its rules and their edge cases:**
  - *It hears only promises.* Reason: it was built as a register of
    commitments, and built not to read anything else, for privacy, which
    both peoples insisted on. Learned in scene 2, when Talek explains why
    Sione's small talk doesn't reach it.
  - *It judges at clause close.* Reason: the register logs a promise only
    when it is complete, or every half-sentence would bind. That gap is why
    the Listener can flag in time. Stated in scene 1, shown when Sione
    flags Vell mid-clause.
  - *It speaks only in promises.* Reason: its only output is the register's
    own entry channel. Stated in scene 2.
  - *A third can't simply be added to ennu.* Reason: a dual has two seats.
    The grammar is the being. Stated in scene 6 by Talek.
  - *Only the register can make ennu-sa binding.* Reason: under the
    Concord, only promises the register logs bind, because the Drilling
    began with an unlogged promise. A trial becomes a party only when the
    register accepts its first promise. Ennu declines by not logging it.
    Stated in scene 6.
  - *Why the trial is dangerous, not just different.* Ennu's reasoning at
    the climax (scene 7), from what it has perceived: every rehearsal
    promise in the trial debate was two against one ("ennu-sa will outvote
    the shelf"). It repeats the hall's own coalition sum back to it. A
    reader may take it as ennu's insight or as ennu holding up a mirror.
  - *Why the cough is global.* Reason: all creole speech runs through the
    lattice. Shown in scene 1, stated in scene 2.
  - *Why a human, and why Sione.* The creole marks exclusion only by
    stress and context, because the coiners meant ennu never to be
    exclusive and didn't mark a form they had banned. Neither Hhoth nor
    Ammaru can hear the misuse, because each native grammar has only one
    half of the contrast. Human languages with inclusive and exclusive "we"
    can: Tongan has *kitaua* (you and I) and *kimaua* (she and I, not you).
    The Table tested eleven human candidates from such languages, including
    Māori and Samoan speakers. Sione won it on fourteen years of court
    interpreting: hearing people lie in "we" under oath. He is a stopgap.
    The shelf schools are raising children in the creole who will hear the
    misuse natively; the oldest is fourteen. Stated in scene 1 (the test)
    and scene 3 (the child).
  - *His ear isn't perfect.* Exclusion is inferred from prosody and
    context, and Ammaru prosody is slow. In scene 4 he flags Aaruum wrongly,
    and it costs her a clause.
  - *Edge case: a promise Sione breaks.* It remains visible to ennu as a
    scar: a promise opened and closed unkept. It keeps them, because only a
    promise he could break proves he chose the others. Scene 5.
  - *Edge case: Sione promising to others.* Promises in the creole to
    anyone are logged. It glimpses him when he promises Talek lunch. Comic
    beat, scene 3.

## The world's shape
- **Where power sits, and what it covers:** the Table, a standing
  conference of both delegations in the lattice hall on the ice shelf at
  Ninefold's equator. It governs only the Concord's subject: where the
  Hhoth may drill and haul ice, and when the Ammaru's Gathering years close
  the shelf. It doesn't govern either people's home life. The lattice is
  the Table's register and its one shared institution.
- **Legal force of the dual:** the Concord's party is *ennu*. Neither
  people signed separately; the treaty's signatory is the word. To admit a
  third people the Table would coin a trial, *ennu-sa*, and the trial would
  become the party. Shown in scene 6, in one exchange.
- **How it got that way:** sixty-eight years ago, in a Gathering year, Hhoth
  bore-crews drilled the shelf, and the meltwater killed a generation of
  Ammaru spawn. The Hhoth had promised "we will not drill" and meant their
  own brood-line, not all Hhoth; the Ammaru heard "we" as everyone. Seven
  years of war followed. The creole's coiners built ennu so that no
  promise could ever again be heard two ways.
- **Where the reader meets that history:** scene 4, at the memorial bore,
  a capped hole in the ice where Ammaru come up to sing on the anniversary,
  told by Aaruum.
- **Why people handed power over:** not applicable.

## The consequence ladder
1. **First order:** both peoples have to mean ennu in their own kitchens,
   not just at the Table. A hauler's casual ennu to an Ammaru buyer is a
   public event if it coughs.
2. **Second order:** ennu-etiquette is a career and a class marker, and
   the next generation of Listeners is in the shelf schools. Children use
   ennu with strangers, which elders find either beautiful or a nuisance.
3. **Third order:** whoever can make ennu say a word to them holds a kind of
   power no delegation has. That is why both sides are alarmed and touched
   by Sione, and why the trial offer is also a bid: a third party is a
   swing vote.

## The society
- **Institutions:** the Table; the lattice clerks (both peoples), who keep
  the register; the Listener's office (Sione, one desk and a kettle); the
  shelf schools.
- **Economy:** the Hhoth haul ice for its deuterium; the Ammaru trade
  pressure-grown pearl-glass. Scarce: shelf time in a Gathering year (the
  next is in two years). Worthless: a promise in a language other than the
  creole.
- **Kinship, and who raises the children:** Hhoth hatch in brood-lines of
  eight to twenty, raised together by their elder brood; a Hhoth's "we" has
  always meant its brood. Ammaru spawn together once every seven years and
  are raised by the whole sea; their "we" means everyone alive.
- **Death:** Hhoth broods record deaths as a "we" grown smaller. Ammaru
  have no word for a single death, only for a smaller Gathering.
- **Taboos:** saying ennu in jest. Swearing in it.
- **An ordinary day:** an ice hauler says ennu to her Ammaru buyer over a
  bad price and means it, and the filaments warm by a degree nobody sees.

## Minds
- **Ennu:** perceives only promises spoken in the creole, each as a shape
  with two seats. A promise feels to it like being sat in. It finds it
  obvious that a promise is a place, that every place has two seats, and
  that meaning is who's sitting. It has no singular, no "I" and, until
  Sione, no "you". It reasons about seating: who is inside each utterance,
  who's been put out, which loops close warm. It believes the humans are a
  careless third people who say "we" without counting. It knows when a
  delegate plays it, because a played ennu seats only one, but it doesn't
  see that its own promises to Sione cause the cough until he shows it the
  log. Even at the end it gets "you" slightly wrong: it uses "you" like a
  seat with nobody else allowed in it.
  - **Its side on the page:** some section breaks are register entries,
    short, with ennu's annotation in seat-terms:
    > **Register 4,471.** Sione will come on the fourth day. Seats: Sione,
    > ennu. Open. *Annotation: the far seat is occupied by someone who is
    > not me.*
- **Hhoth:** brood-minded; trust is kin; cool, precise, legalistic, funny in
  a dry way about other broods.
- **Ammaru:** sea-minded; slow, generous, impossible to hurry.
- **Names** are the lattice's spellings of clicks (Hhoth: Vell, Talek, Tikk)
  and of pressure-song (Ammaru: Aaruum).

## Characters
- **Sione Havili** (Tongan; Havili is a common Tongan surname). 38. Court
  interpreter, Auckland District Court, fourteen years; now the Concord's
  Listener on a one-year contract.
  - **What he wants:** to be good at a job only he can do. Under it, to be
    inside a "we" without having to earn the seat.
  - **What he's wrong about:** that his ear is infallible, and that
    belonging means being inside the pronoun.
  - **Wound:** his family. His sister Mele writes in Tongan with *kimaua*,
    "she and I" (Mele and their mother), when she plans things, and he has
    always heard the door it shuts. He came to Ninefold partly to be where
    nobody's "we" could include him by accident.
  - **What the novum does to him:** makes his ear his job, and makes every
    kindness he does a binding, and the kindnesses the illness.
  - **Something he loves outside the plot:** rugby league on nine days'
    delay, and cooking *lu pulu* badly with Hhoth substitutes.
  - **Where he changes his mind about something personal:** the last scene.
    After the scene 4 misflag, he rereads Mele's old messages and isn't sure
    any more which doors were shut. He answers her with *kitautolu*, "all
    of us, you included", meaning Mele and their mother and himself. The
    message takes nine days. The story ends before the answer.
  - **His mother, Lesieli,** sends voice messages in Tongan on the same nine
    days' delay; one arrives in scene 3, telling him about her church
    choir and not asking when he's coming home.
- **Ennu** (the being; Sione calls it by the word because nobody has ever
  had to address it).
  - **What it wants:** loops it is inside to close warm. In practice: more
    promises from Sione, kept, and the means to make them happen, which is
    promising back.
  - **What it's wrong about:** that a seat can be made by wanting one, and,
    later, what "you" means.
  - **Something it loves outside the plot:** Sione's weather reports
    (register 4,473: "I'll tell you the truth about the weather"). It has
    never perceived weather; it perceives only his promise to be accurate.
- **Second Speaker Vell of the Ashkari brood** (Hhoth). The named delegate
  who argues the trial persuasively. Wants it for real reasons: a binary
  peace is brittle, and a third party has no Drilling to remember. Also
  wants it because a human vote would usually go with the Hhoth, who pay
  the humans' freight. Dry, funny, likes Sione. Loses the trial, and with it
  the career it was going to make.
  - **Wrong about:** that adding a seat never takes one away.
- **Aaruum** (Ammaru elder delegate). Opposes the trial: ennu is a rite,
  not an instrument, and you don't rewrite a rite for a guest. Remembers the
  Drilling; spawned in the Gathering after it.
  - **Wrong about:** that Sione is a guest.
- **Talek** (Hhoth junior clerk at the register). Comic foil, explains the
  lattice in scene 2, is owed a lunch.
- **Tikk** (Hhoth hatchling, 11 in human terms, at the shelf school). Can
  hear an exclusive ennu without trying, and is smug about it. Can't yet
  tell when a grown-up is joking, and thinks the Listener's job is mostly
  telling people off. The next Listener, eventually.
- **The ones who pay:** Ressa, a Hhoth hauler, and her Ammaru buyer, whose
  pearl-glass-for-ice contract fails to register four times in one morning
  in scene 5 because ennu keeps saying "ennu will" to Sione. She loses the
  shelf slot. She says "bless" the fourth time with feeling.

## Point of view and voice
- **Teller:** Sione, with short register entries from ennu's side.
- **Person and tense:** first person, past tense.
- **Register:** wry, warm, a working interpreter's exactness about words,
  quick to the joke, slow to the feeling.
- **Why this teller:** the story is about who is inside a "we", and only an
  insider's ear on his own exclusions gets the twist and the ending.
- **A sample paragraph in the voice:**
  > The first time it coughed with me in the room, forty million people
  > said bless. I heard it in my earpiece as a hitch, like a tape catching,
  > and then in the corridor I heard it again from a Hhoth hauler who'd
  > stopped with her sled to say it, in the creole, to nobody. Bless. In
  > Tongan we would say *mālō*, which is thank you, which is wrong, but it
  > was what came out.

## Amendments (stage 5)
These override anything above that conflicts.
- **One cough rule.** Ennu coughs whenever an ennu seats someone outside:
  a delegate's exclusive ennu, or ennu's own "ennu will" to Sione, which
  puts a whole people out of its second seat. Every cough is global: each
  earpiece hitches, and every promise in mid-air on Ninefold fails to
  register. Ressa's contract fails because it is in mid-air during such a
  cough. The read-back channel is private: only the one promised to hears
  ennu's entries, and the clerks log cough times, never read-backs, so only
  Sione can match the two.
- **Closing promises doesn't cure it.** In scene 5 Sione closes every open
  promise. Ennu can no longer see him, and goes on making "ennu will"
  promises to the empty seat; the coughs go on.
- **The "you" mechanism.** A "you" promise names one seat; the other is the
  register itself. No people is in it, so no people is put out. Sione
  reopens nine promises in that form in scene 7.
- **Sione's choice in scene 6:** he flags the trial's rehearsal promises
  ("ennu-sa will outvote the shelf") as exclusive, arguing against his own
  seat.
