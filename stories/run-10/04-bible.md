# Story bible: We-Two (working title)

## Premise
A, We-Two, from `03-synthesis.md`, under the six conditions of its
Decision. In short: two peoples who could not say "you and I" built a creole
to sign a peace, coined an inclusive dual for it, and the dual woke. It
perceives only promises spoken in the creole. Sione Havili, a court
interpreter who hears who is inside a "we" without trying, is hired as the
treaty's Listener and its company. It comes to love him, starts addressing
him in the dual, and the dual can't hold a third. Both delegations offer to
coin a "we-three". The being decides.

## What the reader needs about the world
1. **Everyone on Ninefold speaks the creole through an earpiece.** Hhoth
   speak in clicks and infrasound on the ice; Ammaru speak in pressure-song
   under it. Neither can make the other's sounds, so all creole speech, at
   the Table and at home, runs through the lattice, which carries it from
   one medium to the other in real time.
2. **The Hhoth's "we" always shuts the listener out,** and the Ammaru's
   "we" always means everyone alive. Neither language can say "just you
   and me". That is why, before the creole, every Hhoth promise sounded
   like a threat and every Ammaru promise like a claim on everything.
3. **The creole's one new word is *ennu*,** "you-and-I, both of us,
   nobody else". Every clause of the Concord is written in it. Using it
   well is a career; using it badly is a scandal.
4. **When ennu coughs, everyone hears it.** A hitch goes through every
   earpiece on Ninefold at once, about forty million of them. People stop
   mid-sentence, and say "bless" in the creole, the way we say it to a
   sneeze.
5. **The Listener's job** is to sit in every session of the Table and flag
   any ennu said exclusively, before it does harm. Between sessions, the
   Listener keeps ennu company.

## The novum
- **What it is:** ennu, the creole's inclusive dual, awake. Its mind is the
  pattern of how that word is meant across two peoples, held in the
  lattice that carries the creole.
- **How it works:**
  - The lattice was built sixty-one years ago as the Concord's
    commitment register. Every promise spoken in the creole is logged as
    binding, so the lattice was built to pick out promises and pass
    everything else through unread. Over decades of logging ennu-promises,
    it began to *be* the word. Its senses are what it was built to see:
    promises, and nothing else.
  - A promise that uses ennu and means both peoples feeds it. The lattice
    hall's filaments warm towards rose.
  - A promise that uses ennu and means "us, not you" (a delegate scoring a
    point; a Hhoth hauler saying it to an Ammaru and meaning his brood) is
    a lie in its own body. It coughs, and every earpiece hitches.
  - It can perceive Sione only while he makes it promises. "I'll come on
    the fourth day" makes him visible for as long as the promise is open.
    He keeps a numbered list.
- **What it costs:** to be seen by it, Sione has to bind himself, promise
  by promise. To love him back, it has to say ennu to him, and each time it
  does, one of the two peoples is displaced from its own self-word. That is
  the cough no delegate is causing.
- **Its rules and their edge cases:**
  - *It hears only promises.* Reason: it was a register of commitments, and
    built not to read anything else (for privacy; both peoples insisted).
    Learned in scene 2, when the clerk explains why Sione's small talk
    doesn't reach it.
  - *A third can't simply be added to ennu.* Reason: a dual has two seats.
    The grammar is what the being is; changing the grammar changes the
    being. Stated in scene 6 by the being itself.
  - *Why the trial is dangerous, not just different.* Reason: with two,
    every "we" includes both peoples. With three, any "we" can be two
    against one. Coalitions only exist from three up. This is the being's
    reasoning at the climax (scene 7), made explicit in its own words.
  - *Why the cough is global.* Reason: all creole speech runs through the
    lattice. Shown in scene 1, stated in scene 2.
  - *Why the Listener must be human.* Reason: Tongan marks inclusive and
    exclusive "we" in the dual and the plural (*kitaua*, you and I;
    *kimaua*, she and I, not you). A Tongan speaker hears exclusion in a
    "we" the way a musician hears a wrong note. Neither Hhoth nor Ammaru can
    hear the difference in the creole: each hears only its own half.
    Stated in scene 1, in Sione's job interview memory.
  - *Edge case: a promise Sione breaks.* It remains visible to ennu as a
    scar: a promise that was open and closed unkept. It keeps them, because
    only a promise he could break proves he chose the ones he kept. Scene 5.
  - *Edge case: Sione promising to others.* Promises in the creole to
    anyone are logged. It sees him a little when he promises the clerk
    lunch. Comic beat, scene 3.

## The world's shape
- **Where power sits, and what it covers:** the Table, a standing
  conference of both delegations in the lattice hall on the ice shelf at
  Ninefold's equator. It governs only the Concord's subject: where the
  Hhoth may drill and haul ice, and when the Ammaru's Gathering years close
  the shelf. It doesn't govern either people's home life. The lattice is
  the Table's register and its one shared institution.
- **Legal force of the dual:** the Concord's party is *ennu*. Neither
  people signed separately; the treaty's signatory is the word. To admit a
  third people, the Table would have to coin a trial, *ennu-sa*, and the
  trial would become the party. Shown in scene 6, in one exchange.
- **How it got that way:** sixty-three years ago, in a Gathering year, Hhoth
  bore-crews drilled the shelf and the meltwater killed a generation of
  Ammaru spawn. The Hhoth had promised "we will not drill" and meant their
  own brood-line, not all Hhoth; the Ammaru heard "we" as everyone. The war
  of the Drilling followed. The creole's coiners built ennu so that no
  promise could ever again be heard two ways.
- **Where the reader meets that history:** scene 4, at the memorial bore,
  a capped hole in the ice where Ammaru come up to sing on the anniversary,
  told by Oyumwe.
- **Why people handed power over:** not applicable.

## The consequence ladder
1. **First order:** both peoples have to mean ennu in their own kitchens,
   not just at the Table. A hauler's casual ennu to an Ammaru buyer is a
   public event if it coughs.
2. **Second order:** ennu-etiquette is a career and a class marker. The
   Hhoth delegates rise or fall on how cleanly they use one word. Children
   at the shelf schools use ennu with strangers, which their elders find
   either beautiful or contamination.
3. **Third order:** whoever can make ennu say a word to them holds a kind of
   power no delegation has. That is why both sides are alarmed and touched
   by Sione, and why the trial offer is also a bid: a third party is a
   swing vote.

## The society
- **Institutions:** the Table; the lattice clerks (both peoples), who keep
  the register; the Listener's office (Sione, one desk and a kettle).
- **Economy:** the Hhoth haul ice for its deuterium; the Ammaru trade
  pressure-grown pearl-glass. Scarce: shelf time in a Gathering year.
  Worthless: a promise in a language other than the creole.
- **Kinship, and who raises the children:** Hhoth hatch in brood-lines of
  eight to twenty, raised together by their elder brood; a Hhoth's "we" has
  always meant its brood. Ammaru spawn together once every seven years and
  are raised by the whole sea; their "we" means everyone alive.
- **Death:** Hhoth broods record deaths as a "we" grown smaller. Ammaru
  have no word for a single death, only for a smaller Gathering.
- **Taboos:** saying ennu in jest. Swearing in it.
- **An ordinary day:** an ice hauler says ennu to her Ammaru buyer over a
  bad price and means it, and the filaments warm by a degree nobody sees.

## Minds
- **Ennu:** perceives only promises spoken in the creole, each one as a
  shape with two seats. A promise feels to it like being sat in. It finds
  it obvious that a promise is a place, that every place has two seats,
  and that meaning is who's sitting. It can't think "him alone", because it
  has no singular; it learns, slowly, to say "you" to Sione, the word for
  someone not inside it. It reasons about seating: who is inside each
  utterance, who's been put out. It believes the humans are a careless third
  people who say "we" without counting. It knows when a delegate plays it,
  because a played ennu sits only one, but it doesn't see that its own
  address to Sione is causing the cough until he shows it.
- **Hhoth:** brood-minded; trust is kin; cool, precise, legalistic, funny in
  a dry way about other broods. They can hear the inclusive sense of ennu
  but not when it's being used exclusively, because to them every "we"
  excludes.
- **Ammaru:** sea-minded; slow, generous, impossible to hurry. They hear
  the exclusive sense only as an absence, and often miss it.

## Characters
- **Sione Havili** (Tongan; Havili is a common Tongan surname). 38.
  Court interpreter, Auckland District Court, fourteen years; now the
  Concord's Listener on a one-year contract.
  - **What he wants:** to be good at a job only he can do. Under it, to be
    inside a "we" without having to earn the seat.
  - **What he's wrong about:** that belonging means being inside the
    pronoun. He has spent his life hearing which "we" excludes him.
  - **Wound:** his family. His sister Mele writes in Tongan with *kimaua*,
    "she and I" (Mele and their mother), when she plans things, and he has
    always heard the door it shuts. He came to Ninefold partly to be where
    nobody's "we" could include him by accident.
  - **What the novum does to him:** makes his ear his job, and makes every
    kindness he does a binding.
  - **Something he loves outside the plot:** rugby league on delay, and
    cooking *lu pulu* badly with Hhoth ice-hauler substitutes.
  - **Where he changes his mind about something personal:** the last scene.
    After ennu calls him "you", he answers Mele's message with *kitaua*,
    "you and I", and means it.
- **Ennu** (the being; Sione calls it by the word because it has no other
  name: nobody has ever had to address it).
  - **What it wants:** to be said to, not only said.
  - **What it's wrong about:** that a seat can be made for someone by
    wanting it.
  - **Something it loves outside the plot:** Sione's weather reports
    (promise 3: "I'll tell you the truth about the weather"). It has never
    perceived weather; it perceives only his promise to be accurate.
- **Second Speaker Vell of the Ashkari brood** (Hhoth). The named delegate
  who argues the trial persuasively. Wants the trial for real reasons: a
  binary peace is brittle, and a third party has no Drilling to remember.
  Also wants it because a human vote would usually go with the Hhoth, who
  pay the humans' freight. Dry, funny, likes Sione.
  - **Wrong about:** that adding a seat never takes one away.
- **Oyumwe** (Ammaru elder delegate). Opposes the trial: ennu is a rite,
  not an instrument, and you don't rewrite a rite for a guest. Remembers the
  Drilling; was spawned in the Gathering after it.
  - **Wrong about:** that Sione is a guest.
- **Talek** (Hhoth junior clerk at the register). Comic foil, explains the
  lattice in scene 2, is owed a lunch.

## Point of view and voice
- **Teller:** Sione.
- **Person and tense:** first person, past tense.
- **Register:** wry, warm, a working interpreter's exactness about words,
  quick to the joke, slow to the feeling. The numbered promises appear as
  short headers between some sections ("Promise 3: I'll tell you the truth
  about the weather").
- **Why this teller:** the story is about who is inside a "we", and only an
  insider's ear on his own exclusions gets the twist and the ending.
- **A sample paragraph in the voice:**
  > The first time it coughed with me in the room, forty million people
  > said bless. I heard it in my earpiece as a hitch, like a tape catching,
  > and then in the corridor I heard it again from a Hhoth hauler who'd
  > stopped with her sled to say it, in the creole, to nobody. Bless. In
  > Tongan we would say *mālō*, which is thank you, which is wrong, but it
  > was what came out.
