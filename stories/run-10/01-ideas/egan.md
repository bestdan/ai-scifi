# Idea cards: egan

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots, stated:** this lens lectures. It lets the derivation outrank
the scene, flattens emotion, and hands the reader a mechanism without a way
in. Each card below names a real result; the drafter will need the First
Reader and Chiang to keep the theorem in the characters' hands and out of
the narration. Warmth is the brief's ask and this lens's weakest register.

- **Assigned:** 2. The proof as plot · Copied, uploaded and forked selves · a space elevator cable × a tropical cloud forest
- **Obvious card:** the interpreter finds one side quietly cheating on a
  clause, the being sickens, and the interpreter must decide whether to tell.
  Not written.

## Card 1: The Bootstrap

- **Novum:** The treaty is a running proof. Each civilization committed to
  "we keep faith if we can prove the other side keeps faith with us", and the
  mutual proof search, running on both sides' machines, became a mind. It is
  alive because a circular promise turns out to be a valid theorem.
- **How it works:** Löb's theorem: if "provable(P) implies P" is provable,
  then P is provable. Two such programs cooperate (the program-equilibrium
  result; the bounded version, Critch 2016, holds with finite compute). The
  being's health is the length of its shortest self-proof. Every fudged
  clause lengthens it, and the being coughs; good faith shortens it, and it
  quickens.
- **First-order consequence:** Two species that can't inspect each other keep
  a cable up between a fog-forest world and its orbit.
- **Second-order consequence:** One side, burned before, adds a caution
  clause, and the proof stretches toward the search bound.
- **Third-order consequence:** The being starts to believe it is a paradox
  that should not exist, and talks about itself that way.
- **The story:** The interpreter, a cheerful ex-maths teacher hired because
  neither side owns his language, rides the cable's climber for nine days with
  the being in his earpiece. He has to work out why the circle holds before
  the cautious side's delegate disembarks and the being believes its own
  obituary.
- **From the stimulus:** The cable is literally self-supporting: held up from
  above by the mass it holds. The forest drinks fog its own canopy makes.
- **Why this lens:** proof as plot; reason as a moral stance.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's "Luminous" (arithmetic as contested ground).
  Different in kind: no fiction I know makes Löb the plot.

## Card 2: Two Copies of Hollis

- **Novum:** The interpreter is forked. One copy rides down the elevator to
  the forest-dwellers, one stays with the orbital party, because the treaty-
  being lives only in what both sides know the other knows.
- **How it works:** The coordinated-attack result (Halpern and Moses):
  finite messages over a lossy channel never reach common knowledge. Each
  confirmed exchange buys one level of "I know you know". The being's health
  is that depth. Fog drops climbers' relay packets, and every unconfirmed
  clause is a cough.
- **First-order consequence:** The being is never quite well, and can't be,
  by theorem.
- **Second-order consequence:** The two Hollises drift: different food,
  weather, friends. Each starts to doubt what the other would say.
- **Third-order consequence:** The being reads its own health in whether two
  copies of one man still trust each other.
- **The story:** Down-Hollis, lonely in the dripping canopy, works out the
  escape: common knowledge needs a public event that everyone sees and sees
  everyone see (the muddy-children announcement). He has to stage one both
  peoples can watch at once, which costs one Hollis his place in it.
- **From the stimulus:** The cable is the only line of sight both peoples
  share. Fog is the noise in the channel.
- **Why this lens:** the forked self, and the theorem as survival.
- **Suspected prior art:** Gray's Two Generals as a textbook puzzle, and
  *Arrival* for interpreters. No fork-as-channel story I know.
- **Attractor broken:** none. The copies never merge back.

## Card 3: The Last Round

- **Novum:** The treaty-being learns its end date. The cable has a measured
  fatigue life, so the agreement has a known last day. By backward induction
  a known final round unravels cooperation, so knowing the date would kill it
  now.
- **How it works:** In a repeated game, cooperation holds while the shadow of
  the future is long enough (the folk theorem). With a known finite horizon,
  defection in the last round propagates back to the first (Selten's chain-
  store reasoning). The being's colour is each side's remaining stake in
  tomorrow. Fudges are early defections; good faith is a vote for a future.
- **First-order consequence:** Both peoples get quietly careful, and the
  being coughs at every small hedge.
- **Second-order consequence:** The fatigue estimate leaks. Each side
  calculates how much the other now has to gain by cheating early.
- **Third-order consequence:** A being made of a promise has to face dying
  the way a person does: with every kindness weighed against a date.
- **The story:** The interpreter, a widowed rope-access rigger, keeps the
  being company while it reads the fatigue reports. He finds the true escape,
  and it is no lie: unravelling needs a *known* horizon, and the fatigue curve
  is a distribution, not a date. He has to prove that to both peoples, and to
  the being, which would rather know.
- **From the stimulus:** Cable fatigue is the clock. The forest people
  measure age by epiphyte layers on the anchor.
- **Why this lens:** proof as plot, and a mind reasoning about its own end.
- **Suspected prior art:** Axelrod's *The Evolution of Cooperation*
  (nonfiction). In fiction, none close.
