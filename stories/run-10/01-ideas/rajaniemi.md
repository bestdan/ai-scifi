# Idea cards: rajaniemi

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** opacity, and density mistaken for depth. I am
apt to front-load coinages and stack reversals until a 6,000-word story
drowns. Every card below keeps to one heist and one turn, and each coinage
names its Job; Asimov and the First Reader should still push back on how
much the drafter has to teach before the caper starts.

- **Assigned:** 3. The heist · Minds that are genuinely not human · slime mould × organ tuner
- **Obvious card:** A crew kidnaps the woken treaty to hold both civilizations to ransom, and the hired interpreter rides along keeping it company. Not written.

## Card 1: The Wolf

- **Novum:** The treaty can't be kept pure in every clause at once, for the same arithmetic reason twelve true fifths overshoot seven octaves. Its chronic cough is that leftover error, and whoever decides which clause carries it decides who the treaty quietly fails.
- **How it works:** One party, the Hollows, speak in held tones in resonant caverns; the other, the Strew, is a world-wide plasmodium that thinks in pulses of streaming. The treaty lives as the beat between them. Its clauses are intervals, so the leftover has to be tempered somewhere. Tuners call the worst interval "the wolf", because it howls.
- **First-order consequence:** Each side secretly hires a crew to move the wolf onto the other side's clauses.
- **Second-order consequence:** Both hire the same crew, which needs the interpreter, the only outsider allowed to sit in the treaty's chamber, as its inside man.
- **Third-order consequence:** The crew plays both commissions and parks the wolf on a clause nobody uses: the one that hires a human companion.
- **The story:** Kofi Asante-Lind, an ex-organ tuner who took the interpreter job for the quiet, runs the retune over one long night. From then on every hour he spends keeping the treaty company sounds sour. The treaty takes this with an alien calm: company is the one thing it never needed in tune.
- **From the stimulus:** Organ tuner to temperament and the wolf; slime mould to the Strew's body.
- **Why this lens:** A caper whose tools are real acoustics.
- **Suspected prior art:** Miéville's *Embassytown* (interpreters, an alien language); the crew-and-tempering heist differs in kind.

## Card 2: Rob Me an Hour

- **Novum:** The treaty is made of the promise, so it can't take part in a fudge without being hurt by it. When it needs saving, it can only ask to be robbed.
- **How it works:** The treaty's body is a plasmodial net grown through a border station, and its pulse slows in the cold, as slime mould's does. One clause binds the Strew to deliver warmth-stock by a fixed beat count. A blight on the Strew's world means the delivery will be late through no one's fault, and a weak treaty may not survive the cough.
- **First-order consequence:** The treaty asks its interpreter to chill it so that a day passes as an hour inside it, and never to tell it when.
- **Second-order consequence:** The heist has to run against a mark who is also the client, and who is trying, in good faith, to notice.
- **Third-order consequence:** The interpreter learns the treaty's senses well enough to cheat them, which is also how anyone could.
- **The story:** Dariusz Ołowski and a Strew cold-keeper who can't lie in chemistry steal nine hours from a being that keeps asking Dariusz whether it's cold. The delivery lands. Then the treaty asks whether he did it, and both answers cost something.
- **From the stimulus:** Slime mould's temperature-paced shuttle streaming. The organ tuner dropped away.
- **Why this lens:** An hour as loot, and a mind with a strange rule.
- **Suspected prior art:** Lem's *Cyberiad* tricks on machines; nothing close.

## Card 3: Everything It Touches

- **Novum:** Anything the treaty touches comes under it: both civilizations are then bound to deal in good faith over it. The treaty has never seen anything outside its own clauses.
- **How it works:** A plasmodium takes in what it grows over, and the treaty's body grew from the first shared conduit, so contact means coverage. Both sides keep it sealed so that it can't widen its own scope.
- **First-order consequence:** The interpreter is the treaty's only window, and it wants one night to see a human market-ring for itself.
- **Second-order consequence:** Breaking it out means planning a route through space where coverage harms nobody. The crew borrows the treaty's own trick, laying oat-flake lures so it finds the shortest safe path.
- **Third-order consequence:** At one bend the route fails, and the treaty touches the getaway pilot, an exile from the Hollows. Both civilizations now owe her a promise she never asked for, and she can never be ignored again.
- **The story:** Ruth Baptiste-Ngata and the pilot sneak a homesick treaty out for a night of food stalls. The treaty finds them more interesting than everything else in the market. The pilot pays.
- **From the stimulus:** Physarum mazes and oat lures; the organ tuner returns only as the Hollows' tones.
- **Why this lens:** A jailbreak run on a novum's own affordances.
- **Suspected prior art:** *E.T.*-shaped outings; Rajaniemi's Oubliette. The rule that touch means coverage is new.
