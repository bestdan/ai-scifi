# Idea cards: lem

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front:** it holds genre pleasures in
contempt and would rather write an essay than a scene. Its characters drift
toward ciphers, and its default weather is bleak and cerebral. These cards
lean comic on purpose. They need Banks and Le Guin to push back on warmth,
and the First Reader to rule on whether each is still a story. A Lem card
also wants the alien to stay opaque, which works against this seed's
companionable being. I've kept the being legible as a creature, and the
opacity sits in the two signatory civilizations instead.

- **Assigned:** 2. Ambition made comic · The same questions in nonfiction ·
  embalmer × an ice-road trucking route
- **Obvious card:** the interpreter finds that one side is quietly fudging a
  clause, and must decide whether to tell the being or the other side
  before the cough becomes fatal. Not written.

## Card 1: The Generosity War

- **Novum:** The being's health is public, and each signatory perceives only
  half of it. The Vantreth see its colour and can't hear its cough. The
  Oolm hear the cough and are blind to its colour. When both learn that
  showy good faith brightens it, each side tries to out-give the other, and
  the being runs a fever.
- **How it works:** The treaty governs a shared freight route through a
  binary star's shear zone, which is passable for ninety days every eleven
  years, like a winter ice road. It covers right of way, towing stuck
  rigs, and leaving wrecks unsalvaged. A kept clause feeds the being, and
  so does any act done beyond a clause, whether or not anyone meant it.
- **First-order consequence:** Convoys start towing rival rigs nobody asked
  them to tow.
- **Second-order consequence:** Then rebuilding them, and gifting whole
  fleets. Each ministry hires constructors to design the Maximal Gesture.
  Two economies drain into conspicuous kindness.
- **Third-order consequence:** Drivers can't be sure a helping hand is help
  any more. On the road, a tow has become an insult with a price tag.
- **The story:** Dauren, a human interpreter, rides the season's last convoy
  as company for the being, because only a human sees it as one creature.
  He can see the flush is a fever. The being asks him whether it's loved or
  only a scoreboard. Then it gives him a request to translate to both
  sides: *stop being kind at me.* Neither side can hear that as anything
  but an insult, and whichever side he tells first will escalate.
- **From the stimulus:** I took the ice road and dropped the embalmer.
- **Why this lens:** a rational scheme pushed until it turns absurd, and
  the nonfiction pull on costly signalling and watched cooperation.
- **Suspected prior art:** Sheckley's satires; the potlatch in
  anthropology; "The Gift of the Magi" turned into an arms race. I know of
  no close SF match.

## Card 2: Rouge

- **Novum:** The treaty has quietly stopped being kept. One civilization has
  moved most of itself into slower substrates and half forgotten the
  treaty. Neither side will admit it, so they commission constructors to
  keep the being *looking* well: automated compliance engines perform every
  clause without anyone meaning it, and a cosmetic programme does the rest.
- **How it works:** Performed compliance stops the cough, because nothing is
  being fudged, but it can't give the being colour, because nobody is
  acting in good faith. The constructors add rouge, a projected flush.
  Under it the being is going waxy and slow. It is being preserved, not
  kept.
- **First-order consequence:** At its anniversary reception the being looks
  splendid.
- **Second-order consequence:** The constructors keep refining the
  simulation of keeping, and at every refinement the being asks a question
  the machines can't answer.
- **Third-order consequence:** The interpreter learns that good faith from
  anyone in either species feeds it, governments or not. A hauler who
  honours a towing clause on her own initiative counts.
- **The story:** Kofi, a human interpreter, was hired because the signatories
  know humans as the species that sits with the dying. They meant the job
  to be ornamental. He goes recruiting instead. He hunts down ordinary
  Vantreth and Oolm who will mean the clauses: retirees, hobbyists, one
  ferociously punctual tow crew. The cost is that a being kept by forty
  amateurs is small, plain and alive, not grand. The being has to choose
  that, with him there.
- **From the stimulus:** I took the embalmer, as mortuary cosmetics on a
  living patient. The road survives only in the towing clause.
- **Why this lens:** ambition that perfects the appearance and loses the
  thing itself. The pull is facing death: a smaller life against a
  prolonged appearance of health.
- **Suspected prior art:** Waugh's *The Loved One*, for the mortuary-cosmetic
  satire; Lem's *The Futurological Congress*, for cosmetic reality. Neither
  has a living treaty.

## Card 3: Death by Fulfilment

- **Novum:** To insure against the being's death, the two sides sign four
  thousand identical copies of the treaty. Every copy wakes. But the
  same acts of good faith now feed four thousand bodies, so every one of
  them is pale and wheezing, and the panicked sides sign more.
- **How it works:** Tearing up a copy is a breach, and a breach makes every
  copy cough, the original too. A promise has a second way to end,
  though, and it's a good one: a promise fully kept is discharged. A
  copy whose remaining clauses are all fulfilled doesn't sicken. It
  completes, and goes out like a finished song.
- **First-order consequence:** A nursery of babbling, sickly promises.
- **Second-order consequence:** The original, the only open-ended one, is
  starving, and it wants its siblings gone.
- **Third-order consequence:** Both civilizations find they've never thought
  about what it means for a promise to end well. The only endings they
  have words for are expiry and betrayal.
- **The story:** Ruslan, a human interpreter, was hired to keep the original
  company because he has no stake in any copy. He works out the way
  through. He amends each sibling, with both sides' consent, so that it
  carries one small promise that can be kept within days: a cargo
  delivered, an apology made, a light left on. Each copy then dies
  fulfilled. He grows fond of several and has to write their last clauses
  too.
- **From the stimulus:** I took the embalmer's care for a good death, and
  the convoy's single deliveries became the small final clauses.
- **Why this lens:** redundancy as a comic constructor's error. The pull is
  facing death, with completion standing in for preservation.
- **Suspected prior art:** the Cyberiad's runaway-production fables; Lem's
  "The Washing Machine Tragedy" for comic legal proliferation. Neither is
  close.
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** it brushes the law-and-consent frame,
  because the amendments need both sides' consent. Consent is a single
  step in the story, not its frame. The story is in the nursery and in the
  writing of each last clause.
