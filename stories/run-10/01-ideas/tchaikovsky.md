# Idea cards: tchaikovsky

**Blind spots, stated:** this lens assumes every mind can be reached with
enough patience, so its contact stories drift toward eventual rapport; Lem
should argue for the unreachable and Watts for a treaty-being with nobody
home. It also likes long timescales that thin out the human, and sprawl.
Chiang's restraint is the corrective. The seed's treaty sits next to the
"law as frame" attractor; every card here comes at it through bodies and
physics, not clauses or rulings.

- **Assigned:** 2. Two levels of understanding · Copied, uploaded and forked
  selves · tardigrade × materials fatigue
- **Obvious card:** the interpreter befriends the coughing treaty-being,
  traces each cough to a hidden violation by one side, and must decide
  whether to tell. Not written.

## Card 1: Endurance Limit

- **Novum:** The treaty-being is not dying of bad faith. It is dying of
  metal fatigue: every reunion between its signatories loads it and unloads
  it, and the cracks grow from the size of the swing, not from anyone
  cheating.
- **How it works:** One signatory, the Tun, dries into glassy dormancy for
  eleven years at a time when their eccentric world swings out to aphelion;
  they sense humidity first and almost nothing else matters to them. The
  other, the Rendh, are short-lived and continuous, and greet each Tun waking
  with enormous reaffirmation festivals. Each festival is a large good-faith
  spike followed by eleven years of quiet. The being's colour flares, then
  fades, and a hairline flaw advances each cycle. Below a certain swing
  (an endurance limit), it could take infinite cycles.
- **First-order consequence:** Each side blames the other's fudging for the
  cough.
- **Second-order consequence:** Both escalate their festivals to prove
  their love, and that deepens the cracks.
- **Third-order consequence:** The cure is to love each other less loudly:
  small, steady, almost boring acts of faith in place of the reunion both
  cultures treasure most.
- **The story:** A sixty-year-old human interpreter, on his fourth Tun
  waking, is the only one who has watched the being across cycles. He has
  to talk two peoples out of their best party, and the being, which loves
  the parties, out of its craving for them.
- **From the stimulus:** both halves arrived intact: the tun state became a
  species' rhythm, and fatigue became the being's illness.
- **Why this lens:** biology (a desiccating species) writes the culture's
  festival calendar; each side is right about its own love and wrong about
  the cough.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's *A Deepness in the Sky* (spiders who
  hibernate through their star's dark phase). The intermittent species is
  close, but the treaty dying of cyclic loading has no match I know of.

## Card 2: See You Tomorrow

- **Novum:** The interpreter is re-instantiated from a stored pattern for
  each visit and retired at shift end, and the treaty-being, which senses
  promises the way skin senses heat, catches a cough every time he says
  "see you tomorrow."
- **How it works:** The treaty binds a species that dries out and re-grows
  its minds from pattern (to them, pattern is the self) to a species that
  holds identity as one unbroken scent-memory from hatching. The being is
  made of both, and it reads a promise's signatory by both definitions at
  once. To one parent the returning interpreter is the same man. To the
  other he is a stranger keeping someone else's word.
- **First-order consequence:** A job no continuous human could do (always
  fresh, never bored) works fine, except for the cough.
- **Second-order consequence:** The treaty's own chronic illness turns out
  to be the same question: its "same delegate" clause has been half-broken
  for forty years by each side's honest reading.
- **Third-order consequence:** Each of the being's parents learns how the
  other dies, from watching a human do it daily.
- **The story:** One instance, on one shift, works out why his companion
  wheezes at the door. He chooses to say goodbye instead, honestly, and
  the being goes pink. Then it asks what the word means.
- **From the stimulus:** tardigrade revival became the pattern-species and
  the interpreter's working life; fatigue dropped away.
- **Why this lens:** two levels of understanding about one man, and the
  made mind (the treaty) owing its sense of self to two parents who
  disagree.
- **Suspected prior art:** Brin's *Kiln People* (disposable dittos), Egan's
  "Learning to Be Me." Neither puts the fork on the receiving end of a
  promise-sensing mind. No merge-back: instances simply end.

## Card 3: Paid in Full

- **Novum:** A promise kept is alive; a promise fulfilled is finished. The
  treaty-being lives on the obligations still outstanding, and one
  signatory, out of pure goodwill, is about to pay everything off early.
- **How it works:** The Skitter think in fast electrical pulses; the Loam
  talk by chemical gradient over days. The being lives at a tempo between
  them, so neither can converse with it. A human auctioneer's speech rate
  is right in the middle, and that is why he was hired. It senses one thing
  the way a tardigrade senses wet: the gap between what was promised and
  what is done. That gap is its body.
- **First-order consequence:** The Skitter read its coughs on their fast
  clock and panic; the Loam read only century-long trends and call it
  robust.
- **Second-order consequence:** The Skitter decide to deliver the treaty's
  final century of payments at once, a magnificent gift.
- **Third-order consequence:** Generosity becomes the threat, and the only
  remedy is a new debt, which means asking the slow Loam to promise
  something they will take a decade to agree to.
- **The story:** The auctioneer, chatting with the being in real time, is
  the only one it can tell it is afraid of being paid in full. Then he has
  to talk one fast species into a long wait and a slow species into a fast
  yes.
- **From the stimulus:** the tardigrade's single overriding sense became the
  being's; fatigue left only a trace in the Skitter's jitter of small loads.
- **Why this lens:** senses decide each side's reading of the same patient;
  the made mind owes its life to the debt its makers want to clear.
- **Suspected prior art:** Forward's *Dragon's Egg* for the tempo mismatch.
  The debt-as-body idea I can't place.
- **Attractor broken:** law as frame, lightly: the treaty's schedule of
  payments is the stake. The story turns on the being's body, not on any
  ruling.
