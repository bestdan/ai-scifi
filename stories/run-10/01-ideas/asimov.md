# Idea cards: asimov

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** thin interiority, and dialogue that tends to carry exposition. The body and the senses barely register. It treats the idea as the character, and it assumes every problem has a solution. These cards need Le Guin, Lem and Rajaniemi to push back on the being's inner life and on whether the puzzle should close.

- **Assigned:** 1. The rule's blind spot · Deep time and vast scale · taxidermist × a generation-old asteroid mine
- **Obvious card:** The interpreter finds out one side has been quietly breaching a clause, and that this is why the being is sick, and has to decide whether to tell. Not written.

## Card 1: Until the Rock Is Spent

- **Novum:** The treaty runs a shared asteroid mine "until the last ore leaves the rock." Keeping it perfectly means finishing it, and both sides assume that finishing it kills the being.
- **How it works:** Every act done under the treaty registers in the being. A kept clause brings colour, and a dodged one brings a cough. A clause that is discharged stops registering at all.
- **First-order consequence:** Sixty years in and fond of the being, both sides start padding the schedule: slow quotas, invented "reassays," a new seam declared and never dug.
- **Second-order consequence:** Every pad is a small bad-faith act, so the being has been coughing for a decade. Each side blames the other's quotas.
- **Third-order consequence:** Miners' children born on the rock grow up on a mine kept open for show, like a mounted animal posed in mid-stride.
- **The story:** The interpreter, a man hired eight years ago to keep the being company, notices that the coughs started the season the first pad was filed, not when the ore ran thin. He also reads the treaty's standing mutual-aid clause, which nobody bothered with. A clause that waits for a trigger is never discharged. So the being won't die when the mine is finished. It will sleep until one side needs help, perhaps for centuries. He has to persuade both sides to finish the job, knowing that he will never speak to it again.
- **From the stimulus:** The mine is the setting. The taxidermy became the padding: a promise kept looking alive after its work is done.
- **Why this lens:** The rule contradicts itself, because honest performance ends the treaty. The puzzle is fair: when did the coughing start?
- **Suspected prior art:** Max Gladstone's Craft Sequence (contracts as living gods). The device here differs: completion as sleep.

## Card 2: The Only Fast Wire

- **Novum:** The two civilizations are 31 light-years apart and meet only at a shared station. The being registers each act of good faith or bad faith the instant it happens, wherever it happens.
- **How it works:** The being is the promise itself, not something carried by signals, so nothing in it travels at light speed. Its complexion changes decades before the courier reports arrive. The treaty's drafters thought of it as a gauge.
- **First-order consequence:** The interpreter sees a flush with no cause, and twenty-nine years later a report explains it. The being is a faster-than-light receiver.
- **Second-order consequence:** Both sides learn to signal through it. Small deliberate acts of good faith (a fee waived, a cargo released early) become a code. Saying "no" means a deliberate breach, which hurts the being.
- **Third-order consequence:** The galaxy's only instant channel can carry kindness cheaply and refusal only at the being's expense. Diplomacy bends toward a grammar of gifts.
- **The story:** The interpreter is the first to read the code, because he is the one at its side. Then a run of coughs starts arriving in a rhythm. One side is shouting a warning the only way it can, by hurting the being. He has to decode the message before the being is too sick to carry it.
- **From the stimulus:** The asteroid mine moved out into the station's ore trade, which supplies the small concessions used as signals. The taxidermist dropped away.
- **Why this lens:** The rule's makers built a meter and got a channel instead. The scale is the reveal.
- **Suspected prior art:** Le Guin's ansible and Card's philotic link. Neither has a channel whose cost depends on what the message means.

## Card 3: Presently

- **Novum:** The treaty was drafted 700 years ago in a human trade language that neither side speaks natively, chosen to favour neither of them. The being thinks in that language, so the interpreter's job is to be someone it can talk to.
- **How it works:** The being is the promise as both sides read it. The drafters chose a neutral third language, and they never foresaw that the third language would change.
- **First-order consequence:** Only a scholar of dead English can talk to the being. The two sides read the text through period dictionaries from different centuries.
- **Second-order consequence:** One word has drifted ("presently" once meant "at once" and now means "soon"). Each side keeps its own reading in perfect good faith, so a limb of the being has a chronic ache that neither side's audit can explain.
- **Third-order consequence:** Both civilizations start freezing their copies of English, and a living language becomes a preserved specimen.
- **The story:** An old male philologist (not a lone woman tender) is hired as company and finds the ache by chatting with the being. He can't settle the meaning, and he won't stand in as a tribunal. Only the being can choose what it means, because what it means is what it is. The choice will heal one side's reading and make the other side a breaker of the treaty.
- **From the stimulus:** The taxidermy became the frozen language. The mine is gone.
- **Why this lens:** Good faith on both sides still produces a breach. A reader shown the clause early can find the drifted word first.
- **Suspected prior art:** Semantic drift in legal texts has been studied for real. I know of no story where it is a living being's illness.
- **Attractor broken:** It comes near "law as the frame," which the seed demands. It avoids the tribunal, because the being makes the choice.
