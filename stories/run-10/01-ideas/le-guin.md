# Idea cards: le-guin

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

- **Assigned:** 3. The word we lack · Minds that are genuinely not human · a salt mine × a music box
- **Obvious card:** the interpreter notices one side quietly fudging a clause, the being sickens, and she must decide whether to tell the other side. Not written.

**Blind spots of this lens:** slow pacing; little interest in hard mechanism or physics; a moral seriousness that can tip into instruction. These cards lean on grammar and custom for their novum, and Banks, Egan or Watts should test whether the engineering holds and whether anything happens fast enough.

## Card 1: The Uncut

- **Novum:** A treaty between slow minds that live as crystal growth in a world-wide salt bed and the fast people who mine it. The treaty is a map of pillars to be left standing, and it woke as a mind living in the load those pillars carry. The deep people's word for it is a noun English lacks: *the uncut*, the thing you chose not to take.
- **How it works:** Room-and-pillar mining leaves columns to hold the roof, and each mapped pillar is a clause. Stress through the pillar web is the being's thought; it hears by creep and microfracture. Robbing a pillar on retreat, a real practice, is a fudge: the roof sags and the being coughs in shudders. Good faith eases load, and halophile archaea bloom pink in relieved salt, which is the colour in its cheeks.
- **First-order consequence:** Surface wealth is counted in tonnes not taken.
- **Second-order consequence:** Crews are paid for what they leave, so an honest crew mines less and the town shrinks as the being thrives.
- **Third-order consequence:** Surface people boast by listing what they refrained from; generosity becomes abstention.
- **The story:** Dawit, raised cutting salt bars in the Danakil, is hired because a human can talk at surface speed and still wait three days for one sentence from below. He keeps the being company with a tapping hammer. It wants to understand the uncut from the other side, and offers a laid-off crew boss one of its own pillars: a gift that would cost it a clause. Dawit has to translate the offer, and she has to decide whether to take it.
- **From the stimulus:** The salt mine became the body; the music box did not survive.
- **Why this lens:** A word that reorganises an economy, and who pays for a good society.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Star Trek*'s "The Devil in the Dark" (miners and a silicon mind); the biblical covenant of salt. Neither has the agreement itself alive.

## Card 2: We-Two

- **Novum:** The treaty creole had to invent a pronoun neither people had: an inclusive dual, "we, you-and-I, nobody else." The being is that pronoun, awake. Everyone it has ever met is half of it, so no one has ever called it "you".
- **How it works:** One people has only exclusive plurals, so every "we" shuts the listener out; the other has only a boundless "all of us". The being lives in the creole's use. When either side says the dual at home and means the other side included, it strengthens; when a delegate says it in session and means "us, not you", it coughs. It perceives the world as utterances it occurs in. Its mind is the grammar of a word that points at people.
- **First-order consequence:** Both peoples have to learn to mean the dual in their own kitchens, not just at the table.
- **Second-order consequence:** Border children start using it with strangers; elders call it contamination.
- **Third-order consequence:** Who counts as "we" becomes a household quarrel on two worlds.
- **The story:** Sione grew up between Tongan, which has *kitaua* and *kimaua*, and English, which has neither. He is hired as the one party outside the treaty who can say "you" to it. It adores him. Then it starts addressing him in the dual, which holds exactly two, and the cough begins because it loves him. He has always wanted to belong somewhere. To keep it well he has to stay outside.
- **From the stimulus:** Travelled far. The salt survives only as the quiet salt chamber where both species can breathe.
- **Why this lens:** Language makes the self; a mind that is genuinely a part of speech.
- **Suspected prior art:** Elgin's *Native Tongue* and Leckie's pronouns, both on grammar and identity. Neither makes a pronoun a person.

## Card 3: First-Saying

- **Novum:** One people speak only from an inherited repertoire of fixed phrases, pinned like a music-box cylinder. Their verb for "promise" is their verb for "say something for the first time." The treaty was their first new sentence in four centuries, and that weight woke it.
- **How it works:** The repertoire people keep the being by daily exact recital, since for them faithfulness is repetition. The other people talk freely and keep it by acting on it in endless new ways. A paraphrase gives it a cough, because to the reciters a paraphrase is a rival vow. When a free-talker repeats a clause word for word, unasked, it blushes.
- **First-order consequence:** The being, made of one new sentence, starves for more. The reciters can give it only old phrases, and the free-talkers give it new ones that weigh nothing.
- **Second-order consequence:** The human hired to talk with it is, to the reciters, vowing with every fresh remark. They log each one against humanity.
- **Third-order consequence:** Young reciters who want to say something new, a first love, see a person who can, and a third kind of speech enters their world.
- **The story:** Anouk, from a Sainte-Croix family of music-box makers, can read the reciters' phrase-pins. She chats to fill silence, as her teenage son, who came with her, finds mortifying. Then she learns that her small talk has bound humanity to the soup being good. Now she must choose: speak to the being only in sentences she will keep, which makes her company sparse and heavy, or go on talking and let her species run up debts.
- **From the stimulus:** The music box became a grammar; the salt dropped away.
- **Why this lens:** A word the culture has and we lack, and the life it permits.
- **Suspected prior art:** Miéville's *Embassytown* (Hosts who cannot lie) and *Star Trek*'s "Darmok" (speech in fixed allusions). The difference: here newness itself is the obligation.
