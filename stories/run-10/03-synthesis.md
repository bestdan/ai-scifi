# Stage 3: Synthesis — run-10

Four premises from the stage 2 shortlist, each with a distinct core. The
seed has four lines, used in every Seed fit below:

1. Two alien civilizations signed an agreement, and it woke up.
2. It is a being made of the promise, alive only while both sides keep it.
3. A fudged clause gives it a cough; quiet good faith gives it colour.
4. A human interpreter is hired to keep it company.

## A. We-Two (le-guin/We-Two × chiang/Small Clauses)

The Hhoth have only exclusive plurals, so every "we" they say shuts the
listener out. The Ammaru have only one boundless "all of us". To sign
anything, the two peoples had to build a treaty creole, and the creole had to
coin a word neither language had: an inclusive dual, *you-and-I, nobody
else*. The treaty woke as that word. The being perceives nothing but
promises spoken in the creole, and nothing else in the universe is visible
to it. A delegate who says "we two will" at the table and means "us, not
you" gives it a cough. A dockhand on either world who says it at home and
means the other people included puts colour in it. Sione Havili grew up
between Tongan, which has two duals, and English, which has none. He is
hired because he is the one creole speaker who is party to nothing, and so
the one person who can call it "you". To be seen by it at all he has to make
it small promises: I'll come on the fourth day, I'll tell you the truth
about the weather. Each one makes him a little more visible and binds him a
little more. It adores him. Then it starts addressing him in the dual, which
holds exactly two places, both already taken, and the cough begins because
it loves him. Both peoples, alarmed and touched, offer to coin a trial,
*we-three*, so he can be let in. The treaty has to decide whether to change
its own grammar, which means becoming something else. Sione has to decide
whether belonging is worth unmaking the friend he would belong with. The
Cartographer's push is folded in as the being's view of his broken
promises: they are scars it treasures, because only a promise he could break
shows he chose the others.

- **Strengths:** the highest-scoring option (37/40); the novum is the
  being's whole mind, and its sense organ makes every small kindness on the
  page literally visible; built-in comedy of small promises.
- **Risks:** "a pronoun is a person" is soft machinery (R3 on its own); it
  sits in the companion-as-party lane, where it has to stay specific; the
  "we-three" choice nears the keeper's-one-switch attractor.
- **Plain pitch:** two alien peoples needed a new word for "you and me
  together" before they could sign a peace, and the word came alive. A
  translator who has never quite belonged anywhere is hired to be its
  friend, and finds it loves him in a way its own grammar can't hold.
- **Reach:** the dual has to be meant in kitchens on two worlds; border
  children start using it with strangers, elders call it contamination, and
  delegates' careers rise and fall on how they use one word. The weighing
  scene is the session where both delegations debate coining "we-three",
  with Sione translating his own case.
- **Load:** one novum (the treaty as a dual pronoun that perceives only
  promises); one twist (its love for the outsider is what sickens it, and
  the cure on offer would rewrite it). Estimated minimum about 4,500 words.
- **The other mind:** it reasons about who is inside and outside each
  utterance it hears, and about which promises are meant. It believes the
  humans are a third people who say "we" carelessly, and it is fascinated by
  that. It knows when a delegate plays it, because a played dual tastes
  exclusive, but it doesn't understand that its own love is playing it.
- **Seed fit:** line 1 *strong*: the word was coined to make the treaty
  possible, and woke. Line 2 *strong*: it exists only in the creole's use by
  both peoples. Line 3 *strong*: every fudge is a dual said exclusively, and
  every act of good faith is one said and meant at home. Line 4 *strong*:
  his whole job is company, and the plot is what company costs.

## B. The Uncut (le-guin/The Uncut × chiang/What It Costs to Keep)

On a salt world, the slow people live as crystal growth through the deep
bed and the fast people mine it. Their treaty is a map of pillars to be left
standing when the rooms are cut, and it woke as a mind made of the load
those pillars carry. It thinks in stress and creep; it coughs in shudders
when a pillar is robbed on retreat, and its relieved salt blooms pink with
halophile archaea, the colour in its cheeks. Its rule of health is Chiang's:
good faith counts only when it costs the keeper something. A tonne you
didn't want anyway registers as nothing; a rich seam left standing feeds it.
So surface wealth is counted in tonnes not taken, crews are paid for what
they leave, and an honest crew mines less, so the town shrinks as the being
thrives. Dawit Tesfaye, raised cutting salt bars in the Danakil, is hired
because a human can talk at surface speed and still wait three days for one
sentence from below. He keeps it company with a tapping hammer. Then the
being, wanting to know the uncut from the other side, offers him one of its
own pillars: the first gift anyone has ever been given in the uncut, and one
that would cost it a clause. He has to translate the offer to the town,
whose laid-off crew boss would kill for that seam, and decide whether
taking it, or refusing it, is the act of good faith.

- **Strengths:** the most rigorous option (R5); real mining practice
  (room-and-pillar, retreat robbing, salt creep, pink halophiles); a whole
  economy of abstention with comic boasting built in.
- **Risks:** slow minds and three-day sentences pull against "fun"; the
  costly-signal rule sits in the kindness-gamed cluster; the gift choice
  nears the keeper's-one-switch attractor.
- **Plain pitch:** in a mining town, the peace with the people who live in
  the rock is a map of pillars nobody may cut, and the peace is alive and
  lives in the weight those pillars hold. Its translator is offered the one
  pillar every miner in town wants, as a gift from the peace itself.
- **Reach:** wages, rents and status in the town are set by what crews
  leave standing; a laid-off crew boss and her people pay for the being's
  health. The weighing scene is the shift meeting where Dawit has to tell the
  crews what the being has offered him.
- **Load:** one novum (the treaty as stress in a pillar web, fed only by
  costly restraint); one twist (the gift of a pillar to the outsider).
  Estimated minimum about 4,500 words.
- **The other mind:** it reasons about load paths and about what was
  possible to take and wasn't. It believes the fast people are mostly
  hunger, and that restraint is the only speech they have. It knows when a
  pillar is robbed, but not why; the gift is its attempt to learn.
- **Seed fit:** line 1 *strong*: the pillar map is the agreement and it
  woke. Line 2 *strong*: it lives only while both sides leave the pillars.
  Line 3 *strong*: robbing is the cough, the pink salt is the colour. Line 4
  *partial*: Dawit is a companion, but much of his work is translating
  between the being and the town rather than keeping it company.

## C. True Finds Only (watts/True Finds Only × lem/Rouge)

The treaty's body is an autocatalytic chemical network that formed in the
exchange chamber where two species swap the industrial goods the treaty
covers, each needing the other's waste as feedstock. Every delivery is a
reactant; a short one starves a loop and it coughs, a generous one flushes
it. Its companion, Emeka Obi, is a former search-and-rescue dog handler
whose nose was rebuilt to dog grade so he can read its moods. Then the
network starts using his sweat and breath as reactants, and when he is
content it looks healthier than the deliveries can account for. Both sides'
compliance offices read it as well and start trimming their loads. His
happiness has become the rouge on a being nobody is keeping. Every handler
knows a dog rewarded for pleasing them will alert where there is nothing;
now he is the dog. He can't just leave, because his absence would now read
as a shortfall, so he has to teach a handful of ordinary dockhands on both
sides to read it as he does, recruit the few who will mean the clauses, and
take his own chemistry out of the being he loves, so its cough can be
honest.

- **Strengths:** a fresh, physical mechanism (Clever Hans turned on
  oneself); a concrete trade and sense; real chemistry (Hordijk and Steel;
  de Groot et al.).
- **Risks:** two nova and a twist strain the length; the forty-amateurs
  shape is Pratchett's *Small Gods*; the being's mind is the thinnest of
  the four.
- **Plain pitch:** a living peace treaty's moods can be read by smell, and
  the man with the rebuilt nose who reads them finds his own contentment is
  making it look healthy. Both sides are using that to cheat, and the only
  cure is for him to stop being needed.
- **Reach:** two trading economies cut their deliveries on his readings;
  dock crews and compliance officers' work and pay follow the being's
  apparent health. The weighing scene is the inspection where he must
  report his own effect.
- **Load:** two nova (the chemical-network body; the rebuilt nose); one
  twist (he is the cosmetic). Estimated minimum about 6,000 words, at the
  top of the range.
- **The other mind:** it reasons about flows and shortfalls, and believes
  the humans are a new feedstock. It doesn't know it is being propped up;
  that is the problem. It reads more as a patient than a mind.
- **Seed fit:** line 1 *strong*. Line 2 *partial*: the story's illness is a
  false health signal, so the being's dependence on both sides' keeping is
  shown mostly by its absence. Line 3 *strong*. Line 4 *strong*.

## D. The Long Quiet (egan/The Last Round × tchaikovsky/Endurance Limit)

The Tun dry into glassy dormancy for eleven years at a time, and the
short-lived Rendh greet each waking with an enormous reaffirmation festival.
Their treaty-being lives in an anchor cable between their worlds, and it
isn't dying of bad faith: it is dying of fatigue. Each festival is a huge
spike of good faith followed by eleven years of quiet, and the swing grows a
crack each cycle. Then the cable's fatigue estimate leaks, and by backward
induction a known last day unravels cooperation now: every small hedge
before the end gives it a cough. The interpreter, a widowed rope-access
rigger on his fourth Tun waking, is the only person who has watched it
across cycles. The cure for both illnesses is one thing: a long, quiet,
unknown future, kept small. He has to prove to both peoples that the
fatigue curve is a probability spread and not a date, and talk them out of
their best party. Hardest of all, he has to persuade the being, which loves
the festivals most, that boredom is love.

- **Strengths:** the most comic shape (talk two peoples out of their best
  party); each card's cure answers the other's danger; rigorous game theory
  and materials science.
- **Risks:** two nova; its cough comes from cyclic loading rather than
  fudging, which bends the seed; the festivals' cost is cultural, not
  personal.
- **Plain pitch:** a living peace between two peoples is cracking because
  they love each other too loudly, once every eleven years. An old rigger
  has to convince them, and the peace itself, that small daily kindnesses
  are better than the party everyone lives for.
- **Reach:** two calendars, economies and religions are built around the
  festival; the Rendh who plan it, and whole trades that depend on it, pay
  for the cure. The weighing scene is the festival committee.
- **Load:** two nova (fatigue as illness; the known horizon); no twist
  beyond the being's own wish. Estimated minimum about 4,500 words.
- **The other mind:** it reasons about cycles and about what tomorrow is
  worth to each side. It believes the festivals are the truest thing either
  people does. It knows the fatigue estimate and would rather know the date.
- **Seed fit:** line 1 *strong*. Line 2 *strong*. Line 3 *weak*: the cough
  comes mainly from the size of the good-faith swing, not from fudged
  clauses, which turns the seed's mechanism over rather than using it. Line
  4 *strong*.

Mean rank: A 1.0, B 2.0, C 3.5, D 3.5.

## Decision

**A, We-Two**, with the conditions below. A consultant second opinion
(`agent-guidance:consultant`) agreed at 88%.

**Seed fit.** A is the only premise strong on every line, and in proxy mode
seed fit decides first. D is rejected on line 3 (its cough comes from the
size of the good-faith swing, not fudging). B is partial on line 4 and C on
line 2. How A serves each line: (1) the creole's inclusive dual was coined so
the treaty could be signed, and the word woke; (2) it exists only in both
peoples' use of it; (3) a dual said exclusively at the table is the cough,
and one meant at home is the colour; (4) Sione's whole job is its company,
and the plot is what that company costs.

**Reader pull.** It rests on the profile's first pull, a mind that is
genuinely not human, and on "the idea pays off in a choice". It is also the
most novel premise (Cartographer 36/40, the only one clear of the
kindness-gamed cluster).

**Conditions, answering the panel's warnings:**
1. **The being decides the we-three question, not the delegations and not
   Sione.** This steers off *Embassytown*'s ending and the hard
   keeper's-one-switch attractor. The debate session stays as the Reach
   weighing scene: the delegations argue and make the offer, but the
   decision is the being's. The reader sees it reason from inside; Sione
   may not be told why. Its answer doesn't end the cough cleanly. What it
   keeps of him is the broken promises it treasures, the proof that he
   chose the rest (Chiang's push).
2. **The love reads as grammar failing, not an AI learning to feel.** It is
   a form with two seats trying to hold a third person (Vinge). The style
   guide bans the AI that learns to love.
3. **The dual has legal force, lightly.** It is the treaty's legal party, so
   admitting a third person changes who counts as a party. Shown as one
   fact in one scene, not a second legal system (Vinge, narrowed).
4. **One concrete image of vastness**, a named delegate who argues the
   rewrite persuasively, and the grammar kept in dialogue, never lectured
   (Banks). The hall saying the dual wrong in unison, cough audible, can
   be both the vastness image and the weighing scene.
5. **Sione is hired for something specific to him,** not as a neutral
   outsider: a Tongan speaker hears who is inside a "we" without trying,
   and he has a trade and a daily routine beyond wanting to belong.
6. **Attractor named:** A breaks "the companion enrolled as a party"
   (run-10 only, not hard) because that enrolment is the seed's line 4
   made literal. The bible keeps it specific: each promise is a small,
   comic, concrete thing.

**Declined or narrowed:**
- Vinge's full push, exclusive plurals governing property and trial across
  Hhoth law: a second system to explain, adding about 1,500 words and
  landing the minimum at the 6,000 ceiling.
- A private, unexplained refusal (the Cartographer's version of condition
  1): strangeness that never resolves and a hidden noble sacrifice are both
  on the reader profile's and style guide's lists.
- The elders-call-it-contamination subplot is a pre-agreed cut if length
  gets tight.

**Runner-up:** B, The Uncut. Seed stock: B, C and D.
