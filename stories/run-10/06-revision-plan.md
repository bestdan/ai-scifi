## Consensus (fixed)

1. **One plain rule for ennu, in scene 1** (Asimov 1, Chiang 7;
   comprehension: Premise; lost-list 2). Say it once in plain words: in
   the Concord, *ennu* has exactly two seats and they are always the same
   two, the shelf and the sea, Hhoth and Ammaru. A dockhand who means both
   peoples uses it right; a delegate who means only his own side shuts the
   other people out of their seat, and it coughs. Cut the gloss paragraph
   ("In Hhoth, native *we* shuts…") by half; keep "Hhoth can't hear the door
   close…" (protected).
2. **The register entries: one voice, seat-grammar, no "I" or "me"**
   (Chiang's one thing, Watts 1, Asimov 6, Cartographer 5; lost-list 6).
   Plant in scene 2 who writes them: the register log, which Sione as the
   one promised to can read on his slate, has an annotation field the clerks
   call ennu's margin. Every entry uses the same flat form, for example:
   > **Register 4,471.** Promise: Sione will come on the fourth day. Seats:
   > Sione; ennu. Status: open. *Annotation: first seat filled from outside
   > ennu.*
   No metaphors, no feelings, no first person. The final entry (scene 7) is
   the first whose Seats line changes form (item 12).
3. **Talek's lattice speech, cut to what it hears and does** (Asimov 9,
   Chiang; comprehension: Premise; lost-list 4, 5). It hears only promises
   in the creole; it speaks only by entering promises, which come to the one
   promised to as the read-back, a flat clerk-voice in the earpiece. Say
   plainly that the read-back voice is ennu. Plant here that each read-back
   is stamped with the recipient's address (needed for item 12).
4. **Cut the repeated "it's a cheat" beats; make Sione suspect himself and
   be wrong informatively** (Cartographer's one thing, Asimov 3, Chiang 3,
   Le Guin 6; lost-list 9). In scene 3 he notices a hitch after a read-back
   and tests it: he stays away from the vault for two days and the hitches go
   on, so he concludes it isn't him. (It goes on because his promises are
   still open and ennu goes on answering them.) Keep one real cheat caught.
   Keep Ressa once in scene 3, briefly, and once in scene 5. Cut "glared for
   a crooked factor" and "closing in on crooked glass-brokers".
5. **The twist mechanism in one plain sentence** (Asimov 1, Watts;
   comprehension: Twist "not that ennu has no 'I'…"; lost-list 8). In scene
   5, after the timestamps: ennu has no word for I. To promise Sione
   anything it must say *ennu will*, and that puts Sione in one of its two
   seats, which belong to the two peoples, so one people is pushed out, and
   it coughs. His open promises are the cause: every one he keeps open, it
   answers. Keep "Nobody was cheating. It hasn't got a word for I…"
   (Twist's explicit line; it also serves Premise).
6. **The broken promises, made mechanical, with Sione's reading marked as
   his** (Watts 3, Chiang 2, Le Guin 4, Cartographer 7; lost-list 10).
   Keep ennu's line "*Ennu* will keep the ones you broke. You could have
   broken all of them." (protected). Then: Talek says a register can't close
   or delete a lapsed promise; it keeps them for good. Sione says what he
   thinks it means (the broken ones prove the kept ones were choices) and
   that he can't tell which is true. Cut the 202/211 arithmetic everywhere.
7. **Sione's plea in the vault is itself a promise** (Watts 4). It hears
   only promises: "I'll close every promise I've made you, if that's what
   it takes." Then he closes all forty. Say plainly: zero open; the nine
   broken ones are lapsed, not open. When ennu goes on promising to the
   empty seat, say why in one line (it answers the address, not his
   presence). Keep the empty-chair dilemma line.
8. **One rehearsal clause in full, two summarised; spend the room on
   Sione's temptation** (Asimov 10, Le Guin 3, Chiang 4, Cartographer 2).
   Give the seat a paragraph: what it offers him, inside a "we" without
   earning it, never on the porch again, his name in the Concord. Then he
   flags the rehearsal anyway. Say "legalizing exclusion inside the pronoun"
   once.
9. **Ennu refuses ennu-sa, visibly, and for a reason in its gradient**
   (Chiang 5, Watts 2, Asimov 2; comprehension: Ending; lost-list 12). Not
   "passed through unread": the register **declines to log it**, and the
   tally-board says so. Its reason, in promises: every ennu-sa in the hall
   put one seat outside, and each one coughed; a logged ennu-sa would cough
   for ever. "*Ennu-sa* would seat three. In this hall, every promise made
   with it seated two against one. *Ennu* can't be that." stays (Ending's
   explicit line), with one added line on the coughs.
10. **The "you" fix: Sione finds it, then one plain sentence**
    (Asimov 4, Chiang 6, Le Guin 5; lost-list 13). After the refusal, Sione
    remembers the address stamp (item 3) and asks ennu, as a promise, to
    promise him to the address on the envelope instead of in *ennu*. It
    says: "You will come on the fourth day. You is the seat nobody else is
    in." (protected). Then one plain sentence: a promise to *you* has two
    seats, Sione's and the register's, and no people in either, so nobody
    is pushed out and it doesn't cough. Keep "It hadn't learned to say *I*;
    it had solved a seating problem with a postal header." Cut the
    chair-borrowing gloss and Sione's long explanation.
11. **The open-promise numbers, one clean sum** (Asimov 5, Watts 8).
    Forty open at the peak; nine broken over the four months; all forty
    closed in scene 5; nine new promises made in the "you" form in scene 7
    (not "matching the broken ones").
12. **The final entry** (Chiang 1; lost-list 13). Same flat form,
    changed grammar, no annotation the reader can't parse:
    > **Register 4,682.** Promise: you will come on the fourth day. Seats:
    > you; the register. Outside: none. Status: open.
    Cut the current annotation.
