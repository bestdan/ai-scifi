# Outline: We-Two (working title)

- **Target length:** about 5,000 words (range 3,000 to 6,000)
- **Shape:** linear, first person, about four months, in seven scenes.
  Six short register entries from ennu's side sit at section breaks. Each
  entry is a promise as ennu perceives it: its seats, whether it's open,
  and an annotation in seat-terms. The entries are the form enacting the
  idea: the reader sees the only thing ennu can see, and by the end can
  read the twist in them before Sione does.
- **Opening question:** what is this word everyone is so careful with, and
  why does a court interpreter from Auckland have its job?
- **Final image:** a register entry. "Register 4,682. Sione will come on the
  fourth day. Seats: you. Open." Its annotation uses "you" slightly wrong.
- **What the ending costs the protagonist:** the seat. He closes most of his
  open promises, so ennu coughs less and sees him less. He stays outside
  the word he loves. Ennu gets a "you", not a "we".

## What each part asks

| Part | The new question it raises |
|---|---|
| I (scenes 1–2) | What is ennu, and how can anyone reach a mind that hears only promises? |
| II (scenes 3–4) | Who is making it sick, and can Sione trust the ear the whole job rests on? |
| III (scene 5) | What if the cause is the friendship itself? |
| IV (scenes 6–7) | Can the peoples make room for him, and what does a word choose when it is offered a new shape? |

**Fair-play plant.** In scenes 2 and 3, each time ennu replies to Sione with
an "ennu will" promise, there is a hitch, which he puts down to the
session, a hauler or a delegate. The register entries show the seats: in
the entries that answer him, the seats read "Sione, ennu" and the
annotation notes someone "outside". An attentive reader can see it before
scene 5.

## The cough rule (one rule, stated in scene 2)
Ennu coughs whenever an ennu seats someone outside: a delegate's exclusive
ennu puts the other people out, and ennu's own "ennu will" to Sione puts a
whole people out, because Sione takes one of its two seats. Every cough is
global: each earpiece hitches, and every promise in mid-air on Ninefold
fails to register. Nobody correlates the hitches with ennu's replies
because the read-back channel is private by design: only the one promised
to hears ennu's entries, and the clerks log cough times but never
read-backs. Sione is the only person on Ninefold being promised to by
ennu, and the only one who could make the match.

## Before and after "you" (the ending's mechanism, stated in scene 7)
- Before: "Register 4,520. Ennu will hear the weather on the fourth day.
  Seats: Sione, ennu. Outside: one people."
- After: "Register 4,682. Sione will come on the fourth day. Seats: you.
  Open." A "you" promise names one seat, and the other is the register
  itself, the flat voice of the read-back, not ennu's dual. No people is
  in it, so no people is put out. Sione states the rule plainly. It is a
  grammar fix, not ennu becoming human: it uses "you" as a seat nobody else
  may sit in, which is slightly wrong.
