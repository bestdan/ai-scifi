# Stage 3 critique: Banks lens, run-10

Blind spots, stated: this lens favours sprawl, set pieces and worldbuilding,
and short stories aren't its natural form. Where I ask for scale below, take
it as one concrete moment, not a tour. Defer to Chiang and the length
estimates where they say cut. I haven't checked novelty or the
materials science; that is the Cartographer's and Vinge's work.

## A. We-Two

**Plain pitch:** holds. A reader outside the genre knows what it is to need a
word for "you and me, nobody else" before a peace can be signed. The world
does this because the peoples' grammars forced it, and that is a reason.

**Friction and dirty work:** the best of the four. A delegate who says "we
two" and means "us, not you" is doing harm for a career, and nobody signed it
off. The sharper material is the ending: two peoples, "alarmed and touched",
offer to rewrite the being's grammar to let one man in. That is a
benevolent hegemon doing violence out of kindness, which is the moral ground
I'd work. Make sure someone on the page actually argues it is fine.

**Humour:** lives in the small promises ("I'll tell you the truth about the
weather"), and a being that tastes a played dual. Keep it there.

**Scale and intimacy:** Reach goes past the household (border children,
elders, careers), but it is stated, not felt. It needs one moment of
vastness: the dual said at once by a whole hall, or one kitchen on each world
at the same hour.

**Under the tone charter:** warm and enjoyable to inhabit. Risk is that the
grammar gets lectured; the creole has to be heard in dialogue.

## B. The Uncut

**Plain pitch:** holds, and is the most concrete: a map of pillars nobody may
cut. Anyone can see why a mining town would do this.

**Friction and dirty work:** strong. The laid-off crew boss pays for the
being's health, and an honest crew mines less, so virtue shrinks the town.
Dirty work is the crews' retreat robbing, and the town's quiet hunger.

**Humour:** comic boasting about tonnes not taken is a real joke, and a
rigorous one. It has to carry the story, because the pace won't.

**Scale and intimacy:** a salt bed under a town is scale you can stand
in. Dawit's tapping hammer is the intimacy. This pairing is the best of the
four.

**Tone:** the three-day sentences pull against "fun and warm". Line 4 is
only partial, since he is half translator. The gift of a pillar is
lovely, but it is the keeper's-one-switch shape.

## C. True Finds Only

**Plain pitch:** holds in the second sentence, less so in the first
("smelled by a rebuilt nose" needs a scene). It is the hardest to say aloud.

**Friction and dirty work:** good. Both compliance offices trim loads on a
reading he knows is contaminated. He is the dirty work, and nobody told him.

**Humour:** dog-handler jokes are there, but I find none in the being, which
the synthesis itself calls "more a patient than a mind".

**Scale and intimacy:** intimacy is high, close to the body. Scale is two
trading economies, stated through compliance numbers. No vastness moment is
named.

**Tone:** a man's sweat and breath as reactants is physical and a little
grim, and two nova and a twist crowd a 6,000-word ceiling. It is the
weakest as company for the seed's "keep it company".

## D. The Long Quiet

**Plain pitch:** holds, and is the funniest: talk two peoples out of their
best party. A reader sees it at once.

**Friction and dirty work:** the festival committee pays, which is real. But
nobody is doing wrong, so the morality is too clean.

**Humour:** the most of any, and the best set piece (the festival).

**Scale and intimacy:** eleven-year cycles and an anchor cable between worlds
are scale you feel. The widowed rigger is intimate.

**Tone:** warm. But the synthesis rates seed line 3 *weak*, and the brief's
gate rejects that in proxy mode. The cough is fatigue, not a fudged clause.
I'd rather it be rescued than rejected, but I can't see how without turning
it into a different premise. Seed stock.

## Ranking

1. **A, We-Two.** All four seed lines strong, the clearest plain pitch, and
   the richest moral friction.
2. **B, The Uncut.** The best scale and the best joke; held back by tone and
   line 4.
3. **C, True Finds Only.** Strong mechanism, thin being, hardest pitch.
4. **D, The Long Quiet.** The funniest, but fails the seed gate on line 3.

## Why A should win

The seed is a being made of a promise, and A is the only premise where the
promise is the being's whole mind, so every line of dialogue is a heartbeat.
It is also the one where the jokes come from the mechanism: a man making
small promises to be visible, and being bound by each. What I'd keep
from the synthesis is the ending, which is a moral trap and not a clean cure.
Two benevolent peoples want to rewrite the being for his sake, and the being
loves him enough to ask for it. Whoever refuses does harm, and whoever agrees
does too. Give that to a named delegate who argues it persuasively. To hold
scale, add one concrete image of vastness, and make the weighing scene a
room full of people saying the dual wrong in unison, and the cough audible.
If A sags on length or the grammar turns to lecture, B is the strong fallback,
and its pillar gift is the better plot moment, but it will need warming.
