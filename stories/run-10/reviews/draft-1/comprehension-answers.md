# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A human interpreter on an ice world listens for lies hidden in "we" for a treaty between two species, the Hhoth and the Ammaru. The treaty's living register has started answering him in the shared word *ennu*, which keeps knocking one of the two peoples out of the deal, until it learns to say "you" to him instead.

2. He stands to keep his job and his place as the one who can hear the door. He risks being made a third seat in the word, or being blamed for the coughing that is stopping everyone's promises from closing. If he fails, nobody on the planet can finish a promise. The Hhoth and the Ammaru would then find out what they say to each other without a word for both. He also risks his own standing with the Table.

3. The word was made after a war. A Hhoth brood said "we will not drill" and meant only itself, and the Ammaru heard "all of us". A generation of Ammaru young died. So the two peoples made a word that can only mean both, with two seats and no hidden exclusion. I believe the register was built to log promises and nothing else, and to leave ordinary speech unread because both sides feared being spied on. I'm less sure why a human was hired. I took it to be because his languages (Tongan, Samoan, Māori) split "we" into with-you and without-you.

4. Late on, I learned the hitches were the glass answering him with *ennu*, not cheats on the ice. It was also late that the Drilling was the war behind the word, and that Aaruum's warning about hearing doors applied to his sister. That recast the earlier hitches, Ressa's trouble and his pleased hunt for "crooked brokers". It also made the early scene with Vell's "breakfast" and the test errors look like setup for the same theme.

5. The Table tried to add a third seat for him (*ennu-sa*), and the rehearsal clauses showed that three seats just let two gang up on one. The unison vow went unlogged. The glass then said "You will come on the fourth day", using "you" as a seat only he can sit in, and no people got pushed out. He reopened nine promises in the new form and Vell lost his chairmanship. He wrote his sister with *kitautolu* (all of us, including him), offering to help paint the fence. It worked because "you" names one seat and no one else.

6. Sione Havili: the narrator, a Tongan-New Zealand court interpreter, thirty-eight, the Listener. Vell: Second Speaker, a Hhoth politician, dry and fond, who pushes *ennu-sa* and loses his chairmanship. Talek: the junior Hhoth clerk in the vault, fussy and kind. Aaruum: an old Ammaru elder who was wrong to be cut off and who tells Sione the story of the Drilling. Ressa: a Hhoth hauler on the ice whose sleds keep losing slots. Tikk: an eleven-year-old Hhoth pupil who catches Sione's exclusive "we". Lesieli: Sione's mother, in Auckland. Mele: his sister, whose messages he misread. I don't recall a name for the glass beyond "the register" or "the lattice".

7. Whether the glass is a person now. Why it treasures his broken promises. What the last annotation means about someone else sitting in "you". Whether Mele writes back. Why the lattice woke after sixty-one years and not earlier. Whether the "you" fix lasts. I'm also unsure of the exact numbers (nine broken, forty open, two hundred and eleven made).

8. *Ennu*: from Talek and the examples, "you and I, both, nobody else". Hhoth and Ammaru: from context, the two species, one clicking on ice, one singing in water. Lattice and register: Talek's explanation; roughly a glass promise-logger. Read-back channel: context; the private voice only he hears. Flag-key: context, his chime button. Creole: context, the shared language. *Kitaua*, *kimaua*, *kitautolu*: the mother's message and the last message showed they mean you-and-I, she-and-I, all of us. *Lu pulu*: a Tongan dish, I guessed from the corned beef and kelp. The Drilling: from Aaruum, the war. *Ennu-sa*: Vell says it's "we-three". "Bless": the world's reflex for the hitch, from context, like sneezing.

9. For the forty million speakers it means promises can close again. The word stays two-seated and nobody is excluded by hidden meaning. It also means that a person who is not in either people can have a private seat with the register without costing anyone theirs. More broadly, the story suggests a "we" is only safe if no one is quietly left outside it, and that one seat can be given without taking another.

10. Wry, tender, clever. The line that set it for me was the opening: "when a man says *we* under oath, he usually means himself and the brother-in-law who owns the truck."
