# Critique of draft 1: le-guin

## Scores

| Group | Axis | Score | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Felt | Atmosphere | 4 | Ice, kelp tea, "Bless" said to nobody: a world that sneezes together. Home life of the two peoples is thin. |
| Felt | Weight | 3 | Aaruum at the bore and Mele land. Ressa and the Ammaru side stay light. |
| Craft | Structure | 3 | Gathering shape is right in the middle; the plenary reverts to a debate with three identical rehearsals. |
| Craft | Character | 3 | Sione is a person (mother, fence, lu pulu). The being is moving but is told more than shown. |
| Craft | Consequence | 3 | Vell pays visibly. Aaruum's five lost days and Ressa's slot are paid for in a line. |
| Craft | Prose | 3 | Strong jokes and images; several overpacked sentences. |

## The one thing
Make the people who pay into people. Aaruum's lost five drift-lane days (the
Gathering year, two winters off) and Ressa's slot get one scene each where
someone lives the loss, and Sione's temptation at the plenary ("a permanent
seat inside the pronoun itself") gets a scene instead of a sentence.

## Notes
1. **Scene 4, Aaruum's forgiveness (structural):** Sione's misflag "cost an
   elder a two-year buffer", then she forgives him at the bore in a page of
   gentle proverbs. Nobody on the west shelf is shown paying, and the cost
   is never revisited. Either let it come back in the plenary (Aaruum
   speaking against his seat with the lapsed clause in her throat) or let it
   bite harder. Her speech is also close to instruction ("a man who spends
   his life listening for a door...") and then the Mele paragraph explains it:
   "she hadn't been shutting the door, only sparing me the guilt". Trust the
   proverb; cut the explanation, or dramatise it as a reply from Mele.
2. **Ressa and the buyer (structural):** the bible's "ones who pay" are
   a running joke. The Ammaru buyer has no line and no name. The Ammaru as a
   people appear only as Aaruum and chorus; Hhoth carry the plot. Give the
   buyer one exchange with Ressa, in the lock, that shows a lived trade.
3. **Scene 6, the three rehearsals (structural):** the second and third do
   the first's work, and the first reader had it after one. Keep one,
   and spend the room on what the seat offers Sione. He wants to be inside a
   "we" without earning it (bible). That want is paid off by "Look the other
   way for thirty seconds" and one thumb-press. This is the story's real
   choice and it costs him nothing on the page.
4. **Scene 5, "it treasured them" (line-level):** the narrator tells the
   being's feeling and the reasoning ("only a promise I could break proved...")
   lost the cold reader. The clerk-voice line alone is stronger; cut the
   paragraph after it.
5. **Scene 7, "you" (structural):** "the other side of the line... is the
   register's own clerk-voice reading the address tag" is muddled. The gloss
   about borrowing chairs repeats it. One plain sentence, then stop.
6. **Weeks six to ten (line-level):** the reader was ahead of Sione here and
   found it stalling. Ressa's "bless" and "closing in on crooked glass-brokers"
   repeat what scene 1 already did.
7. **Society (line-level):** the bible's brood-hatching, once-in-seven-years
   spawning, and "a smaller Gathering" for death never reach the page. One
   meal or one chore among the Hhoth or in the salt-tanks would make the grammar
   a life. The school scene is the model.
8. **Language:** *ennu*, *bless*, *kimaua/kitautolu* all earn their place and
   imply a culture. *ennu-sa* is a clean coinage. Keep the register
   entries' voice but settle whose it is (reader asked).

## What to protect
- The carrier-bag heart: "keeping a light on in a cellar for a friend who
  couldn't see the stairs unless you owed him an errand: *I'll bring the
  chessboard on Thursday; I'll count the ice-petrels...*" Promise as gift.
- "Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door."
- Tikk and the copper token, and Vell's nephew joke at the end.
- The lu pulu with winch-grease kelp; the hauler saying "Bless" to nobody.
- "*You* is the seat nobody else is in." Keep it unglossed past one sentence.
- Mele's *kitautolu* and the unanswered ending.

## Blind spot disclosure
I prize gathering and cost, so I will over-ask for slow scenes, and I lean
toward moral explanation myself, which is why I flag it in Aaruum. I cannot
judge the mechanism's rigour (seats, timestamps, the cough rule); Egan and
Lem should. My pacing tolerance is high: the First Reader's boredom in
weeks six to ten should outweigh my comfort there.
