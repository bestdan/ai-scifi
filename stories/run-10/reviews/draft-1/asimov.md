## The one thing
State the rule of *ennu* in plain terms in scene 1 and keep it consistent. Right now it has two incompatible definitions, and the resolution depends on the one the page hides until scene 5.

## Notes
1. **Structural. The rule contradicts itself.** Scene 1 defines it as "*Ennu*... You and I, both of us, nobody else." The dockhand "says *ennu* to an Ammaru buyer and means both of them." By that rule the glass saying *ennu* to Sione is correct use: speaker plus listener. The diagnosis needs *ennu*'s seats to be the two peoples, and only Talek's "A dual has two seats: Hhoth and Ammaru" (scene 5) says so. I can't state the rules in three sentences by the midpoint. Pick one seat definition, plant it in scene 1 or 2, and have the cough punish "someone is shut out of a seat they hold".
2. **Structural. Cough mechanics wobble.** The cough means "the glass detected an exclusive *ennu*", yet the glass causes its own cough by speaking. The *ennu-sa* unison vow draws the "spruce beam" cough, then "passed through the glass unread". Was it heard or not? Say what the register does with an unrecognised word.
3. **Structural. Clever people wait for the plot.** Sione notes in the sixth week that the hitch arrives with each answer ("murmuring *Bless* each time its answer came with a hitch"), then hunts "crooked glass-brokers" through week ten. The first reader was "impatient" and ahead of him. He should suspect himself early, test it, and be wrong in an informative way. The retrospective narrator is hiding what he knew.
4. **Structural. The solver is the glass, not the detective.** The *you* fix arrives from the register's own mouth. Sione explains it afterwards ("Look at the subject"). Fair play needs the address tag planted as a usable fact: it appears in passing only ("private to the recipient's address by statute", "calling my seat"). Let Sione propose the test that makes the glass reach for it.
5. **Structural. Open-promise counts don't reconcile.** He closes "all forty", says "Zero open vows", yet the nine broken ones stay open in the glass (Register 4,602). Later he reopens nine and leaves "the other thirty-one closed", which assumes the nine were inside the forty. Also "the other two hundred and two" and 211 total. The first reader flagged the arithmetic twice. Give one clean sum.
6. **Line-level. The register annotations break the rule they illustrate.** "the far seat is occupied by someone who is not me" and "one half of me" use *I*-forms, while the story says the glass has no *I*. The first reader couldn't tell whose voice they are. Decide: the clerk-statute's voice, or the glass, and keep it third person.
7. **Line-level. Timeline conflict.** The creole is sixty-one years old, yet "the oldest of whom was fourteen" among children who hear it natively. Adults would have grown up in it.
8. **Line-level. Dangling debt.** "I owe the west shelf five days of quiet, Elder" is never paid. Consequence for Aaruum should surface in the ending.
9. **Line-level. Exposition in dialogue.** Talek's lattice speech ("woke as the word itself", "feels the lie in its ribs", "its only output is the register's entry channel") lost the reader. Cut to what the glass hears and what it does.
10. **Line-level. Rehearsal triplet.** The three *ennu-sa* clauses, each annotated, run long; the reader got it after one. Keep one fully, summarise two.

## What to protect
- "Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it."
- Aaruum's "a clever minnow who has learned to whistle in two keys" and the Tongan *kimaua* thread, which gave the cold reader a person.
- The first cough, with the hauler saying "*Bless*" to nobody.
- The timestamp matching in the vault: the one real clue-and-solution beat. Keep it central.
- "*You* is the seat nobody else is in."

## Blind spot disclosure
I weight rule-consistency and fair play over feeling, so I may overrate fixing the arithmetic and underrate the Mele thread, which the cold reader found strongest. I can't judge whether the glass's loneliness works emotionally. Defer to Le Guin and Lem on the *you* ending's weight.
