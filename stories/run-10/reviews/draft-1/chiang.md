## The one thing
Make the register entries the form that enacts the idea, and make them hold to the idea's own grammar. Ennu has no "I", and the entries are its only voice. Entry 4,471 ends "*the far seat is occupied by someone who is not me*". That "me" breaks the story's central rule on page 1 of the form. Either every annotation is written in seat-terms with no first person, so the reader feels the lack before Sione names it, or the entries are dropped. Right now the reader couldn't say who wrote them (first-reader item 6), so the form isn't yet doing work.

## Notes

1. **Register entries (all six), structural.** They are the story's one formal device and they aren't consistent. 4,471 uses "me". 4,473 narrates Sione in third person ("He has no ice to sell"). 4,520 uses "the other half stands in the corridor and shakes the door", which is Sione's metaphor, not a seat-thinker's. Pick one grammar: seats, open or closed, and who is in them. The final entry, 4,682, should be the first one in which the grammar changes to "you". It currently reads "Seats: you", then an annotation the cold reader could not parse. Let the form show the change instead.

2. **The vault, "only a promise I could break proved I had chosen to keep the other two hundred and two", structural.** This is the idea's emotional centre, and the page narrates it in one sentence of Sione's inference. The reader took it "on trust" and was unsure it was right. Dramatise it. Ennu could keep a broken promise as an entry that hasn't closed, and Sione could ask it why. Cut the arithmetic (202, 211, "thirty-one") from the last section. It reads as bookkeeping where the story wants a moment between them.

3. **The coughs on the second reading, structural.** The mechanism is that ennu's "ennu will" to Sione seats a people out. Scene 5 makes it plain, but the reader was ahead of Sione from week six. That is dramatic irony and it can stay, but Ressa's "bless, damn it" and "crooked glass-brokers" in weeks six to ten spend four hundred words on an investigation the reader has already finished. Cut the Ressa-on-the-apron beat in scene 3, and keep her once, at the failed contract.

4. **Plenary, three rehearsal clauses, structural.** Three clauses and Sione glossing each one is one beat said thrice. Keep one, with the other two as a sentence of rumour. The narration then needs only the line "legalizing exclusion right inside the pronoun" once.

5. **Why *ennu-sa* passes "unread", structural.** The bible says ennu declines to log it. The page says it "passed through the glass unread", which is the register's rule for ordinary speech. These are different facts: refusal is a choice, unread is a filter. The reader got lost here (item 12). A refusal is the climax, so say refusal. Ennu must visibly decline.

6. **The ending answers too neatly, structural.** "A postal header" solves the seating problem, and the story then states the solution twice: the read-back voice, then Sione's paragraph. "*You* is the seat nobody else is in" is the line to keep. Cut Sione's explanation ("A *you* promise names only one seat...") to two sentences, and let the reader do the work. The Mele message, which works, then closes the question.

7. **Scene 4, line-level.** Aaruum's account of the Drilling is the history, and it is told as speech, at length. The story gets it right by setting it at the bore with the song underneath. Keep it. The line "The Hhoth can't hear the door..." in scene 1 is stronger than the exposition in "In Hhoth, native *we* shuts the listener out". Cut the plain gloss in paragraph 3 down by half.

8. **Line-level.** Several sentences stack three causal clauses: "I had cost an elder a two-year buffer because I hadn't learned how a seventy-year-old throat drops pitch at the end of a breath, and Vell didn't look toward my desk for the rest of the morning." Split them.

## What to protect

- "Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it."
- Aaruum: "A man who spends his life listening for a door shutting will hear one in his own mother's house." The Mele reread that follows is the idea producing the feeling, which is the story at its best.
- "*You* is the seat nobody else is in."
- The final *kitautolu* message to Mele, and that nine days' delay leaves the answer open.
- "Feeling like a barrister swearing an oath to a radiator."
- The world sneezing together: a hauler saying "*Bless*" to nobody.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour the pronoun-logic and will over-weight its precision, so I may under-value Ressa and Vell, who give the story its friction and its humour. A cut I ask for (Ressa on the apron, two of the three rehearsal clauses) removes some of the plot momentum Banks would defend. I am also wary of the story's warmth and may be too severe on the canteen scene; it may be the one scene that earns its place by being plain fun.
