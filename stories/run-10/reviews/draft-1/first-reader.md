# First Reader report: draft 1 ("The Seat Across")

## Where I got lost

1. **Paragraph 2 (the Table, the lattice, the two species).** Fifteen hundred of one kind above, fifteen hundred below, a "creole by earpiece", a "flag-key", a "Listener's desk". I took in the picture but had to hold a lot of names at once. I understood it roughly by the end of the paragraph. The ice-tonnage joke helped.
2. **Paragraph 3 ("Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door.").** I liked the line, but I didn't fully understand why a "we" that shuts people out is a problem until later. I worked it out slowly from the examples. I never got a clean, plain statement of what *ennu* does.
3. **The first hitch (the junior attaché and "Bless").** I couldn't tell what had gone wrong. A verb was "bitten off", the earpieces coughed, and everybody said "bless". It read to me as a glitch with no cause. I only guessed that it was a machine reacting to a lie. Talek explained that in the next scene, so I worked it out there.
4. **Talek's explanation of the lattice.** I was lost for several sentences. A register that "hears promises", "woke as the word itself", and "feels the lie in its ribs" is a lot to take in. I followed the gist: the glass is alive and the word is its only voice. But "its only output is the register's entry channel" lost me.
5. **The read-back voice.** I wasn't sure for a while whether it was the lattice talking, a recording, or a clerk's voice. I settled on "the glass talking to him alone".
6. **The italic register entries between scenes.** I couldn't tell who wrote them or who they were for. The "Annotation" lines read as if the glass wrote them, but sometimes they sounded like a person. I never settled this. I liked them anyway, but I don't know whose voice that is.
7. **The Tongan paragraph (*kitaua*, *kimaua*).** It confused me at first. I got it once the mother's message used *kimaua* ("she and Mele, not me"). Then it was clear and good.
8. **The turn at "I looked back at the rose-lit glass warming my shin; it had only one first-person pronoun..."** I didn't follow that sentence the first time.
9. **Why the hitches were happening (the middle).** I was ahead of the narrator here. The read-back said "*Ennu* will..." right as the hitches struck, and I guessed it was him well before he did. I wasn't lost. I was impatient, which I note under boredom.
10. **The vault scene with the timestamps.** I followed it, but "I've broken nine promises ... it treasured them because only a promise I could break proved I had chosen to keep the other two hundred and two" lost me on the numbers. I couldn't see why broken promises mattered to it, or why it keeps them. I partly worked it out as "it values that he chose to keep the rest". I'm not sure that's right.
11. **Releasing all forty promises and the glass promising to a chair.** I got that it didn't need to see him to keep promising. I didn't understand the mechanism and moved on.
12. **The plenary and *ennu-sa*.** I understood a third seat means a third vote. I was lost on why the unison vow "passed through the glass unread". I worked that out from the clerk-voice explanation, which was long.
13. **The ending's "you" explanation.** "It had solved a seating problem with a postal header" and "you wasn't a chair anybody could borrow" lost me for a bit. I got the main point: "you" is the one seat nobody else can sit in. The register's last annotation about "if another person ever tries to sit in you" I did not understand.

## Where I got bored

- **The second half of the opening scene, from the junior attaché onward.** I was waiting for something to be at stake. It's all clever and well observed, but it's setup, and no one I cared about was in trouble yet.
- **Weeks six to ten (the classroom, the mother's voicemail, Ressa shouting).** I was waiting for the story to turn. I liked Tikk and the token. Ressa's repeated "bless" and the narrator "sure I was closing in on crooked glass-brokers" felt like stalling. I knew by then it was the glass, so I was just waiting for him to catch up.
- **The long middle explanation of the committee, the eleven candidates and Vell's recap.** I was waiting for the scene to move.
- **The plenary, once the three rehearsal clauses started.** Three nearly identical clauses, each followed by the narrator spelling out what was wrong with it. I got it after the first one.
- **The last two pages after the plenary.** The wrap-up in the canteen and the long "reopened nine promises, the other thirty-one closed" paragraph. I wanted it to end sooner than it did, and the arithmetic bogged me down.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- Opening line: "when a man says *we* under oath, he usually means himself and the brother-in-law who owns the truck." I was hooked at once.
- "Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it." This is where I decided I trusted the story.
- The first hitch spreading across the planet, with a hauler on the ice saying "Bless" to nobody. That picture of a whole world sneezing together delighted me.
- The lattice scene: "Feeling like a barrister swearing an oath to a radiator" made me laugh. The glass leaning toward his boot like a coal under ash was lovely.
- "Nobody was cheating... It hasn't got a word for I." I was happy to be right, and the line landed.
- Aaruum at the bore: "a clever minnow who has learned to whistle in two keys. You were hired to guard a lock, so every shadow looks like a crowbar." And then: "A man who spends his life listening for a door shutting will hear one in his own mother's house." This was the best moment for me. It stung because it was about him, not the world.
- The glass answering "*Ennu* will keep the ones you broke... You could have broken all of them." Strange and tender. I didn't fully follow the reasoning, but I was moved by the feeling of it.
- "*You* is the seat nobody else is in." This is the line I'll remember.
- Tikk fining him a copper token, and Vell's nephew line at the end about fining him for clearing his throat. Warm and funny.
- Mele and the fence: his realising she wasn't shutting him out. The last message ("kitautolu will have to paint that back fence") worked on me.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about **Sione**, mostly because of the mother and sister thread. The hurt of being left on the porch in his own family is what made him a person to me, not just a clever ear. I cared about **the glass**, which I found myself feeling sorry for. It is lonely, it is trying hard, and it keeps hurting people by accident. I liked **Talek** and **Tikk** and **Vell**, in that order. **Aaruum** I respected more than liked. **Ressa** is a joke that runs through the story, and I never cared about her much.

Yes, I'd have kept reading past page 2. The opening line and the "door" paragraph carried me. I'd say the first real pull was the lattice scene, where the glass answered him. Before that I was reading on trust, because the voice was good and not because I was worried about anyone. Page 2 itself is crowded with terms, and I'd have been close to putting it down if the voice weren't so funny.

## The question I'm left with

Is the glass a person now, or is it still only a word that learned to talk? It says "you" but not "I", and he wrote his sister "kitautolu". I want to know if the sister writes back, and whether anyone ever sits in the glass's other seat. I'm also left wondering whether Sione is being kind to the glass or keeping it on a string.
