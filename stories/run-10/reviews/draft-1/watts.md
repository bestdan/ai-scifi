## The one thing
Give the being a selective logic and keep it from sounding like a lonely person. Decide what the cough does to it, and what in its gradient makes it drop *ennu-sa* and learn "you". Then remove every line where it uses "I" or "me", or states a feeling in fluent English.

## Notes

1. **Register 4,471, "someone who is not me" (structural):** The glass has no "I", and the twist is built on that. Its own annotation says "me". Register 4,520 ("one half of me... shakes the door") reads as Sione's voice. The First Reader never settled whose voice the annotations are (lost-list 6). Hold them to seat-grammar: "Seats: Sione, ennu. The far seat is occupied."

2. **Plenary, "Ennu can't be that" (structural):** This is the flinch. A gradient that closes loops warm is turned into a being that reflects on what it is. Nothing shows that coughing costs it anything. It coughed 240 times into an empty vault and went on. Put the pressure on the page: the cough kills loops it is inside, and an *ennu-sa* the register logs would produce coughs it cannot close. Then the refusal is self-interest that happens to look like grace. The bible's two readings, insight or mirror, don't survive the draft's tone. The First Reader "felt sorry for it".

3. **Vault, "it treasured them because only a promise I could break proved I had chosen" (structural):** This is the narrator's story about the glass, stated as fact. It is also the line the First Reader couldn't follow (lost-list 10). A gradient has a more concrete reason to keep a scar: an unclosed loop is a record it can't unlearn. Mark the "treasured" reading as Sione's confabulation, or replace it with the mechanical one. Question 3: what is the glass doing that he doesn't know?

4. **Rule leak, "Stop answering me... Let those loops go" (structural):** The lattice hears only promises and passes everything else through unread (Talek, scene 2). Sione's imperative and his "I've broken nine promises" are not promises, yet the glass answers them in the same breath. Either make the plea a promise ("I'll stop... if you'll...") or have the glass answer the open loops, not his words.

5. **Mele paragraph, "she hadn't been shutting the door, only sparing me the guilt" (line-level):** One midnight with five years of texts turns the sister's motives into a tidy, kind reading. It is the epiphany resolving an arc in a paragraph, which the style guide bans. It is also a man who misjudged one ear that afternoon now over-correcting with certainty, which is how confirmation bias works in reverse. Let him be unsure. The *kitautolu* ending works better as a risk than as a fix.

6. **Aaruum, "A man who spends his life listening for a door shutting will hear one in his own mother's house" (line-level):** The theme is said aloud. The First Reader loved it, so keep it, but the Mele paragraph then needs to do less.

7. **Numbers, Scene 4 (line-level):** The Drilling was 68 years ago, in a Gathering year, with the next one in two winters. That is ten seven-year cycles (70), not "nine cycles back". Fix the count.

8. **Open vows, scenes 3 to 5 (line-level):** "211 made, 9 broken, 202 kept, 40 open" and "nine reopened to match the nine broken" read as bookkeeping. Why reopen the broken nine in "you" form? Cut to one number.

## What to protect

- "*Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it.*" The clusivity premise is real linguistics, and Tongan is the right source.
- Sione feeling "sure I was closing in on crooked glass-brokers" while his own kindnesses cause the hitches. A professional liar-catcher blind to himself is the best neuroscience in the draft.
- The misflag of Aaruum. His ear is fallible, and it costs a clause and a person.
- "*Ennu will keep the ones you broke... You could have broken all of them.*" Strange and unhuman, if its reason is made mechanical.
- Tikk's "Flag!". The next Listener hears this natively, and the story stays warm.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour mechanism over meaning, so I may be undervaluing the register's tenderness, which the First Reader found the most moving thing in the draft. Le Guin and Banks may rightly want the glass warmer than I do. I did not check the lint output.
