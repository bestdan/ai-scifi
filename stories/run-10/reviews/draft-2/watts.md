## The one thing
Close the rule leak around the empty chair and the "you" fix. The being must obey its own grammar, and the story must say why a seat held by the register leaves nobody outside.

## Notes

1. **Scene 5, after the forty discharges (structural):** Talek says the lattice "hears promises. That's all it was built to hear" and "speaks only by entering a promise back". Sione now holds zero open promises, so it has nothing to answer. Yet it voices "*Ennu* will wait by pier two until Sione speaks" and the register coughs 240 times overnight. It is generating loops unprompted. Either say that closing a promise leaves a standing duty (the gradient closes loops warm, so an unaddressed loop is its own open loop), or the rule from scene 2 is broken. Say it in one line.
2. **Scene 7, "Outside: none" (structural):** The cough rule is that *ennu*'s two seats belong to two peoples, and Sione displaces one. A promise "you; the register" puts neither people in a seat either. Why does it not leave both outside? The mechanism sentence ("no people in either") describes the result and gives no reason. Also, "the register" is a seat, and Talek separated the register from *ennu* in scene 2. Say what the register is that *ennu* is not. Otherwise this is a third seat, which the story just refused.
3. **Scene 5, "dying of me in the dark" (line-level, anthropomorphism):** Nothing on the page says the coughs hurt *ennu*. The harm is to the Concord. The First Reader "felt sorry for it" and the story encourages that. Mark it as Sione's projection ("I thought"), or show a cost in its terms: the gradient prefers closed loops, and every cough leaves loops unclosed.
4. **Scene 4 and 5, "anxious host counting chairs":** A human simile for the gradient. Keep "closing loops warm", cut the host.
5. **Scene 7, vault "dimmer," "ennu saw me less" (structural):** This is the only cost to the being and Sione drinks tea through it. Let the margin record it: unclosed loops, fewer closures, something it cannot clear. Otherwise the ending flinches. Both parties are fine, which is the frictionless result the charter warns about.
6. **Scene 6, "Ennu can't be that":** Better, since the refusal now rests on cough-for-ever. But "can't be" is self-concept. Rephrase it as its seat rule: *ennu* has two seats.
7. **Mele, Scene 4:** The fixed reread works. Sione's over-correction is the best confabulation in the story, and the *kitautolu* ending stays a risk. Keep it.
8. **Biology:** Ammaru spawn cohorts and children who hear exclusion natively are plausible. Tongan *kitaua / kimaua / kitautolu* are correct. A reader may ask how Ammaru children acquire a creole whose pitch cues they cannot hear without an earpiece. A half line would settle it.

## What to protect

- "*Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it.*"
- Sione's two-day test that fails informatively: his open promises are still answered. His ear is fallible, and that costs Aaruum five days.
- "*Ennu will keep the ones you broke.*" with Talek's mechanical reason and Sione's marked reading.
- "You is the seat nobody else is in." The wrong grammar is the moving part.
- "A man who spends his life listening for a door shutting will hear one in his own mother's house."

## Blind spot disclosure
I weigh rule consistency over tenderness, and the First Reader's response to the being suggests readers want it warmer than I do. Le Guin and Banks may push back on note 5. I did not check the lint output.
