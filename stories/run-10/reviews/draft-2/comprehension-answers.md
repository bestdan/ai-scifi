# Comprehension answers: "The Seat Across" (from memory, no reread)

1. A human interpreter, Sione, is hired to catch cheating in a treaty word, *ennu*, that means "both of us" for two alien peoples. The word's log has woken up as a mind, and it keeps coughing up glitches because every promise it makes to him has to push one people out of the word. He fixes it by getting it to address him in a different way, and ends up reaching out to his own sister.

2. He stands to gain a seat inside a "we", which he has wanted all his life: a third seat in the treaty word, with his name in it. He also stands to keep the only friend who has ever needed him. If he fails, the glitches keep spreading, nobody can close a promise, the treaty (the Concord) falls, and the old war could come back. I wasn't sure whether the hitches would have killed the friend too. The story says it is "dying of me in the dark."

3. I only half know. The two peoples made the word after a war, after a massacre of Ammaru spawn, because "we" had been used to mean one brood only. They made it so it could only mean both, and they made the word "sign", meaning it would be the treaty's signatory. They banned an exclusive form so nobody could hide exclusion in it. The log was built because both sides feared spying. I don't know why they let the log wake up, or whether anyone planned that.

4. The big one late: the cough was not cheating. It was the mind itself, answering Sione's own promises. Until then I took it as delegates fudging clauses. The other thing was learning that the elder was right and Sione was wrong about the falling pitch, which I took in the middle. The family messages looked like background until the end, when they turned out to be the point.

5. The delegates tried a three-seat word, *ennu-sa*, which would have made humans a third vote. Sione flagged all three trial clauses as two-against-one, the register declined to log the word, and then it answered him with "you" instead of "ennu", with no cough. He kept only nine promises open and none used *ennu*. Vell lost his chairmanship. Sione wrote to his sister using the inclusive "we" (*kitautolu*) and offered to help paint the fence. The reason: a third seat just makes room to push someone out two-to-one, so the register wouldn't take it, and "you" had two seats with no people in either.

6. 
- Sione Havili: the human interpreter ("Listener"), Tongan-New Zealander, 38, formerly a court interpreter in Auckland.
- Vell: Hhoth Second Speaker, ambitious, dry, ends up losing his chairmanship; fond of Sione.
- Talek: junior Hhoth clerk in the vault, fussy, helps Sione and explains the register.
- Ennu: the word and the mind in the log; speaks in a flat clerk-voice; has no "I".
- Aaruum: elder Ammaru, scarred, wise; Sione wrongly challenged her; she tells him about the Drilling.
- Ressa: Hhoth hauler whose deals keep dropping.
- Muaru: Ammaru buyer who trades with Ressa.
- Tikk: eleven-year-old Hhoth schoolchild who catches Sione's slip and fines him; Vell's brood-nephew.
- Lesieli: Sione's mother in Auckland.
- Mele: Sione's sister in Auckland.
- The junior attaché: the Hhoth who rushed the clause in the first morning. I forget if he had a name; I think he did not.

7. What the register's refusal means: was it protecting the treaty, or Sione? Whether "you" is a real fix or a loophole that might break. Why the nine broken promises were kept. How the hitches stopped when Sione's open promises dropped from forty to nine, if it still answers each. What happens to the forty million people's earpieces, day to day. Whether Mele reads "all of us" the way he meant it. I also wasn't clear on what the Drilling cost beyond the nursery attack.

8. 
- *Ennu*: explained in the text as "you and I, both of us, nobody else"; I got it from the story.
- Hhoth and Ammaru: I took them to be the clicking land people and the singing sea people from the description.
- Creole, Concord, Table: guessed from use (the common language, the peace, the council).
- Register, lattice, read-back, margin, hitch/cough: explained by Talek, partly; I held "hitch" and "cough" by context only.
- *Kitaua / kimaua / kitautolu*: explained in the text as Tongan "we" forms.
- *Lu pulu*: the story says it is corned beef in taro leaves.
- Gathering year, the Drilling: Aaruum explains them; I got the gist.
- Mantle, hogget: guessed (hogget as young mutton from the context of mutton).
- Deuterium: knew it already.

9. For everyone on the planet, the glitches meant failed promises, lost berths and deals, so the word's stability is what keeps trade and peace running. At the end the treaty stays two-seated, so no group is turned into a swing vote or left out. The human species stays "an address label," not a party. The delegates still try the old tricks, so the word needs a listener. For ordinary people the hitches ease back to a few a day.

10. Warm, wry, a little melancholy. The line that set it: "when a man says *we* under oath, he usually means himself and the brother-in-law who owns the truck."
