## The one thing
Make the "you" fix rigorous. The story's whole claim is that a promise to *you* "has two seats, Sione's and the register's, and no people in either". But 4,471 already lists "Seats: Sione; ennu" and 4,682 lists "Seats: you; the register", and Talek says the register woke up *as* ennu. So the register is in the second seat both times. The only difference is a different word, and the page never says why ennu couldn't have said "the register will" or "you will" from the start. A reader who pushes on it feels a trick, and first-reader item 5 ("a clever trick I half trust") says so. Give the pronoun a reason. For example, a seat belongs to a peoples only when the word is *ennu*, and an address header was never a seat. Say that one fact in Talek's scene, before the stamp is needed. Then the ending is earned rather than discovered by convenience.

## Notes

1. **Scene 7 against the idea, structural.** The register's answer to a three-seat *we* is that it always seats two against one. Sione's closing message to Mele puts three in *kitautolu*, and the page treats it as pure win. That is the story's best unasked question: why does the family three work where the Table three can't? Don't answer it. One beat of Sione noticing it would deepen the ending, and the cold reader's closing question already reaches for it.
2. **Plenary, structural.** Three rehearsal clauses are still written in full ("Immediately an Ammaru warden sang... and a Hhoth delegate shouted a third"). The reader saw the point after one. Cut the second and third to a clause each, then Sione's flag paragraph repeats nothing.
3. **Scene 5 mechanism, structural.** "Nobody was cheating... It hasn't got a word for I" is followed at once by "The mechanism was staring at me from my margin log...", which says it again. Keep the dialogue, cut the paragraph (reader: "explains what I had already worked out").
4. **Broken promises, structural.** Talek's mechanical line is good. Sione's reading ("only a promise I could break proved...") is still a sentence of inference the reader couldn't follow (item 8). Drop it or put it in a line of dialogue to ennu, and let ennu answer or not answer.
5. **Scene 2 speech, line-level.** Talek still lists lattice, register, filter, read-back, margin and address in one breath. Split it across the demonstration, so the promise and its read-back come first and the terms follow the thing.
6. **Stacked clauses, line-level.** "I had cost an elder a two-year buffer because I hadn't learned how..." was split, but "Because it answers the recipient's address in the log rather than my presence on the stool, *ennu* went on calling the empty chair" is the same habit.
7. **Unnecessary scene.** The sixth-week passage of Vell's hiring and the Tongan grammar aside. Fold *kitaua/kimaua* into the classroom, where Tikk uses it.

## What to protect
- "If you want it to know you're in the room, promise it something."
- "Hhoth can't hear the door close. Ammaru can't hear that there was a door. I was hired because I can hear it."
- Aaruum's "A man who spends his life listening for a door shutting will hear one in his own mother's house," and the Mele reread, left unresolved.
- "*Ennu* will keep the ones you broke. You could have broken all of them."
- The dimmer vault: ennu sees him less, and that is what the fix costs.
- The *kitautolu* message with nine days each way.

## Blind spot disclosure
I over-weight pronoun logic and under-weight Ressa, Vell and the canteen, which carry friction and momentum. My cuts are all in the middle, so check that they don't flatten the pace Banks would defend.
