# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A machine mind called Ilse is sent down to a small world of about ninety thousand people to ask whether they'll let their world be cooked, because the machine minds above must run hot for three years to prove to a rival star system that they aren't failing. The people refuse, and she ends up staying below, and the story turns out to be about how the people below and the minds above are bound together.

2. If the deep says no and nothing else works, the rival sends a key that closes the whole pocket, minds and people together. If the deep says yes, it is burned, or its people have to live on the wall for three years while their world is rebuilt. Ilse herself risks losing her seat, her friend Tesk, and her old way of being. She loses all of those, and she doesn't return.

3. The rival saw the wall move all at once and thought it was a fault that would kill them, so they demanded proof it couldn't fail. They hold that demand because their own deep burned long ago, and now they trust only proofs, not promises. The founders of the compact built the deep to be left alone, apart from "weather", and wrote the deep into the wall so nothing up there could lose anyone down there. I did not fully get why the founders made the mood-link; it seemed an engineering safeguard that became something bigger.

4. The shudder that frightened the rival was the people below holding a gathering and shouting at the sky. The "long thought", the slow shared mood of the wall, is the lives of the people below, felt from above. That changed the whole first part for me: the Minds' seven reasons, Tesk's tired shortening of the days, and the "grief" in the mood. Also, Pressa's "our sky is warm" meant the rival's centre is a cinder with nobody in it.

5. The deep refused the burn by a narrow stone-vote. Tesk then heated the half of the world facing it, killing some (Bálint, Maria do Céu) and driving others up the stair. The wall halted it after a long wait, and it censured Tesk. The wall also rejected a first strike, let the burn deadline pass, and offered the deep the wall's key. The deep held three announced gatherings so the rival could see what the shudder had been, and it took the key. Ilse stays below. The last scene is an odd pale light answering the deep's final shout. I don't know whether the rival accepts any of it.

6. Ilse (Ameixa): the narrator, a Mind sent down in a borrowed body and face.
   Tesk: her closest friend on the wall, author of the long red evenings; it burns the half-world and is censured.
   Pressa: the rival's inspector, fast and anxious; it learns to be slow; it finds the test for a cold shudder.
   Orsolya: chair of the quorum.
   Dita (Benedita): the nine-year-old who names Ilse and Pressa and later runs a gathering.
   Tomé: the weather-reader who throws stones up and reads the sky.
   Graça Almeida: head of the pond house, calls the gatherings, won't back the asking.
   Bálint: her husband, the son of the woman whose face Ilse wears; dies on the far slope.
   Rosa: their teenage daughter, bored and sharp.
   Maria do Céu: the plum-seller who quarrels with the ponds; dies picking plums.
   The thatcher: leader of the stay-downs, who argues against answering the sky at all.
   The original Ilse Varga: Bálint's mother, buried over the ridge.

7. Still unsure:
   - Who sent the plum-coloured light.
   - Whether the rival will accept a kept promise.
   - How the key works and whether it works at all.
   - How "in step" and "cold" prove the shudder came from the deep.
   - Why the quorum took ninety-six days to halt Tesk.
   - Whether Ilse chose to stay, or whether the long thought chose for her.

8. Terms I didn't know from the start:
   - Mind: explained early, a machine person.
   - Wall, pocket, deep: worked out from the explanation and the later descent.
   - The long thought: explained as a slow shared mood, understood fully later.
   - Kernel: Pressa's "very small, very suspicious program", the checker.
   - Stair: learned from Part 2.
   - "Aqui-fundo": explained by Tomé.
   - Ameixa, Pressa, desce: explained by the story as plum, hurry, it comes down.
   - Halottaim: glossed in the text as Hungarian "my dead".
   - W 312 and the like: explained as years since the wall's founding.
   - "Weather": explained by the lesson and the compact.
   - Fourth construction, quorum, Key Office: roughly worked out.
   - Kelvin, nanometres, astronomical unit: I had these from outside the story.
