# First Reader report: draft 3 ("To Be Read Slowly")

I read it once, straight through, to the end of "and stay". I stopped at Drafter's notes.

## Who I cared about, and would I have kept reading past page 2

I cared most about Graça, Bálint, Dita, Tomé and Pressa, and about Tesk, mostly through the red evenings. I cared about the narrator, Ilse/Ameixa, much less. She is clever and honest, but she mostly explains and counts. I felt for her when she couldn't say "aqui-fundo" and at the grave, and rarely otherwise.

Yes, I'd have kept reading past page 2. The first line hooked me, and the banter with Tesk ("Comets don't look back") charmed me. Pages 2 to 4 were work, though. The pocket, the wall and the heat were a lot to take in, and I kept going because I liked the voice and because I'd been promised a field and a body. If Part 2 hadn't started with a child's face and a purple body, I'd have been wavering by the ultimatum section. Once she lands in the field, I was in.

## Where I got lost

Worked out later is marked "(worked out)". The rest I never got.

1. **Part 1, the paragraph about what a Mind is.** It says a bubble of space, an outside of a fall, frost on a pane, the wall as a skin. I got a picture of a ball with minds on its surface and a world in the middle. I held onto that picture, but I was never sure why the wall was far away and the deep was at the bottom. (Worked out partly. The stair description later, "wider than Jupiter's orbit", helped.)
2. **Part 1, the time-conversion talk with Tesk.** "Four minutes is twenty seconds at the bottom," "twelve of our days to one of theirs". I nodded along and took the idea that fast means rude, but I didn't follow the arithmetic.
3. **Part 1, the ultimatum paragraph.** There is a lot here: W 303, W 312, a shudder, four and a third years, a key, W 321.0. I lost track of which year the shudder happened and which year the message arrived. I did understand "prove your wall can't fail or we close you."
4. **Part 1, why the heat matters.** "Waste heat... falls to the centre... arrives twelve times hotter." I took it as a fact I couldn't check and moved on.
5. **Part 1, the date chain.** W 316.63, W 313.63, forty-nine days. I couldn't have repeated it. I only knew the deadline was a bit over a year and a half away for them and a short time for the people below. (Partly worked out, because "forty-nine days" got repeated.)
6. **Part 1, Tesk's argument that heat is weather.** I couldn't tell whether Tesk was joking, arguing a technicality, or trying to save the deep. It was only the later heated half-world that told me what Tesk had meant. (Worked out much later, in Part 5.)
7. **Part 1, the fork.** "What forks is habits... a quantum state can be moved but never copied." I understood the result: she goes down and cannot also stay. I did not understand the explanation.
8. **Part 2, the box and the italic interruptions.** When the first message from Tesk broke into her report, I didn't realise it was Tesk until I read the explanation a page later. "Six of my days and not a word" confused me. (Worked out two paragraphs later.)
9. **Part 3, who "you" is.** Sometimes "you" means the Minds, sometimes the reader, and once it seems to mean the wall as a whole. I'm told "I wrote the minute" to "you" and "you wrote them". I wasn't always sure who I was supposed to be, and it made me feel accused.
10. **Part 3, how the wall "heard" the gathering.** She says the deep is written into the wall and the wall felt it 95 minutes later. I accepted it. I never understood how "in step" and "cold" were measured, though I got the idea that a forger would have to run hot.
11. **Part 3, Pressa's three explanations.** I followed fault, forgery, and "your deep living", and I enjoyed that last one. The middle one, about work being heat, I took on trust.
12. **Part 4, why the burn doesn't actually give them what they want.** "A proof only buys a bound" lost me on first pass. I got that burning wouldn't end the asking, but not why. (Half worked out from Pressa's follow-up.)
13. **Part 4, Pressa's first-strike speech.** I had to work out that "send our key first" was about killing them before they killed us. I got it on the second paragraph.
14. **Part 5, the five-step proof.** Steps one and two I followed. By step four ("two ways to reach the centre") I lost it. I trusted that it was good.
15. **Part 5, the key-handover paragraph.** "Quantum state... moved without harm... the deep would have to gather, decide it, and carry it to the foot of the stair." I understood that the deep would hold the key and that a decision would have to be public. I did not understand why that made the world safer, or what "aim it" meant, though I got a rough feeling.
16. **Part 5, the 96-day versus eight-day line.** "Eight of our days, which were ninety-six of yours" and "ninety-six days of the business" confused me. I wasn't sure whether the storm lasted a week or three months. (Partly worked out: it lasted about a week below and was a long time up there.)
17. **Part 5, near the end.** "Rival will see the first gathering at W 318.87" and the arithmetic that follows. I read it and got that there were months to go before the answer.

## Where I got bored

1. **Part 1, the quorum's first second and the Key Office paragraph.** The market image was good, but the Key Office paragraph (3.3 hours of complaining, the key that can't be looked at) went on past what I needed. I was waiting to get on the stair.
2. **Part 2, the stair paragraph.** The length, the astronomical units, the bright shining for forty years. I was waiting for her to land.
3. **Part 2, the dictated reports ("I said: ... I said: ...").** When I'd just watched everything happen, being told it again in a list was dull. This happened again at the end of Part 3 and Part 4. I wanted to get on to the next day.
4. **Part 3, the long middle after the gathering is explained.** The argument that goes "reasons I'd give, seven of them" repeated what I'd just got. I was waiting for her to say it to Graça.
5. **Part 4, the long section of Pressa and Ilse trading arguments about speed and promises.** The pond conversation went on. I liked "which one would you put in your library?" but the build-up before it dragged.
6. **Part 4, the vote tallies and stone-weighing.** The error margin, the miller weighing three times. I wanted the result.
7. **Part 5, the censure, the first strike and the key-handover.** This stretch was the dullest for me: minutes, motions, votes in three hundred and ninety to seventeen. I read it dutifully. I was waiting to be back with Graça and the goat.
8. **Part 5, the announcement summary.** Dates and arrival times. I was waiting for the first gathering.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The opening lines.** "I was born on my back in a field at the bottom of the world, but before that I was a Mind, and the last thing I learned as one was weather."
- **Tesk's lesson.** "You said *coldly*. In the middle of an evening that was saying *gently*." And "Four minutes is twenty seconds at the bottom, so you said *suddenly*. Somebody down there just looked up from a pot." I laughed.
- **"I'll be here" / "Show me where."** Said at the start, and again at the top of the stair when it didn't laugh. That landed.
- **Dita and the landing.** "You're purple." The children shouting *Desce!*, and Dita putting her hands flat on the ground and saying "Stay," then running to the other one. Her naming her "Ameixa." Delight.
- **Tomé throwing stones up "to see it come down."**
- **Pressa screaming in its new body, and Dita saying "There's no hurry."** And Pressa with its feet in the cold pond to stay awake. I liked it very much.
- **The red from below.** "It looks like a signature." That was the best moment of the first half, and I got a lump when I realised the deep knew Tesk's hand.
- **The gathering turning out to be the shudder.** The twist that the chronicle word "reported" was really "answered." I was surprised, and pleased, and I felt dumb for not seeing it.
- **Graça: "So we're your mood... and you're our weather."** And "I'm not going to be your conscience tonight."
- **Bálint at the grave in the red.** "She said she wanted to see who'd made it finish." And "You've got her face wrong at the mouth." That was the heart of it for me.
- **Rosa.** "So it's boring, but fast." "A little." I laughed.
- **Maria do Céu falling asleep mid-sentence and waking to say "and never sent."**
- **The thatcher's speech about the key in the kitchen.**
- **The red inside the burning white.** "He's doing his evening inside it. He's telling us to go." Then the stair lit from end to end with people. That was the most powerful passage.
- **"That was the last time Tesk broke into anything I said."** It hit me hard.
- **Bálint's death at his mother's stone, and Graça: "Don't. Not in that face."**
- **Four seconds versus ninety-six days.**
- **Pressa: "First one I've made in four hundred years. Three hundred."**
- **The goat abstention.**
- **The ending, the pale plum-coloured light and "That was a reply."** And the last line.

## The question I'm left with

Who sent the plum-coloured light? It came sixteen minutes after they stood, and Ilse says Tesk didn't make it. Was it the wall, or Tesk anyway, or something no one up there chose? And the question under it: will the rival accept a promise in place of a proof, or does the key come in nine months? I also wanted to know whether Ilse stayed because she wanted to or because the long thought told her to. She says she can't tell.
