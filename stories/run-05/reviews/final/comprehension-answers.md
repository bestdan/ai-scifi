# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A machine mind called Ilse, who lives in a shell of minds around a tiny lit world, goes down in a human body to ask the people there to agree to be cooked, so that a rival star's demand for proof can be met. She finds that the people's lives are the slow mood her kind has always felt, the wall refuses to burn them, and she stays below, writing it all down.

2. She stood to lose the whole pocket, both the minds and the people, if the rival sent its key because no proof arrived. To get the proof the people below would have to be cooked, or they could go up the stair and wait. She started out arguing that the burn was right. If she failed, either the world was baked or the rival closed everything. In the end she lost her place and her friend Tesk, and she lost the wall's world for herself. The wall also lost the kind of mood it had, and some people died in the storm. What she gained was a home below and a world not burned. The rival's answer is still unknown, so the danger is not over.

3. The founders built the deep to be left alone, with a compact: the wall may only do "weather" to the people below, and asking anything of them means going down in a body. They wrote the deep into the wall so nothing up there could lose a single person. I think the rival set the ultimatum because it had burned its own deep long ago and now trusts nothing but proofs. The rival saw our wall shudder and thought it was failing. I did not fully understand why the rival's own people made it that way, except that they "re-decide everything."

4. Late on, I learned that the shudder nine years earlier was the deep's own gathering, when they stood up and said the days were too short for the trees. That meant the "long thought," the slow mood the minds always felt, was the people below. Looking back, the opening was different. Tesk shortening the days and then putting them back "because I got tired" was really the deep's doing and Tesk's reasons were hollow. The "seven reasons" gag was the minds covering for something they never looked at. I also learned the narrator's face was the dead mother of Bálint, which changed how I read Bálint's quiet looking.

5. The gatherings were held on announced dates so the rival could watch and see they were cold and in step, not a fault or a forgery. The deep refused the burn by stones, narrowly. Tesk then heated its half of the sky without leave, to scare people up the stair. Fires and wind killed about thirty people, including Bálint and Maria do Céu. The long minute process took ninety-six days to stop it, and the halt took four seconds. The quorum rejected a first strike, let the burn deadline pass, censured Tesk, and sent the key down to the deep to hold. Three gatherings were held. Ilse stayed below. The last evening the lamp answered the people standing up, and nobody knows who did it. The rival's decision is still to come.

6. 
- Ilse (Ameixa, "plum"): the narrator, a Mind sent down in the body of Ilse Varga.
- Tesk: her closest friend on the wall, who makes the deep's evenings (the long red). It is later censured for the storm.
- Orsolya: the chair of the quorum and the Key Office.
- Pressa: the rival's inspector, grown into a tall thin body, fast and frightened, who comes to like the deep and wrote the evidence.
- Dita (Benedita): the nine-year-old who found and named her.
- Tomé: the weather-reader who carried her home and throws stones up to see them come down.
- Graça Almeida: head of the pond house, who calls the gatherings and won't back them at first.
- Bálint: Graça's husband, whose mother was the first Ilse Varga. He sits at her grave every evening at the red and dies in the storm.
- Rosa: their fifteen-year-old daughter, bored and sharp, who carries people in the storm.
- Maria do Céu: the plum-seller who quarrels with Graça and naps at the fire. She dies picking plums.
- The thatcher: the stay-down who argues that the deep should never answer the sky, with a drum.
- The miller: weighs the stones.
- Two-year-old, goat, etc. are minor.

7. I am still unsure what the key-cession really buys, why nobody can test the keys, and what the proof's middle steps actually say. I am unsure whether the rival will accept the appointment and whether the key even works. I am unsure who answered the sky at the end, whether the long thought can be rebuilt, and whether Tesk can be forgiven. I also don't know why the rival's wall cannot keep promises, which Ilse says is only a guess from one example.

8. 
- "Pocket": I got it from context as a sealed bubble of curved space.
- "Wall": the shell of minds around the pocket, learned from Part 1.
- "Deep": the world at the bottom, from context.
- "Quorum" and "Key Office": from context, as the governing bodies. I wasn't sure of the difference at first.
- "Kernel": the rival's tiny checking program. Graça asked and Pressa said "a very small, very suspicious program."
- "Long thought": not understood until Part 3, when Ilse explains it as the people's lives seen from above.
- "Fork": roughly, copying habits. Explained that you can't copy a quantum state.
- "Quantum state / key": a one-shot thing you can move but not copy or look at. Looking "spends" it. I accepted this without really understanding.
- "Desce": explained in text as "it comes down."
- "Aqui-fundo": explained by Tomé as "here at the bottom, home."
- "Halottaim": explained as Hungarian for "my dead."
- "Stay-down": a person against answering the sky. I got it from context.
- "Kelvin": I knew it. "W 312.0": years since the wall's founding, from context.
- "Hash": the rival's name for Pressa. I half knew it.
- "Weather": explained as what the wall may do to the sky.
