# First Reader report: "To Be Read Slowly" (final.md)

One straight read, start to finish. I stopped nowhere to look anything up.

## Where I got lost

I list every place, with whether I worked it out and how.

1. **The pocket and the wall (Part 1, "We were both doing other things..." through "We didn't visit. We gave them light").** I couldn't picture it. A bubble of curved space, everything falling inward, a world at the bottom, "frost on a pane". I took it as "a tiny world lit from every side by a shell of minds around it". I worked that out later, from Part 2, when the sky came from every side and shadows fell only straight down. Part 1 alone didn't give it to me.
2. **The twelve-times heat (the burn paragraph, "twenty-four kelvin on the wall... two hundred and eighty-nine at the bottom").** I never understood why heat gets multiplied on the way down. I took it on trust that the burn cooks the world, and I never worked out the mechanism.
3. **"Nothing would be lost... the way a burned letter is still in its smoke."** I didn't know what it meant or why it mattered. I never worked it out. It seemed to be a bad argument the narrator was ashamed of, which I got from the "I'm fairly sure it was me."
4. **"The long thought" (first in Orsolya's question, "our slow shared mood").** I didn't know what it was. I nodded along until Part 3, when Ilse says it is the deep's lives seen from above. I worked it out there, and the first mention then made sense.
5. **The dates (paragraph beginning "Then Orsolya asked for the dates", and the many later ones).** W 316.63, W 313.63, 4.37 years, forty-nine days, "twelve of ours to one of theirs." I lost the thread. I kept only that there was a deadline of about forty-nine days down below, and that I should keep watching a clock.
6. **Who "you" is.** The narrator says "people who have never been anything else" and then "four hundred of you." For a while I thought she was writing to the people in the deep. I worked out she is writing to the Minds on the wall when she said "you wrote them and may not remember" in Part 5.
7. **The italic interruptions from Tesk.** The explanation (a reply arrives before you finish) was clear. The sixteen-minute delay was not, until the box scene in Part 2. After that I had it.
8. **The first-strike business and the key (Part 4, Pressa's "The first strike. You hold ours.").** I followed the idea, which is that they could kill the other side first. I did not follow why nobody could know whether the keys work, or why looking at one spends it, past the bare statement.
9. **Pressa on why a proof can't end the asking (Part 4, "Nobody can prove that... an old result").** I lost it partway through. I got "even if you burn, they can ask again."
10. **The five-step proof (Part 5, "First... Second... Third... Fourth... Fifth").** I was lost from step two on. I could not tell the lamp brightness from the wall "keeping in step". I took the conclusion on faith: a gathering shows up cold and everywhere at once, and a fake would show up hot.
11. **The key being sent down to the deep (Part 5, "The key is a quantum state... Here is what that buys").** This is where I was most lost. I couldn't tell why giving the key to the deep makes anything safer. I got only that "it can come only from a gathering." The line "a closed pocket can't answer anyone, so neither key could ever strike back" lost me completely. I did not work it out.
12. **The ninety-six days versus eight days.** I worked it out: eight days at the bottom is ninety-six on the wall. I still had to stop and do the sum. The "four seconds" line then landed.
13. **Wall years versus deep days in the ending ("A wall year is about thirty of our days down here...").** I half followed it. I understood only "the end could come in about nine months."

## Where I got bored

- **Part 1, the paragraphs of numbers and rules around the ultimatum and the Key Office** (from "The program attached was their kernel" through Orsolya's dates). I was waiting for someone to do something. The market comparison and the quorum arguing helped. The numbers did not.
- **Part 3, Ilse's sum matching the dates ("This is the deep's twenty-sixth year... Multiply by 12.04...").** I was waiting for her to just say it was them.
- **Part 4, Pressa and Ilse by the pond arguing about whether faster science was worth the lost deep (from "And since we lost it, our science has been faster" to the four-hundred-line proof).** The people had been alive and now they were debating. I wanted to be back at the house.
- **Part 5, the proof and the key-cession explanation (the long paragraphs after "I wrote the proof that night" and after "The first strike was rejected").** I skimmed. I was waiting to get back to the fires.
- **The arithmetic near the end ("The rival will see the first gathering at W 318.87...").** I skimmed that too.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **Opening lines:** "I was born on my back in a field at the bottom of the world, but before that I was a Mind, and the last thing I learned as one was weather." I wanted to know all of it.
- **Tesk and the sky as a language:** "You said *coldly*... In the middle of an evening that was saying *gently*." I loved that an evening had a grammar. "Somebody down there just looked up from a pot and wondered what they'd done."
- **"I'll be here" / "Show me where."** It became a joke, then a goodbye, then the last line of the story.
- **Dita's first scene.** Nine years old, deciding, naming the narrator Ameixa, "You're purple," putting the hands flat on the ground like setting down a pot. This is where I actually fell in.
- **Tomé throwing the stone up "to see it come down."** And *aqui-fundo*, with the palm on the ground. I loved that the word had no equivalent upstairs.
- **The long red seen from below, "like someone choosing not to leave yet,"** then recognised as Tesk's signature by a stranger who had never heard its name.
- **The chronicle's word "reported."** When Graça says they had answered the sky and the word was bent, and Ilse says she left her wrong gloss where it was. That surprised me.
- **Bálint at the grave.** "You've got her face wrong at the mouth. That's all." This is the line I'd quote.
- **Graça: "Don't. Not in that face."**
- **Maria do Céu falling asleep mid-sentence at the fire and waking to finish it: "and never sent."**
- **The vote by stones.** Children vote, the margin is weighed on a grain scale, and Graça looks at the left cart and then at the narrator and drops her stone in the right one. "I think the sky had asked, and I was standing there."
- **The white sky with the red evening inside it.** "He's telling us to go." I did not expect the friend to be the one who hurt them, kindly. The stair lit end to end with people made me stop reading for a moment.
- **The list of the dead,** especially the children's names with ages. It is cold and it hit hard.
- **Four seconds after ninety-six days.**
- **The goat's abstention.** I laughed and was glad.
- **Pressa: "First one I've made in four hundred years... Three hundred."**
- **The last evening:** the sky answers back after sixteen minutes, and nobody knows who did it.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared most about **Graça, Dita and Tomé**, then **Bálint**, then **Maria do Céu**, then **Pressa**. I cared about **Rosa**, the teenager who is bored by everything and then carries a boy of six down the hill. I cared about Tesk early on and then in a more complicated way. I was angry at it, and I also understood it. I cared about **Ilse** less at the start, because she is cold and tells me she argued for the burn loudest. I came round to her slowly, around the pond-wall scene and Bálint's slope. By the ending I was with her.

Would I have kept reading past page 2? **Yes, but narrowly.** The first line and Tesk's weather lesson hooked me. Then the quorum, the numbers and the Key Office made me tired, and if the first section had gone on another few pages in that register I might have stopped. What carried me through was the narrator's voice, which kept saying things like "I was sure of it" and "I believed it while I said it" in a way I could tell was going to come back. Dita in Part 2 is where I stopped reading to be informed and started reading because I wanted to be there.

## The question I'm left with

**Who answered the sky at the end?** Tesk is barred. The text says nobody on the wall may have meant it. I wanted to know whether the long thought answered by itself, which I think is the idea, or whether a person did it.

Underneath that: will the other star's wall accept any of this? Nobody knows, and the story stops before it finds out. I'm also left wondering whether Tesk will ever write to her again.
