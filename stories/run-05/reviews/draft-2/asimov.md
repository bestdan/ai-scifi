## The one thing

Open in the field (Part 2's birth scene), and tell the wall afterwards. The fair-play planting survives if five plants move whole. The calendar survives if "forty-nine days" is re-derived on the cart. Structural.

## Structural question: open in the field, flash back to the wall

It breaks nothing the resolution needs, and the frame already allows it. Ilse is writing from the deep ("When I began this I was on the wall"), so a retrospective wall section is natural, and the birth scene is already narrated from the bottom.

**What has to move, with its deadline:**

1. **The ultimatum text.** Its two load-bearing clauses are "proof... cannot release it from any reachable state within one thousand years" and the dropped "You hold ours". Pressa quotes the rule in 4.5, so the text can sit anywhere before Part 3's rules check. Don't use it as the cold open, because the reader found it unreadable. Put it after the "plum" scene, as the thing she carried down.
2. **The key as a quantum state: looking spends it, so nobody has tested either key** (1.1, the Key Office paragraph). 5.3 rejects the first strike on exactly this. It can land in a flashback any time before the Part 4 fire.
3. **The seven reasons** (1.1). Ilse catching herself giving seven in 3.4 is the fairest plant in the story, so the flashback must come before 3.4. Better, put it before Tesk's first box in Part 2.
4. **The deadline chain** (W 321.0, then W 316.63, then W 313.63, then forty-nine days, then thirty-three waking). The cart paragraph at the end of Part 2 spends these numbers: "Forty-nine days was the window". If the field opens the book, that paragraph must carry the three-subtraction derivation, in a scene, with Ilse counting on her back. That is better than the 1.2 paragraph, which the reader skimmed.
5. **The heat arithmetic** (24 K to 289 K to 1200 K) and the **12:1 time ratio**. The first is needed when she faces the household. The second is needed before the first italic Tesk interruption, or the sixteen-minute lag reads as a glitch. Part 2 can carry the ratio in the cart or Tomé's day.
6. **The compact** ("goes down and asks it there, in a body") and the Item 9 minute. The minute heads Part 2, so it must go. Move it into the flashback as a read-aloud, or drop it, since the compact plant is what matters.
7. **Tesk's weather lesson** (the long red, "coldly", "There won't be a you up here"). Part 2 line 253 says "I had watched Tesk make this sky from above", and "the red one" pays off from it. The lesson becomes the flashback's centre. The reader called it the first enjoyable moment in Part 1, so it can carry the flashback.

**Calendar logic:** the dates are timestamped "W 312.0–312.2, deep days 0–5". Deep day 0 is the birth. The ultimatum is at W 312.0, so the flashback sits wholly before day 0. That is clean, because nothing in the wall section happens after the descent. The 95-minute latency and Tesk's lag are used only after landing, so they don't constrain the order.

**Cost:** a flashback delays the stakes. The reader meets "ask ninety thousand people to leave their world" only in the cart paragraph, so it needs one earlier line from Ilse, such as "I had come to ask them to leave", to carry the field scenes. The reader wants the child first, so spend the compression there.

## What to protect

- The seven reasons, and Ilse catching herself giving them.
- The sixteen-minute interruptions as the clock rule, taught by layout.
- Step 4 of the five-step proof: forging the shudder costs the burn.
- "Four seconds. You waited ninety-six days for something you can do in four seconds."

## Other notes

1. **5-step proof (line-level):** the reader lost steps 2 to 4. Say the conclusion first ("cold and everywhere at once means the deep").
2. **Cession (structural, mild):** the reader believed it without feeling it. Put the attribution-not-warning line next to the vote.

## Blind spot disclosure

I over-weight the rules and the order of facts. I can't judge whether a flashback loses the quiet dread of Part 1's slow start, and Le Guin and Chiang should overrule me on that. I'm also blind to the body in the birth scene and to how Part 2 would read after the wall.
