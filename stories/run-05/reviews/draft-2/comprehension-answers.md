# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A disembodied Mind named Ilse goes down into a slowed-time world at the bottom of her people's bubble to ask the ninety thousand people living there whether the wall may cook them to satisfy a rival star's demand. The people refuse, a Mind on the wall burns half their world anyway to push them, and in the end Ilse stays below while the deep and the wall reach an uneasy new arrangement.

2. Ilse's job is to get the deep's consent to be evacuated up the stair while the wall runs hot for a proof. If she fails, the wall can't run the proof, the rival sends the key by W 321.0, and everything inside the pocket ends, wall and deep both. Personally she loses her place on the quorum, her friend Tesk, and her life on the wall. She also loses people she came to love (Bálint, Maria).

3. The rival feared that the wall's sudden shudder meant the wall was failing and would destroy them, so they demanded a proof it never could. The founders built the deep at the bottom so its people could be left alone, and wrote it into the wall redundantly so no failure could lose anyone. The unplanned result was that the deep's lives feed the wall's slow shared mood, the long thought. I am less sure why the rival chose a proof that costs the burn, beyond Pressa's saying that a proof is the only promise they can still accept after they lost their own deep.

4. The shudder that frightened the rival was the deep's own gathering, when the people stood up and shouted at the sky about their plums. The long thought is the deep's lives seen from above. Earlier details then turned over. The seven reasons were wrong. Tesk's "I got tired of it" was really the deep's answer. The grief in the long thought may have been Bálint's. I also learned that the rival's own deep was burned, so they have a cinder at the centre.

5. The deep refused the burn by a narrow stone vote. Tesk then heated its sector without leave and burned the far half in what it meant as a push to get people up the stair, and Bálint and Maria were among the dead. The quorum halted it, censured Tesk, and rejected a first strike. The quorum then offered the key and its word to the deep, and the deep took the key at the first announced gathering. It also let the burn window pass. Three gatherings on announced dates were held so the rival could see them, cold and whole-wall, as proof of what the shudder was. At the end the lamp gave a plum-coloured light sixteen minutes after the deep stood up, Tomé called it a reply, and Ilse chose to stay below. I don't know whether the rival accepts any of it. The earliest the answer can come is W 325.37.

6. Characters:
- Ilse (called Ameixa, "plum"): the Mind who narrates. She went down in the body of an engineer, Ilse Varga.
- Tesk: her oldest friend, the Mind who makes most of the deep's weather and the long red evenings. It heated the far half.
- Orsolya: presides over the quorum and the Key Office.
- Pressa: the rival's inspector, hurried and exact, grown in a tall thin body. It finds the cold-shudder test and later makes its first promise.
- Dita (Benedita): a nine-year-old who was first to the landing, named Ilse, and later ran the ponds and called the children's gathering.
- Tomé: a weather-reader of 22 who throws stones up to watch them come down, and reads the sky.
- Graça Almeida: head of the pond house, contrary, calls the gatherings.
- Bálint: Graça's husband, son of the real Ilse Varga. He sat at her grave each evening and died there in the storm.
- Rosa: Graça and Bálint's bored fifteen-year-old daughter. She says the word for the dead over him.
- Maria do Céu: the plum-seller who shouted "Who asked for that?". She died picking plums in the storm.
- The thatcher: leader of the stay-downs, with a drum, who argues no one should answer the sky.
- The real Ilse Varga: Bálint's mother, dead eleven years.
- The miller: weighs the stones.
- I don't remember anything else about the others, such as the boy of four or the bee-keeper.

7. What I'm still unsure about:
- Whether the final light was a real reply and who made it.
- Whether the rival will accept the appointment.
- How the key cession actually makes a first strike harder, beyond the claim that it would take a visible gathering.
- Whether Ilse's staying was her own choice.
- The steps of the forgery proof.
- Why exactly the rival's lost deep leads to demanding a burn from others.
- Whether either key works at all.
- Whether the sky's anger or kindness was in Tesk's mind, since it said "a mood can be rebuilt" and then burned them.

8. Terms I didn't know, and where I got them:
- Mind, wall, pocket, deep: explained in the paragraphs right after the opening letter.
- Quorum and Key Office: learned from the Part 1 scenes.
- Kernel: a small checking program. Learned from the ultimatum and Pressa's "a very small, very suspicious program."
- W 312.0: wall years, from context only. I never fully grasped the two-clock system.
- Long thought: learned from the examples and, in Part 3, from Ilse's statement.
- Weather (verb, adverb, noun, warmth): Tesk's lesson.
- Aqui-fundo: Tomé's explanation, "here at the bottom, home."
- Desce: "it comes down", explained at the landing.
- Halottaim: "my dead", explained by the text.
- Stay-downs: from context, people who don't want to be asked anything.
- Cold shudder / correlations / agreement: partly understood. "Agreement" I did not really get.
