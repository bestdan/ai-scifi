# First Reader report: draft 2, "To Be Read Slowly"

I read the whole story once, to "and stay". I stopped at the Drafter's notes.

## Where I got lost

1. **The first page, the ultimatum (the quoted block).** "W 312.0", "deep day 0", "a pocket", "the kernel attached", "your pocket will close cleanly". I understood almost none of it. I took it for a threatening letter between two groups, and that was enough to go on. The paragraphs after it explained what a Mind, the wall, the pocket and the deep are. I worked it out by reading on. Until then I only knew somebody was threatening somebody.

2. **The paragraph that begins "Here is what they were afraid of".** The sentence about the wall failing "unmaking ordinary space" and "Each pocket holds a key to the other" is a lot to take in at once. I got the gist: a threat, and a way to carry it out. I wasn't sure what the "key" physically was until much later.

3. **The heat arithmetic ("Twenty-four kelvin... 289... twelve hundred").** I lost the numbers. I took away that the deep would cook, and that was all I needed. I did not check the sums.

4. **The deadline sums (end of Part 1).** "W 316.63", "W 313.63", "1.63 of our years", "forty-nine days". I let the figures go by and kept only "forty-nine days". That worked, but the dates came back later and I was never sure which was which. The "thirty-three waking days" and the later countdowns ("thirteen days left... nine of them waking") felt like a ticking clock I trusted without following.

5. **"The long thought."** I was lost for a good stretch. The examples (a fashion in music, two lineages forgetting a quarrel) helped. I still didn't know whether it was a thing or a metaphor. I only really got it in Part 3, when Ilse says it is the deep's lives seen from above. That explanation worked for me. It was also the moment I understood what the story was about.

6. **The italic interruptions from Tesk.** The line saying they are "where they broke into mine" helped. The first one, in the middle of her acceptance, still made me stop and wonder whether I'd missed something. Later, when the box delays are explained (sixteen minutes), I understood why the reply lands in the middle of a sentence. I worked it out from that explanation.

7. **Part 2, the "patch" scene.** Ilse thinks the patch means "listen to me". Tomé reads it as one half of the sky being robbed to light the other. I followed his reading. I wasn't sure for a while whether Tomé was right or just being local. The orchard and pond feud was new to me at that point, and I took it on trust.

8. **Part 3, the gathering and the date match.** The multiplication to show the shudder fell on the day the deep stood up (25.17 times 12.04) went past me as numbers. I got it from Graça's "So it heard."

9. **Pressa's three possible causes (fault, forgery, "your deep, living").** Fault and forgery I understood. The "cold" test, no heat means it can't have been written by the wall, took me two reads of the dialogue in my head, but I got it when Pressa says "A cold shudder can't be forged."

10. **The Part 5 proof in five steps.** I did not follow steps 2 to 4 (brightness versus "agreement", writing at the centre). I accepted the last line: wall changes together and stays cold, so it came from the deep. I couldn't have explained why steps 1 to 5 get there.

11. **The "cession" of the key.** I lost the logic. I understood that the Minds send the key down to the people, that nobody may look at it, and that this somehow makes a first strike harder. I believed the conclusion because Ilse argued it. I did not feel why it was safer.

12. **The final light.** Tomé says it was a reply. Ilse says Tesk didn't make it and she doesn't know who did. I wasn't sure what I was meant to feel, whether it was hope or an accident. I liked it anyway.

## Where I got bored

- **The first section of Part 1, after the opening definitions.** The quorum scene, the Key Office, the 3.3 hours of complaining, the rule about not looking at the key. I was waiting for a person to show up. Tesk's weather lesson was the first place I enjoyed myself, "You said *coldly*". Before that, I was reading definitions.

- **The Part 1 account of the rival's kernel and why they are afraid**, and the section on "the deadline", where she sets out the dates again. I wanted to be down in the deep.

- **Part 4's long explanation of Pressa's century of faster proofs** ("a proof its people had finished forty years before... four hundred lines"). I understood the point: speed costs them something and they gain something. I was waiting for Pressa to be a person again.

- **Part 5, the minutes about the long thought "unsettled" for eight days**, and the first-strike vote, with its numbers (390 to 17), the Key Office again and the complaining. I was impatient to get back to Graça and the burying. I did like "Four seconds." The cession passage after it was heavy going.

- **Part 5, the third gathering** (the second half of the section). It's about the tax of being watched, which I got, but I'd already been told it in the section before.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **"You're purple," she said.** (The first sight of the child in Part 2.) It made me laugh. This is where the story began for me.

- **Dita setting Ilse's hands on the ground: "Stay," and running to the other one.** Funny and touching at once.

- **"Ameixa... It means plum."** The naming, and "That's from up there. She was right, and I have been Ameixa in the deep ever since."

- **Tomé throwing the stone up "to see it come down."** I loved this, and the way he never looks bored by it.

- **The long red "looks like a signature."** The weather-reader knowing a particular maker's evenings, "the red one", and the line that Tesk's goodnight is the thing the deep named it for. This was the point where the Minds stopped being abstract.

- **Pressa with its feet in the pond, refusing to sleep: "You stop... Every night the body keeps going and the thread stops."** Strange and sad.

- **Graça seeing Ilse's face: "Ilse Varga is under a stone over the ridge. I helped dig it." Then "She was his mother."** This was the best moment so far. I felt the horror of it before I had finished reading the sentence.

- **The plum put on the bread, the sour face, the table laughing, and Bálint: "My mother never could eat them either."**

- **Graça: "So we're your mood... and you're our weather." Then "I'm not going to be your conscience tonight. Find another one."** Hard and right.

- **Bálint at his mother's grave in the red evening, and "You've got her face wrong at the mouth. That's all."** I was moved. I did not expect the red evening to turn into this. Everything the story said about the red light came together here.

- **Maria do Céu falling asleep in the middle of a sentence and waking to say "and never sent."** The best comic moment.

- **The vote with stones, and the thatcher's speech, "We're not their conscience. They can grow their own."** I liked that the children vote and that goats come up later.

- **Graça dropping her stone in the right-hand cart after looking at Ilse.** "I think the sky had asked, and I was standing there."

- **The storm: the sky over the far half goes white and Tesk puts his long red inside it.** "He's telling us to go." The stair lit end to end with people was the biggest image in the story. I understood that the kindness and the harm were the same act, and that was a great deal to feel at once.

- **Bálint going out to the grave anyway: "It's her evening." / "It's *his* evening."** Then he doesn't come back. I was upset.

- **Maria "had filled one basket."** And the basket buried with her.

- **"Don't. Not in that face."** Graça at Bálint's grave. I put the book down for a moment after that.

- **"Four seconds. You waited ninety-six days for something you can do in four seconds."** A cold line, and it worked.

- **Rosa saying the word over her father.** I had forgotten the word, and its return hit me.

- **The goat vote.** Delightful, and also a relief after the storm.

- **"Not now. They're reading."** I liked how it showed the cost of the plan without a speech.

- **The ending: the light the colour of the inside of a plum, and "We'll need a word for that."** Then the pencil and "stay". It moved me. I liked that it was not explained.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

**I cared about Graça most.** After her, Dita, then Tomé, then Bálint and Maria do Céu (both gone by the end, and I minded), then Rosa and Pressa. Ilse I cared about a little, and mostly by way of the others, as the person who gets named and disapproved of. I wanted her to be forgiven and she wasn't. I didn't much care about Tesk until the storm, and then I cared a lot. Orsolya and the Key Office I didn't care about at all.

**Page 2: I would not have kept reading on my own account. I would have kept reading because I was asked.** The first page is a threatening letter I couldn't follow. Pages 2 to 4 are definitions, then numbers and a committee. I would have put it down. The first moment that held me was Tesk teaching Ilse weather ("You said *coldly*"), in Part 1, a long way in. The first moment that made me keep going without effort was "You're purple" in Part 2. If the story had begun there, I'd have read on gladly. As it stands, Part 1 is something I got through to reach the story.

After Part 2 began, I would have read to the end.

## The question I'm left with

Was the sky's last light an answer? Someone on the wall made it, and Ilse says Tesk didn't. If the Minds can now answer the deep with a gift that only the deep can read, what do the people down there owe in return? I was also left wondering whether the rival will believe the goat, and whether the answer from Alpha Centauri will come. Ilse tells us the earliest it can come, and the story stops before it does.

A smaller thing. Was Ilse right to stay, or did she simply find a home? She says she can't tell whether staying was her choice or the long thought's. I wasn't sure I could tell either.
