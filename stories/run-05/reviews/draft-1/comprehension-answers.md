# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A mind that lives in the wall around a sealed pocket of space goes down, in a human body, to ask the people who live at the bottom to leave their world so the wall can run hot for three years. This is meant to satisfy a rival pocket that threatens to destroy the narrator's pocket. The people at the bottom refuse, and the story is about what that refusal costs, and about how the wall's slow shared mood turns out to be those people.

2. Ilse's job is to get the deep's consent to the burn and to bring the people up the stair. If the burn doesn't happen, the rival sends its key at W 321.0 and everything in the pocket ends, wall and deep together. If it does happen, the deep is cooked. What she loses in practice is her place on the wall, her friend Tesk, her library and her seat. What she gains is a place among the deep, and a new name.

3. The founders built the deep to be left alone and wrote a compact that the wall leaves it alone apart from weather. They also wrote the deep into the wall redundantly so that nothing could lose a person below. They did it that way so nothing up there could harm the people below, and so the people could be safe and private. They did not foresee that this would make the wall's mood the deep's life. The rival pocket also holds a key to close ours, and we hold theirs, as a kind of mutual safeguard against a wall that fails and spreads. I don't fully know why the founders chose keys that nobody can look at.

4. Three things. (a) The shudder of W 303, the whole wall moving at once, was the deep's gathering, when Graça called everyone to complain that the days were too short for the trees. I'd taken it as a mystery of the wall. (b) The long thought, the shared slow mood that all the Minds feel, is the deep's lives seen from above. That changed my reading of the early talk about moods, and of Tesk's "I got tired of it". (c) The rival's own deep was cooked a century ago, so their "our sky is warm" line was grim, not a remark about climate.

5. The deep voted no with stones, by a narrow margin. Tesk, trying to frighten people up the stair, ran its sector hot against the half of the world facing it. A storm followed, and thirty-one people died, including Bálint and Maria do Céu. The Key Office halted it after the Minds waited ninety-six of their days. The quorum censured Tesk, let the window for the burn pass, and ceded the key: from then on the pocket's word is given only by a gathering of the deep. The deep held three gatherings on announced dates, so the rival could see that a cold whole-wall shudder is a gathering and not a fault. Ilse stays below. A strange plum-coloured light follows the last gathering, and she can't say if it was a reply. We don't know yet whether the rival will send the key.

6. Named characters:
- Ilse (Ameixa): the narrator, a Mind of the wall in a body; "plum" to the deep; she argued for the burn.
- Tesk: her oldest friend, who makes the deep's weather, famous for its long red evenings; later ran its sector hot and was censured.
- Orsolya: the Mind who presides over the quorum and sends Ilse down.
- Pressa: the rival's inspector, a fast mind in a tall thin body, named by Dita; it finds the cold-shudder test.
- Dita (Benedita): a nine-year-old who names everybody and runs the children and the ponds in the storm.
- Tomé: a weather-reader who throws stones to see them come down, and reads the patch.
- Graça: head of the pond house, calls the gatherings but won't back the question.
- Bálint: her husband, son of the engineer Ilse Varga whose face and name Ilse wears; he goes over the ridge to sit at his mother's grave at the red; he dies in the storm.
- Rosa: Graça and Bálint's fifteen-year-old daughter, bored and sharp; says the Hungarian word at the burial.
- Maria do Céu: plum-seller with trees on the far slope; dies in the storm with one basket filled.
- The thatcher: a stay-down with a drum who argues that the deep should answer no once.
- The miller and the bee-keeper: minor.

I'm unsure about: whether the rival will accept a kept word in place of a proof (the story says it doesn't meet their rule); whether the keys work at all; how the cold-versus-hot test is supposed to be airtight; whether the final light was a reply and who made it; why a gathering is "cold" while Tesk's storm is "hot"; and how many Minds actually knew about the deep being the long thought and when.

8. Terms:
- "The wall" / "the pocket": worked out from context in Part 1; the wall is a quantum skin where Minds live, the pocket is the whole sealed volume.
- "The deep": from the dissenters-at-the-bottom paragraph.
- "Mind": from context, a patterned person on the wall.
- "W" dates: I took W as a count of years; I guessed the time-dilation factor from the 12.04 line.
- "Kernel": I got "small checker" from Part 1, and Graça's "A very small, very suspicious program. Like you."
- "Long thought": unclear until Part 3's reveal.
- "Quorum" and "Key Office": from context, as governing bodies.
- "The stair": from context, a column of light carrying matter and people.
- "Desce": explained as "it comes down".
- "Aqui-fundo": explained by Tomé, "where you are when you're where you live".
- "Halottaim": explained as Hungarian, "my dead".
- "Stay-downs": I inferred them as people who oppose answering the sky.
- "The strike": I guessed it meant the proposal to strike first with the key.
- "Ceded": inferred as handing over the right to send the key.
- "Compact": explained as the founders' short rule.
