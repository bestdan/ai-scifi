## The one thing

Close the gap between what the rival demanded and what the appointment gives. The letter asks for a kernel-checked proof about the wall's program. The ending (Ilse in 4.5: "It doesn't touch their rule. It asks them to change it.") offers something else. A reader who is paying attention can't tell why this rival, which "can't take anyone's word for anything", would accept it. Either find the edge case in the rival's own rule, so the appointment satisfies the letter's wording, or make it a stated gamble and say why Pressa's testimony makes it a fair one. Structural.

## Notes

1. **4.5 and 5.3, the resolution (structural):** The posed puzzle is "prove the wall can't release within a thousand years". Pressa shows it is unprovable except by the burn, and the story never solves it. It solves a different problem, "what was the shudder", and hopes. The first reader said it plainly: "I'm not sure why this is supposed to satisfy a rival that wanted a proof." Fair play wants the fact the resolution needs, which is why the rival's rule would bend, planted before the end. Pressa's "We re-decide everything" is the nearest plant, and it cuts both ways.
2. **The key-first option (structural):** The ultimatum itself ends "You hold ours" (Part 1 opener). Yet in 4.5 Pressa says "The one none of you has said", and Ilse adds that 400 Minds had "been not saying it for thirty-five days". These can't both stand. Four hundred and eleven Minds took 900 positions in one second, and key-first would be position one. This is a place where the plot needs clever people to be stupid. Better: they said it on day zero and rejected it for reasons the reader is given early.
3. **5.3, rejecting the faction (structural):** The reason given is "a rival who would see it coming". A key travels at light speed, and the rival can't see it before it arrives. The honest reasons are that the key is untested and that it kills 4.37 light-years of neighbours. State those. As written, a sharp reader will answer the motion before the characters do, and be right.
4. **The cession (structural):** The rival "would watch the gathering that decided it build for months". But watching it build is 4.37 years delayed, so the rival could only respond to the start of a gathering by firing its own key first. The cession may make pre-emption more tempting. Either answer this or have Ilse notice it.
5. **Geometry (line-level):** The stair is "two astronomical units" (1.2, 2.2). Half the circumference is 3.3 light-hours and the wall's crossing time is "two hours", which give a radius of about one light-hour, or 7 AU. A signal that takes "ninety-five minutes" to reach the wall doesn't fit 2 AU either. Reconcile the numbers, or drop the AU figure.
6. **Part 1, 1.2 dates paragraph (line-level):** Seven figures in one paragraph. The first reader skimmed it, and so did I. State the deadline as a chain of three subtractions, one short sentence each, and put the 12.04 conversion in a scene. Tesk's "six of my days and not a word" already does it.
7. **The Key Office rule (line-level):** "I'd set the rest down as a joke about the Office" has no antecedent. The plant that matters is "nobody had ever tested whether our key... would work", and it is buried in a joke. Lead with it.
8. **Pressa's 4.1 paragraph (line-level):** A good exposition of the undecidability result, but it is a speech. Break it with Pressa wringing out the shirt, or an interruption from Ilse.
9. **Part 3 rules check (line-level):** By the midpoint a reader can state the rules (the burn, the clock, the long thought, the cold test). Good. The "three things that can make a wall shudder" speech is the least clear link. Say the conclusion first: "cold and everywhere at once means the deep".

## What to protect

- The seven reasons (1.2), then Ilse catching herself giving seven (3.4). It's the fairest plant in the story, and its repeat pays off.
- The sixteen-minute interruptions as the form of the redshift rule. The reader learns the clock from the page layout.
- Step 4 of the five-step proof, where the cost of forging is the burn itself. Heat as the unforgeable signal is the best idea in the piece.
- "Four seconds. You waited ninety-six days for something you can do in four seconds. I have done that division too."
- "You know what they meant. I know what it says. Down here those are two different things."
- The cession as a rule with edges: "takes days to call and days to sit... in plain view."

## Blind spot disclosure

I read for rules and fair play, so I may over-weight the climax's logic and under-weight what the first reader loved, which is Bálint at the grave, Dita and the goat. A gap that the grief carries may be forgivable. I also can't judge whether Tesk's red, the body, or the gathering's texture earn their length, and the Le Guin and Lem lenses should overrule me there. I also like a puzzle with a clean answer, and this story may be right to refuse one.
