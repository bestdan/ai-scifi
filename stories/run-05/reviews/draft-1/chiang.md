## The one thing
The question is "was the no free?" Say it once, at the carts (4.4), and cut the
restatements. Cut it at 3.4 ("Neither description is the false one... whole,
and blind") and at the final page ("And I still can't tell you whether the no
was free... I don't know how much of it was theirs"). The plum-coloured light
already poses it better than any paragraph. Ending on the light and Tomé's "We'll
need a word for that" would deepen the question. Ending on the narrator's
explicit tally closes it into a thesis.

## Notes
1. **Structural. Part 1, the Key Office hours and the seven causes.** This is the
   unnecessary scene. The first reader called the causes "homework". Keep the
   confabulation, and make it one line. The untested-key fact ("nobody had ever
   tested whether our key... would work") is the plant that pays off in 5.3, so
   keep that sentence. Cut the three hours of complaint and the bulleted list.
   Ideas should come to the reader through a person, and the first person
   arrives at 2.1.
2. **Structural. The idea runs beside the feeling in two places.** The
   long-thought reveal (3.4) is explained twice: Ilse says it to Graça, then
   restates it "for the wall" in a paragraph that begins "I'll say it again
   here". Graça's reply, "So we're your mood... and you're our weather", is the
   version where the idea produces the feeling. Cut the restatement.
3. **Structural. The ending's logic.** The first reader couldn't see why the
   cession satisfies a rival who wants a proof. The story half-admits this
   ("It doesn't touch their rule. It asks them to change it"). I like that
   honesty and wouldn't tidy it into a win. But 5.3's reasoning ("a gathering
   can't be called quickly, or held secretly, or forged") needs to sit near the
   key-first strike, not after it. At the moment the ceding reads as a footnote
   to the vote.
4. **Line-level. Tally fatigue.** "Thirty-six days left... about twenty-four
   waking" recurs about eight times. Keep it where the number changes the choice
   (2.2, 4.5) and drop it from the rest. The first reader began "waiting for it".
5. **Line-level. 3.4's "I made it in order, because I was still the kind of
   Mind who thinks order is a kindness."** This is a good line about the form,
   and the draft uses it. The same device appears again at 5.1 ("because I still
   couldn't stop doing that"). Once is enough.
6. **Line-level. Tesk goes silent at 5.1.** "That was the last time Tesk broke
   into anything I said... nothing has interrupted me." This is the form
   becoming grief, which is exactly the move I'd ask for. Make sure nothing earlier
   in 5.1 pre-announces it.

## What to protect
- The Tesk interruptions, set in italics, mid-sentence, arriving sixteen
  minutes late. This is the form requiring the idea, and the formal payoff is that
  they stop.
- "Four seconds. You waited ninety-six days for something you can do in four
  seconds. I have done that division too."
- Tesk's long red as signature ("it looks like a signature"), and Bálint's
  "She said she wanted to see who'd made it finish."
- The patch misread, and Tomé's "You know what they meant. I know what it says.
  Down here those are two different things."
- The chronicle's "reported", corrected in 3.2: "They had not reported the sky.
  They had answered it." The concept gets built into the grammar of a language.
- "I'd be here" and "Show me where" as a running joke that turns into the ending
  ("and stay").
- The marked conjecture in 4.1: "the shortest proof of anything is one example
  that you want to be true."

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour measured, idea-first stories, so I may be too hard on the arithmetic
and on tallies that give other readers momentum. I have no standing on the
action beats: the storm and the grave are carried by Banks and Watts, and I
don't judge whether the Minds' faction scene has enough conflict. I also
tend to like a concept explained cleanly, so the first reader's confusion
in Part 1 matters more than my own comfort with it.
