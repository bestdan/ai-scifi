## The one thing
Make the story's own limit bite. Bible and text both say nobody can map a person to the mood. Then Part 3 maps one anyway: the Minds' "steady grief" is Bálint's. Cut the near-claim or turn it into a marked confabulation. The seven-reasons motif is the story's best biology, and Ilse breaks her own rule within a page of stating it.

## Notes
1. **Structural. Pressa's speed.** Pressa wakes in a human brain and screams because "every thought had come slowly and alone". Yet at the cairn it sorts burn victims "faster than anyone else could have done it" ("Deciding quickly"), answers children "at once", and times arguments. A grown cortex has human latency, and the story's own premise says so. Either its skill is trained triage, which would be a nice reversal, or the speed claim goes. Same for Ilse's "mass to within a few kilograms and her distance from the centre … within a metre" on waking in 2.1. Her visual cortex cannot do that. If it is Mind habit, mark it as confabulated estimation. She is "often wrong about [her body]", but here she is right.

2. **Structural. The flinch, 3.6.** "I can't tell you that it was his. Nobody can say which part of the mood is whose. But it was in there, and he was in there, and I had carried him for a hundred and thirty-two years." That is the case-study hiding inside a hedge, and it hands the reader the comfort the premise denies. Keep the grief as general ("of no known cause", as 5.3 has it). Let Ilse want it to be his and be unable to know. That is much stronger, and costs nothing.

3. **Structural. Unflagged confabulation.** In the 3.4 dictation Ilse reproaches the seven reasons, then says of Tesk's shortened days: "I think the long thought settled it … without a vote." That is an eighth reason, and the story hands it to us as fact. The loop is the real novum: the wall's mood is the deep's, the wall's weather shaped the deep's grievance, and the grievance moved the mood. Say once, plainly, that the Minds cannot separate cause from echo. Then Tesk's storm reads as the long thought acting on itself.

4. **Structural. Pressa's overhead argument is won by rhetoric.** "Which one would you put in your library?" is answered by silence, and Ilse's counter is a single-case conjecture she disowns. That honesty is good. But the rival's faster science since the loss is conceded and never costed. What does a slow layer buy that shows up in the data? The ending then affirms the deep's worth by sentiment (plum light, "stay"). One concrete cost on the wall's side, such as a measurable commitment failure the rival has made, would stop the charcoal from deciding the case.

5. **Line-level. Everyone is articulate.** Rosa ("So it's boring, but fast"), Dita, Tomé and Graça all deliver sharp, self-aware lines. Graça's "I'm not going to be your conscience tonight" is close to therapy-speak. Give one household member a response that misses the point, or a dull one.

6. **Line-level. Sleep and body.** Pressa's feet in the pond "because … cold keeps a body awake" is folk physiology. Sleep pressure beats cold, so let it lose and fall asleep in the water. Ilse's "pressure low in her belly" read as fear is good interoception and should stay. The two-thirds waking rule conflicts with "four hours a night" in 4.4, so the countdown quietly stops being biology.

7. **Line-level. Storm.** The ponds "stayed cool because [they were] deep". Give a plausible reason, such as thermal mass against a hot sky. Otherwise a reader with a physics degree will not buy it.

## What to protect
- The seven-reasons gag in the Key Office minute and Ilse's later catching herself giving seven ("I noticed that I had given myself seven, and stopped"). That is confabulation done right.
- "I got tired of it, I think", said "with the same ease with which I had given the minute my slower music." It is the story's cleanest self-knowledge beat.
- The belly pressure read as bravery until the boy leads her to the privy.
- "Four seconds. You waited ninety-six days for something you can do in four seconds."
- The patch misread: "You know what they meant. I know what it says."
- Ilse's refusal to resolve whether the no was free: "three centuries of our evenings may have written" it. Do not soften it.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour the cold, case-study version and may undervalue what the reader loved, which is the field, the goat vote and the plum light. Those carry the story's joy, and Le Guin and Banks should defend them against my cuts. Note 5 is a taste judgement more than biology. I cannot judge the holography numbers, only the minds and bodies.
