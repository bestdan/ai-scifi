## The one thing
Move the novum forward and cut Part 1's charted machinery. The off-map claim is that the long thought is the deep people (3.4) and that a gathering is the one signal that can't be forged (3.3, 5.2). Part 1 spends about 6,000 words on beats the map already holds: the mutual doomsday key, the ultimatum clock, relativistic message delay and the cost-of-proof sums. The first reader wasn't hooked until Dita's face in Part 2, and skimmed the deadline paragraph. Plant the long thought and the seven reasons earlier and shorter. Let the sums arrive as consequences, not setup.

## Notes

1. **Part 1, ultimatum, key and sums** (structural). Nearest I know of: mutual-assured-destruction keys (Vinge, *The Peace War*, 1984, for the "sealed doom" device; Liu's dark forest, 2008), and Cold War doomsday fiction generally. Confidence medium. Distance: the key is background, and the real novelty is what it forces. It still reads as familiar for the first fifth.
2. **Parts 2 to 5, the time-dilated descent** (structural, low risk). Closest: *Interstellar* (Nolan, 2014), where a gravity well sets a message lag, and Forward's *Dragon's Egg* (1980), where a fast people lives at the bottom of a well. Confidence high on both. Here the slow people are at the bottom and the faster ones are the rulers, which reverses Forward. Don't spend more words on lag arithmetic. Tesk's "Six of my days and not a word" does the job.
3. **Ilse's arc** (structural). The charted-list entry is "the AI that wants to be human", and the nearest neighbour is Banks's Culture avatars (*Excession*, 1996; *Look to Windward*, 2000). The draft guards it well: she is warm on the wall, and embodiment is clumsy rather than redemptive. But "smug Mind, humbled by hunger and a child, chooses to stay" is the stock curve. The best off-map move is already in the text, that she can't tell whether her staying is hers. Lean on it.
4. **The long thought as the people** (praise with a flag). Nearest: Lem, *Solaris* (1961), an ocean whose moods are unreadable; Stapledon, *Star Maker* (1937); Asimov's Galaxia in *Foundation's Edge* (1982). Confidence medium. None makes the slow layer a literal, checkable description of a free people who vote. The "mood" in 3.4 is close to Galaxia-as-Gaia in tone, so the stones, the carts and the goat matter. Keep them.
5. **Pressa and the cinder** (line-level). A rival who has lost its own deep, and so cannot keep a word, is uncharted as I know it. The stock beat is the enemy emissary who thaws in the field (pond, charcoal, "later"). The gesture is familiar even though the content isn't. Let Pressa stay a little harder to like.
6. **Storm and sacrifice** (line-level). Bálint dying at the stone and Maria with her one basket are good, but the quiet widower who won't leave the grave is a known beat. The tone charter is satisfied because the cinder and the storm are the novum, not decoration.
7. **The plum-coloured reply** (structural, protect). Contact that ends unresolved, with a signal nobody can attribute, is off the "mutual understanding" shelf. Good.

## What to protect
- The appointment mechanism: announce three gatherings in advance, so a cold whole-wall shudder is a promise a rival can watch. Nothing on the map treats a people's argument as a verifiable treaty signal.
- The stones with a known weighing error: "The error in the weighing was about three hundred stones either way. The margin was fifteen hundred."
- "They had not reported the sky. They had answered it."
- Ilse's ending: whether the no was free, left open.

## Blind spot disclosure
I judge distance from other books, so I over-flag shared furniture (keys, delays, avatars) that readers may take as ordinary scaffolding. I can't judge whether Part 1 hooks a cold reader beyond the first reader's report. My knowledge of the recent field, 2020 onward, is thin. "Nearest I know of" is exactly that, and I did not web-check these citations this round. I can't see the sentence-level prose.
