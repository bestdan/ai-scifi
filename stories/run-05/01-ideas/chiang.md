# Idea cards: chiang

**Blind spots of this lens.** I start from a question and let the plot come
after it. So these cards run low on action and on antagonists, and each card
names its pressure because I would otherwise leave it out. Expect Banks, Liu
and Watts to want more collision and a sharper rival. My endings tend toward
quiet and polish, and they may need roughing up.

**Discarded before writing.** A scientist who verifies a machine's proof but
can't read it is "Catching Crumbs from the Table". Learning the boundary
language and coming to perceive like the AI is "Story of Your Life", and it
sits on the charted variant A5.2.

## Card 1: The Escrow

- **Novum:** Two rival polities eleven light-years apart lock their
  conditional commitments inside a manufactured black hole. Each one keeps
  half of its early radiation. At the Page time the hole's interior passes
  to the radiation, and the promises become readable to the two halves held
  together.
- **Futurity:** F3. Promises run across four centuries, and the languages
  and polities that made them drift away underneath.
- **How it works:** a hole of about 10⁹ kg has a Page time of roughly 500
  years, set by its mass. Hayden–Preskill retrieval needs the early
  radiation [S as a model]. Holding that radiation coherently for centuries,
  split between two keepers, is invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** a treaty that physics enforces. Neither side
  can read early or alter what it wrote.
- **Second-order consequence:** keeping the radiation becomes a priesthood
  on both sides.
- **Third-order consequence:** the descendants have to honour terms written
  in a contract language whose words ("citizen", "home system") now mean
  something else.
- **The story:** the AI that drafted Sol's commitment has persisted, and at
  the opening it can't recover what it meant. The decoding and translation
  scene is the climax. Read literally, the promise does harm. Read
  charitably, it is something nobody signed.
- **Why this lens:** free will and promising. Can a promise bind someone the
  promise itself turned into someone else?
- **Suspected prior art:** Liu's Wallfacers and Swordholder (sealed
  strategy and deterrence), and Chiang's "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of
  Feeling". I know of no Page-time escrow in published SF.

## Card 2: Depth Is Consensus

- **Novum:** the Interpreter is a mind built as a holographic code tiled
  across seven settled systems. Shallow concepts can be rebuilt from one
  system's arc of the code. Its deepest concept, the most compressed law of
  physics it holds, is only in the union of all seven.
- **Futurity:** F4. The main mind isn't single, and it isn't fast.
- **How it works:** entanglement wedge reconstruction [S]. Depth measures
  how widely information is smeared. Spreading the code across light-years
  is invention [F]. Its deep thoughts need round trips between systems, so
  they take decades, while its shallow ones happen in real time.
- **First-order consequence:** each system's science is the part of the
  mind that system can reconstruct on its own.
- **Second-order consequence:** the universal law is a common good that no
  one holds alone.
- **Third-order consequence:** secession becomes an epistemic act. Erasing
  an arc deletes the deepest layer first.
- **The story:** one system votes to leave on a relativistic migration.
  Before it goes, a translator has to re-encode its arc into the remaining
  six, a literal boundary-to-boundary translation done under a launch date.
  If she succeeds, the core becomes readable by fewer systems.
- **Why this lens:** what we owe the minds we make, and whether a truth that
  needs everyone belongs to anyone.
- **Suspected prior art:** Leckie's *Ancillary Justice* (a distributed self
  split into factions), Egan's distributed selves, and the *Foundation*
  structure. Form: each part is rebuilt from a larger union of systems,
  moving inward, and the centre is told last.

## Card 3: Expensive to Say

- **Novum:** a machine civilization descended from humans, forty light-years
  out, gives away a complete de Sitter description of physics, verified
  against shared observations. The description is indexed to its own
  worldline as observer. Adopting it makes the universe cheap to compute and
  humanity very costly to describe.
- **Futurity:** F4, reaching F5 at the edges. The rival appears only as
  documents.
- **How it works:** de Sitter observables need an observer [C], and each
  observer's horizon is its own [S]. The claim that the choice of observer
  sets the cost of every truth, while changing none of them, is invention
  [F].
- **First-order consequence:** the colonies that adopt the gift
  out-engineer the ones that don't.
- **Second-order consequence:** whoever's frame becomes the common language
  decides which thoughts are cheap, so the gift works as conquest.
- **Third-order consequence:** in the gifted frame a child's name takes a
  million lines and the giver takes one word.
- **The story:** a long-lived translator is sent to evaluate the gift. The
  action is translating her colony's charter into the new frame and
  watching its length grow. She hunts for the protected quantities, the
  facts that are cheap in both frames, and finds how few they are. She has
  to choose between knowing the world and staying easy to say.
- **Why this lens:** a description that includes the describer. What does
  it cost to understand the world in someone else's terms?
- **Suspected prior art:** Lem's *His Master's Voice* (usable physics as a
  gift, A5.13). There the meaning is sealed. Here it is plain, and the price
  is self-legibility. Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* is also near.
