# Idea cards: vinge

**Blind spots, stated up front.** This lens pushes futurity even where a
story's power is in its human scale, and it leans toward technothriller
plotting and characters who exist to carry a function. The prose register it
reaches for is plain. Let Le Guin and Chiang push back on the people, and let
the drafter push back on the cast doing too many jobs. Every card names the
trick that makes its far side writeable.

## Card 1: The Long Wavelength

- **Novum:** A posthuman civilization lives in the boundary theory of an engineered anti-de Sitter bubble. Its slow neighbours in the deep bulk turn out to be that same civilization, described at long wavelength. They are not its subjects and not its copies.
- **Futurity:** F5. The boundary minds are never seen. Their motives arrive only as weather and geometry in the interior.
- **How it works:** The radial direction is zoom [E/S]. Deep bulk means coarse, slow, low-energy physics. The bubble is a human-built AdS region inside our de Sitter universe [F, owned as invention]. A bulk dweller's choices are boundary dynamics, exactly, at the scale they can see.
- **First-order consequence:** Gods who can be studied, in principle, by studying yourself.
- **Second-order consequence:** The boundary is heating up as it computes harder. In the bulk, a Hawking–Page black hole is nucleating at the centre [S].
- **Third-order consequence:** The deepest settlements fall first, and the bulk has to decide whether their own descendants are the thing killing them.
- **The story:** Teodor Aske is a surveyor born in the deep interior. He builds an HKLL "smearing" sentence, a bulk act phrased as boundary operators. Trying to say it is the translation scene. He learns there is no one upstairs to send it to.
- **Trick:** Zones of Thought from renormalization. The slow viewpoint *is* the transcendence, blurred.
- **Why this lens:** The unwriteable future, and which mind is slow enough to narrate.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan, *Schild's Ladder* (life inside a new vacuum); Vinge, Zones of Thought. Neither makes the inner people identical to the outer.

## Card 2: Quorum

- **Novum:** Humanity's colonies share one mind, run as a holographic error-correcting code over entangled memories that ships carried between the stars. Each colony's dialect and its AI translator form one boundary region. The deep concepts, the mind's "promise" and "kin", can be rebuilt only when enough colonies pool their regions.
- **Futurity:** F4. The main mind is not single. Its smallest unit is a colony.
- **How it works:** Entanglement wedge reconstruction [S], built deliberately [F as engineering]. Each convening uses up shared entanglement. Fresh pairs arrive only at sublight speed. Drain it and the shared space pinches off (Van Raamsdonk) [S as analogy, invention as hardware].
- **First-order consequence:** A species that can mean things no world can mean alone.
- **Second-order consequence:** Reconstruction is state-dependent [C]. What earlier exchanges said changes what a deep word turns out to mean.
- **Third-order consequence:** The colonies ration meaning. Speaking deeply spends the bond that makes them one people.
- **The story:** A rival intelligence wants a binding promise from "humanity" by a deadline. Oyelaran is translator for a drifted colony that holds the swing share. She can tilt what "promise" reconstructs to, and it may be the last convening the stock allows.
- **Trick:** The posthuman is shown only through quorum. It speaks in convenings decades apart.
- **Why this lens:** Distributed minds (the Tines), and who owns the intelligence.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines; Leckie, *Ancillary Justice*; Reynolds, *House of Suns*. None ties depth of meaning to the size of the quorum.

## Card 3: Python's Lunch

- **Novum:** The Powers withdrew into a small black hole they made themselves. After its Page time, the interior belongs to whoever collected the radiation. Its geometry is a calibrated time-lock: decoding it takes a computation sized to what the left-behind could build in six centuries.
- **Futurity:** F5. The interior's minds are shown only as cost.
- **How it works:** Islands put the interior inside the radiation's entanglement wedge [S in models]. Decoding cost is exponential in the gap between the bulge and the neck [C]. The Powers tuned that gap [F]. Complexity tracks volume [C], so a decoder must grow about as complex as the interior it reads.
- **First-order consequence:** The custodians, slow humans and their AI translators, legally own a transcendence.
- **Second-order consequence:** A rival custodial polity holds the other hemisphere of the radiation. Neither side can decode without the other's share.
- **Third-order consequence:** Decoding swallows each civilization's compute for centuries. Whoever finishes has become the kind of mind the message was written for.
- **The story:** Sunniva Holt, a custodian who is still human, must decide whether to hand her share to the rivals. In the scene she gets one cheap question answered as a protected quantity: is anyone in there?
- **Trick:** The edge is shown as a bill, never as the inside.
- **Why this lens:** What tells you a transcendence has happened.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Blight archive; Lem, *His Master's Voice*; Sagan, *Contact*. Here reading is the change.
