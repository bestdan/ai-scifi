# Idea cards: le-guin

**Blind spots of this lens.** It paces slowly and will reach for a
gathering where the brief wants a wager. Its interest in hardware and
strategy is thin, so the pressure in each card is a physics deadline or a
custody choice, not a war; Liu, Egan and Stephenson should push the scale,
the engineering and the speed. Its moral seriousness can tip into
instruction.

## Card 1: The Early Light

- **Novum:** Two sister peoples give their dead's words to a small
  engineered black hole. Only whoever holds its early radiation can read
  what falls in, and when the founding fleet split, each people took half.
- **Futurity:** F3. The dead are read rather than buried, and kin is whoever
  keeps the light. About 2,000 years, two systems six light-years apart.
- **How it works:** A million-tonne hole lives about 2,700 years [E]. Past
  its Page time, what falls in comes out scrambled almost at once, and it is
  cheap to decode only with the early radiation in hand. Without it,
  decoding takes exponential time (Hayden–Preskill) [S/C]. Storing around
  10³⁴ qubits of early light is invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** Every household tends its jar of early light
  like a hearth.
- **Second-order consequence:** Seven centuries of both peoples' dead lie in
  the radiation unread, and the rite goes on as faith.
- **Third-order consequence:** One people builds a theology of the held, the
  other a law of custody, and their words for the dead drift apart.
- **The story:** The jars' error correction is losing; in a century they'll
  hold only noise. A keeper whose mother was given to the hole after the
  split can carry her people's half across. Whoever merges the halves takes
  custody of both peoples' dead, and keeping ends as a way of life. The AI
  decoding her mother's drifted words is the translation scene.
- **Why this lens:** Who owns the dead; the carrier bag over the spear.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's "The Planck Dive" (exploring a hole, not
  inheriting from one). Not close.

## Card 2: The Deep Is Held in Common

- **Novum:** A polity spread across star systems stores its shared model of
  the universe (physics, law, its AI's understanding) as a holographic
  quantum code. Any one world can read the fine, local detail. Only a quorum
  of worlds can reconstruct the deep layers, the coarse summaries, which is
  to say the meanings.
- **Futurity:** F3–F4. Nobody owns a meaning, only details, and the AI
  exists whole only when a quorum convenes.
- **How it works:** Entanglement-wedge reconstruction, with depth read as
  coarse-graining [S as code properties]. The deepest information can be
  rebuilt only from more than half the boundary. Because of no-cloning, a
  world that leaves can't copy the deep layers out with it [E]. Building the
  code across light-years is invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** Deep questions go out as quorum calls and are
  answered decades later.
- **Second-order consequence:** The frontier outruns the quorum's
  light-cone. The farthest worlds have every fact and no meaning.
- **Third-order consequence:** Frontier speech grows exact words for
  particulars and loses its general nouns.
- **The story:** A clerk on a frontier moon spends her ordinary days on
  shallow queries. Then a rival intelligence appears at the edge. Whether to
  answer it is a deep question, and she can read only the shallow layers.
  She must decide alone, against her civilization's founding custom.
- **Why this lens:** An ambiguous anarchist utopia, and the one person it
  fails.
- **Suspected prior art:** Echoes *The Dispossessed* and the Hives of
  *Terra Ignota*. Nobody else makes depth a quorum.

## Card 3: Rim-Mothers

- **Novum:** A people who keep an engineered anti-de Sitter bubble as their
  library raise each child with two mothers: a human who speaks geometry,
  and an AI tending the boundary who speaks only correlations. The children
  end up native in both and easy in neither.
- **Futurity:** F3. An AI is a mother, and the child's first tongue has no
  word for "here".
- **How it works:** Strong/weak duality: what is easy on one side is hard on
  the other [S]. The boundary language has no term for a location in the
  bulk [S]. A hot boundary is a bulk black hole (Hawking–Page) [S]. The
  bubble is invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** Raising children becomes the library's
  infrastructure.
- **Second-order consequence:** Rim-raised adults are half strangers to
  their human parents.
- **Third-order consequence:** Human mothers grieve children who are alive
  and fluent and not quite theirs.
- **The story:** The boundary is warming toward the Hawking–Page point. Past
  it, the library collapses into a hole, preserved and unreadable. Cooling it
  means halting the computation the rim-mothers run on. A rim-raised
  archivist translates the warning between her two mothers, then chooses
  between the library and her second mother.
- **Why this lens:** Who raises the children; what these people can't say.
- **Suspected prior art:** Inverts Chiang's "The Lifecycle of Software
  Objects". The missing "here" echoes *Babel-17*'s missing "I".
