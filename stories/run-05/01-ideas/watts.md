# Idea cards: watts

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front.** It runs bleak, and it will
take the unsettling answer over the warm one. It builds characters as case
studies, and it packs ideas in densely enough to tire a reader. It
underrates meaning, ritual and joy as real forces. Every card below needs
Le Guin or Banks to push on whether its people are people, and Stephenson to
check whether it's any fun. Physics tags follow the physics dossier's
E/S/C/F scale. Biology citations are named inline.

## Card 1: The Deep Goes First

- **Novum:** A person encoded holographically across seven star systems so
  that no single loss can kill her. The code puts her core self at the
  centre of the bulk. That spot survives the loss of any one system, and it
  is the first thing to go once enough systems are lost. Human amnesia
  loses the newest memories first (Ribot's law). She loses the oldest and
  deepest first.
- **Futurity:** F4. The main mind isn't single and isn't continuous.
- **How it works:** An AI translates a dying founder's life into boundary
  form: a HaPPY-style code (Pastawski et al. 2015), with the code itself
  [E] and its use on a mind [F]. Small shares rebuild only the shallow
  bulk, meaning her skills and recent life, so each system runs a
  local instance. Her deepest layer, the most compressed summary of who she
  is, lies only in the entanglement wedge of a majority of shares (Dong,
  Harlow and Wall 2016) [S].
- **First-order consequence:** The deep self speaks only at a convocation,
  when ships bring the shares together. That happens once a century or two.
- **Second-order consequence:** Between convocations each instance has to
  guess what the deep self would want, and makes up its reasons the way
  Gazzaniga's split-brain interpreter does.
- **Third-order consequence:** A rival takes systems. The instances keep
  every memory and lose the reasons behind them.
- **The story:** The carrier is the person, across her convocations. At the
  last viable one she must choose. She can re-encode shallower and survive
  as less, or spend the quorum on one final decision made whole.
- **Why this lens:** The self is a story, here with the physics of its
  storage spelled out.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines (pack minds that change when they
  lose members) and Leckie's *Ancillary Justice*. Neither has the
  deep-first loss rule, and that rule is the core of this card.

## Card 2: Placeless

- **Novum:** Two polities light-years apart keep a treaty by publishing
  compliance proofs in boundary form. Translating a deep fact into bulk
  language would take longer than the audit window. So each side raises
  auditors whose hippocampal maps were tuned from infancy to boundary
  correlations instead of to space.
- **Futurity:** F3. Self, place and memory become different categories for
  a caste.
- **How it works:** Grid cells also map abstract spaces (Constantinescu,
  O'Reilly and Behrens 2016). These children's maps are fixed during a
  sensitive period. The cost is real: episodic memory and imagining the
  future both depend on the hippocampus building scenes (Hassabis et al.
  2007). Auditors can verify the proofs. They can't picture tomorrow. The
  translation cost behind all this comes from the Python's Lunch work [C].
- **First-order consequence:** Proofs get checked inside the light-lag
  windows.
- **Second-order consequence:** The people who check the strategy can't
  imagine any of its outcomes. The people who can imagine outcomes can't
  check anything.
- **Third-order consequence:** Parents decide before age seven whether a
  child will ever have a future it can picture.
- **The story:** An auditor finds a fact in the rival's proof that is one
  line long on the boundary. She has to make geometric councillors act on
  it before the window closes, and she can't construct the scene they need
  to see. The translation scene is the climax.
- **Why this lens:** It changes a mind to fit a hostile environment, as
  with the rifters, and counts the cost of the change.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Blindsight* (Siri, a synthesist whose brain was
  altered); *Ender's Game*. Neither touches place cells or verification.

## Card 3: The Anechoic Century

- **Novum:** An alien species evolved in a sealed, ice-capped ocean that
  reverberates almost without loss. In that ocean a source can only be
  located by inverting the echoes, so its minds think in correlations, not
  places. Its mathematics is native boundary language. Its tidal heating is
  rising, and in about three centuries the ice will open.
- **Futurity:** F3 for the humans. The aliens' minds sit near F4.
- **How it works:** In a chaotic reverberant cavity, a single sensor's
  recording holds enough to refocus the source (time-reversal acoustics:
  Draeger and Fink 1997) [E]. A box with reflecting walls is AdS's own
  picture [E as an analogy]. This species does strong-coupling field
  theory by intuition and finds geometry exponentially hard [F, invention].
  Sensory maps rewire to match the environment during sensitive periods
  (Knudsen's prism-reared barn owls).
- **First-order consequence:** Humans, whose geometry is easy, trade with
  minds for whom the boundary is easy. An AI keeps the only dictionary
  between them.
- **Second-order consequence:** Once the ice opens, young born into an open
  ocean will grow up as geometers.
- **Third-order consequence:** Humans want to hold the ice shut. Some of
  their reason is respect for the species. Some of it is that this species
  is the only mind that can check their AI.
- **The story:** A human translator and an elder have to decide whether to
  seal the ocean, keeping an irreplaceable mind and keeping it captive, or
  let the open sky make the young into strangers to their elders.
- **Why this lens:** It removes the pressure that selected a mind, as the
  lens's method asks, and the biology is marine.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Starfish*; Vinge's *A Deepness in the Sky*;
  *Children of Time*. None ties a sensorium to a physical duality.
