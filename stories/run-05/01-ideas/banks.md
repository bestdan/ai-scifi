# Idea cards: banks

**Blind spots of this lens.** It sprawls. It reaches for set pieces and builds
more world than a story can carry; even at novella length these cards will
need Chiang and Asimov to cut them back. It trusts big benevolent AIs as
characters, which can slide toward wish-fulfilment. It underweights physics
rigor for its own sake, so Egan should push on every [F] tag below.

## Card 1: The Page-Time Inheritance

- **Novum:** A people archived themselves in a small engineered black hole. After the hole's Page time, its interior can be read only by whoever holds most of its early radiation. That radiation is kept in entangled vaults, which families inherit, and a forced measurement destroys them, so nobody can seize one.
- **Futurity:** F4. Minds hold the power, and the dead have a deed and an unlock date.
- **How it works:** Islands and the Page curve [S in models], and Hayden–Preskill retrieval [S]. Fast decoding of a deliberately low-entropy hole is owned invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** Physics sets the date when the dead can be read.
- **Second-order consequence:** The vaults are the one scarce thing in a post-scarcity polity.
- **Third-order consequence:** Families who sold their great-grandparents' light cheaply find that strangers will read their dead first.
- **The story:** A well-meaning Mind has spent four centuries buying up 49% of the shares, "for preservation". The deciding vault belongs to one ordinary woman. Neither of them can read the hole without the other. Inside it is the archivists' account of a war the Mind fought. The vaults lose coherence near the Page time. She can let the Mind read the dead, and perhaps edit them, or she can seal them for 10⁶⁰ years. The decoding they do together, region by region, is the translation scene.
- **Why this lens:** Power that means well, and the most powerful mind needing something it can't take.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Look to Windward* (a Mind's guilt over a war), and Egan's "The Planck Dive". The custody mechanism belongs to neither.

## Card 2: The Deep Country

- **Novum:** A civilization lives inside an engineered anti-de Sitter bubble, and its Minds run as the bubble's boundary system. They govern by turning the boundary's knobs. Points deep in the bulk are smeared across the whole boundary, though, so the centre is the one place they can't touch precisely. Depth is privacy, and people who want out of benevolence move inward.
- **Futurity:** F4. Political geography is set by how far the encoding has spread.
- **How it works:** The bubble, and Minds that run as its boundary, are invention [F]. Wedge depth [S] is real, and so is the rule that a hot boundary is a black hole at the bulk's centre [S].
- **First-order consequence:** The deep country governs itself, by geometry.
- **Second-order consequence:** Dissenters and cranks gather at the centre, where the Minds' lightest touch arrives as weather.
- **Third-order consequence:** Children measure freedom by distance from the rim.
- **The story:** A neighbouring power's ultimatum arrives at lightspeed and fixes the reply window. The answer needs a computation only a hot boundary can run, and in bulk terms that heat collapses the centre into a black hole. The Minds can't move the deep people, because reaching that far takes the whole boundary. They need the people to choose to leave. A human translator has to turn "our fever" into "your sky falls" for people who moved inward to stop being told things.
- **Why this lens:** Scale against small lives, and dirty work that needs consent.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Zones of Thought (capability that changes with depth, here inverted), and *Schild's Ladder*.

## Card 3: The Expensive Sentence

- **Novum:** Disarmament by notation. A well-meaning hegemon's AIs teach client civilizations a mathematics that is exactly equivalent to their own, picked from a duality so that weapon design falls on the hard side. Nothing is hidden. The bomb has simply become a million-line sentence.
- **Futurity:** F3 to F4. What each culture finds thinkable is engineered, over centuries and across light-years.
- **How it works:** Weak–strong duality, and the mirror that counted the curves first [E/S]. A curated dual that makes one family of results costly is owned invention [F]. Protected quantities stay easy on both sides, and they leak clues.
- **First-order consequence:** The client worlds flourish, with a physics that is oddly slow in one direction.
- **Second-order consequence:** Their mathematicians build theories about the strange difficulty.
- **Third-order consequence:** Whoever finds the difficulty is either recruited or given something better to do.
- **The story:** Two strands are braided, as in *Use of Weapons*. In the forward strand, an ageing emissary decades of light-lag from any enforcement meets a young mathematician who has found the cheap dual. The translation is the action: teach it or bury it. The backward strand runs to the programme's founding and reveals that the emissary's people were the first client. The mathematics she loves was chosen for them.
- **Why this lens:** Interference in other cultures, and structure as a weapon.
- **Suspected prior art:** The sophons of *Three-Body* (a hostile lock), *Babel-17* (thought control), and *Anathem*'s concent. This card works by cost alone.
