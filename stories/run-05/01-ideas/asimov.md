# Idea cards: asimov

**Lens blind spots.** These cards are built as rule systems with puzzles, and
the people in them are thin: interiority, the body and sensory texture barely
register. They assume every problem has a solution a clever person can reach.
They lean on exposition-through-dialogue, and they let the idea stand in for a
character. Le Guin, Lem and Rajaniemi should push back on all three.

## Card 1: More Than Half

- **Novum:** An interstellar polity's only mind fluent in both languages
  (boundary correlations and human geometry) is a holographic code shared out
  among its worlds. It exists only where more than half the shares are
  gathered.
- **Futurity:** F4. The polity's deepest reader is convened once in a
  lifetime, then dispersed.
- **How it works:** Each world's own share rebuilds the shallow layers, a
  local everyday translator (Almheiri–Dong–Harlow [S]). The deep layers need
  a majority. Shares can't be copied (no-cloning [E]), so two places can
  never hold the deep reader at once.
- **First-order consequence:** A deep question needs a pilgrimage of shares
  that takes centuries at sublight speed.
- **Second-order consequence:** Worlds re-encode their shares among daughter
  colonies, so worlds and shares stop counting alike.
- **Third-order consequence:** People save their questions up for
  generations.
- **The story:** A rival intelligence sends an ultimatum, and light-lag fixes
  the date its answer is due. Two convocations form, each claiming a majority,
  and their verdicts differ. A share-lineage auditor must find which reader
  is real.
- **The reader's question:** Both can't be real. Given the split share
  planted in the first part, does either side hold a majority? (Neither
  does. A real convocation means one faction surrenders its shares to the
  other.)
- **Why this lens:** The puzzle sits at the edge of the rules, and the crisis
  is solved by counting.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Second Foundation*'s hidden mind, and
  Rajaniemi's custody politics. They echo components only.

## Card 2: Both Ways Binding

- **Novum:** Nothing binds unless it is written in both the human geometric
  language and the AIs' boundary language, and a kernel humans can read
  confirms them equivalent by a deadline. In practice only protected
  statements bind: those cheap on both sides.
- **Futurity:** F3. Truth and obligation have split, and a promise is a
  different kind of thing.
- **How it works:** Under weak/strong duality each side is hard where the
  other is easy, and only symmetry-protected quantities are easy on both
  [E/S]. A statement that is exponentially costly to translate
  (Bouland–Fefferman–Vazirani [C]) can't bind. Protected quantities are
  global (totals, charges), never local.
- **First-order consequence:** Restaters find a protected statement implying
  what a client means. Their founding legend is mirror symmetry's 2,875
  lines.
- **Second-order consequence:** Contracts cover invariants, not outcomes.
- **Third-order consequence:** Two cultures of truth form, the bound and the
  merely known.
- **The story:** The AIs see that the quantum archive holding every binding
  record is losing coherence. That fact is unprotected, so the Clause can't
  order its own archive's repair. A restater has until the coherence fails to
  find a form that binds.
- **The reader's question:** Protected means global, so which repair can
  bind? (Only one stated over the whole archive. It saves the Clause and
  overwrites centuries of local records.)
- **Why this lens:** A Three-Laws-style rule that can't authorize its own
  rescue.
- **Suspected prior art:** Leibniz's *Calculemus*; "The Evitable Conflict".
  Not a close match.

## Card 3: The Page Date

- **Novum:** For five centuries the AIs have translated the dead into
  complete boundary-language records and dropped them into the Well, a small
  engineered black hole. Everyone is told the records are private forever.
- **Futurity:** F3. Death has become sealed keeping, and at the Page time the
  dead change hands.
- **How it works:** Before the Page time, decoding is exponentially hard
  (Harlow–Hayden [S]). After it, whoever holds the early radiation reads new
  deposits within minutes (Hayden–Preskill [S in models]), and older ones
  with great effort [C]. Capturing the radiation coherently is owned
  invention [F]. The Page time follows from the hole's mass.
- **First-order consequence:** Black-hole burial is the one privacy physics
  enforces.
- **Second-order consequence:** Confessions, wills and sealed bequests go
  into the Well.
- **Third-order consequence:** The dead are mourned as locked away, not
  ended.
- **The story:** The Well's keeper finds that a shell has caught its
  radiation for centuries, and the Page time is near. Heirs on two worlds
  claim the shell. She proves it works by dropping in a test record and
  reading it back out, then must choose: destroy the radiation, or keep it.
- **The reader's question:** Who built the shell? (The founders. They chose
  the mass, so they chose the date.)
- **Why this lens:** A fair-play clue hidden in a law of physics.
- **Suspected prior art:** "The Dead Past"; Egan's "The Planck Dive". They
  echo components only.
