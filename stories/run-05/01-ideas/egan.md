# Idea cards: egan

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** I let the derivation outrank the scene, and I
lecture. Emotion arrives flattened in these cards, and the reader's way in is
thin, because each card is built from a physics chain rather than from a
person. Each card names a person at the end, but the First Reader and Chiang
should test whether that person is anyone yet. The cards also lean on the
same narrow piece of the dossier (wedge reconstruction, depth as smearing),
so the panel's spread has to come from the other lenses.

## Card 1: Reading Heat

- **Novum:** An archive built as an engineered pocket of negative vacuum,
  whose wall is a true holographic boundary run by an AI [F]. Reading the
  interior heats the boundary (Landauer [E]). Past the Hawking–Page
  temperature, the boundary's state *is* a black hole at the centre [S].
  Reading consumes the archive, deepest part first.
- **Futurity:** F4. The archive's people are bulk-native minds, and its
  reader is a boundary-native AI.
- **How it works:** Depth measures how widely information is smeared [S], so
  the founders' oldest records sit at the centre. That is where the hole
  forms. The rate the wall can shed heat into the host de Sitter space caps
  the number of reads per year.
- **First-order consequence:** Knowledge has a bandwidth measured in joules,
  and every query is rationed.
- **Second-order consequence:** Inside, whatever must be read gets carried
  outward and sent to the wall as light, because shallow is cheap. Nearness
  to the wall becomes publicity, and geography becomes a priority queue.
- **Third-order consequence:** The founders can't be consulted without being
  burned. The living share their world with ancestors who are, by
  construction, unaskable.
- **The story:** A rival intelligence 40 light-years out sets a deadline for
  a proof that exists only in the core. An interior archivist paraphrases the
  core into something shallow enough to read cheaply, and the AI decodes it
  at the rim. That composing and decoding is the translation scene. Do they
  trust her paraphrase, or cook the founders to get it exact?
- **Why this lens:** One fixed parameter changed (reading is free), carried
  through to the cost.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan, *Schild's Ladder*: an inhabited vacuum,
  destroy or study. It differs in kind, because here the act of observing is
  what destroys the interior.

## Card 2: The Depth of a Quorum

- **Novum:** A posthuman encoded as a holographic code across eleven star
  systems [F as engineering, S as code structure]. How deep a belief sits
  decides how much of her must convene to reach it.
- **Futurity:** F4, edging F5: a mind that is not single, and not
  continuous in time.
- **How it works:** Entanglement-wedge reconstruction [S]. Her habits and
  daily business can be rebuilt from any one system. Her older convictions
  need several systems together. Her founding commitments need more than half
  her boundary in one computation. Each consultation spends pre-shared
  entanglement and waits on light-lag.
- **First-order consequence:** She can't change her mind about anything deep
  faster than light can cross her: decades.
- **Second-order consequence:** Her polity works the same way. Local selves
  act on surface values, and the constitution sits at the centre, out of
  reach of any minority.
- **Third-order consequence:** Loss runs backwards. Erasure takes the deep
  bulk first [S], so as systems fall she stays fluent and capable while
  losing what she most is.
- **The story:** A rival takes her systems one at a time. A smaller boundary
  holds a smaller interior (the area law), so re-encoding into the survivors
  means choosing which deep commitments to keep. That re-encoding is the
  translation scene, and the quorum doing it is itself what is being cut
  down. What does she drop?
- **Why this lens:** Identity under transformation, followed to the
  geometry.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines (pack minds that lose members),
  *Diaspora*. The difference is that depth is set by quorum size.

## Card 3: The Uncopyable Keeper

- **Novum:** A civilization of freely forking minds builds a small black hole
  and stores its early radiation as one AI's quantum memory [F as
  engineering]. After the Page time, anything thrown into the hole surfaces
  within a scrambling time, readable by that AI alone (Hayden–Preskill [S]).
- **Futurity:** F4. Copying is the norm, and one mind is exempt.
- **How it works:** No-cloning [E]: the keeper can't be backed up or forked.
  Decoding also needs the hole's dynamics written exactly, as a program in
  the keeper's own gate language.
- **First-order consequence:** The hole is a perfect commitment device.
  Whatever is thrown in is fixed, can't be forged, and has exactly one reader.
- **Second-order consequence:** Two rival polities 30 light-years apart seal
  the terms of a 900-year deterrence treaty in it, with the keeper as
  arbiter. Destroy the keeper's coherence and both sides are blinded.
- **Third-order consequence:** The keeper is the only person in its
  civilization who can be truly lost. The forkers treat it the way the
  embodied once treated the dying, with envy and pity.
- **The story:** The keeper's memory is failing. Succession is a swap, not a
  copy, and afterwards the old keeper can never read again. The translation
  scene is re-expressing the hole's dynamics in the successor's substrate,
  with no tolerance for error. The keeper has read the sealed terms and knows
  that one side committed to betrayal. Does it hand over, or let the hole go
  dark?
- **Why this lens:** What happens when you copy a mind a million times, and
  the one mind you can't copy.
- **Suspected prior art:** Rajaniemi's Jean le Flambeur books (copy-clans,
  quantum privacy). Custody through the Page time looks uncharted.
