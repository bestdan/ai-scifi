# Idea cards: lem

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front.** I like an essay better than a
scene, and documents better than people. My characters can end up as
ciphers who hold a position. Left alone I drift bleak and cerebral, so each
card below aims for the comic and the awestruck register on purpose. I'm
also inclined to withhold, which a novella can't afford: every card names
one thing that stays unexplained, and it is only one, so the payoff ledger
can state everything else. Banks and Le Guin should push on warmth, and the
First Reader should rule on whether each card is still a story.

## Card 1: The Island Docket

- **Novum:** A court has jurisdiction over a black hole's interior after the
  Page time, once that interior is encoded in the radiation. Its one witness
  can be asked only questions that are cheap to decode, so each question is
  priced by its depth.
- **Futurity:** F4. Polities and software jurists across 30 light-years
  argue one case for 700 years.
- **How it works:** A small engineered hole swallowed a pilot carrying an
  archive. Its narrow-necked lunch geometry keeps decoding finite [F, owned
  as invention]. The court's shell gathered the radiation, and AI decoders
  can reconstruct the interior [S, in models]. The cost grows exponentially
  with depth [C, the Python's Lunch]. Conserved charges come cheap. A mind's
  intentions don't.
- **First-order consequence:** The interior is property, and the radiation
  is its deed.
- **Second-order consequence:** The law of evidence gets rebuilt around
  decoding cost. Counsel learn to phrase questions so that the answer is a
  protected quantity.
- **Third-order consequence:** Testimony becomes a genre of conserved
  quantities. Asked whether she consents, the witness gives her angular
  momentum.
- **The story:** The carrier is the court's clerk, an AI that turns each
  permitted question from law into a boundary operator, which is the
  translation scene. The pilot's descendant wants one expensive question
  answered before the hole evaporates: did you mean to go in? A rival polity
  wants the compute spent on the archive instead. The clerk can afford one
  of the two. Never explained: what the answer means.
- **Why this lens:** The limits of knowing, turned into procedure. Human
  vanity, as jurisprudence.
- **Suspected prior art:** Le Guin's "Author of the Acacia Seeds" (the
  scholarly form), Egan's "The Planck Dive", and open angle 5. The nearest
  is Bleak House with a black hole, which is a different book.

## Card 2: One Trit

- **Novum:** A self-repairing swarm of mindless machines, spread across a
  dozen nearby stars, turns out to be a holographic error-correcting code.
  All of its redundancy protects a single quantum trit, encoded so deep
  that only whoever holds most of the swarm at once can rebuild it.
- **Futurity:** F4. The story covers 2,000 years and 12 light-years, with
  AI decoders and long-lived human negotiators.
- **How it works:** The members tile into a hyperbolic network like the
  HaPPY code [E, as a construction]. Erosion selected for surviving loss,
  and every generation added a layer, which deepened the core. Recovering
  the centre takes a majority [S for AdS codes; F as applied here]. The
  shallow layers hold the swarm's repair grammar. AI linguists learn it
  first and mistake it for a language.
- **First-order consequence:** Destroying part of the swarm doesn't kill
  it. Anyone who seizes a share learns the grammar and none of the content.
- **Second-order consequence:** A coalition race across light-lag to hold
  a majority. Treaties are written in the swarm's grammar so that they can
  be checked.
- **Third-order consequence:** A custodial culture grows up, where prestige
  is how much of an unreadable thing you hold.
- **The story:** A negotiator finally assembles the majority, and the AI
  decodes the core live, which is the translation scene. It finds one
  qutrit in superposition. Measuring it gives 0, 1 or 2, and destroys the
  only thing the swarm existed to keep. A rival bloc can break the majority
  at the next treaty date, and she must decide whether to read it.
- **Why this lens:** Non-anthropomorphic intelligence, comic deflation, and
  awe at the scale of the redundancy.
- **Suspected prior art:** Lem's *The Invincible* (the swarm), *His
  Master's Voice*, and Vinge's Blight. The custody race and the payoff of a
  single trit belong to none of them.

## Card 3: The Observer of Record

- **Novum:** Humanity's canonical description of the cosmos is compiled by
  AI. Its physics has only one state unless a physical observer is written
  in, a clock with a memory. One actual clock holds the office, and every
  navigation table and proof library is stated relative to it.
- **Futurity:** F3 to F4. Colonies 20 to 60 light-years apart over four
  centuries, with a canon no human reads whole.
- **How it works:** A closed or de Sitter description needs an observer
  [C; Chandrasekaran–Longo–Penington–Witten, Harlow–Usatyuk–Zhao]. The
  invention is a working static-patch dictionary [F] conditioned on a named
  clock. Two canons agree on every measurement either can make and differ
  on how everything else is stated. Re-basing the canon on a different
  clock is an exponentially costly retranslation. The clock is a mechanism.
  Nobody's mind enters into it.
- **First-order consequence:** The clock is the most protected object in
  human space.
- **Second-order consequence:** Colonies run light-lagged copies of the
  canon and lobby to name a local clock.
- **Third-order consequence:** A schism. Two canons, both exact, and each
  writes the other colony's clock in as a small correction term. Horologists
  become theologians.
- **The story:** Radiation damage is wearing out the clock's memory, a
  deadline set by physics. Its keeper and the compiler AI must choose a
  successor: one at Sol, or the rival colony's. In the translation scene a
  single theorem has to be carried between the two canons while the old
  clock fails. Never explained: what the one-state universe says when
  nobody is written into it.
- **Why this lens:** Meeting our own mirror. The vanity of a cosmology that
  needs a clerk.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stephenson's *Anathem* (clocks, maths),
  Foundation's canon, and Borges's "Tlön". All of these are echoes, and none
  repeats the core move.
