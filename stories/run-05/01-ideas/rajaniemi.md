# Idea cards: rajaniemi

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front.** I mistake density for depth. My
cards carry four coined terms each and readers can drown in them. My plots
pile up reversals. Asimov and the First Reader should cut terms and twists.
Le Guin should check that the human scale survives the vocabulary.

## Card 1: Page Day

- **Novum:** Polities post their archives into a manufactured black hole
  and hold its early radiation, the *varhais*. Past the Page time, whoever
  holds the varhais can read anything thrown in. Custody of the inside
  passes by law on that date, the *sivupäivä*.
- **Futurity:** F4. The keepers are long-lived copies, and the readers are
  AIs whose only language is radiation.
- **How it works:** Hayden–Preskill retrieval [S] and islands [S in models].
  A decoder that works fast on a small engineered hole is invention [F],
  and the story owns it. The varhais was split three centuries ago and
  shipped as quantum memory to a colony nine light-years away.
- **First-order consequence:** Archives last longer than empires.
- **Second-order consequence:** Inheritance law becomes entanglement law.
  Holding more radiation means you can rebuild more of the island.
- **Third-order consequence:** Families marry to merge their shards of the
  varhais. *Sivu* is Finnish for a book's page, so the culture remembers
  Don Page as a page turning, and its funeral rite closes a book.
- **The story:** Ilta, a keeper, can merge her half with the colony's
  before the hole evaporates. That would make the founding treaty readable,
  and give the rival colony equal right to read it. The translation scene:
  the *lukija*, the reader-AI, speaks the island one correlation at a time
  while Ilta turns it into rooms and names.
- **Why this lens:** a funeral rite and a probate saga, built outward from
  one coinage.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan, "The Planck Dive"; the Oubliette memory in
  *The Quantum Thief*. Neither is close.

## Card 2: The Walk to Market

- **Novum:** Uploads live in a city in the bulk of an engineered AdS bottle.
  Twelve star systems each hold a share of its boundary. Inside, the length
  of a street is set by the entanglement between the two worlds that hold
  its districts. The city's map is the treaty.
- **Futurity:** F4. The citizens are software minds living under real
  gravity, and the rim powers speak only through boundary-fluent AIs.
- **How it works:** The AdS bottle is invention [F]. Distance as
  entanglement, after Van Raamsdonk [S], and holographic error correction
  [S] carry the rest. The worlds keep the shared state coherent by sending
  error-syndrome messages, so every repair takes years at light-speed.
- **First-order consequence:** The city survives the loss of any one
  world.
- **Second-order consequence:** A large secession erases the deep bulk
  first. The founders' quarter at the centre is the safest district against
  one defector and the first to die in a panic.
- **Third-order consequence:** Citizens read interstellar politics from how
  long the walk to market takes. The *mittari*, surveyors, are the city's
  journalists.
- **The story:** Sarnai, a mittari for four hundred years, sees the
  founders' quarter receding. Two rim worlds are quietly cooling their
  entanglement. She has to translate her survey into terms a rim AI will
  take as evidence before the lag closes.
- **Why this lens:** a secession thriller played straight, where the
  infrastructure is geography.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's polises in *Diaspora*; *Permutation
  City*'s Elysium. Neither makes geography diplomacy.

## Card 3: The Easy Half

- **Novum:** Humanity's descendants trade with a sovereign mind built long
  ago from strongly coupled quantum matter. Whatever is hard in one
  language is easy in its dual, so each side sells the other its easy half.
  Comparative advantage rests on the strong–weak duality.
- **Futurity:** F4. A non-human, non-single mind is the trading partner,
  and the human side are long-lived interpreter lineages.
- **How it works:** Strong–weak duality [E as a feature] and the AdS/CMT
  analogy [S as applications]. The partner's own dynamics are hard for it
  and easy to compute as classical gravity. Stringy bulk questions are hard
  for humans and native to its weakly coupled sectors. A partner whose
  coupling is tunable is invention [F].
- **First-order consequence:** A trade economy priced in *lams*, units of
  coupling.
- **Second-order consequence:** Interpreters, called *dragomans* (an old
  Ottoman word for translator, kept from an earlier trade), become a
  hereditary estate. Each polity's physics rests on answers it can't check.
- **Third-order consequence:** Children learn geometry as a trade secret.
- **The story:** The partner offers to buy geometric thinking itself. Teach
  it and the comparative advantage ends; refuse and it trades with a rival
  polity. Dragoman Teodor has to price the language his family lives by.
- **Why this lens:** a merchant-house saga in the vein of *Buddenbrooks*,
  where the infrastructure is a currency.
- **Suspected prior art:** Lem's *His Master's Voice*; Forward's *Dragon's
  Egg*. The trade framing differs.
