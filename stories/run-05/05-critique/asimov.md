# Stage 5 critique: Asimov lens

Blind spots: I read rules, clues and who-knows-what. I don't judge interiority, the body, or the grief scenes. Le Guin-type and Liu-type notes should outrank mine there. I assume problems can be solved.

Scene sums match the arc map (35,000), and each part's scenes add up. The first third (11,700 words) ends inside part 2, so facts 1-5 land in time.

1. **[must-fix] The stage file's calendar section is missing.** The story hinges on dates, and the outline plants none of them. The payoff needs these inputs on the page before 5.4:
   - transmit date W 321;
   - light-lag 4.37 years;
   - burn length 3 years;
   - therefore the last burn start is 313.63 and the last proof departure is 316.63;
   - each gathering arrives at the rival before 321.0.

   Add the section. Plant the first three in 1.1, have Ilse derive 313.63 and 316.63 aloud in 1.2, and give the arrival dates in 4.5. Also check the Tesk "interrupts a sentence" joke (2.4). A reply can't beat the 7.9-minute light trip each way, so make the interruption land on her second thought, not her first.

2. **[must-fix] Nothing says why a kept appointment answers the demand.** The rival asked for a proof that the wall can't fail. The appointment shows a cold, whole-wall deep event. A reader will ask how that proves anything about the wall. 4.5's "Everything you could fake would cost you a fever" covers forgery only. Add a plain line, from Ilse or Pressa, on what the rival is meant to infer. The deep exists, it was not cooked, and it keeps its dates, so a civilization that holds a commitment is one that doesn't release its wall. Tie it to the conjecture from 4.1, and say it is the conjecture.

3. **[must-fix] Plant the rule earlier.** The forgery test is "stated in 4.5", after the midpoint, and Graça's idea is the solution. Move the statement of the test to 3.4. Pressa gives the two checks there: the whole wall changes in step, and there is no rise in heat. Then an attentive reader can build the appointment before Graça does. Also, "the whole wall registers a deep event" (the redundant encoding) isn't on the world-fact table. Put it in 2.4 or 3.3, so the midpoint's rules are statable: redshift, weather costs heat, whole-wall registration, and the shadow limit.

4. **[must-fix] The Minds' decision is offstage and the timing is unexplained.**
   - The story's biggest choice (to risk annihilation) gets 1,200 words of minutes in 5.2. State the reasons on the page, and have them fail or succeed on Ilse's argument.
   - A storm of 0.25 wall years (about 91 days) before a halt contradicts the Minds as "quick, never wait". Use the Key Office rule from 1.2, that the key can't be examined during a vote about it. Plant it in 1.2 as comedy, and let it pay off as the reason the quorum deadlocks.
   - Order: the bible sends the vote at 313.60 with Ilse's proof, but 5.3 writes the proof after 5.2. Put the proof before the vote, or say it goes up inside the same window. Three days between the storm's end (day 44) and the vote (day 47) also has to hold a burial.

5. **[should] Who knows what, and who is slow.**
   - Ilse counts everything. Tomé hands her the twist in 2.4 ("the last time the sky did this, we gathered") and she dates W 303 in 2.5, yet she says it only in 3.5. Either give her a reason to deny it, such as the Minds' confabulation habit applied to herself, or let her guess in 2.5 and spend part 3 testing the guess. Otherwise the reader is simply ahead of a clever narrator.
   - 1.1 introduces Ilse, Orsolya, the wall, the rival and the burn. Keep Orsolya as "the presiding Mind" until 1.2.
   - Part 5's opener names Bálint among the dead, before 5.1. Decide whether the irony is worth the spoiled turn.
