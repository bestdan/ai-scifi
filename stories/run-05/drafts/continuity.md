## Tesk's interruptions
**Device (fixed for all parts):** the interrupted paragraph stops mid-sentence with no closing punctuation. Tesk's words follow as their own paragraph in italics, led by its name in capitals: `*TESK: They won't want a place on the wall.*` Ilse's sentence then resumes in a new paragraph that starts lower-case, where it left off. Only Tesk gets this device, and only during Ilse's long dictations.

- **1.3** (the one in part 1): Ilse dictates her acceptance for the minute. Tesk breaks in at wall speed: "They won't want a place on the wall." The narration notes that breaking in is manners on the wall ("a Mind that waits for the end of yours is telling you it wasn't listening"), which sets up the 16-deep-minute joke in 2.3.

## Voice notes
- Retrospective first person, past tense, addressed to the wall as "you" ("You remember how we spent them"). Ilse writes from the deep after the events and sometimes translates wall experience into body terms she has learned since (the market, the flush). Keep that sparing.
- The register is quick and numerate, with long multi-clause sentences, dry wit, and numbers stated exactly. Part 1 makes small concessions of the form "I believed it while I said it."
- Minds: no "here". Distance is answer-time and agreement ("near because it answered me in under a millisecond"). Pauses are measured in milliseconds, and Tesk's waits get longer as it grows upset (one, two, then "a little late").
- The Minds use "here" only in the archive joke and loosely ("up here"). Ilse doesn't remark on it.
- Concrete objects per scene: the kernel (1.1), the key held still (1.2), the long red at 640 nm (1.3), Pressa's 2 mK flush and the stair's brightening (1.4).
- Em-dashes: none used. Keep the zinger closers down; part 1 runs at 5.1 per 1,000.
- Part 2 should slow the sentences only a little at first: Ilse in a body still counts everything.
