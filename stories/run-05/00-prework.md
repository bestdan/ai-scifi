## The seed, as given

Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence, combined with the idea that humans
invented language (natural, mathematical and programming) and now use AI to
represent the realities of the universe in language. Feel: Greg Egan, Liu
Cixin's Three-Body trilogy, Neal Stephenson. Set further into the future,
over a longer span of time and possibly greater distances. Substantially
longer than earlier runs.

## The bridge between the two halves

The seed's two halves meet in one fact. In AdS/CFT, a universe with gravity
and a quantum system without gravity are the *same physics in two
languages*. The dictionary between them is exact, incomplete in practice,
and sometimes exponentially expensive to use. A fact that is one short
sentence in one language can be an astronomically long one in the other.
Physical dualities are the paradigm case of two descriptions that are
exactly equivalent and still mutually unintelligible. That is also the live
question about AI representations: correct, and possibly unreadable.

Supporting facts the stories can stand on:

- **Each side is easy where the other is hard** (the strong/weak duality).
- **Space behaves like an error-correcting code.** Depth measures how widely
  information is smeared, and no patch of boundary holds anything alone.
- **Translating a black hole's interior may take exponential time** even
  though nothing is hidden (Harlow–Hayden, the Python's Lunch,
  Bouland–Fefferman–Vazirani).
- **Our universe is de Sitter, not AdS.** Its holography is open, and recent
  work suggests a closed universe's description needs an observer written
  into it.
- **Formal verification has split trust from understanding.** Machines now
  produce checked proofs that nobody has read.
- **The live dispute is whether a model found the law.** One that predicts
  orbits well may not have found Newton (Vafa et al., 2025).

## Open territory (seed stock for stage 1, not a constraint)

1. The dictionary is exact, and translation is the problem anyway: humans
   fluent in geometry, an AI fluent in boundary correlations, with both
   needed to say one sentence of physics.
2. Holographic error correction as the design of memory or culture.
3. Entanglement wedges as territory: holding a stretch of boundary decides
   what the interior can be rebuilt as.
4. A deliberately built AdS region as a library, a place where description
   is exact.
5. The Page time as a deep-time postal system: a message readable only by
   whoever kept the early radiation.
6. Language drift against physics that never changes.
7. The AI as a fluent native speaker, not an oracle.
8. Proof, program and sentence as three dialects of one description.

## Strategies for a long story

The pipeline was built for 2,500 to 7,000 words. At 30,000 or more these
parts change:

- **Outline in two levels (stage 5).** First an **arc map**: one row per
  era or part, with columns for the idea arc (what the civilization can
  say about the universe, and what it gets wrong), the character arc, the
  pressure arc (the threat, mystery or wager driving events), and the
  carrier (who or what persists). Then a scene table per part. Each part
  turns and lands on its own, as a novelette does, so the whole survives
  as a fix-up even if one part is weak.
- **A carrier across eras.** The prior-art survey's clearest lesson: the
  best-loved era-spanners have one continuous mind (Cheng Xin, Yatima, the
  Wandering Earth narrator). Candidates: a long-lived or software human, a
  persistent AI, a lineage, or the message itself.
- **Label every jump.** Era and place headers, plus an in-world document at
  each section's head (a dictionary entry, a history excerpt) that carries
  the infodump and puts each coined term on the page before it's used.
- **Bible additions (stage 4):** an era timeline with dates, distances and
  light-lag; a physics rulebook in which each in-world claim carries the
  dossier's E/S/C/F tag, so the Egan lens can audit what is invented; a
  global payoff ledger plus one per part; and a cast list per era, capped
  at about three new names each.
- **Drafting in parts (stage 6).** One drafter writes each part in turn and
  is resumed between parts so the voice holds. A running
  `drafts/continuity.md` records names, first uses of terms, the timeline,
  open threads and planted clues, and it's updated after every part. The
  orchestrator reads each part before the next starts.
- **Review in two passes (stage 7).** Per-part lens notes as each part
  lands, which are cheap and catch drift early, then a whole-draft review
  with a fresh First Reader and the comprehension gate.
- **Gate tuning from the run-03 debrief:** grade Twist on whether the motive
  carried, not on recall. Scale the lost-list threshold with length rather
  than keeping a fixed count of three.
- **Lint budgets are absolute counts.** At this length they need to become
  rates per 1,000 words, or the draft fails them on volume alone.
- **Panel:** add Liu and Stephenson lenses (new persona files). Liu covers
  cosmic sociology, deep-time strategy, scale and ruthless consequence.
  Stephenson covers systems and institutions, digression as pleasure,
  technical virtuosity and wit. Each needs a named tension with an existing
  lens: Liu against Le Guin (the human scale), Stephenson against Chiang
  (restraint).
