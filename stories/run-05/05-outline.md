# Outline: The Deep Country (working title)

- **Target length:** 35,000 words, in five parts of 6,000 to 9,000 each.
- **Shape:** five labelled parts. Ilse tells them in first person, looking
  back, "for the wall, to be read slowly". Each part opens with a header
  giving both clocks (wall year, and deep day since Ilse's arrival),
  followed by an in-world document of no more than about 150 words. The
  form enacts the idea in two ways. The register slows as Ilse does: exact,
  quick and numerate in part 1, concrete and plain by part 5. The two-clock
  headers make the reader feel the redshift. Twelve wall days pass for
  every one of hers, and a part that covers weeks for her covers years
  above.
- **Opening question:** will the people at the bottom of the pocket agree
  to be burned for the sake of a proof, and why did the wall's mood change
  nine years ago?
- **Final image:** the Minds' first weather that replies. The lamp answers
  the third gathering, and Tomé says they'll need a word for that.
- **What the ending costs:**
  - **Ilse:** her seat, her fast self, Tesk, and Bálint, whose mother's face
    she wears and who dies in the storm.
  - **The deep:** 31 dead, its isolation, and its freedom to argue about
    trivia.
  - **The Minds:** their sovereignty over treaties, and any guarantee of
    survival.

## Drafting plan

The drafter writes in parts, one turn per part, and is resumed between
parts. After each part, the drafter updates `drafts/continuity.md`: names,
the first use of each term, the deep-day count, open threads and planted
clues. The orchestrator reads each part before the next starts.
