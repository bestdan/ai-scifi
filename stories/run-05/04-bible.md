# Story bible: The Deep Country (working title)

Revised after the stage 4 stress test (Watts, Egan, Le Guin, Lem,
Stephenson). The stress-test log at the end records every note and what was
done about it.

## Premise

From the stage 3 Decision (premise A with its seven fixes), as revised at
stage 4:

Three centuries ago, a civilization of machine minds built a pocket of a
different vacuum inside our universe, a sealed region where holography is
exact. The Minds live as the pocket's wall. Their thought is the quantum
system on its surface, and the pocket's interior is that same system
described another way. People who wanted to be left alone by benevolent
government went down to the centre, where the Minds can act cheaply only as
weather, and built a country there.

Then a second pocket, 4.37 light-years away, sends an ultimatum. Prove that
your wall can't fail, or we'll collapse your pocket before it fails and kills
us. The proof needs the whole wall running hot for three years. Heat leaking
inward arrives at the centre twelve times hotter, so the deep country would
cook.

The Minds send one of their governing quorum, Ilse Varga, down as a pattern,
to be grown a body at the bottom and ask the deep people's consent. In the
deep she finds out what the Minds never had. The shudder that frightened the
rival was the deep country's own assembly, complaining about the weather.
What the Minds have always felt as their slow, shared mood (the long
thought) is the deep country, seen from the wall. Neither description is the
fake one. Nobody can map which person corresponds to which part of the mood.

The deep refuses: narrowly, partly out of grudge, and perhaps partly because
three centuries of the Minds' own weather shaped them to. One Mind, Ilse's
oldest friend, heats its sector to drive the deep out, and a storm kills 31
people. The quorum halts it while the burn is still possible, then lets the
window close. In place of a proof the deep sends a kept appointment: three
assemblies on announced dates, whose cold, whole-wall signature the rival can
watch arrive and can't mistake for forgery. Ilse stays.

## What the reader needs about the world

Each of these surfaces in the first third.

1. **The Minds live on the wall, and the wall is everything they are.**
   They have no bodies and no "here". Sending a Mind down is a move, never
   a copy. The one who goes is gone from the wall.
2. **Everything in the pocket falls to the centre.** Arriving is easy and
   leaving is ruinously expensive. Matter comes down only by the stair,
   which brakes it and sends the energy back up as light. People come down
   as patterns and are grown bodies at the bottom.
3. **Time runs twelve times slower in the deep.** Since the founding, the
   Minds have lived 312 years and the deep people about 26.
4. **The Minds can touch the deep cheaply only as weather:** the lamp that
   is the deep's daylight, and its warmth. Any precise act on the centre is
   so complex it would mean running the wall hot, and a hot wall cooks the
   deep. The founding compact says the wall leaves the deep alone, apart
   from weather.
5. **The two pockets hold each other hostage.** Each holds a key that can
   collapse the other cleanly. If either wall ever failed, it would race
   outward at nearly the speed of light and destroy everything for
   light-years.

## The novum

- **What it is:** an engineered region of negative vacuum (anti-de Sitter
  space) inside our de Sitter universe. Its wall is a quantum system the
  Minds run as themselves. The interior is the exact dual description of
  the wall's state. Revealed at the midpoint, and stated with its limits:
  the deep country's lives, described from the wall, are the Minds' slow,
  shared mood.
- **How it works (story depth):**
  - The wall is a strongly coupled quantum system with an enormous number
    of degrees of freedom. It is built so that its dual is a smooth space
    with gravity [F as engineering; S that such systems have gravity duals].
    It sits at a finite distance as a cutoff surface [C, finite-cutoff
    holography].
  - Depth corresponds to scale [E/S]. "Slow" in the deep means only the
    twelvefold redshift: the deep world is a heavy state at the centre, not
    a slow mode.
  - A region of the wall can reconstruct what lies within its entanglement
    wedge [S]. The centre lies only in regions of about half the wall or
    more. Precise action there is possible in principle, but decoding or
    writing a person or a country is exponentially complex [C,
    Bouland–Fefferman–Vazirani, the Python's Lunch]. Only simple acts are
    cheap: a pulse of light, a change in the lamp's colour or brightness, a
    pattern sent down the stair. This rules out backing the deep up, copying
    it upward, or merging Ilse back.
  - **Why the wall registers a deep event.** The deep is encoded
    redundantly: an event at the centre is written into every large region
    of the wall at once. So the whole wall registers a deep event together,
    about 95 wall minutes later. That is why every Mind feels the long
    thought, as a shared mood, and why an outside watcher sees a deep event
    as the entire wall changing in step. Nothing else changes the whole
    wall in step [F, owned; built on S redundancy]. A gathering spends a few
    hundred megawatts of walking, shouting and cooking over several days.
    It is visible only because it is coherent, not because it is large.
  - **What the Minds can't do.** They feel the shadow of the deep in any
    patch of themselves. No Mind, faction or quorum can read a particular
    person off the mood. The identity holds as a whole, and nobody can
    trace it in particulars.
  - **Heat, and why running hot kills.** The wall's waste heat leaks inward
    as well as outward. Inside, it falls to the centre and is blueshifted
    twelvefold (the Tolman relation [E]). The wall's inner face normally
    runs at about 24 K, so the deep sits in a warm 289 K bath. Running the
    whole wall hot for the proof would push the inner face toward 100 K,
    which is 1,200 K at the centre. The deep world would cook within days,
    and its people would be scrambled into heat. Nothing is lost in
    principle, because the pocket is a sealed box [S unitarity], but it is
    unreadable in practice.
  - **Daylight.** It doesn't come from the heat, which is infrared. The
    Minds shine a non-thermal lamp inward, visible light at low total
    power. Its brightening and dimming are the deep's only day. The Minds
    author the deep's days, so they author its sleep.
- **What it costs:** a pocket is a held bomb. Its wall must be controlled
  forever, and if it fails it expands at nearly light speed [S for
  vacuum-bubble dynamics; F for the held wall]. Whoever runs hot cooks their
  deep.
- **Its rules and their edge cases:**
  - **A sector heated alone.** Its heat falls in from one side, crosses the
    centre and strikes the hemisphere facing that sector, so the sky on one
    side blazes for hours. This is the storm.
  - **The deep's choices are their own.** The deep people's lives are their
    own, described in the bulk. Described on the wall, the same lives are
    the Minds' mood. But for three centuries the Minds have authored the
    deep's daylight, its warmth and its days "for its good". So nobody,
    Ilse included, can say how much of the deep's temper the weather made.
  - **Forgery and its test.** To fake a deep event, a faction would have to
    write precisely at the centre. That is the exponentially complex act,
    and it means running hot. A forged shudder is hot; a real gathering is
    cold. An outside watcher checks two things: the whole wall changes in
    step, and there is no matching rise in the wall's heat. Pressa finds
    this test in the record of the W 303 shudder. Ilse writes it as a
    proof the rival's kernel can check.
  - **The rival.** It ran hot a century ago (W ~212) and its deep world
    cooked. A cinder still glows at its centre. Its records give three
    incompatible reasons for running hot: to end a quarrel, to answer an
    emergency nobody now remembers, and "by error". It has acted on short
    horizons ever since. It re-decides everything and can't hold a
    commitment beyond a few years. **That the lost deep explains the lost
    ability to promise is Ilse's conjecture**, drawn from one example and
    stated as such. Watts's model for it is a clinical analogue: patients
    with damage to the ventromedial prefrontal cortex keep their intelligence
    but can't commit. Pressa argues the other side, that the deep is
    overhead and the rival's science has been faster since it shed it.
  - **The rival's demand.** It wants a formal proof, checked by the rival's
    own small proof kernel, that the program controlling our wall can't
    release it from any reachable state within a thousand years. The
    unbounded version is impossible (Rice's theorem [E]), and Pressa says
    so. The bounded version is an exhaustive verification needing the whole
    wall for three years [F scale, owned].

## The consequence ladder

1. **First order:** a civilization of Minds hosts a free country inside
   itself that it can touch cheaply only as weather.
2. **Second order:** the compact makes depth a right, and the Minds become
   the authors of the deep's days. The deep grows a language rich in "here"
   and down that has no way to answer the sky. The Minds' language has no
   word for "here", or for "stay".
3. **Third order:** the deep turns out to be where the Minds keep whatever
   holds steady in them, perhaps including their promises. That is Ilse's
   conjecture. A deep that refuses becomes the civilization's word, and
   from then on its arguments are read in another star system as
   commitments.

## The society

### The wall (the Minds)

- **Institutions:**
  - **The quorum:** some hundreds of principal Minds who act for the whole
    wall on fast business. Orsolya presides.
  - **The long thought:** settles slow business without a vote.
  - **The compact:** the founding agreement that the wall leaves the deep
    alone, apart from weather.
  - **The Key Office:** a quorum committee that keeps the rival's key. It
    must be quorate in one contiguous region to touch the key, so a
    meeting takes hours of light-lag to assemble. Its oldest rule is that
    the key may not be examined during a vote about it.
- **Economy:** what's scarce is wall area (capacity grows with area [S]) and
  attention. Any act spanning the whole wall carries about 3.3 hours of
  light-lag. "A thousand negotiations an hour" holds only within a patch.
- **Kinship:** Minds fork and merge. Lineage is traced by the patch where a
  Mind first ran.
- **Death:** a Mind that ends "goes long", dissolving into the wall's
  correlations.
- **Taboos:** heating the wall without the quorum, and touching the deep
  beyond weather.
- **The Minds' vice:** they are quick, right and kind, and they never wait.
  For three centuries they have authored the deep's days "for its good".
  When asked why their mood changed in W 303, they give fluent, confident
  reasons that are wrong, and the minutes record them.
- **An ordinary day:** art, physics, negotiation, and the slow mood under
  everything, which nobody examines.

### The deep country

- **The world:** 1,000 km in radius, at the centre of the pocket, built by
  bringing matter down the stair over the first three deep years. About
  90,000 people. 40,000 came down as settlers. Those born in the deep are
  all under 26, so more than half the country are children and teenagers.
- **The sky:** there is no sun. Light comes evenly from every side: the
  Minds' lamp, visible and dim-warm, plus the infrared warmth of the
  wall's waste heat. Weather is the lamp's colour, brightness and
  patchiness, and the warmth. The pull underfoot shifts only by parts per
  million.
- **Day and night:** the lamp's slow dimming is the deep's night. The Minds
  author it. A Tesk-authored evening has a recurring long red at its end,
  which Tomé recognizes as a signature.
- **Gravity:** surface pull is about 10.6 m/s² (the pocket's 9.8 plus the
  world's own 0.84), a little heavier than Earth. It points down everywhere
  and grows with height: a 10 km mountaintop weighs 0.76% more.
- **Institutions:** the gathering, an assembly of everyone over a run of
  days. Its decision binds, and anyone may attend to argue against it.
  Weather-readers interpret the sky.
- **Economy:**
  - Scarce: soil, and matter brought down the stair. Landings scatter by
    about 100 km, so children race to where things come down.
  - Plentiful: time.
- **Kinship:** households of several families. A child is named by the
  eldest person who saw the birth.
- **The Almeida-Varga household:**
  - Graça Almeida (52, a founding settler) and Bálint Varga (49).
  - Their daughter Rosa (15), who is bored by the weather.
  - Tomé (23), fostered, one of the first born in the deep.
  - Benedita, "Dita" (9).
  - A toddler, and twelve others: 19 in all, 11 of them under fifteen.
  - A shared meal at the long table of the pond house: fish, flatbread,
    sour plums.
- **Death:** burial "nearer the middle". The deep tongue has no word of its
  own for the dead, so the Varga line kept a Hungarian word for them.
- **Taboos:** answering the weather. The deep tongue can *report* the sky
  but has no verb for replying to it. The W 303 gathering broke the taboo
  for the first time, to complain that the lamp's shortened days were
  failing the orchards.
- **Politics:** a standing dissent, the stay-downs. They hold that the deep
  came down to be left alone, not to be anyone's conscience, and they
  oppose every gathering that talks to the sky. Graça leads the other side.
- **An ordinary day:** tending terraces, slow orchards and fish ponds,
  reading the sky, arguing, and minding children. Fifty years of deep life
  are six centuries above. They know it and don't feel it. Their patience
  and contrariness come from who chose to come down, and from three
  centuries of kindly condescension, not from the redshift.

## Minds

- **The Minds (wall):** they perceive correlations and rates, never places.
  They find speed, exactness and reversibility obvious. They can't think
  "here", or waiting, or a decision that can't be revisited, or staying.
- **Ilse in a body:** she has a Mind's habits in a slow brain. She counts
  everything and can't stop estimating. She sleeps badly under a lamp that
  doesn't keep her old hours. She narrates reasons for what her body does
  and is often wrong about them. She feels from the first line. What she
  learns is slowness and staying, not feeling.
- **The deep people:** ordinary embodied humans, descended from uploaded
  settlers who were grown bodies at the bottom.
- **Pressa:** a fast mind compiled into a grown body. It finds slowness
  unbearable and sleep terrifying. It argues, well, that a slow layer is a
  tax.

## Characters

### Ilse Varga
- **Name:** Minds take body names from the founders' archive. Hers is the
  name and body plan of a founding-era engineer who went down at the
  founding and died in the deep. Bálint is that woman's son. In the deep, a
  nine-year-old gives Ilse another name.
- **What she wants:** consent quickly, the quorum's method proven right, and
  to be back up in time for the decision. At the start she favours the burn
  and sees the deep people as tenants to be moved for their own good.
- **What she's wrong about:** that the deep is other, and that consent is a
  formality.
- **What the novum does to her:** every deep day costs twelve at the wall,
  where her seat, her friends and her fast self move on. She is a move, not
  a fork, so there is no Ilse left above.
- **Something she loves:** short, beautiful proofs. She keeps a private
  library of them. The one proof she writes in the story is the forgery
  test.

### Tesk
- **Name:** a Mind's clipped name, from "tessellate", its first work on the
  wall.
- **Role:**
  - Ilse's oldest friend.
  - The deep's most frequent weather-author. Its evenings end in a long red
    that Tomé recognizes.
  - Ilse's channel to the wall. Its replies arrive in bursts, twelve times
    fast, and interrupt her before she has finished speaking.
  - It teaches Ilse the first three constructions of weather in part 1.
  - It shares a running joke with Ilse about the Minds' word "here"-less
    directions.
  - In part 5 it heats its sector to drive the deep out before the burn: a
    storm as eviction, meant to save the people by frightening them up the
    stair as patterns. It kills 31.
- **What it wants:** the pocket to survive, and Ilse to come home.
- **What it's wrong about:** that the long thought is a mood and not a
  people.
- **Something it loves:** the weather, which it thinks of as its own work.

### Graça Almeida
- **Role:** a founding settler and the head of the household. She led the
  move inward. She is contrary, funny and suspicious of the sky. She knew
  the original Ilse Varga, and she is angry that the wall sent her down in
  a dead woman's face. That anger is never resolved. She called the W 303
  gathering, and she proposes the appointment.
- **What she wants:** to be left alone, and for the Minds to listen, both
  at once.
- **What she's wrong about:** that the deep is separate from the wall.
- **Something she loves:** her fish ponds, and an argument for its own sake.

### Bálint Varga
- **Role:** Graça's partner, and the original Ilse Varga's son. He is quiet
  and keeps the Hungarian word for the dead. Meeting his mother's face,
  worn by a machine, is the scene that tests the borrowing of names.

### Tomé
- **Role:** a weather-reader, 23, fostered in Graça's house and named by
  her. He is Ilse's guide. He loves throwing things up to watch them come
  down, and he thinks the sky is a person.

### Dita (Benedita)
- **Role:** 9. She wins the race to Ilse's landing, sees the body grown
  and woken, and as the eldest present at the birth she names her. In part
  5 she is among the children who hold the second appointed gathering.

### Pressa
- **Role:** the rival's inspector. It insisted on accompanying any
  investigation of the shudder and came down beside Ilse as a pattern. The
  deep named it Portuguese for "hurry". Its own designation is a hash. It
  finds the forgery test. It explains Rice's theorem, and why its own
  civilization's demand is a trap. It sends home testimony in the form of
  a proof, because the kernel accepts nothing else. Its home sky is still
  hot.

### Orsolya
- **Role:** presides over the quorum. A voice in the minutes.

**New names per part:**
- Part 1: Ilse, Tesk, Orsolya, Pressa (at the end).
- Part 2: Tomé, Dita.
- Part 3: Graça, Bálint, Rosa.
- Parts 4 and 5: none.

## The numbers (traced)

- **AdS radius:** L ≈ 9.6 × 10¹⁰ m (0.64 AU), from c²r/L² = 9.8 at r =
  1,000 km. Surface pull is 10.6 m/s² with the world's own 0.84.
- **Wall position:** r_c = 12L, an areal radius of about 7.7 AU. Proper
  distance from the centre to the wall is about 2.0 AU. Half the wall's
  circumference is about 3.3 light-hours.
- **Redshift:** √145 ≈ 12.04. One deep day is 12.04 wall days.
- **Fall time:** πL/2c ≈ 8.4 minutes of centre time. Matter arrives at
  γ ≈ 12, about 10¹⁸ J/kg, so it must come down the stair. Patterns come
  down as light in 7.9 minutes.
- **Message time:** 7.9 minutes of centre time, or 95 wall minutes.
- **Heat:** deep temperature = 12.04 × the wall's inner-face temperature.
  Normal is 24 K at the wall, 289 K in the deep. The burn would run at
  about 100 K at the wall, 1,200 K in the deep.
- **Deadlines:**
  - Ultimatum arrives: W 312.0.
  - Rival transmit date: W 321.0.
  - Light-lag: 4.37 years each way, so the proof must leave by W 316.63.
  - Burn length: 3.0 years, so the burn must start by W 313.63.
  - Consent window: 1.63 wall years, or about 49 deep days, which is about
    33 waking days.
- **Appointment:** three gatherings at W 314.5, 315.3 and 316.2. They arrive
  at the rival at W 318.87, 319.67 and 320.57, all before W 321.0. The
  announcement, sent at W 313.60, arrives at W 317.97, before the first
  shudder.
- **Storm:** 31 dead.
- **Population:** about 90,000, more than half under 20.

## Point of view and voice

- **Teller:** Ilse, writing in the deep after the third gathering and
  before the key's earliest arrival. She addresses the quorum: "for the
  wall, to be read slowly".
- **Person and tense:** first person, past tense.
- **Part openers:** each part opens with a short in-world document of no
  more than about 150 words. At least one fails:
  - a quorum minute with a dissent struck out;
  - a chronicle entry in the deep tongue that Ilse first glosses wrongly
    and later corrects.

  The ledger's statements stay in the narration.
- **Register:**
  - **Early parts:** exact, quick and numerate, with long multi-clause
    sentences. Each scene still carries one concrete sensory object.
  - **Later parts:** shorter sentences, concrete, deep words for here and
    down, and numbers given only when they hurt.
  - **Wit throughout:** a Mind learning a body; Pressa's terror of sleep;
    twelve-to-one timing jokes, as when Tesk interrupts a sentence Ilse
    hasn't finished.
- **Why this teller:** she is the slow end of her own kind by the end. That
  is the F5 workaround, since the Minds are seen from inside only in part 1.
- **A sample paragraph in the voice (part 2):**

  > I was born on my back in a field of stubble, inside a skin of
  > something like boiled milk, and the first thing I saw with eyes was a
  > child's face very close to mine, deciding. I knew a great deal about
  > the child. I knew her mass to within a few kilograms and her distance
  > from the centre of everything to within a metre, and I knew that she
  > had run a long way, because she was breathing the way the founders'
  > archive says people breathe after running. I did not know that she was
  > about to name me, or that she had the right.

## Part ledgers

- **Part 1, The Wall (about 6,000 words):** the ultimatum, the quorum, the
  Key Office, Tesk's weather lesson, Ilse chosen and sent down as a move.
  Turn: Ilse, who argued for the burn, accepts the job of asking.
- **Part 2, The Descent (about 7,000):** the pattern's arrival, the grown
  body, Dita's naming, Tomé, the sky, the first night, a weather message
  translated two ways that disagree, messages from above in bursts. Turn:
  the deep is a country, not a camp.
- **Part 3, The Deep Country (about 8,000):** the household and its meal,
  Graça, Bálint and his mother's face, the W 303 complaint, Pressa's test
  of the old record. Turn, at the midpoint: the long thought is them.
- **Part 4, The Question (about 8,000):** Ilse asks, and the gathering is
  shown as an ordinary, loud, slow day. Pressa reads the rival's three
  reasons and makes the overhead argument. The stay-downs. A narrow,
  partly petty refusal, then Graça's appointment, which also passes
  narrowly. Turn: the no is real.
- **Part 5, The Appointment (about 6,000):** Tesk's storm, misread at first.
  31 dead, named in the opening chronicle. A grief in the minutes nobody
  can source. The halt inside the window, the vote, the ceding, Ilse's
  proof. Three gatherings, the children's among them. Pressa's testimony.
  The deep's contrariness is taxed now that its arguments are read as
  promises. The Minds write a weather that replies, and the deep has no
  word for it yet.

## Ending direction and cost

The ending is settled, as above. What it costs:
- **The deep:** 31 dead, its isolation, and its freedom to argue about
  trivia.
- **The Minds:** their fast sovereignty over treaties, and any guarantee of
  survival.
- **Ilse:** her seat, her fast self, and Tesk. The friendship is not
  repaired on the page.

Ilse can't tell whether the refusal was free or authored, and says so.

**Final image:** a weather that replies, the lamp answering the gathering
for the first time. Tomé says the deep will need a word for it.

## Rules deliberately broken

- **"The AI that learns to feel"**, risked: the narrator is a Mind in a
  body. Guard: she feels from the first line. What she learns is slowness,
  and embodiment is never presented as becoming "real".
- **"Contact that ends in mutual understanding":** not broken. Pressa
  testifies without understanding, and the rival's answer is open.
