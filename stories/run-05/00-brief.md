# Brief: run-05

- **Form and length range:** novella, 30,000 to 40,000 words. The range's
  top is the drafter's hard ceiling. Drafting goes in parts (see Notes).
- **Gates:** hybrid. The human decides stage 3 (the premise) and stage 5
  (the arc map and outline). The orchestrator decides every other gate by
  proxy and logs each decision, with its reasons and runner-up, in
  `STATUS.md`. The human knows the seed and, after stage 5, the shape; the
  drafts, the turns inside each part, and the ending stay unseen until the
  finished read. Only the human lands the story.
- **Futurity floor:** F3 (see `process/rubric.md`). The story is set well
  beyond the present and spans at least several centuries and interstellar
  distances, so its later eras will likely reach F4. A card that reaches F5
  at the edges, using one of the rubric's workarounds or a new one, is
  welcome.
- **Tone:** wonder with real stakes, under strategic pressure. Decisions
  across eras carry civilizational cost. The tone charter in
  `process/style-guide.md` always applies: Liu's scale and strategy, without
  his bleakness as a default. A hard ending is fine.
- **Seed (fixed):** Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence, and holography more
  generally, combined with the idea that humans invented language (natural,
  mathematical and programming) and use AI to represent the realities of the
  universe in language. Every card keeps both halves, and the novum lives
  where they meet. Lenses may wander in what follows from it: what is
  translated, who does the translating, what it costs, and where it leads.
- **Feel:** Greg Egan (rigor carried to its end), Liu Cixin's Three-Body
  trilogy (deep-time strategy, scale, an external pressure that forces
  choices), Neal Stephenson (systems, institutions, delight in technical
  detail, wit).
- **Pressure:** a real external pressure drives the plot: a wager, a rival
  intelligence, a deadline set by physics, or something a card invents. The
  story is not only the pursuit of understanding.
- **Research:** every stage 1 ideator and every stage 4 stress-tester reads
  the three dossiers in `research/`. In-world physics claims are tagged in
  the bible with the physics dossier's E/S/C/F scale. An invented claim is
  fine if the story owns it as invention.
- **Novelty: not a repeat book.** The test is that the story as a whole
  doesn't retell a known book: its core move, its shape and its payoff
  together aren't anyone else's. A component that echoes another story is
  fine, including any of the charted variants in
  `research/prior-art-and-form.md` §A5. The Cartographer flags echoes but
  marks a card charted only when the whole card repeats a known work, not
  when one part of it does. Pure novelty isn't required.
- **Off-limits:** the simulation reveal, which the style guide already rules
  out. Neither side of a duality is "the fake one".
- **Must include:** the two halves of the seed meeting on the page, shown
  rather than lectured: at least one scene where a translation between
  descriptions is itself the action.
- **Panel changes:**
  - Two new author lenses: **Liu** (cosmic sociology, deep-time strategy,
    scale, consequence pursued ruthlessly; pulls against Le Guin) and
    **Stephenson** (systems and institutions, digression as pleasure,
    technical virtuosity, wit; pulls against Chiang). Persona files are
    written before stage 1.
  - Stage 1: the default nine plus Liu and Stephenson, 3 cards each.
  - Stage 3: add Liu.
  - Stage 4: add Stephenson.
  - Stage 5: add Liu.
  - Stage 7: add Egan, for physics rigor across the length.
- **Notes (long-form adaptations for this run):**
  - **Two-level outline.** An arc map comes first: one row per era or part,
    tracking the idea arc, the character arc, the pressure arc and the
    carrier. Under it, a scene table per part. Each part turns and lands on
    its own.
  - **A carrier.** Prefer a continuous mind across eras: a person, an AI, a
    lineage, or a message.
  - **Labelled jumps.** Era and place headers. Coined terms are put on the
    page, preferably in an in-world document, before the scene relies on
    them.
  - **Bible additions:** an era timeline (dates, distances, light-lag), a
    physics rulebook with E/S/C/F tags, a global payoff ledger plus one per
    part, and at most about three new names per era.
  - **Drafting in parts.** The same drafter is resumed for each part, and a
    running `drafts/continuity.md` is updated after each one.
  - **Review.** Lens notes on each part as it lands, then a full-draft
    review with a fresh First Reader and the comprehension gate.
  - **Gate tuning from run-03's debrief.** Twist is graded on whether the
    motive carried, not only on recall. The lost-list threshold scales
    with length (a starting point: 6 entries at this length).
  - **Lint budgets** are applied per 1,000 words for this run. The linter
    counts budgets per file today, where `contrast-shape` allows one per
    story. Scaling them per 1,000 words is a small tooling change, made on
    its own branch before stage 6.
