# Stage 4 critique: Stephenson lens (run-05, The Deep Country)

**Blind spots, stated.** I favour system detail over restraint, so I may
over-ask for machinery. I don't judge whether the ending deepens the question
(Chiang's job), clarity for a cold reader (Asimov's), or how the novella
feels at 35,000 words. Egan owns the physics tags.

**1. [must-fix] The appointment is forgeable, and that is the exploit.**
The ledger says a gathering "registers as a wall shudder visible from
outside", so a promise "can be checked by watching it arrive". But the wall
is the Minds. Any faction with half the wall can drive a long-wavelength
correlation on a schedule. A rival whose kernel is built to distrust
promises will ask the obvious question: who is to say the shudder isn't
authored? The bible never answers it. Give a physical answer the rival's
inspector can state: a real gathering's signature is slow coherence over a
run of days (about a year of wall time), which nobody can fake cheaply,
because holding that coherence is what the long thought is. Pressa should be
the one who finds the test, since she is the competent outsider.

**2. [must-fix] The timeline lets the Minds off the hook.** The burn must
start by W 313.63, and the storm falls at "W ~313.7", after the window
closed. So when the quorum "honours the refusal", the burn is already
impossible and the honour costs nothing. Either move Tesk's heating inside
the window, so the quorum has to choose while the burn is still live, or
write the quorum's vote at W 313.63 as the actual decision. A sector-only
heat can't make the proof (it needs the whole wall), so state what Tesk
thinks it achieves: perhaps storm as eviction, a weather the deep must flee
from.

**3. [should] The institution that keeps the keys is missing.** The mutual
keys hold the peace, and the bible mentions "the keepers of the rival's
key" in one clause. Who are they? How do they rotate, and what do their
rituals look like after three centuries? Give them one scene or minute:
a quorum committee that must physically be quorate to touch the key, with a
comic procedural rule (the key can't be inspected while anyone is
looking, say). It also gives Orsolya a second function.

**4. [should] Make the craft visible: authoring weather.** Tesk's job is
the story's best competence-porn and is currently one line. Show Tesk
composing a message as a sky: what is the grammar, how do you say "do not
plant the east terrace" with brightness and pull, how does a weather-reader
parse it? Part 1 can have Tesk teach Ilse the first three constructions.
Part 5 pays it off when the quorum's reversal has to be written as weather,
the deep's own reply. It is also the concrete language-half scene the brief
requires, with translation as the action.

**5. [should] Digressions need a payoff job.** Ilse's library of short proofs
is lovely and so far only characterisation. Make it load-bearing: the
rival's kernel accepts only proofs, so Pressa's "testimony" must be
formatted as one. Ilse writes the kernel-checkable form of "we will be at
these places on these dates", and a proof of a schedule is the delight
(a tiny proof the reader holds in their head). Same for Rice's theorem: one
paragraph, from Pressa explaining why the rival's own demand is a trap.

**6. [could] Time dilation as texture and comedy.** Twelve-to-one means the
quorum arrives in the deep as bursts of frantic speech, and Ilse's replies
reach the wall as slowed drawls that take Tesk a day to hear. Use it for
timing jokes (Tesk interrupting a sentence that hasn't finished), and put
the first exchange in part 2. Also watch the register shift: a voice that
loses detail in parts 1-2 risks flat infodump. Seed one concrete
sensory object per scene there (the capsule's neck pain is the model).

**Must-fix summary:** (1) answer how a forged shudder is detected;
(2) put the storm and the quorum's real choice inside the burn window.
