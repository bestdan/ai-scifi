# Stage 4 critique: Lem lens

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens prefers essay to scene, tolerates cipher characters, and leans cerebral. I did not check the physics tags (Watts and Egan will). Weigh my notes against Le Guin's on whether anyone here is a person.

1. **[must-fix] The twist is an allegory, and it flatters.** "The long thought is them, living" maps the Minds' slow consensus onto a country that is patient, contrary, argues in assemblies and keeps appointments. That is a human virtue assigned to a mood, and it's the one explanation the reader would have guessed. The bible's rule that "neither description is the fake one" is fine. But the identification is too clean, with every property of the long thought matched by a property of the deep. Suggestion: Ilse states the identity plainly, as the ledger demands, and also states its limits. She knows the match at one point (the W 303 shudder against the mood change). She can't say which deep person, argument or harvest corresponds to which part of the Minds' mood, and neither can anyone else. The fact is explicit and the mapping stays unreadable.

2. **[must-fix] The deep is made the Minds' conscience, which resolves too much.** The slowest self vetoes the fast self, Ilse learns patience, the Minds learn the rival's lesson, and the deep's refusal is wise. Where is the irony? The Minds' real vice, per the bible, is that for three centuries they steered their own long thought through "kind" weather without knowing it. That is the comic engine and it is left as a footnote. Suggestion: make the refusal partly bad. The gathering refuses for reasons that are petty or wrong (harvest grudges, Graça's contrariness), and the Minds must honour a no whose causes they find silly. Honouring it then costs something real.

3. **[must-fix] Pressa and the rival are legible humans in costume.** "Brilliant, frightened and funny", loses its nerve, can't promise. We understand every motive, and the cause (the burned deep → no commitment) is stated as fact. The rival ran hot "to end a quarrel quickly", also a human reason. Suggestion: the causal link between the lost deep and the lost ability to keep promises is Ilse's conjecture from a sample of one rival, and she says so. The rival's own records (the part 4 document) give two or three incompatible reasons for the burn, and none is confirmed. The "Why" row is satisfied by stating what happened, not why.

4. **[should] The weather is the one truly alien channel and the bible barely uses it.** Tesk "authors weather" and the deep "reads" it, yet it's treated as a lossless code. The brief's must-include is a translation that is itself the action. Suggestion: a scene in part 2 or 3 where Tomé and Ilse translate a weather message into deep speech and into wall mathematics, and the two renderings disagree. Part 5's storm is then read wrongly at first. Never resolve which reading was right.

5. **[should] The part-opening documents are too obedient.** A minute, an ultimatum, a chronicle entry, each under 150 words, all informative. A document does more when it fails. Suggestion: one opener is an item the reader sees only partly: a quorum minute with a redacted dissent, or a chronicle entry in the deep tongue that Ilse glosses wrongly and corrects later. Keep the plain statement in narration so the ledger holds.

6. **[could] The ending's gift is too tidy.** The kept appointment can be "watched arrive", which solves trust by arithmetic. The cost to the deep is the treaty voice, which is moral weight and not a joke. Suggestion: the final image stays, but add one absurd consequence. The deep, now the civilization's word, must stop arguing about trivia because every gathering is now read in another system as a promise. Their contrariness is taxed.

**Payoff-ledger check:** every row can be made explicit without explaining the alien away, provided the mapping in note 1 and the cause in note 3 stay marked as Ilse's inference.
