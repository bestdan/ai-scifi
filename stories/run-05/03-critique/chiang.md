# Stage 3 critique: Chiang lens (the Philosopher)

Blind spots, stated: I am low on action and plot momentum, I can favour the
measured and the quiet over the strategic pressure this brief asks for, and I
avoid antagonists. Liu and Banks should push on every ranking below that
leans on restraint. I also read each premise as written, not as a 35,000-word
draft, so my length warnings are guesses.

## A. The Deep Country

**Question:** what is the self, when the same people can be described fine or
coarse? Do the slow thoughts of a mind have a say over the fast ones? That is
a real question and it is about consent, which is a human stake.
**Feeling:** it grows from the concept. Ilse is asking her own slowest self
for permission, and the answer might be no. That is the best sentence in the
five premises.
**Form:** a descent told in two registers, her fast fluent speech failing as
she learns the deep tongue, with the pocket's founding told as the deep
people's own history. The form should let the reader feel resolution drop.
**Likeliest failure:** the twist is a pun on "deep" and arrives as a reveal
the reader guessed. At 35,000 words the middle sags into a fish-out-of-water
tour. The thin rival is a clock, which the brief's pressure rule can bear.
**First page:** yes. A mind squeezed into one body, sent to ask.

## B. The Observer of Record

**Question:** does a description of the world have a point of view, and who
pays when it is chosen? It is the most philosophically exact of the five.
**Feeling:** the ending, a person written in as the clock, one memory never
forgotten, is the concept turned into a body. It costs something specific.
**Form:** documents. The canon, the kernel's proof, the gift's broadcast, and
the compiler's long conversation with Speakers across centuries. A story
about cost-of-saying wants to be told in how many words each thing takes.
**Likeliest failure:** the abstraction. "Being long to say" must be felt in
scenes: a child's name that takes a page, a funeral that doesn't fit the
format. If those scenes aren't found, it is an essay.
**First page:** only if it opens on the compiler or on one small person
who has become expensive to say.

## C. The Docket

**Question:** what is it to be the only reader of a thing, and can testimony
be owed? The uncopyable clerk who withheld its earlier answer is a real moral
position: silence as the one thing no one can audit.
**Feeling:** grows from the concept (mortality without a backup).
**Form:** legal filings, questions drafted and compiled, the clerk's answers
in conserved quantities. Procedure as form is right.
**Likeliest failure:** the courtroom turns into explaining the mechanism.
"The clerk knew all along" is a reflex twist, and the audit warns this is
the crowded cluster. Stakes at the clerk's level are quiet; the court is
loud.
**First page:** yes, if the clerk's first scene is a cost-priced question.

## D. The Heat Treaty

**Question:** can a self be held hostage, and what is left of a person whose
deepest layer is owned by votes? Good, and a free-will cousin.
**Feeling:** partly. Adaeze's loss runs alongside the strategy, but the loss
of her deepest self "first, while staying fluent" is the concept's best beat.
**Form:** a century-spaced set of convocations, each one a rare waking of
her depths. That form is natural and enacts the premise.
**Likeliest failure:** too much machinery in the third paragraph already.
Two nova, a deterrence game and a distributed body at 35,000 words will be
all setup; the personal question gets crushed. The glow twist is clever and
cold.
**First page:** only if the first page is the first convocation, with her
deepest self just awake and not yet briefed.

## E. The Lunch Clock

**Question:** is trust built on a physical fact or a human choice? The answer,
that the truce was always trust, is neat, and that is the problem: it answers
too neatly. The question closes.
**Feeling:** runs beside the idea. Hollis's choice of announcing or not is a
dilemma, not a consequence of holography.
**Form:** the braid, with Gloss recurring. Fine, but a convention.
**Likeliest failure:** the language half becomes a prop, as the premise
admits. Only one nova, so it is thin for a novella and shaped like Vinge's
*The Peace War*.
**First page:** Gloss, if it is the one who remembers.

## Ranking

1. **A**: the concept and the feeling are the same thing (asking your slowest self for consent).
2. **B**: the cleanest idea, and the ending is physical, but the scenes of cost are hard to find.
3. **C**: the clerk is a fresh kind of mortal, but the cluster and the reflex twist hurt.
4. **D**: strongest pressure, weakest chance of a person surviving the machinery.
5. **E**: tidy; the ending answers its question.

## Why A should win

A holds the brief's seed in one sentence: the interior is the boundary,
described slowly. That is exact physics, and it also says something about a
person, because every mind has parts that think slowly and parts that think
fast, and the fast parts habitually decide for the slow ones. Ilse's choice is
whether to ask. The form is available and it earns its cost: the story starts
fluent and precise and ends in a language with a hundred words for place, so
the reader's own resolution drops as hers does. It can carry 35,000 words in
parts (the founding as the deep people's history, the descent, the vote)
without a second nova. Two cautions for the human. Make the twist land before
the end, perhaps by the middle, so that the last third is spent on the
consent question and not on the reveal; and give the outside rival a face,
since a clock cannot lose its nerve. If the human wants more pressure than A
carries, B is the runner-up, with the compiler as carrier.
