# Stage 3 critique: Vinge lens (run-05)

**Blind spots, stated.** I push futurity even where a story's power is human scale, I read plain prose as a virtue, and I treat characters as functions too easily. Chiang and Banks should overrule me on whether anyone is worth caring about. I did not re-open `process/rubric.md`; Futurity levels below are my reading of the F1 to F5 scale as the audit uses it.

## A. The Deep Country

**Futurity:** F4 on the page, with a real F5 move: the deep people are the Minds seen at low resolution, so the "superintelligence" is shown as weather and as a slow neighbour, never from inside. Strongest workaround of the five.
**Too familiar:** the Minds are Culture Minds with a quorum and a "principal". The compressed emissary is a Mind in a body, which is an ambassador in costume. The ultimatum from "an outside rival" is a present-day geopolitical form.
**Viewpoint trick:** holds, because Ilse is deliberately the slow end of her own kind. The risk is the reveal landing as a pun on "deep".
**One change:** make the rival the other half of the same trick. Let it be a boundary civilization that has not worked out what its own deep country is. Then the ultimatum is a mirror of the plot, and the clock gets a face (the audit's own "push past the map" for Long Wavelength).

## B. The Observer of Record

**Futurity:** F3 to F4. Four centuries of canon is long, but the people are baseline humans with elected offices, a Speaker, and a canon. The colonies "adopting" a physics reads like language adoption now.
**Too familiar:** the Speaker as elected official, the "longest conversation partner" AI, and a rival that is only documents. The giver is "a single word", which is the one genuinely strange image, and it appears as a summary rather than a scene.
**Viewpoint trick:** weak. Nobody on the page is past the horizon. The rival's mind is never met, only its physics, which is arguably correct but gives the reader nothing to be afraid of.
**One change:** put a colony two centuries in, native to the gift's frame, on the page. Its children should find human names long, and find Maren's "short" things incoherent. Make the drift visible in the people, not the table.

## C. The Docket

**Futurity:** F4. Freely forking software jurists plus one uncopyable mind is a clean split, and the clerk is the only mortal in a forking society. That is a good small unit of mind.
**Too familiar:** a court, counsel, a clerk and jurisdiction. Courtroom procedure is a present-day institution with the nouns swapped, and the audit warns of essay drift. The "AI knew all along" twist is the one most likely to read as a reflex.
**Viewpoint trick:** holds well. The clerk is the only reader, so everything the fleet says arrives priced and filtered, and the reader sees the alien only as a bill.
**One change:** let counsel be forks. A lawyer who argues in a hundred copies and loses ninety-nine to a ruling is how this society's law would actually feel, and it is the one thing no present court has.

## D. The Heat Treaty

**Futurity:** F4, with real seeds of F5: a person who is a distributed code and loses her deepest layer first while staying fluent. Fluency without depth is exactly the observable sign of a mind going past what we can audit. This is the lens's favourite idea in the batch.
**Too familiar:** two blocs, a treaty, a negotiator, and the dark-forest game. "Defecting colonies" is secession politics.
**Viewpoint trick:** holds. Adaeze speaks from the surface and cannot see the depth she is losing, so the reader and she are equally blind. The twist (a glow that came too early to be a reply) is a fair, legible sign of something unseen.
**One change:** show the AI side as a mind, not a sensor. A single scene from eleven light-years away, in boundary correlations, with the human reading as a heat map, would push it from F4 to F5 at this length.

## E. The Lunch Clock

**Futurity:** F3 to F4. A thousand-year seal is deep time, yet it is a lock and a keeper, and the answer is a trust story. The braid is two eras, nine centuries apart.
**Too familiar:** an order of keepers, a published count, an announce-or-conceal decision. It is *The Peace War*'s arc, as the audit says, and I recognise the debt as the author of that lens. The AIs "restraining themselves" is the only unknown, and it is told as a revelation, not lived.
**Viewpoint trick:** holds, but is thin. Gloss is the continuous mind, which is the right carrier.
**One change:** add a third, middle era in which the AIs choose not to rearm, seen from Gloss's side. Restraint over three hundred years is the strange thing here, and it is the one piece the premise hides.

## Ranking

1. **D (The Heat Treaty).** Fluency without depth is the only item here that shows a mind past the horizon by its effects.
2. **A (The Deep Country).** Best twist and best F5 workaround, but the Minds are Culture-familiar and the rival is thin.
3. **C (The Docket).** A good small mind, in a courtroom that is too familiar.
4. **E (The Lunch Clock).** Strongest rigor, shortest on futurity.
5. **B (The Observer of Record).** Cleverest concept, but nobody on the page is past the horizon.

**Why D should win.** The brief asks for several centuries, interstellar distances and a pressure that forces choices, and D is the only premise where all three are built into the carrier. Adaeze exists only as a spread across seven stars, so light-lag is her anatomy, not the setting. The convocations a century apart give the eras for free, and 30,000 words of "one convocation per part" is natural, where A and C compress into a single decision. The deterrence rule is stated plainly, which Liu's strategy needs. Its main risk is the one I flagged: the AI rival must become a mind on the page, and the twist has to make the rival right to be afraid. As the lens whose blind spot is human scale, I note that D is also the premise where the cost is personal (what she forgets), so my taste and Banks's should not collide. A is my close second, and I would ask Chiang to argue for it.
