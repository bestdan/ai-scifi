# Stage 3 critique: Cartographer (run-05)

**Blind spots.** I am a memory of the field, not a reader of it. I can't judge
whether a premise is enjoyable to inhabit, whether the prose will land, or
whether the physics is right beyond what the audit already checked. Attributions
below are from memory unless marked. Four web searches this session, one per
premise's core move, turned up nothing close. That has the limit the audit
already named: a search that finds nothing is not proof of absence, so
confidence stays medium. No new prior art is confirmed by search; the echoes
below are from memory.

Scoring follows the brief's rule: a premise is charted only if its core move,
shape and payoff together retell a known work. Novelty is rescored on the
premise as now written. The synthesis folded in the audit's pushes, so each
score is for the combined card, and the tone charter's "charted (tone)" mark
applies to none of them.

## A. The Deep Country
Nearest prior art: "The Circular Ruins" (Borges, 1940), confidence high, for
the reversal alone. *Schild's Ladder* (Egan, 2002) and the Zones of Thought
(Vinge, 1992) are the setting echoes. Also near: *Solaris* (Lem, 1961) for a
power learning it is the thing it studies; Culture avatars (Banks) for a Mind
compressed into a body.
Distance: real. Borges's reversal is a dream, and here it is exact physics
with a vote attached. A government has to get consent from its own coarse
self, and nothing I know of does that. The pitch has two conditions to meet.
The twist must be visible fairly early as a puzzle (Vinge's own warning), and
"neither side is the fake one" must hold when the emissary says the deep
people are the Minds seen coarsely. They are a description of the Minds that
also thinks and votes, so the text has to give them standing, or the brief's
off-limits clause reads as broken.
Revised Novelty: **5**. Status: **uncharted**. Cluster flag: sits in the
engineered-bubble cluster (5 cards), but the premise has left that cluster's
shared move (the heated-boundary deadline) mostly intact. "Hot boundary equals
black hole at the centre" is the cluster's template. The identity twist is
what makes it different, so protect that over the engineering.
Push past the map: the deep people's refusal must be a real option the story
honours. Let the slow self say no, and let the Minds survive the no at a
visible price.

## B. The Observer of Record
Nearest prior art: *Babel-17* (Delany, 1966) and Sapir-Whorf SF generally,
confidence high, for "the language you describe in changes what is cheap to
think". *Ninefox Gambit* (Lee, 2016) for a calendar as throne. *Embassytown*
(Miéville, 2011) for language as conquest.
Distance: those are about what can be thought. This is about what is cheap to
compute, with the same facts true in every frame. That is the real
difference, and the invariance-theorem idea (description length depends on the
reference machine) is mathematics, not a published plot, as far as I know. The
ending (a person written in as the fixed clock) has a faint cousin in Borges's
"Funes the Memorious" and in uploaded-witness stories, but not as a physics
fix. The whole shape (gift, drift, a fair frame at a personal price) isn't
anyone's book.
Revised Novelty: **5**, held, with the Whorf echo flagged as the component a
reader will name. Status: **uncharted**. Cluster: the two-card de Sitter
observer group, the least crowded ground in the run.
Push past the map: avoid the "ordinary elected woman holds the deciding
choice" template the audit named. Maren is a Speaker, a principal, which
helps. Make the cost something nobody else could pay, rather than a choice
only she happened to face.

## C. The Docket
Nearest prior art: *His Master's Voice* (Lem, 1968), a message that can't be
read, confidence high. *The Hercules Text* (McDevitt, 1986), confidence
medium, for a decoded message and a team. *Bleak House* (Dickens, 1853) for
the case that outlives its parties. "The Planck Dive" (Egan, 1998) for the
hole.
Distance: the decoding-cost law of evidence is new, and the uncopyable clerk
is new as a protagonist. But the premise now carries a fleet on its way and a
first-and-only alien message, which pulls it toward the alien-message-decoded
tradition. The twist ("the clerk already knows") is the audit's flagged
"founders chose it" reflex in a new coat: a hidden prior act that the
institution never asked about.
Revised Novelty: **4** (down from 5). Status: **edge-of-map**. Cluster: the
black-hole custody cluster, the biggest in the run (10 cards), with the hole
as the thing that holds the secret, close to the "hole as grave" attractor.
Push past the map: drop the arriving fleet. The decoding-cost law is the
novum. Make the court's pressure a dispute between two polities over which
cheap question to ask, and let the clerk's earlier reading be something the
court could have asked but didn't, with no hidden knowledge.

## D. The Heat Treaty
Nearest prior art: *The Dark Forest* (Liu, 2008) for the deterrence logic,
confidence high, and the charted-territory item "Fermi paradox as dark
forest". "Dark Integers" (Egan, 2007) for two sides watching each other
compute. *Ancillary Justice* (Leckie, 2013) and the Tines for the distributed
self. Shamir-style secret sharing is the real-world root of the "no faction
short of a majority" part.
Distance: the split dictionary is a good new thing, and a person as the
unstealable half of a treaty is new. The twist (both glows, nobody can say
which broke the treaty) is the dark forest's own ambiguity with a clock
attached, so it turns the charted logic over only if the story says plainly
that the rival is right to be afraid. Two nova plus a deterrence game is also
the premise most likely to read as a Liu pastiche with a person pasted in.
Revised Novelty: **4**. Status: **edge-of-map** (up a notch from the audit's
uncharted, for the dark-forest shape). Cluster: quorum-self cluster (10), the
Watts/Egan near-duplicate pair.
Push past the map: let the glow ambiguity be settled by a fact that is
cheap on both sides' notations, so the answer was visible all along and the
treaty's design is what hid it.

## E. The Lunch Clock
Nearest prior art: *The Peace War* (Vinge, 1984), confidence high, and the
audit's note stands. Also near: *Anathem* (Stephenson, 2008) for orders and
clocks; *Dr. Strangelove* (Kubrick, 1964), film, for a deterrent that only
works if known; "Dark Integers" (Egan) again.
Distance: the lock as hardness-you-trust is new. But the revealed twist ("the
rival could have rearmed and never did, so the peace was trust") is the
oldest move in the deterrence genre, and it is the stable-peace-turns-out-to-
rest-on-restraint arc that Vinge's Peace War turned over. With one nova and
one twist it also has the thinnest load of the five, and the audit's own
warning that its language half is a prop applies here.
Revised Novelty: **3** (down from 4). Status: **edge-of-map**. Cluster: none;
it is outside all five, which is its only novelty credit.
Push past the map: make the AIs' proof of the exploit the thing the human
side can learn only by opening the seal early, so keeping the secret and
keeping the peace pull apart.

## Ranking (novelty alone)
1. **A**: the identity twist is exact physics with a vote attached, and no
   neighbour does that.
2. **B**: the least crowded ground and a cost-function conquest with no
   neighbour; one point back for the Whorf echo being easy to name.
3. **D**: a person as a treaty's unstealable half is new, but dark-forest logic
   is charted and the twist depends on a reversal the page must supply.
4. **C**: fresh law of evidence, but the fleet, the alien message and "the AI
   knew all along" pull it toward charted ground and into the biggest cluster.
5. **E**: the oldest payoff in the deterrence genre; it escapes the clusters
   but not Vinge.

## Why A should win
A is the only premise where the twist is not a hidden fact but the setting
seen at another scale. Every other premise has a secret (the clerk knew, the
seal was opened, the glow came first); A has an identity, and an identity can
be shown on the page, so the reader can see it coming without being able to
dodge it. That makes it the one that survives a reader who has read the
neighbours: they will name Borges and Egan within a page and then watch the
story do something those books don't. It also keeps both halves of the seed
in the same object, with the geography as the holography and the weather
warning as the language problem. The audit's cluster warning applies, and the
mitigation is to spend the word count on the deep tongue and the consent
vote, not on the pocket's engineering. B is the runner-up, and the better
choice if the human wants the language half carried by the whole premise.
