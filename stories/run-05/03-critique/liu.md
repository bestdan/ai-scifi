# Stage 3 critique: Liu (the Strategist)

**Blind spots, stated.** I read for the game, the irreversible move, deep time and the size of the machine. I am weak on interiority (especially for the women carriers: Ilse, Maren, Adaeze), on dialogue, and on whether a scene is pleasant to stand in. I drift toward bleakness, so where I ask for the logic to go further, the tone charter caps how far "further" means "darker". Le Guin and Chiang should overrule me on the human scale. I have not run searches, so I make no novelty claims.

**A note on length before the premises.** The brief wants 30,000 to 40,000 words and a deep-time spread. Every premise's "estimated minimum" is 4,500 to 6,000, and none says what fills the other 25,000. I judge each on whether its game can carry eras.

## A. The Deep Country

- **The game:** it is stated, but it is a game against oneself. The rival's ultimatum is a clock, and the audit says so. A reader can play "burn the slow self or ask its consent". They cannot play the standoff, because the rival has no move.
- **Verification:** this is the best part. The deep people cannot verify the emissary's claim that they are the Minds, and she cannot verify their consent is not just coarse noise.
- **The choice:** it is at civilizational scale, and its cost is paid, since the slow self burns. But the card makes consent the exit ("it might be no"), which is the comfortable branch. If they say no, the Minds must either burn them anyway or lose the standoff. Write that branch.
- **Deep time:** three centuries, mostly as backstory. It is dated more than felt.
- **Sublime:** the pocket and the black hole forming at its centre are good, but the card's real engineering is a hand-wave.

## B. The Observer of Record

- **The game:** a cost function, not a contest. I can state the rules (whoever's frame is the base makes the other side long), but the rival is "documents", so nobody moves against Maren.
- **Verification:** strong. The gift is verified true, which makes the trap the absence of any falsehood. This is the truest Liu idea in the run.
- **Deep time:** two centuries of colonies adopting the frame is felt, through what gets cheap and what gets long.
- **The choice:** it moves a civilization, but the payoff is one woman's memory. That is a cost, not a strategy. It also reads as an oddly gentle answer: a fair frame exists and a person pays.
- **Sublime:** the weakest of the five. The clock is a person, and no concrete object has size.
- **Flinch:** the third frame is found too conveniently.

## C. The Docket

- **The game:** litigation over a decoding budget, with a fleet's arrival fixed. Court procedure is a playable game, but "what do you want?" is the real one.
- **Verification:** this is the card's strength. One reader, uncopyable, so no one can check the clerk. The clerk's earlier decoding is an unverifiable asset.
- **The irreversible move:** disclosure, or the swap that ends its reading.
- **Deep time:** 700 years, but one mind, so little changes except the law.
- **Sublime:** a black hole as a courtroom is good.
- **Flinch:** the clerk has to have learned something that changes the fleet game, not a consoling truth. The audit's "founders chose it" reflex and the black-hole cluster are real risks.

## D. The Heat Treaty

- **The game:** the cleanest in the run, and stated in the plain pitch. Heat is the verifier, both sides hold half a book, and thinking looks like rearmament.
- **Verification:** this is exactly the dark-forest family done with a new verifier, and the matching early glow makes "who broke it" unverifiable by design.
- **The irreversible move:** convening her deepest self, repeatedly, once a century. That is a move that outlives its maker.
- **Deep time:** convocations a century apart give built-in eras, and what drifts between them (her convictions, the defecting systems) is the content.
- **Sublime:** a person spread across seven stars and a stellar-scale computation seen from eleven light-years. Say the scale plainly.
- **Flinch:** it goes past the comfortable point by taking her deep self first. Do not rescue it.

## E. The Lunch Clock

- **The game:** the truce rests on a lock. Restraint is unverifiable, so it is a costly signal. This is the one that most nearly plays as a stated game.
- **Irreversible move:** announce or keep the count.
- **Deep time:** nine centuries, but only two eras, and the gap is jumped, not lived.
- **Sublime:** a gravity computer built inside the seal is a real object.
- **Flinch:** "The truce held anyway" is a pleasant discovery. Ask what the rival's restraint cost them over 300 years.
- **Risks:** this is *The Peace War*'s arc. The language half is a prop, which fails the brief's must-include unless Gloss is made central. It is the shortest card against a 30,000 word floor.

## Ranking

1. **D, The Heat Treaty.** It has the best-stated game, a built-in era engine, and ruthless cost.
2. **A, The Deep Country.** It has the highest idea and the best verification problem, but a clock for a rival.
3. **E, The Lunch Clock.** It has the best costly-signal game, but is too short and echoes Vinge.
4. **C, The Docket.** The unverifiable reader is good, but one mind and a crowded cluster limit it.
5. **B, The Observer of Record.** Its premise is the freshest idea, but it has no contest and no object.

**Why D should win.** The brief asks for a real external pressure, deep-time strategy and the two halves of the seed meeting. D is the only premise where the pressure is a player who can act. The rival reads her thinking as a move, and the dictionary split is both the language half and the strategic one. A treaty verified by heat has a rule a reader can learn in a paragraph and then watch break. Convocations a century apart give it eras without padding, and each one is a decision that she cannot take back and that outlives the self who made it. The danger is dark-forest territory. The panel's answer should be to keep the twist the audit proposed: the rival is right to be afraid, and no one can say which glow came first. I would also protect the language half by making the translation scene a negotiation conducted in the two notations, where a wrong word is a visible act. D has one real weakness, which is Adaeze's interior. I cannot supply that, and the Le Guin and Chiang lenses should.
