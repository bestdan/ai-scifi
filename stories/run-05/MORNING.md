## The run

- **Stages:** all ten, with three review rounds. The gates were hybrid:
  you decided the brief, the premise and the outline, and the orchestrator

- **Agents:** about 75 dispatches.

  - Ideation: 11 lenses × 3 cards.
  - The audit, five stage 3 critiques, five stage 4 stress tests and four

  - Interim checks on parts 1–3.

  - One consult at the loop cap.
  - The polish, two spot-checks, and the final gate.

  `bestdan/agent-a8e83c3f73f501565`, merged into the story branch. **Land
  that branch on `main` first.**

## What the long-form changes did

### Worked

- **Research dossiers.** The physics and language dossiers gave every
  later critic something to check claims against, and the Egan lens used
  them hard.
- **The two-level outline** (an arc map, then scene tables) plus a
  calendar section. Every date the plot hangs on was planted and checked,
  and Asimov's checks found the arithmetic consistent after each round.
- **Drafting in parts with one resumed drafter.** The voice held across
  more than 30,000 words and three revisions. A continuity file kept
  names, ages and dates straight across parts. The orchestrator read each
  part before the next, which caught two calendar slips early.
- **Interim checks on parts 1–3**, made while part 4 was being written.
  They were cheap and high-yield. They found the first-strike gap (the
  Minds hold a key too), the key's quantum nature, the founding glow's
  energy, and a dozen date fixes, all before parts 4–5 depended on them.
- **The Liu and Stephenson lenses.** Liu gave the strategic game its
  shape: the rival's decision rule stated, and why nothing beats light.
  Stephenson found the forgery exploit at stage 4.

### Didn't work, or cost a lot

- **The open-territory list in the research steered ideation.** 31 of 33
  "blind" cards took one of its eight angles as their core. Next time,
  give ideators the mental-model dossiers only, and the open-territory
  list to the Cartographer only.
- **Part 1 cost two rounds.** Round 1's compression turned it into a
  glossary, and the page-2 verdict got *worse*. Round 3's in-place rewrite
  as scenes (Chiang's fix, confirmed by a consult) is what hooked the
  readers. Lesson for long-form: the opening should enact the world, not
  define it, and the brief could require one scene on page 1.
- **The comprehension gate never passed.**
  - Twist, Ending and Who's who passed all four times.
  - Premise, Stakes and Why moved around with each fresh reader. For
    instance, a reader would get the twist right under question 4 and
    leave it out of their two-sentence summary under question 1.
  - Terms failed on real units (kelvin, nanometres, AU) and on "key".
  - The lost-list ran 11–17, mostly hard-SF mechanics the reader "took on
    trust": the proof, the key handover, the heat factor.

  It was proceeded past under the logged stop rule, as in run-03.
  - **Candidate change:** at this length and density, the lost-list
    should probably count only confusions the reader did *not* resolve.
  - **Candidate change:** Terms should exclude real-world units.
  - **Something for you to judge as you read:** whether the grader's
    partial rows match your experience.

## Gate failures to check against your read

From `STATUS.md`:
- **Premise.** Readers rarely combined "the people below are the Minds'
  slow mood" with "whether a mind may burn its slowest part to win an
  argument" in one summary.
- **Stakes.** That burning the deep would cost the Minds their long
  thought.
- **Why.** Why the rival demands a proof rather than a promise.
- **The key handover in part 5,** and the five-step proof, are where every
  reader got lost.

## Judgment calls for you

- **The ending.** The lamp's plum-coloured change stays ambiguous. Tomé
  says it was a reply, and Ilse can't tell. Readers loved it, and every one
  of them wanted to know who sent it.
- **Part 5's machinery.** The minutes, the first-strike vote and the key
  handover are where readers were most bored. They carry the strategic
  game, though, which was what you asked Three-Body for.
- **Part 1's opening.** It is now a field-frame sentence, then Tesk's
  weather lesson, as in-place scenes. The alternative was a cold open in
  the field with the wall told in flashback, which was declined twice (a
  consult agreed at 75%).
