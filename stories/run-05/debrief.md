## 3. The run's open questions

1. **The plum-coloured lamp at the end,** left open as to who sent it.

   > I would have liked a bit more definition. It did just seem throwaway
   > mysterious.

   **[story]** The final image needs more definition. As it stands it
   reads as mystery for its own sake. **[process: stage 9]** Every test
   reader asked who sent the light. The run read that as a sign the ending
   worked, but it was the same signal the human gives here.

2. **Part 5's machinery** (the minutes, the first-strike vote, the key
   handover), where test readers were most bored.

   > 3 it was good!

   The human found part 5 worth it. The "3" is recorded verbatim; it is
   not clear whether it is a score.

3. **Part 1's opening.** The alternative was a cold open in the deep, with
   the wall told in flashback.

   > Starting from the deep allows you to begin with a human senses
   > reference which would allow you to describe the environments and
   > concepts of the wall easier, so I would say yes.

   **[story]** Open in the deep. **[process: stage 7–8]** Asimov and
   Le Guin proposed this alternative in round 2, and it was declined twice:
   by the orchestrator, and by a consult at 75%. The human would have taken
   it. The human's reason is a general one: a human-senses reference point
   makes it easier to explain an alien setting. It could become a rule for
   any story whose world is far from human scale.

## 5. Process takeaways

- **[process: stage 7] The comprehension gate under-predicted this
  human.** The cold reader graded Premise, Stakes and Why partial. The
  human understood all three and found the long thought "beautiful". Terms
  failed with the reader but not with the human. Together with
  `MORNING.md`'s candidate changes (count only confusions the reader didn't
  resolve, and exclude real-world units from Terms), this argues for a
  looser gate at novella length.
- **[process: stage 7] The gate did predict the one shared snag:** the
  opening in the wall space was the cold reader's first lost-list entry and
  the human's only flow complaint.
- **[process: stage 7–8] Open far-from-human worlds in a human-senses
  frame.** Asimov and Le Guin proposed opening in the deep, with the wall
  in flashback, and the run declined it twice. The human would have taken
  it, for a reason that generalises.
- **[process: stage 7–8] A narrowed review round must still score
  Character.** Le Guin's round 2 note on the narrator ("still mostly a
  reporter") was lost when round 3 ran with only Asimov and Chiang. The
  human's main criticism is that Ilse is flat.
- **[process: stage 9] Readers asking "who sent it?" is not proof that an
  ambiguous ending works.** Every test reader asked it, and the human found
  the ending "throwaway mysterious".
- **[process: stage 7] Section 1 of the debrief under-reports a human
  reader who answers briefly.** The interview should follow a short answer
  with the orchestrator's own summary, as this debrief did, before grading
  the human against the gate.
