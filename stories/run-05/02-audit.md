# Stage 2: Audit (run-05)

The Cartographer's audit of the 33 stage 1 cards: 11 lenses, 3 cards each.

**How this audit reads the brief.** The brief's novelty rule is "not a repeat
book". A card is marked `charted` only when its core move, its shape and its
payoff together retell a known work. A component that echoes another story is
flagged under "Also near" and is not a reason to mark the card charted.
`edge-of-map` means no whole-book repeat, but a component echo strong enough
that a well-read reader will name the source, so the twist has to be visible
early. No card in this run is a repeat book, so none is marked `charted`.
Yield is judged at 35,000 words (the middle of the brief's range), for a
multi-era story driven by an external strategic pressure. Under the brief, a
deadline set by physics counts as that pressure. A card whose natural shape is
one decision in one era scores lower on Yield than a card that builds eras in.

**Novelty is scored against published work, not against the other cards.**
Ten cards share one mechanism, but that is a fact about this run and not about
the field. It goes in the Convergence section, and the orchestrator should
weigh it at the gate.

**Sourcing.** Attributions are from memory unless marked otherwise. Web
searches this session confirmed Sheffield's kernels ("Killing Vector", 1978,
collected in *The McAndrew Chronicles*, 1983). Searches for fiction built on
Hayden–Preskill, the Page time, the Python's Lunch or AdS/CFT found none. That
agrees with the dossier's §A1 finding and has the same limit: absence from
search isn't absence, so confidence is medium. A confidence of "high" below
means I'm sure the work exists, who wrote it and what it does. It doesn't mean
I'm sure it's the nearest neighbour.

**Physics checks run while scoring.** These are Rigor findings, and the
drafter can fix any of them in a line.
- A 10⁹ kg hole (a million tonnes) has a Hawking lifetime of about 2,700
  years. Its Page time, when it has lost half its entropy, comes at about 0.65
  of that lifetime, around 1,750 years. Le Guin 1 and Stephenson 3 are right.
  Liu 2's 1,450 years is close.
- **Chiang 1's "Page time of roughly 500 years" for 10⁹ kg is wrong** by a
  factor of about 3.5. A Page time of 500 years needs about 6.6 × 10⁸ kg.
- **Liu 2 says "billion-tonne"**, which is 10¹² kg and has a lifetime of
  about 2.7 trillion years. The card's own figures (10⁹ kg, 2,700 years,
  360 TW) are for a million tonnes. The 360 TW and Stephenson 3's 10¹⁴ K both
  check out for 10⁹ kg.
- Le Guin 1's "around 10³⁴ qubits of early light" is right: a 10⁹ kg hole
  carries about 2.6 × 10³⁴ nats of entropy.
- **Banks 1's "seal them for 10⁶⁰ years"** doesn't fit a small engineered
  hole. A hole that small is gone within millennia, and once the early
  radiation is destroyed the record is lost, not sealed.
- **Asimov 2's founding legend is misattributed.** The 2,875 lines on the
  quintic threefold were counted in the nineteenth century (Schubert). Mirror
  symmetry's coup, in 1991 (Candelas, de la Ossa, Green and Parkes), was the
  count of rational curves of every degree, which corrected the
  enumerative-geometry figure for degree 3. Banks 3 gets this right ("the
  mirror that counted the curves first").
- Stephenson 1's LCS35 anecdote is accurate. The puzzle was set in 1999 and
  solved in 2019 by Bernard Fabrot on an ordinary desktop over about three
  and a half years. Its account of Bouland–Fefferman–Vazirani is also
  accurate: either the dictionary is exponentially complex, or quantum
  gravity can't be simulated efficiently on a quantum computer.

## Convergence

**Bottom line.** Thirty-one of the 33 cards take one of the dossier's §A6 open
angles (1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 7) as their core. The other two (lem/3 and the
observer half of chiang/3) come from the physics dossier's de Sitter observer
section. The run's clusters follow that list almost exactly, so most of the
convergence was caused by the shared reading list. On top of it sit several
generating-model attractors that the list doesn't explain. Very little comes
from charted territory, because the territory the lenses converged on is the
territory the dossier chose for being uncharted.

### Clusters, with their cause

1. **Black-hole custody via early radiation (A6.5). 10 cards:** vinge/3,
   chiang/1, lem/1, banks/1, rajaniemi/1, le-guin/1, stephenson/3, egan/3,
   liu/2, asimov/3. Stephenson/1 is adjacent: it uses a Lunch as a seal, with
   no hole. **Cause: the reading list.** The dossier pitched this angle as the
   least charted, and the physics dossier supplied the 10⁹ kg worked example,
   so four cards use the same mass. Against published SF the cluster is still
   uncharted (I know of no work, confidence medium).

   the deep layer (A6.2, A6.3). 10 cards:** vinge/2, chiang/2, lem/2,
   watts/1, le-guin/2, stephenson/2, egan/2, liu/1, asimov/1, and
   rajaniemi/2, which applies the same code to a city's map. The
   orchestrator's tally of seven was low. **Cause: the reading list,** plus
   the physics dossier's wedge-reconstruction section. Two pairs are close
   to duplicates: watts/1 and egan/2 (a person losing her deep self first),
   and liu/1 and stephenson/2 (a charter at the centre, a bloc near the

### Attractors the reading list doesn't explain (generating model)

These are the patterns to steer the next run away from. The dossier didn't
suggest any of them.

- **The black hole as a grave.** A6.5 offers a postal system and "the record
  of a civilization". Five cards independently turn it into a place the dead
  are deposited and later read: banks/1, rajaniemi/1, le-guin/1,
  stephenson/3, asimov/3. Liu/2 deposits a living mind. Hole equals death is
  the model's association, not the brief's.
- **The founders at the centre.** Depth in a holographic code is
  coarse-graining, so the centre holds summaries. Six cards equate the
  centre with the oldest, the founding or the sacred instead: the founders'
  quarter (rajaniemi/2), the founders' records (egan/1), the Charter
  (stephenson/2, liu/1), the core self (watts/1), the founding commitments
  (egan/2). The physics doesn't require "deep" to mean "old", and none of
  these cards argues that it does.
- **The swing share held by one woman.** banks/1 ("one ordinary woman"),
  vinge/2 (Oyelaran), liu/1 (the delegate), and stephenson/2 and asimov/1 in
  variant form. The deciding vote is a person, almost always a woman, almost
  always ordinary or junior. This is a generated plot template, and five
  cards share it.
- **A decaying store as the deadline.** Jars losing error correction
  (le-guin/1), vaults losing coherence (banks/1), the keeper's memory
  failing (egan/3), the clock's memory wearing out (lem/3), the archive
  decohering (asimov/2). The brief asked for "a deadline set by physics", and
  the model answered with the same one five times.
- **The professions.** Nearly every protagonist is a translator, keeper,
  clerk, archivist, auditor or surveyor, and the run has three surveyors
  (vinge/1, stephenson/2, the mittari of rajaniemi/2). Nobody is a
  politician, soldier, engineer of the thing itself, merchant (except
  rajaniemi/3), or child. The "translation scene" the brief requires partly
  explains the translators. It doesn't explain why the protagonist is always
  the functionary and never the principal.
- **The naming register.** "Teodor" appears twice in lenses working blind

  names lean Nordic (Aske, Sunniva Holt) where nothing in the cards' worlds

  tell too: seven star systems twice (chiang/2, watts/1), 11 light-years
  twice (chiang/1, liu/3), 30 light-years three times (lem/1, liu/2, egan/3),
  40 light-years twice (chiang/3, egan/1).
- **"The founders chose it."** Asimov/3 (the founders chose the mass and so
  the date), banks/3 (her mathematics was chosen for her people), vinge/3
