# Story bible: run-08 (working title: Forty)

## Premise
A, The Prior, as decided in `03-synthesis.md`: the service issues its
field agents a near-certain belief that the target world is in crisis. The
agent lands among a bird civilization that hides anything valuable the
instant it is watched. The one observation improbable enough to swamp her
belief is a cache a bird chooses to show her, and a cache an outsider has
seen is spent. Turn: the birds have worked out that the belief is a
separate watcher inside her, and they have been hiding from it, not from
her.

## What the reader needs about the world
The outline places all five in the first third.

1. **The birds hide what they value whenever they are watched,** and move
   it later. Anything an outsider has seen hidden is *spent*: abandoned,
   because a watcher can rob it in thought. Surface life is kept scruffy on
   purpose, because the surface is where you are watched.
2. **Showing a cache to a trusted one is the deepest intimacy the birds
   have.** A trusted one's seeing doesn't spend anything. Trust is made by
   giving and taking things without looking: a thing pressed into your
   hand from behind, accepted unseen.
3. **Under the scruff, the birds are an advanced people.** Their deep
   workshops, furnaces and one particle accelerator exist for their art,
   the table: every element held. The stable ones are held as pure
   samples; the heavy ones, which last seconds or less, are held only as
   atoms made live in the accelerator and seen by a trusted one.
   Everything else they make (medicine, glass, power) is a by-product of
   the art.
4. **The agent carries an issued belief she can read as a number.** It is
   the base-10 log of the odds that this world is in crisis. It started at
   40, and everything she sees and hears is logged and sent home.
5. **The Concern fixes worlds.** It decides by an assessment window: if an
   agent's number is above zero at the end of 120 days, Remediation comes.

## The novum
- **What it is:** two nova that lock together. (1) The Concern installs a
  prior of near-certainty in its agents, because agents who doubt don't
  act. (2) A bird people whose deepest social sense is being watched, who
  hide from sight and share by touch.
- **How it works:**
  - The prior is an implant, the *installation*. It updates only on
    logged evidence, which means what her eyes and ears record, and it
    colours her judgement: while it is high, she can't feel doubt about
    the crisis, though she can know facts that contradict it. She can read
    her number in the corner of her sight.
  - A world that is fine but good at public relations, and a world in
    crisis that is good at public relations, produce nearly the same
    evidence, so ordinary evidence barely moves the number (see the
    Arithmetic row).
  - The birds' hiding is real evolutionary behaviour pushed to a culture.
    Earth's western scrub-jays re-cache food when a rival watched them
    bury it, and only jays that have stolen do it. These birds' ancestors
    stole everything from each other for forty million years.
- **What it costs:** the agent's ability to doubt; the birds' ability to
  be surveyed, so every survey has logged them as primitive and in
  distress.
- **Its rules and their edge cases:**
  - **Why near-certainty, and not just orders:** the Hessary Inquiry (see
    the world's shape). Orders can be argued with; a belief can't. Reader
    learns this in scene 2, when Lusk explains her number to Cistern.
  - **Why not exactly 1:** a belief at exactly 1 can never move, so an
    agent could never judge a world fixed and come home. The actuaries set
    it as high as possible short of that. Scene 2.
  - **Why she can't edit it out:** she can. Every edit is logged and read
    at home, and the Hessary rules void the testimony of any agent who
    lowered her own prior: her report is discarded and a fresh agent with a
    fresh prior is sent. Editing frees her and does nothing for the world.
    Scene 4, when she is tempted.
  - **Why the installation only counts what she sees and hears:** the
    Hessary Inquiry ruled that nothing goes into a judgement that can't be
    audited, and only sight and sound are recorded. Touch isn't logged.
    So what she learns with her hands she knows, but the number doesn't
    move. Scene 3, when the first thing is pressed into her hand.
  - **Why 120 days:** after Hessary waited thirty-one years for an
    open-minded assessor, the Concern set a hard window. Scene 2.
  - **Why a seen cache is spent:** a watcher knows where it is and what
    is in it, so it is no longer only yours; the birds treat knowledge as
    half of possession. Scene 1 shows it (a bird abandons a cache Lusk
    saw), scene 2 explains it.
  - **Why a trusted one's sight doesn't spend:** what a trusted one knows
    counts as yours. Scene 3 states it; scene 7 turns on it.
  - **Why the birds can hide a whole civilization from orbit:** they can
    see the glint of a survey probe in ultraviolet, and a probe overhead
    sends the whole surface into a hiding frenzy. Their real works are
    underground already, where caches always were. The frenzy is what the
    surveys logged as famine panic. Scene 2.
  - **Edge case: the proof spends the cache.** The installation is an
    outsider that reports home. If it sees a cache, the cache is spent,
    whatever Lusk is. This is the ending's price.

## The world's shape
- **Where power sits, and what it covers:** the Concern's Actuarial
  Office sets every agent's prior and every assessment window. Field
  officers carry the priors and do the fixing. The Office covers only
  worlds a survey has flagged; it does not assess worlds itself, ever, and
  it reads only logs.
- **How it got that way:** the Concern began as a plain aid service whose
  assessors arrived open-minded. On Hessary, a world in real famine, the
  assessor spent thirty-one years gathering evidence, being careful, and
  the famine killed two billion people while she did. The Hessary Inquiry
  concluded that doubt kills, and gave the Concern its three rules:
  issued near-certainty, the 120-day window, and audit-only evidence.
- **Where the reader meets that history:** scene 2. Lusk trained on the
  Hessary footage, as every officer does; she tells Cistern about it, and
  the telling is the first time she sounds like a person rather than a
  procedure.
- **Why people handed power over:** not applicable.

## The consequence ladder
1. **First order:** Lusk keeps fixing small things, a well, a wheel, a
   bird's splinted wing that was healing fine. The birds are politely
   furious, and everything she looks at vanishes.
2. **Second order:** the harder the birds try to prove they're fine, the
   less it counts. She can see herself reasoning like a conspiracy
   theorist and can't stop.
3. **Third order:** the only way out is a single observation so
   improbable under "crisis" that it swamps forty orders of magnitude, and
   on this world, the only such observation is a cache, and seeing it
   spends it. If she fails, Remediation lands ten thousand staff with
   cameras, and every cache on the planet is spent by spring.

## The society
- **Institutions:** flocks, named for the districts their caches lie
  under. A guild of assayers, who purify and keep the elements. Flock
  moots, where a matter is argued with everyone facing outward, so no one
  is watched while they speak.
- **Economy:** scarce: pure elements, trust, unwatched space. Worthless:
  anything visible on the surface, which is why the surface is a mess of
  straw, rubble and broken pots.
- **Kinship, and who raises the children:** pairs raise chicks, with the
  flock's help. A parent feeds a chick from behind; a chick's first lesson
  is to take what it's given without turning round. A fledgling is
  trusted with its first shown cache at about two years old.
- **Death:** a dying bird shows its caches to its trusted ones, who
  inherit by knowing. A bird who dies with no trusted ones takes its
  caches with it, and that is the worst death they know.
- **Taboos:** looking at someone while they dig. Looking at the ground
  where someone has dug.
- **An ordinary day:** forage and argue on the surface; work underground,
  in workshops lit by glass the furnaces made; gossip; hide things; move
  them.

## Minds
- **The birds:** they see in ultraviolet, track gaze direction as the
  first and loudest fact about anyone nearby, and remember thousands of
  cache sites. To them it is obvious that knowledge is half of possession
  and that being looked at is being taken from. They can't easily think
  that a look could be neutral. They reason about Lusk's gaze constantly
  on the page: where she is looking, what that look will cost them, what
  she thinks they are, and, after scene 2, what the *installation* will
  make of what she sees. Cistern knows when Lusk is trying not to look,
  and plays it.
- **Lusk with the installation:** she can know a fact and still be
  certain of the crisis; the installation makes doubt about the crisis
  feel like a symptom. She reasons well about everything except the one
  thing she is certain of, and watches herself do it.

## Characters
- **Perpetua Lusk** (a Concern name; the Concern's officers come from
  many worlds and take ordinary names): Concern field officer, on her
  third mission.
  - **What she wants:** promotion to the Actuarial Office, to set the
    priors instead of carrying them. She thinks the people who set
    certainty are the ones who are free of it.
  - **Her wound:** on her first mission, on the world Gennet, she built a
    desalination grid, and they thanked her. She has never been able to
    know whether they needed it, because she couldn't doubt it at the
    time and the log is all she has.
  - **What she's wrong about:** that the Office is free of certainty. It
    is the place certainty is manufactured.
  - **What the novum does to her:** she knows the world is fine with her
    hands and is certain it is dying in her head.
  - **Something she loves outside the plot:** jigsaw puzzles. She brought
    a 2,000-piece one (a harbour at dusk), and she does it face up in the
    open, which the birds find obscene.
  - **Where she changes her mind about something personal:** scene 8. She
    no longer wants the Office. Her report says the issued prior was
    wrong, plainly, knowing it ends that promotion.
- **Under-the-Cistern, "Cistern"** (bird names come from the place of
  your first cache, given at fledging): the assayer her flock assigned
  to "deal with it". Old for a bird, grey at the throat.
  - **What she wants:** to finish her table. She has every element
    through 117 in pure form; 118 to 120 last milliseconds and count only
    if a trusted one sees them made. Her trusted one, her mate, died nine
    years ago, and she has had no witness since.
  - **What she's wrong about:** that Lusk is a thief. Then, later, she is
    right that the installation is one.
  - **What the novum does to her:** her life's work can save her world
    only by being spent.
  - **Something she loves outside the plot:** gossip about the other
    flocks, delivered facing away from the listener, which is polite.
- **Seven-Holes:** the flock's eldest. Argues at the moot that a cache
  spent on an outsider is a theft from every bird. Speaks Reach.
- **The Desk:** the Concern's dispatcher, a voice with a six-hour lag.
  Two or three messages, no more.

## Point of view and voice
- **Teller:** Lusk.
- **Person and tense:** first person, past tense, told from a few years
  after.
- **Register:** funny and wry, deadpan and precise; the comedy is in the
  mechanism (a certain woman among birds who hide from her), not in
  adjectives about her. Murderbot is the register reference only. No
  borrowing of premise or prose.
- **Form:** each section is headed with the day of the window and her
  number, e.g. "Day 1. 40.0000". The number barely moves until the last
  section, which is the joke and then the payoff.
- **Why this teller:** only she can show certainty from the inside while
  her hands learn otherwise.
- **A sample paragraph in the voice:** The first thing I fixed on the
  planet was a wheel. It belonged to a cart in a yard full of straw and
  broken pots, and it wobbled, and I am qualified in wheels. I trued it
  in twenty minutes. When I looked up, the cart was gone, the straw was
  gone, and a grey bird the size of a heron was standing where the cart
  had been, facing the other way, very deliberately not watching me. My
  number said 40.0000. It always says 40.0000. I took this as evidence of
  a society too traumatised to own a cart.

## Stage 4 amendments (binding)

These supersede anything above that they contradict.

- **The table, made physical.** Elements 1 to 99 are held as pure
  samples. From 100 up, a heavy element is held only as atoms made live in
  the birds' one accelerator and seen by a trusted one, at the detector,
  as it happens; nothing records it, because a record would be a cache.
  Cistern holds 1 to 117 this way. She has made 118, 119 and 120, alone,
  and they don't count. Her mate, her trusted one, died nine years ago.
- **Cistern's reason is the bird rule, not only grief.** A bird with no
  trusted one takes her caches with her when she dies. Cistern is old
  and has begun to forget sites. The installation's log would be the only
  witness that outlives her: that is part of why she lets it see.
- **Names.** At fledging every chick makes one decoy cache in public,
  which the whole flock is meant to see, and is named for it. It is the
  only cache a bird ever shows everyone. Under-the-Cistern's was under
  the old cistern. Seven-Holes dug seven holes as a fledgling and filled
  only one, which the flock still tells as a joke.
- **The fledgling.** Under-the-Steps, about two years old, newly named.
  Fascinated by the jigsaw, and the first to press something into Lusk's
  hand from behind (a warm pebble), on day nine. What a bird this age
  can't do yet: not look. Under-the-Steps keeps peeking, gets scolded for
  it, and runs the eyes-shut tests on Lusk because she has been told to
  and thinks it is a game.
- **How the birds found the installation.** In scenes 3 and 4 the
  birds test it: Under-the-Steps asks Lusk to shut her eyes and then
  open them, over and over, and watches the faint ultraviolet glint at
  her left eye come and go. Then they hand her things only when it is
  dark. Their model is partly wrong: they think it is an eye, and it
  also hears. In scene 7 Cistern whispers an element's name and the
  number moves a hair (39.9968 to 39.9961), which is how they learn it
  hears. Lusk warns her to say nothing after that.
- **Body plan.** Zygodactyl feet, two toes forward and two back, grip
  tools and hold work; the beak does fine work, wire, glass, the
  accelerator's coils, wound by beak over generations. Furnaces run on
  bellows worked by many birds at once, a social job they sing through.
  Heat is vented into deep cold aquifers, so nothing shows from orbit.
  Shown in scene 7.
- **The birds' mistake.** They read Lusk's face-up jigsaw as a hoard
  displayed to everyone, which to them is either madness or a desperate
  bid for a trusted one. Under-the-Steps steals one piece to make it
  "safe", so it can't be spent. The harbour has a hole in it for good, a
  small echo of Cistern's unfinished table. The cost to the birds: the
  flock decides Lusk is a trusted-one-seeker, which is wrong at the time,
  and gives Cistern a reason to start.
- **Spent caches carried to the end.** By day 118 Lusk has spent
  dozens of caches by looking. The ending names that they are not
  forgiven; Seven-Holes never forgives the one under her nest.
- **The moot votes.** Scene 6 shows the vote on whether Cistern may
  spend her table on the installation. Seven-Holes argues that an
  outsider who sees one cache learns how caches are made, so every cache
  on the world is weaker for it. The vote carries; Seven-Holes dissents
  and is not reconciled.
- **Hessary gets its own beat,** in scene 3, and the audit rule is shown
  there when the Desk refuses her plain-language report. Scene 2 keeps
  only the number, why not 1, the arithmetic and the window.
- **An earlier personal turn.** Scene 4: tempted to edit, Lusk thinks of
  Gennet and admits to Cistern that she doesn't know whether Gennet needed
  her grid, and that she would like to know. It is the first doubt she
  has had that isn't about this world, so the installation can't touch
  it.
- **The ending's certainty.** At −5 she is not uncertain: she is as sure
  as before, the other way, on the word of the same model that called
  the birds primitive. She notices. The joke and the residue are the
  same thing.
- **The Office closes the trick.** The coda's Desk message says the
  Office is reviewing every issued prior and adding touch to the logs.
  The next agent will not be teachable by hand. Lusk boxed Cistern's gift
  before her refit and has never opened it.
- **Scene 7 is touch only; scene 8 is the one sighted spend.** Cistern
  takes Lusk through the table eyes shut (scene 7). Element 120 is made
  live with Lusk's eyes open (scene 8).
