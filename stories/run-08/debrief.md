## 3. Against the gate

- **The gate under-predicted the human.** The cold reader on final.md was partial on Premise, Stakes, Twist, Ending and Reach; the human picked all five correctly. Per the stage 10 rule, that is the reader recalling less, not the story failing: each of these is carried in a scene (the moot for Stakes, Day 117 for Twist, Day 118 for Ending, the coda for Reach). The two persistent lost-list items (the opening number; completed-and-spent) did not trip the human. This matches run-05 and run-06: the reader's partials on recall-heavy rows were rows the human picked easily.
- **No row the reader passed did the human miss**, except Who's who on Harriet Baines, who came back once prompted: a one-scene coda character, recall, not text.
- **Terms, the Desk:** missed by both the human ("No idea") and, in part, the reader ("person or system, I couldn't tell"). The page never says what the Desk is. A real text gap, small.
- **Tone:** the brief asked for funny and wry. The human: "The tone didn't come across as funny or wry." No lens or reader flagged this. The gate has no tone row, so the pipeline had no way to catch it.
- **Prose friction:** "a few turns of phrase, very small things" made it hard to understand, though nothing was missed. Matches the panel's lowest Craft axis (Prose 2–3) and the reader's dense first page.

## 4. Process takeaways

- **Check tone early, not at the end.** The human, on the missing funny/wry tone: "It turns out, but early enough on to catch issues rather than at the end when it's already written." So a tone check belongs before the full draft is built on, e.g. on the first sections of draft 1, against the brief's tone, while the voice can still change.
- **The gate under-predicts a human reader on recall-heavy rows** (third run in a row). Consider weighting the cold reader's partials less when the row is dramatised in a scene.
