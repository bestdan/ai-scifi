# Stage 3 critique: Chiang lens (run-08)

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens is low on action, comic momentum and
antagonists, and it can prefer a measured, polished story over the funny one.
The brief asks for funny and wry. I can judge whether a joke comes out of the
idea. I can't judge whether it lands, so defer to Banks on that.

## A. The Prior

**Question.** What does evidence mean when your credence was issued to you,
and what does it cost to hand someone proof? The second half is the better
question. Proof that is spent by being seen is a real epistemology, and it
matches how testimony works in a civilization that hides.

**Feeling.** It grows from the concept. The bird's gift is the Bayesian update,
so rigor and intimacy are one act. That is the best thing in the run.

**Form.** The agent can read her own log-odds, so the log-odds can be the
structure: a running figure at each section head, barely moving, then one jump.
The form then enacts four thousand years of updating, and it makes the
arithmetic light.

**Likeliest failure.** Two nova plus the turn. "The cache shows her something
about the service" is the extra load and the one scene to cut. The idea is
complete without it.

**First page.** Yes. A competent person forbidden to look at anything is funny
at once, and the reader wants to see her fail.

**Other mind.** The birds seem to think. They reason about her gaze and know
when she is trying not to look. Keep that on the page as dialogue and
manoeuvre, not as the agent's inference.

## B. The Ending Technician

**Question.** Is a life its remembered shape? Does the remembering self
deserve care, or is flattering it a kind of theft from the living one? That is
a real question, and the town's answer (a second person you don't flatter)
is a strong, unexpected position.

**Feeling.** It grows from the concept. The warm ending is a joy and a risk
in the same gesture.

**Form.** The idea asks for a shift told minute by minute, counting down to the
hatch. Ending-shaped structure for a story about endings. Anything looser
wastes it.

**Likeliest failure.** The sentimental landing. The retiring woman is the
first warm ending in thirty years, so the story is pushed to make it work.
Make the ending cost something the town can measure, and don't settle whether
it was right.

**First page.** Yes, through the technician's trade and the cold room.

**Weakness.** The seed asks for a service fixing a fine world, and the
service sits offstage here. The fit to the seed is the loosest of the five.

## C. The Signatory

**Question.** Can a mind that can always revise itself promise anything? What
is a commitment? The push, being permanently committed to changing your mind,
is the best paradox in the set.

**Feeling.** It would grow from the concept. The agent's dread of the kiln is
the idea.

**Form.** The charter's own clause could be the frame, with the signature as
the last line. Nothing else suggests itself.

**Likeliest failure.** Three ideas (fork-and-rollback, the firing, the
slowing) at 7,500 words. The slowing is the one to drop. Without it, the
story is the charter and the kiln-master, which is enough. Dropping it also
steps off the "agent made smaller" attractor.

**First page.** Weak. The reader meets a vast civilization before a person.

**Other mind.** The agent reasons, but at a million-fold speed it will read as
narration of a mind unless the kiln-master visibly out-argues it.

## D. The Fee

**Question.** What do we owe the people who raised us, and can a debt be paid
if the debtor has nothing left to be paid in? The fee as help is a clean
concept.

**Feeling.** It grows from the concept. The mother forging the need is the
whole idea in one act: love as a counterfeit currency.

**Form.** A ledger or fee schedule between scenes, each entry a thing that was
once broken and fixed. The form carries the world's history in one line each.

**Likeliest failure.** The tender family story swallows the comic register,
and the agent's decision to accept a visible forgery gets answered too neatly.
Let her accept it and let it cost her the post.

**First page.** Yes. A woman coming home to a dumpling stall carries it.

**Weakness.** It is the obvious card with a push. The idea is strong and the
premise is charted.

## E. North for Lunch

**Question.** How does persuasion differ from truth when the persuader can no
longer tell? That is a real question, and the best form in the run follows
from it: the three-hundred-page case, shown shrinking in sections, from
argument to "you'd like it".

**Feeling.** It grows from the concept, and the funniest beat is the saddest:
the agent can't tell whether it still believes.

**Likeliest failure.** The stake is soft and the agent is made smaller, which
the audit names as an attractor. The ending also has to say whether the gift
is good, or it is the unearned open question.

**First page.** Yes, if the case is shown first at full strength.

**Other mind.** The gift is a mind it cannot argue for. Show the gift reasoning
on the page, even briefly, or the story argues over an object.

## Ranking

1. **A, The Prior.** The idea and the feeling are one act, and the form is
   obvious.
2. **B, The Ending Technician.** The most exact fit of form to concept, and the
   cheapest, but it fits the seed least.
3. **D, The Fee.** A strong concept, undercut by the charted obvious card.
4. **C, The Signatory.** The best paradox, and too heavy for the length.
5. **E, North for Lunch.** The best form, with a soft stake and the diminished-agent
   attractor.

**Why A should win.** The question it asks is real and unsolved: what a
reasoner should do when her own priors are installed and the evidence is
withheld by design. The birds' hiding is not a obstacle added to the plot. It
is the world's answer to being watched, and the agent's prior is the service's
answer to being doubted, so each side's habit is the other's problem. The
decisive proof must be given, so the climax grows out of the premise instead of
running beside it, and what the agent understands at the end (that
her certainty was always someone else's gift, and a witness is the thing
you can't get without spending it) costs her something concrete: either her
testimony or the bird's cache. The risks are manageable. Cut the "cache shows
her the service" turn, since it is a third idea the story doesn't need. Let the
log-odds be the form, keep the birds reasoning on the page, and the
estimated 6,000 words holds. B is the safer story and a worse fit to the
seed, so if A's arithmetic proves heavy, B is the runner-up.
