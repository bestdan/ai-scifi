## The one thing
Fix the glint. The twist is that the birds hide from the lens, not from Lusk. The page proves it with a scene where the number moves while her eyes are shut. Day 117: "Behind my closed eyelids, the green digits... flickered from 39.9968 to 39.9961." Then Cistern says "It shines when it looks... It doesn't shine when you listen, and nothing we ever told you moved your number." The number just moved on what she heard. So the scene contradicts the line meant to explain it, and the first reader said so: "I'm still not sure why it moved." Two separate rules are tangled. One says the installation weighs testimony by how hard it is to fake. The other says the installation sees only through open eyes. Let the whisper be a measurement the model can score (hearing is logged, as stated), and let Cistern's line be about her own mistake: she thought *only claims* were safe to speak, and a measurement is not a claim. Drop "It doesn't shine when you listen." The glint then explains only the hiding, and the number explains only the whisper.

## Notes

1. **Day 4 and Day 114 (structural):** Hessary and the "ten thousand staff, all of them with cameras" line are still stated twice, and the first reader was bored at both. The moot's gold-ingot exchange ("two hundred ingots of pure gold") is the unnecessary scene. Cut it. Keep Seven-Holes and the vote.
2. **Day 118, the relief (structural):** The three-step beat toward the stairs is the right fix. But the paragraph that follows ("The same unaudited model that had watched two hundred birds scatter chaff... handing me a fresh certainty") explains the irony the line before it had just landed. Cut it. The scene needs "exactly as sure as I had been, the other way" and nothing more.
3. **Prose (line):** Draft 2 is wordier than draft 1. Examples: "Frantically it hooked the wire and beads back out of the ruined hiding place with its rear claws, bolted ten paces behind the rain-barrel, pried up a cobblestone with its beak, and shoved its hoard into the dark earth underneath." Another: "the smell of wet clay gave way to a warm, dry draft of ozone, hot beeswax insulation, and deep limestone water." The sense-triplet rule is back in force. The Day 1 opening works because it is short and exact. Return Day 117 and 118 to that scale.
4. **Day 4 (line):** Cistern and Lusk both deliver the premise as speeches. The "four thousand years" paragraph is 200 words in one mouth. Break it with Cistern dodging.
5. **The coda (structural):** The birds' fate is now answered ("struck from the crisis list... the probes stopped coming"), and the first reader still ended on "did she damage it?" That is the right question. Don't answer it further. The Gennet closing keeps it open. Leave it.
6. **Lusk's want (structural):** Mercer's Ring is named twice and then spent at the end in one clause ("knowing that indicting the system ended my promotion"). Give it one earlier moment of cost, perhaps when she opens the override menu on Day 37.

## Blind spot disclosure
I weigh the idea over pace, so I may be too hard on the moot as scenery. I hold the physics to internal logic only. I am not the lens for the bird comedy. Watts should test the hearing rule in note 1 of "The one thing", and Banks should judge whether the prose swell is texture or fat.
