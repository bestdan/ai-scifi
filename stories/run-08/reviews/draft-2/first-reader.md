# First Reader report: "Everything You Look At" (draft 2)

## Where I got lost

1. **Opening paragraph, the number.** "My number said 40.0000... how sure I am that this world is dying." I had no idea what the number was or why a wobbly wheel led to it. I got the idea over the next two pages, and by the Day 4 explanation I had it. For the first page I was reading without knowing what was at stake.

2. **The cart vanishing / the bird "not watching me" (paragraphs 1-3).** I didn't know why things kept disappearing when she looked. I worked it out around Cistern's speech about "spent" caches, which I had to take slowly. It cleared up, but it took a long while.

3. **Cistern's "spent" speech (the paragraph beginning "It is in the dirt").** "The other half is inside your head... moving it while you watch only spends the new hole too." I followed the gist and lost the details. I couldn't have said exactly why the second hole is spent.

4. **Day 4, "Our real works are a hundred metres down... the table: every element held, while everything else we make... is a by-product of the art."** I didn't know what "the table" was until I made the connection to the periodic table much later, at the bench in Day 117. For a long stretch I thought it was a literal table or a guild ranking. The accelerator ring dropped in with no warning, and I stopped and went "wait, what?"

5. **Day 4, the number's mechanics.** The big speech ("base-ten log of the odds... four thousand years") lost me in the middle. I got "testimony barely moves it, so I'm stuck at forty." I didn't follow why they'd set it so high on purpose, and I took that on trust.

6. **Day 9, who is talking to the Desk.** I was unsure whether the Desk was a person or a machine. I decided it was a bureaucratic office. The answer was "Narrative assessments are not evidence," and that cleared it up.

7. **Day 37, californium and the short-lived elements.** Cistern's long speech ("astatine... francium... everything heavier than einsteinium") went past me. I understood that some things can't be kept in a bottle and that only a witness's memory holds them. I did not follow the specifics, and I didn't need them. I did understand "I am forgetting my corners."

8. **Day 114, the vote and the "one in ten to the forty-fifth" argument.** I got that making element 120 would move her number by a huge amount. The arithmetic about "thirty-seven" and "three orders of magnitude" for gold I skimmed.

9. **Day 117, the ultraviolet star.** This was the biggest lost-then-found moment. When the number flickered as Cistern whispered, I didn't see why it moved when "talk" had never moved it. Then the explanation arrived: the lens glows when her eyes are open, and the birds had been hiding from it, not from her. I worked it out from that passage and the "Shut. Open. Shut." game on the salt-barrel, which I now understood was a test. I'd had no idea what the game was for when I read it on Day 9. (Her whisper came when her eyes were closed, so I was confused about why the number would move at all. I stayed a little confused there, and I'm still not sure why it moved.)

10. **Day 118, the minus five.** I stumbled over the arithmetic ("subtracted the forty-five points... from 39.9961") because the number comes out at -5.0039, and I had to stop and work out that 45 off 39.9961 is -5.0039. I got there.

11. **The ending, the steel box and the fingertips.** I was a bit lost on why she would weld the gift shut by touch with her eyes closed, then why the sensors mattered. I worked it out: the box stays unlooked-at and untouched so the machine can't log it.

## Where I got bored

- **Day 4, the long speech about the number** (the "At this rate I'd stop being sure in about four thousand years" paragraph) and the Hessary Inquiry paragraph right after it. I knew all this was setup, and I was waiting for something to happen to Perpetua or the bird. It's a lot of explanation in one stretch.
- **Day 9, the first half.** The anchovies, the root cellar, the jar threads, the six-hour wait for the signal. I was waiting for the fledgling to arrive.
- **Day 114, the moot.** The gold, the orders-of-magnitude explanation, and the vote. It's an assembly scene and it read like one. I was waiting for the descent into the workshop.
- **The long eleven-hour wait is handled quickly, which I liked.** I wasn't bored there.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The grey bird facing the other way, "very deliberately not watching me."** First paragraph. That made me smile and I wanted more of it.
- **The brown bird with the hoard, being seen, and walking away "leaving the wire and beads in the mud as ruined beyond saving."** Funny and sad together.
- **"Somebody has to remember where I put things."** Cistern, Day 4. This was the first time I cared about her.
- **The pebble pressed into her palm from behind, with the rear toes folding her fist closed.** Day 9. Strange, tender, and a real surprise. This is the passage I'd remember.
- **The jigsaw puzzle with the piece hidden for her.** "She is making your board safe." I loved that. The missing piece, and her thumb in the empty dent at the end, is the line I'll keep.
- **Gennet, "I would very much like to know."** The only time Perpetua sounds like a person rather than a mouthpiece for the machine. The "cold and sharp and entirely mine" line landed.
- **Cistern forgetting where she put her own samples.** "I am forgetting my corners."
- **The moment of relief, then "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine."** Day 118. Big turn. I didn't see it coming, and it made me go back over the feeling I'd just had with her.
- **The birds walking out of the workshop without once turning round, and the elements "like cracked pots in a mud yard."** This echoed the first paragraph, and I felt it.
- **The ending with the welded box.** I found it quietly sad.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about **Cistern**, most of all. I came to care about **Under-the-Steps** and, a bit less, **Seven-Holes**, whose refusal to forgive is the one note of real anger in the story. I cared about Perpetua only in patches: the Gennet passage, the scarf and the welded box. For much of the story she is explaining things to someone, and the explaining kept me at a distance from her.

Would I have kept reading past page 2? **Yes, narrowly.** What kept me through the first two pages was the birds: the cart, the bird walking away from its own hoard. What nearly lost me was the number, which I couldn't make sense of until Day 4. If the birds had been less odd and funny, I'd have drifted off in the explanation on page 2 or 3. By Day 9, with the pebble, I was in.

## The question I'm left with

Did the birds' world ever need saving, or did she damage it? She wrote the report, the world is struck off the list, and the probes stop. But Cistern's whole workshop was "spent," and the birds walked out without turning around. I don't know whether the furnaces and the medicine benches went cold, and I don't know whether the birds can live as they did. The story's last image is Perpetua wondering about Gennet, so I think I'm meant to wonder the same about this world, and I did.

I'm also still wondering what was in the six-sided thing Cistern gave her.
