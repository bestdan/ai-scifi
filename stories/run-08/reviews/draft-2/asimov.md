## The one thing
Make the rules agree on what counts as evidence. Day 4 says a cache of advanced work "shown openly to my eyes" would move her number. Day 114 says only a decaying atom can, because metal and glass can come from a tomb. Then on Day 118 she sits eleven hours in front of a working two-kilometre ring, four hundred birds and the furnaces, and the number stays at 39.9961 until the bell. A reader who has the rule cannot say why the ring did nothing. One line from Lusk at the foot of the stairs would fix it: a ring is something a dead golden age could leave standing, and only the live atom cannot be left behind. Make Day 4's wording match.

## Notes

1. **Structural. Day 117, the whisper.** "a measurement specific enough that the installation's physics model scored them as hard to fake unless you had actually run the element." If that is so, why does it move the number 0.0007 when the live atom moves it 45? Say that the model can only compare a claim with its tables, not confirm where it came from, so it scores the claim low. The word "hard to fake" implies the opposite.
2. **Structural. Day 114 against Day 118.** Cistern promises "only the ring hall and my bench are spent, and your workshops stay dark and working." On Day 118 Lusk's eyes take in the bellows scaffolds, the vacuum pumps and the benches with "horn crimpers and glass blowpipes", all in the same hall, and the birds walk out of them. By the story's own rule Seven-Holes was right. That is good, but the page never says it, and the First Reader's closing question ("did the furnaces go cold?") comes from here. Have Lusk note it once.
3. **Structural. Day 117, the whisper again.** Cistern's secret decay data is now in the Concern's log. By Day 1's rule (outsider who knows what is in the cache) that is a spend, and "a tiny whistle of dry embarrassment" is too mild. One clause on what it cost her.
4. **Line-level. Day 4, "the table."** The First Reader read it as furniture or a guild rank until Day 117. Say "periodic table" once, in Cistern's two sentences.
5. **Line-level. Day 118, "I was exactly as sure as I had been."** −5 is odds of 10^5, not 10^40, so "exactly" is wrong in size. The line is protected. Keep it, but let the next sentence put the sameness in the machine, not the figure ("as sure of the model as before").
6. **Line-level. Day 37 and Day 118, Cistern's speeches.** "Because the installation taps both of your optic nerves... It is the exact same look, completing my work and spending it together" is a bird who lived this explaining it to a human who just told her so. Split it with an action, or cut it to its last two sentences.
7. **Line-level. Day 4 and Day 114.** The repeated "ten thousand staff, all of them with cameras" reads as a pasted line. The second use could be the birds' word for it.

## What to protect
- "The first thing I fixed on the planet was a wheel... and I am qualified in wheels."
- The Day 4 plant (birds see ultraviolet, the probe glints) and the Day 9 eyes-shut game: the twist is fair, and the cold reader solved it.
- "If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."
- "a doubt about Gennet slipped right past the gate, cold and sharp and entirely mine."
- The section headings as a ledger, with 39.9961 − 45 = −5.0039 now correct.
- "Every cache you have is spent by spring" and the fingertip-sensor coda: the rule is closed and the irony is stated once.

## Blind spot disclosure
I weight rule consistency over feeling, so I am likelier than the others to treat the ring-sight gap and the −5 wording as serious. Thin interiority is my own blind spot, so check Lusk's coldness against Le Guin. I also pay little attention to texture, and the machinery paragraphs may bore readers more than they bore me. I did not look at the lint output.
