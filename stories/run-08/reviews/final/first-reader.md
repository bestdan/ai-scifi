# First Reader report: "Everything You Look At"

## Where I got lost

1. **Opening paragraph, "my number."** The number 40.0000 is explained as certainty the world is dying, but "forty is as sure as they make anyone" and the later "base-ten log of the odds" made me unsure what the scale meant for a good while. I accepted "high number = sure of doom" and moved on. Day 4 helped, but only partly.

2. **Day 1, the cart and the birds.** I didn't understand why the cart vanished when she looked up. I worked it out over the next two scenes, when the brown bird moved its hoard because it was seen. The explanation Cistern gives ("once an outsider has seen a cache, it is spent") made it clear. The "spent" idea took me about three scenes to hold.

3. **Day 4, the long speech after the probe drill.** The paragraph starting "I see the flux on your feathers..." and the next one run together. I lost track of who said what, partly because Cistern is described "taking two dry steps" in the middle of Perpetua's speech. I also couldn't follow "I'll stay certain for four thousand years." I took it to mean the number will barely move whatever she's told. I didn't fully work out the arithmetic.

4. **Day 4, "ten to the fortieth power."** Cistern "understands you cannot argue with ten to the fortieth power." I took this as the odds behind the number. I wasn't sure how a person feels odds like that.

5. **Day 9, the Desk's three-light-hour relay.** "Three light-hours back" and then "six hours" of waiting made sense once I did the sum. It cost me a moment.

6. **Day 37, why the hole in the puzzle makes it "safe."** I got it only from Cistern's line, "Now that she has hidden one piece under the clay for you, no passing stranger can ever have a look at the whole harbour." It still felt like a stretch to me, though I liked it.

7. **Day 37, the Gennet story.** I was unsure why she brings up an old mission here. I worked it out: it's the root of her doubt about her own certainty. Then it came back in the last line, which helped.

8. **Day 37, "the installation only policed certainty about this world, so a doubt about Gennet slipped right past the gate."** I didn't follow this. I assumed it meant the machine only cares about the current mission.

9. **Day 37, the californium speech.** Cistern talks about "117," "118 and 119," and "nobody has ever made 120." The elements and numbers went past me. I understood only that there is a collection, that she is old, that she's forgetting where things are, and that element 120 is the unmade one. The detail about short-lived elements living only "in someone's memory of seeing them made" was lovely, but I wasn't sure of it.

10. **Day 114, "Why a pile of gold won't stop it."** The voice asking about two hundred ingots, and Perpetua's answer about moving her number "thirty-seven" and "forty-five," lost me. I got the gist: gold can be faked, one very rare event can't. The point values came too fast.

11. **Day 117, the big reveal about the eyes.** The violet star behind her left eye, which the birds can see, was a shock. I had to take the whole explanation at face value: "we hide from the thing that looks out of you." I worked out that the eyelid game on the salt-barrel on day 9 was the birds checking her eye. I didn't see that coming, but it made sense once said.

12. **Day 117, "my number only moved 0.0007."** Her number went from 39.9968 to 39.9961, and she said it moved 0.0007. I did the subtraction and it checked, but I didn't understand why speech counted but a hush didn't. I took "its ears never close" to mean the machine hears her.

13. **Day 118, the −5.0039.** She sees the atom, the number drops by forty-five, and the result is −5.0039. I followed the arithmetic. The point took me a second: she's now just as sure the world is fine as she was sure it was dying. I understood it the moment she said "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way."

14. **The final section, "the installation behind the optic nerves stays in for life."** I'd assumed the machine came out at the end of the mission. It doesn't, and I had to adjust. Then the hand-sensor refit, the box welded shut by touch, and the clerk (Harriet Baines): the logic held, but it took effort.

15. **The very end, "thirty thousand people."** I didn't remember a number of people on Gennet being given earlier. I took it as a figure she'd known.

## Where I got bored

- **Day 1, the paragraphs about the brown bird hiding the wire and beads** (the one that begins "Forty paces off by a nettle ditch" and the one after). The same trick happens twice, hide then move then hide again, and the second round felt like a repeat. I was waiting for someone to talk. It did pay off when Cistern explained it.

- **Day 9, the first half**, the jars with machine-cut threads and the report to the Desk. I wanted the bird scene and the pebble.

- **Day 114, the vote.** After the argument I had a feeling of procedure: Seven-Holes, claws on stone, taps. I was waiting to see what happened next, and the outcome was never really in doubt. I didn't mind the vote itself.

- **Day 118's long wait.** The eleven hours were fine. The paragraph with the pump scaffolds and singing and the stage-setting before it was slower than what followed.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The hand from behind.** Day 9, the pebble: "A bird's beak tapped once against my right shoulder-blade... pressed a small warm pebble into my palm from behind while two rear toes folded my fist closed over it." This is where I started to care. It reads as a tender thing that I only half understood.

- **"If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."** (Day 4.) This was the line where the birds went from a puzzle to a people. It was what made me want to continue.

- **The puzzle with the missing piece.** Day 37: Under-the-Steps pinching one dark-blue piece out of the harbour to keep it safe. I smiled. The empty dent in the water, and Perpetua resting her thumb in it, is the image that stayed with me through the rest.

- **Under-the-Steps and the eyelid game** (Day 9). A child playing a game she's been coached on, while twenty adults listen in silence. When I learned on Day 117 what the game was, it hit hard.

- **Gennet.** "I have never known, because my log was written at forty, and I would very much like to know." I believed her there more than anywhere else. A person who built a thing and can't know if it was needed.

- **"Dying with no trusted ones takes your caches with you, the worst death we know."** This made me sad and made the end land.

- **The moment of the atom, then the birds leaving.** "Four hundred birds stepped off the bellows bars... and walked out in silence through the side-tunnels without turning round." The silence cost something. And "She stepped back, leaving forty-two winters of purified elements under the green light like cracked pots in a mud yard." That line stopped me.

- **The −5.0039.** The relief turned to dread in one sentence: "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine." I didn't see it coming and it was the best turn in the piece for me.

- **"It's yours. Don't look."** The gift at the top of the stairs. Then the box welded shut by touch, and the clerk saying touch sensors are coming. I felt trapped for her.

- **The closing picture:** the new yard three ridges away, "its straw and broken pots already in place."

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

**Cistern**, by a long way. Her dry voice, the way she speaks to weeds and stones instead of to Perpetua, the lost sample plug, the mate nine winters dead, and the grief under it all. I cared next about **Under-the-Steps**, the child who still thinks being looked at is a game. I cared about **Perpetua** more slowly. At first she's just a competent, slightly smug narrator who explains too much. She became someone to me at Gennet, and again at the −5.0039, when she sees she's being lied to by her own head.

Past page 2: yes, though only just. The first page is dense with objects and rules and I was held by the strangeness of the birds and by Cistern's voice, not by the number. I'd have stayed for the birds. The opening paragraph, with its heavy explanation of the Concern, the number, the Actuarial Office, and the jigsaw, nearly lost me, and it's the stretch where I was most tempted to skim. By the end of Day 4, when the trust line landed, I was in.

## The question I'm left with

Was Gennet's grid ever needed? She never finds out, and the story won't tell me. The story seems to say that nobody can know, and that it matters most when you can't. And what happens to the box? She keeps it unopened for the rest of her life. I can't tell whether that's a way of honouring Cistern or a way of never finding out what it was. I'm left unsure whether keeping it shut is love or just fear.
