# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A field officer named Perpetua Lusk is sent to a world of bird-people, with a certainty-of-doom number planted in her head, and finds that the birds hide everything they value from anyone who looks at it. To save the birds' world from being swarmed by cameras, an old bird called Cistern lets Perpetua watch her make a new element, which destroys the secrecy she lives by.

2. Perpetua stands to gain a promotion to the Actuarial Office if she gets three clean missions. She loses it if she indicts the system, and she does. If she fails, on day 120 a task force of about ten thousand people with cameras arrives. That would "spend" every hidden thing the birds have, and with them the furnaces, the medicines and the glass. For Cistern, failing means her table of elements dies unwitnessed when she dies.

3. The Concern's rule came from the Hessary Inquiry. An assessor there was careful for thirty-one years while two billion people starved, so they decided doubt kills and made officers start at near-certainty of collapse, with a 120-day window and evidence from cameras and microphones only. They did it so officers would act and not dither. The story shows the cost: the number is so high that nothing short of one impossible event can move it, and it can hide a healthy world as easily as a dying one.

4. Late on, I learned that the machine behind her left eye glows in ultraviolet when her eyes are open, and the birds can see it. They had stopped hiding from her on day nine and were hiding from the thing that looks out of her. The pebbles pressed into her hand in the dark were Cistern making her a "trusted one", a second memory. The eyelid game with Under-the-Steps was the birds checking her eye. Also late, the machine's drop of forty-five points sent her to −5.0039, which is just as certain as before, the other way.

5. Perpetua kept her eyes open for eleven hours until the atom of element 120 flashed. The machine logged it and sent it home, and her number crashed to −5.0039. Task Force Seven was stood down. The birds walked out in silence and the hall was spent. They rebuilt a yard elsewhere. Cistern gave her a last small object and said "Don't look." Perpetua welded it into a lead-lined box by touch. She wrote a report saying the forty-point start was defective, which ended her promotion. A clerk told her touch sensors would be added to the installation, so she took a dock instructor's post and keeps the box and the unfinished puzzle.

6. Characters:
   - Perpetua Lusk: the narrator, a Concern field officer on her third mission, skilled at mending things.
   - Cistern (Under-the-Cistern): an old bird, an assayer of the guild, keeper of the table of elements; her mate died nine winters ago. She becomes Perpetua's trusted one.
   - Under-the-Steps: a young bird, about two, named for a buckle she buried; plays the eyelid game and takes a puzzle piece to make the board safe.
   - Seven-Holes: the flock's eldest, who argues against showing Perpetua the ring hall and never forgives her.
   - Harriet Baines: a senior clerk in the Actuarial Office who tells Perpetua about the fingertip refit.
   - The Desk: her dispatcher, which sends flat replies and appears to be a person or a system, I couldn't tell which.
   - The assessor on Hessary: a historical figure whose notebooks are in the archive.
   - Her sister: sent the puzzle.

7. Still unsure: whether the Desk is a person; why speech moves the number but a hush didn't protect her; how the forty-five-point figure was fixed; whether the final object was ever identified (I think six-sided, heavy, never opened); whether Gennet's grid was needed; and whether the birds' civilisation really survives with the hall spent.

8. Terms I didn't know and where I got them:
   - "Spent": from Cistern's explanation on day 1 and the repeated scenes.
   - "Trusted one": from day 4 ("what you know is mine").
   - "The Concern", "the Desk", "Actuarial Office": roughly from context.
   - "Remediation": from the day 4 explanation (ten thousand staff with cameras).
   - "Installation": from her description on day 4, the implant behind the left eye.
   - "The table": the collection of elements, from Cistern.
   - Element numbers (californium, 115, 117, 120, alpha-decay): never fully understood. I got that 120 is unmade and short-lived ones exist only as witnessed events.
   - "Zygodactyl": gathered as two toes forward, two back.
   - "Assayer": guessed from context.
   - "Moot": explained inline as the assembly.

9. For the birds, the central change means the secrecy their culture runs on has partly broken: one hall of their work, and one person's witness, has gone into the Concern's archive. Cistern says that was the price of buying the world off the crisis list. That ended the danger of the task force. Beyond them, the report triggers a review of the starting certainty for every mission, so other worlds may be judged less wrongly. But the touch-sensor refit shows the Concern closing the gap I'd call a loophole, which means fewer places for anyone to keep things hidden from its machines. Both effects point the same way: it saves one world and tightens the system around the rest.
