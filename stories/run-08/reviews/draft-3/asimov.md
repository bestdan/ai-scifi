## The one thing
Say what happened to the furnaces. The page sets the rule: "the furnaces were only ever for the caches," and medicine and glass are by-products. On Day 118 Lusk's look takes in "the whole hall, the workshops and furnaces too, so Seven-Holes had been right," and four hundred birds walk out. By the story's own rules the medicine benches have gone cold. The coda never says whether they restarted. Whichever way it goes, one sentence from Lusk or Harriet Baines settles it, and it makes the cost real. The First Reader's question ("whether the birds ever forgave her") is the soft form of this one.

## Notes

1. **Structural. Day 118, "a ruin can have a hall and survivors to work it."** The new fix works as a rule. But Day 37 has Cistern say californium is bred "in our reactors" and Day 118 shows the spinning target drum, so a reader can ask why a running beam is evidence of a ruin. Tie the sentence to the Day 114 principle: the model discounts anything that can stand on a shelf, and only the event itself can't. Add nothing new.
2. **Structural. Day 117, the whisper.** The rule now holds (ears never close; numbers a hair above talk). But "a ruin's old records could hold them too" is the only reason given, and the First Reader still didn't follow it (lost-list 9). Say plainly that the model checks a claim against its tables and cannot tell whether the speaker made it.
3. **Line-level. Day 118, "I was exactly as sure as I had been."** This was draft 2's note 5 and it is still "exactly" at the wrong size. Minus five is odds of 10^5, not 10^40. Keep the line and let the next sentence carry the sameness in the model.
4. **Line-level. Day 118, Cistern's "you cannot look for me without the machine looking for your office."** The phrase is hard to parse, and it is a bird explaining to Lusk what Lusk already knows. Cut it to the last two sentences.
5. **Line-level. Day 4, Hessary and the arithmetic.** The First Reader was bored and lost here. Break it with one action beat, or move the "four thousand years" figure to Cistern's reaction.
6. **Line-level. Day 114, "ten thousand staff, all of them with cameras"** is still a pasted repeat. Let the birds' word for it appear instead.

I checked the arithmetic: the daily drift matches the headings, and 39.9961 − 45 = −5.0039. The facts the resolution needs (ultraviolet sight, the eyes-shut game, touch not logged, the Desk's refusals) are all planted by Day 9.

## What to protect
- "The first thing I fixed on the planet was a wheel... and I am qualified in wheels."
- The Day 9 eyes-shut game and the pressed pebble: the clue is fair, and the cold reader enjoyed it.
- "If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."
- "a doubt about Gennet slipped right past the gate, cold and sharp and entirely mine."
- "My number said −5.0039. I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine."
- The jigsaw with the missing piece, and the fingertip-sensor coda.

## Blind spot disclosure
I weight rule consistency over feeling, so I may overrate the furnace and ruin-hall gaps. I give little weight to texture and body, so the underground descriptions may bore me less than they bore the First Reader. I judge Lusk by whether she reasons well, and Le Guin is better placed to judge her coldness. I did not run the linter.
