# First Reader report: draft 3

## Where I got lost

1. **The opening paragraph, "My number said 40.0000."** I couldn't tell what the number was. By the end of the paragraph I had "how sure I am that this world is dying", but I didn't know why anyone would carry one or why it never changed. I kept going and it was explained on Day 4. I worked it out from the narrator's own explanation there.
2. **The cart and the bird in the first paragraph.** I read "the cart was gone... a grey bird... standing where the cart had been" as a magic trick. I wasn't sure the bird was a person until she spoke on Day 1. I worked it out when she talked.
3. **The brown bird hiding the wire and beads.** The idea is that a seen hiding place is ruined. I followed the actions, but I didn't understand why it walked off leaving the treasure in the mud until Cistern explained "spent". I worked it out from her speech. It took me a few rereads of the logic in my head, and I felt a beat behind.
4. **Day 4, the two-kilometre particle accelerator and "the table: every element held".** Cistern says it in one breath about the underground, and I couldn't picture what "the table" was. Birds with a periodic table as their art form? I was confused for a long stretch, and it only came clear on Day 37 and Day 117. By then I understood that the table was a collection of every element, held as samples, and that the short-lived ones are held in memory.
5. **Day 4, the number's arithmetic.** "Base-ten log of the odds", then "at this rate I'd stop being sure in about four thousand years", then "ten to the fortieth power". I lost the thread of what moved it by how much. I didn't work this out. I just took it as "very sure, hard to move".
6. **Day 9, the relay and the six-hour wait.** I couldn't tell who the Desk was at first. I gathered it was her superiors, from the reply that came back.
7. **Day 9, the pebble.** Why she hid it in a sock and under a blanket wasn't clear to me until later. I assumed she was hiding it from the birds, then from the camera. I worked it out from "Touch doesn't go in the log."
8. **Day 114 and Day 117, the long exposition about why eyes shut vs. open.** This was the worst place. The reveal is that the implant glows violet when her eyes are open, and the birds could see it. I followed that, but the paragraph about "its tiny relay lens" and her mistake about talk versus touch mixed several rules together. I lost track of what the machine could hear versus see. I worked out in the end that eyes shut means no sight, but ears still work.
9. **Day 117, "the number moved from 39.9968 to 39.9961" when Cistern whispered the decay numbers.** I understood she was told to be quiet, but I wasn't sure why her facts counted for more than any other talk. The explanation said "a ruin's old records could hold them too" and I didn't really follow.
10. **Day 118, the two kinds of looking.** Cistern says the same look both completes her table and spends it. I got the feeling of it, but the logic took me a while. I didn't fully work it out. I took it as "she knows, and picks the loss anyway".
11. **The minus five point zero zero three nine.** I understood the point (the machine swung her from sure of one thing to sure of the opposite), but the arithmetic of 39.9961 minus 45 didn't quite give that number in my head, and I didn't stop to check.
12. **The ending, "Narrative addendum separated from evidentiary record and forwarded to Administrative Review"** and then Harriet Baines. I got it, but who she was took a moment.

## Where I got bored

- **Day 4, the long speech about the Hessary Inquiry** ("the assessor spent thirty-one years gathering evidence..."). I was waiting for the birds to do something. It is a lot of backstory in one block, and I wanted to get back to the hill.
- **Day 114, the vote paragraphs.** Seven-Holes's speech and the arguing about what is spent. I wanted the moot to end and for us to get to the ring. I skimmed the middle of Cistern's reply.
- **Day 117, the walk through the workshop**: the wire, the slate bench, the alcoves. Nice detail, but a lot of it, and I was waiting for the thing that would happen. It was the decay-number whisper, which came late.
- **Day 118, the eleven hours of waiting.** The waiting itself was fine. The "silver bell" and the three decay tracks were quick. The description of the hall before that was long.
- Throughout, the long sentences with lists in them ("the stone breakwater, the copper-lit herring boats, and most of the slate-blue water") started to blur together for me.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The opening wheel.** "I am qualified in wheels" made me smile, and the bird standing where the cart had been, "very deliberately not watching me", is funny and strange.
- **The bird walking away and leaving the wire and beads in the mud.** It is small and sad. I felt for the bird before I understood the rule.
- **Under-the-Steps and the eye game on the salt-barrel.** "Shut. Wait. Now open. Now shut. Open again." I liked it a lot, and I liked that the grown-ups were listening in silence to a game a child had been coached to play. Then the pebble pressed into her palm from behind, and her fist held closed for three seconds. That was the best moment of the first half for me.
- **The jigsaw with one piece taken.** A bird hides a piece of her sister's puzzle "to make it safe". I laughed, and then it hurt a little. It is a gift, and I understood it as one at once.
- **"If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."** This is where I started to care about Cistern.
- **Gennet.** Her not knowing whether the desalination grid was ever needed, and the pink salt block. "I would very much like to know." This moved me more than the big plot did.
- **The birds walking out in silence, leaving the glass bulbs untouched, "like cracked pots in a mud yard".** The echo of the opening hit me hard. I felt it as a loss even though the world was saved.
- **"My number said −5.0039. I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine."** I sat up at this. It was the turn I hadn't expected, and I didn't see it coming.
- **The last gift in the dark: "It's yours. Don't look."** And then the welded box she can never open. I found that very moving.
- **The ending, the missing piece and her thumb in the dent.** It closed the circle with the puzzle and Gennet together.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Cistern first, and Perpetua second. Cistern is old, losing her memory, and her mate is dead. She has chosen to be seen at the cost of everything. I also cared for Under-the-Steps for her few scenes.

Perpetua took longer. In the first pages she is a bit cold and sure of herself, which is the point, but it kept me at a distance. I warmed to her at the Gennet confession and the jigsaw.

Would I have kept reading past page 2? Yes, but narrowly. What kept me was the odd, funny image of the birds hiding things from being looked at, and Cistern's dry voice. The number and the rules of the Concern were a lot to carry early, and if the pebble scene hadn't come on Day 9, I might have drifted off.

## The question I'm left with

Did Perpetua do right by Cistern? She was trusted with her table, then logged it for the machine, and her gift sits in a box she'll never open. I'm also left wondering whether the birds ever forgave her, and whether Gennet needed the grid. I don't think the story lets either one be answered.
