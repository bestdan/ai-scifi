## The one thing
Fix the rules of the audio channel, because the climax stands on them. The installation hears everything Lusk hears. Yet Cistern announces her accelerator, her table and "elements one through ninety-nine" aloud on Day 4 (number: 40.0000 to 39.9999). She confesses her whole programme on Day 37, and the moot lists the galleries on Day 114. None of it moves the number. Then one whispered decay chain at alcove 115 moves it by 0.0007. A reader asked to state the rules by the midpoint cannot say why this speech counts and the others don't. Pick the rule and put it on the page. For example, the parser weighs only checkable specifics (a cross-section), and chat is cheap. Or the spoken disclosures do count, and they are what the Hessary rules call narrative.

## Notes

1. **Structural. Day 4, Day 37, Day 114 against Day 117.** Same rule problem as above, with a second edge. The story's own rule says a watcher who knows where a cache is and what is in it has spent it. The moot announces the "three medical synthesis galleries under the east meadow" and the "fourth gallery" gold to a hall the birds believe is deaf to words. Day 117 then tells us it parses words. By the story's own rule, the installation's audio has already spent a lot. Cistern's "tiny whistle of dry embarrassment" is far too mild. Say what hearing spends, or what it doesn't.
2. **Structural. Day 118, "My number said −5.0000."** Her number was 39.9961, and 39.9961 minus 45 is −5.0039. The ending turns on arithmetic the heads of the sections have taught the reader to check. Either print −5.0039, which also lets her note that the last four decimals are her ordinary evidence, or say the model resets. First Reader item 10 had to take it "on trust".
3. **Structural. Day 9, "six light-hours back."** The Desk is six light-hours away, she waits "six hours... to go there and come back," and the reply arrives "four hours later." Pick one lag. First Reader item 6 flagged it. A reader doubts every later number once a given one fails.
4. **Structural. Coda, "reduction from forty to thirty-six point five."** The story's own proof says no cheap evidence clears forty, and the −45 jump is what mattered. A cut of 3.5 orders of magnitude changes nothing. That is probably the joke, but Lusk never says so, and a reader may hear a reform. One line from Lusk on why 36.5 is still a wall would fix it.
5. **Structural. Day 118 close.** Her eyes were open on the whole hall, and the ring hall and table are logged and sent home. Nothing says what the Office does with a log that maps a civilization. Is the world marked closed? Seven-Holes' fear is "they will see why we dig". One camera has now seen it. Give the coda one clause.
6. **Structural. Days 117 and 118.** Both are tours of the same hall (basalt, bellows, singing, the table), the first by touch and the second by sight. The First Reader skimmed both lists. The turn at alcove 115 is the only event in Day 117. Cut the element list to three named cells, and give Day 118 one image of the hall instead of the whole inventory.
7. **Line-level. Day 118, first paragraph.** "four hundred birds were treading the ash-wood rocker bars... their grey and russet feathers rippling under the green glass light" is stacked clauses that the idea has to push through. Same with the experiment sentence on capacitor banks, a californium drum, a scintillator and silver witness-bells. The reader needs only this: she pressed the pedal, a star flashed three times in forty microseconds, and a bell rang three times.
8. **Line-level. Coda, Baines.** The exposition rides on dialogue ("we have closed the channel divergence you suffered"). I tolerate that more than the others will. Even so, "channel divergence" is jargon where "the birds' trick" would do.

## What to protect
- "The first thing I fixed on the planet was a wheel... and I am qualified in wheels."
- The Day 9 plant: Under-the-Steps staring "directly into my left eye" and the eyes-shut game, with the UV clue arriving first on Day 4 (birds see in ultraviolet, the probe glints). A reader can solve the twist before Lusk does. That is fair play, and it should not be explained earlier.
- "If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."
- "a doubt about Gennet slipped right past the gate, cold and sharp and entirely mine."
- The section headings as a ledger: the number barely moves, and the last heading is the joke.
- "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine."

## Blind spot disclosure
I weight rule consistency and arithmetic above everything, so I will over-flag the audio channel and the decimals. A reader may forgive both for the sake of Cistern. I also discount sensory texture, so the machinery paragraphs may bore me less than they bore the First Reader. Le Guin and Lem should say whether the birds' hiding costs enough on the page. I did not look at the lint output.
