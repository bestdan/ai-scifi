## The one thing
Make the narration enact the installation. The story's idea is certainty held against the evidence of your own hands. The form (headings: 40.0000, 39.9999...) shows the number, but Lusk's prose is never certain. By Day 9 she reports "this world was doing completely fine without us", so she has already doubted, and nothing on the page costs her the number. Let her reasoning keep reaching for the crisis reading after her hands have refuted it, and let the reader catch her at it. Then "Looking at the green figure... I recognized the exact texture of that relief" is a payoff, not a lecture.

## Notes

1. **Day 4 against Day 9 (structural):** "I'm certain you're dying" (Day 4) and then the Day 9 report that the squalor is camouflage. The novum says she can know the facts and still feel certain. Only the Day 1 sentence ("felt certain they needed me terribly") shows it. A form that serves the idea would let the day-by-day sections show her certainty surviving contrary evidence, line by line.

2. **The installation hears, so Cistern talks too freely (structural):** Day 4 puts "particle accelerator ring" and the whole table in Cistern's mouth with the log running. Day 37 has "Curium-248... I have made 118, 119, and 120 three times each". Yet one whispered decay chain on Day 117 moves the number by 0.0007, and none of these do. The "lexicon match" excuse is thin. Either those earlier speeches also shift the number, or Cistern is careful with her speech. A reader who spots this stops trusting the mechanism.

3. **Day 114 repeats Day 4 (structural):** Lusk gives the audience "ten thousand staff, all of them with cameras" a second time, almost word for word, and explains the number again. The vote's result is obvious, and the first reader said as much. Keep Seven-Holes's speech, which is the story's one real cost, and cut the gold-ingot Q&A and the explanation. This is the unnecessary scene.

4. **The box (structural):** "as long as I never see it, it isn't spent." But the mission prior has been stripped from her head and she declined the refit. Looking would spend nothing, and she is Cistern's trusted one. The story's own rule says she could open it. Either state why she can't (a camera in the dock? the rule that ownership needs not-knowing?) or let her choice be the cost. As written the ending answers the question too neatly, with a rule the story has just broken.

5. **Birds' fate is missing (structural):** The Concern now holds a recording of the ring hall and element 120. The coda deals only with the Office's refit. A line of Lusk's not-knowing about the birds, parallel to her not-knowing about Gennet, would deepen the question. The first reader was left asking whether they ever got their table back.

6. **Day 118 opening paragraph (line):** One 170-word sentence-chain of scaffolds, bellows and glass. The wry teller disappears. Cut it to what a single set of eyes would notice.

7. **Day 9 (line):** "six light-hours back" but the reply arrives in four hours. Fix the arithmetic.

8. **Lists and triplets (line):** "wet feathers, hot tallow, and crushed mint"; "ozone, hot beeswax insulation, and deep limestone water". Cut to one sense each.

9. **Day 4 (line):** "Why didn't your people set your certainty to one" is the least necessary question in the story. The reader took it on trust, and the story survives that.

## What to protect
- "I took this as evidence of a society too traumatised to own a cart."
- "Somebody has to remember where I put things." This is the idea producing the feeling.
- The stolen harbour piece, and the empty dent she rests her thumb in.
- "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine."
- Harriet Baines's "thirty-six point five", and touch-logging as the reform that closes the trick.
- Gennet kept as a doubt "entirely mine".
- The table "spent" in the same forty microseconds it is complete, and the birds walking out without turning round.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour the idea over momentum and may be too hard on a story that already has motion and jokes. I am not the lens for the bird humour and the pacing of the comic set pieces, and I hold the physics only to its internal logic, not to real accelerator plausibility. Watts and Egan should check the mechanism in note 2, and Banks the tone of the catalogues.
