# First Reader report: draft 1 ("Everything You Look At")

## Where I got lost

1. **Opening paragraph, "I took this as evidence of a society too traumatised to own a cart."** I didn't know what the number was or why she was reading it. I also didn't know why a missing cart pointed to trauma. I didn't work it out until the Day 4 section, where she explains it. By then I'd been carrying the number around for several pages.

2. **Day 1, the bird with the wire and beads (paragraphs 4 and 5).** I followed the action, but I was well into the scene before I understood why the bird was panicking. It was only when Cistern spoke that I saw the point: being looked at "spends" a hidden thing. I worked this out from Cistern's speech about the "spent" cache. I did lose a few paragraphs there.

3. **Day 4, the long speech about the table of elements.** "One through ninety-nine we hold as pure samples; from a hundred up... atoms made live in the accelerator and seen at the detector by a trusted one." I got the general idea that these birds are doing real physics. I didn't follow the mechanics, and I didn't know why a witness mattered. I only half worked it out later, when the 120 test happens and a bird watches it. I never fully understood why the witness is needed.

4. **Day 4, the number explanation ("base-ten log of the odds").** I followed that it's a gauge of certainty that barely moves. The line "A world that is fine and good at hiding, and a world in crisis that is good at public relations, produce nearly the same surface evidence" helped. The arithmetic (the daily rate, four thousand years) went past me, and I just took it as "very slow."

5. **Day 4, "Why didn't your people set your certainty to one..."** I had to stop and think about why a belief at exactly one couldn't move. I accepted it and moved on without really getting it.

6. **Day 9, the Desk is "six light-hours back."** I wasn't sure how she could get a reply in four hours when the trip was six each way. I took it as sloppiness or something I missed. I did not work it out.

7. **Day 9, the dodecahedron.** I understood that she was handed a heavy, odd object in the dark. I was lost on why it mattered or what it was for. I later got that these gifts are a way of trusting her, which I worked out from Day 37 and Day 117.

8. **Day 117, the flicker from 39.9968 to 39.9961.** I understood that her number moved when Cistern spoke the physics terms. I didn't get why speech registered but touch didn't, until Cistern said they thought it was deaf to words. Then I partly got it. The next bit, "the beam-splitter behind my left pupil fluoresces in the ultraviolet," and the link to the shutting-eyes game, lost me for a few lines. I worked it out from "Every time your eyelids came up, that greedy violet star lit up." The birds can see UV, so they saw her eye light up.

9. **Day 118, the experiment itself.** This was a thick wall of equipment: capacitor banks, californium target, scintillator, witness-bells. I couldn't picture it. I got that it worked and that it counted as proof.

10. **Day 118, the number going from 40 to −5.** I wasn't sure whether to be glad or sorry, and then the story told me. "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way" was clear. But "forty minus forty-five" is arithmetic I had to take on trust.

11. **The last section, the box.** I was unsure what was in it. I think that's intended. I did wonder whether the last scene's jump forward (Harriet Baines, the refit) was where the story was going, because I thought it had ended on Day 118.

## Where I got bored

- **Day 118, the whole opening paragraph** (the hall, "three tiered timber scaffolds," "four hundred birds"). It's one very long description. I was waiting for the experiment to happen and to learn what it did to her.
- **Day 117, the walk along the catwalk and the tour of the table** ("chill liquid hydrogen at one, a dense bead of arc-melted osmium at seventy-six..."). It's a list of elements, and I skimmed it. I was waiting for the moment something happened between the two of them. It did, at alcove 115.
- **Day 4, the speech about the guild and the deep workshops.** I wanted to get back to the two of them.
- **Day 114, the vote in the dome.** Once the flock was counting what would be lost, I felt I knew what the vote would be.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- **The opening.** "I trued it in twenty minutes. When I looked up, the cart was gone." A cart vanishing because she fixed it is funny and a bit sad, and it pulled me in.
- **Cistern's voice when she first talks:** "The flock drew straws behind the granary this morning and I lost, so I am here to deal with you while you ruin the furniture." I laughed. Addressing her remarks to the weeds and not to Perpetua is a lovely habit.
- **"If I trust you, what you know is mine. That's what trust is for. Somebody has to remember where I put things."** This made me care about Cistern. It's the best line in the story for me.
- **The first gift in the dark** (Day 9): the claws prying her fingers open and closing them over a pebble. I was surprised and moved. It felt intimate, and I didn't yet know what it meant.
- **The jigsaw puzzle on the camp table, and the bird who takes a piece "to make it safe."** I loved this. A bird stealing one piece so that no stranger can see the whole picture is odd and tender.
- **Gennet.** "I have never known, because my log was written at forty, and I would very much like to know." This was the first time I felt for Perpetua herself, not only for the birds. "A doubt about Gennet slipped right past the gate, cold and sharp and entirely mine" is a strong line.
- **Cistern's confession:** "I am forgetting my corners, Perpetua Lusk." That hurt.
- **The twist at alcove 115** (the number flickers when Cistern whispers). I was surprised. I had not seen it coming that the machine heard but didn't feel.
- **The reveal that the birds had been hiding from the glow in her eye since day nine.** I felt the story turn over under me. It re-explained the whole game with the salt-barrel. It was my favourite moment.
- **The −5.0000.** "I was exactly as sure as I had been, the other way, on the word of the same machine." This hit me hard. I thought the relief was real, and then it was a trap too.
- **The last gift at the top of the stairs, and "Don't look."**
- **The ending, the empty square in the puzzle and the unopened box.** "As long as I never see it, it isn't spent." That worked for me. It was quiet and sad.

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

I cared about Cistern first, and then Perpetua. Cistern is funny, stubborn and afraid of forgetting. I cared about Perpetua more once she told the Gennet story, so not right away. I was a little wary of her early on, because she's so sure she's right and she's wrecking things. The fledgling, Under-the-Steps, was a minor delight.

I'd have kept reading past page 2, but only just. The cart opening and Cistern's first speech carried me. The long explanations in Day 4 tested me. What kept me going was wanting to know what the number was and what Cistern would do. I did feel some drag in the middle, where the story explains rules at length.

## The question I'm left with

What is in the box? And did the people on Gennet ever need the grid? The box one I think I'm meant to leave alone. The Gennet one I want answered, and I know I won't get that.

I'm also left wondering whether the birds ever got their table back, or whether it's gone for good once it has been seen.
