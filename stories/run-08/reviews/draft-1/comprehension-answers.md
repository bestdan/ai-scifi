# Comprehension answers (from memory, no reread)

1. A field officer for an organisation that "fixes worlds" is sent to a planet of hiding, cache-burying birds. Her implanted certainty that the world is dying makes her unable to doubt it. The birds, who lose a hidden thing whenever an outsider sees it, teach her to trust by touch in the dark. In the end she proves the world is thriving, but the proof destroys what the birds were hiding.

2. She wants a promotion to the Actuarial Office, where she would set the numbers. If her certainty is still above zero on day 120, a huge task force with cameras lands and exposes every hidden cache, and the birds lose all of them. In the end she gets the proof, but Cistern's whole table is spent when she watches it. She also gives up the promotion by writing that the issued certainty was defective.

3. The Concern set it up this way after the Hessary disaster. An assessor there spent thirty-one years being careful while two billion people starved, so they decided doubt kills. They issue officers near-certainty, give them 120 days, and accept only camera and microphone evidence, so the officers can't talk themselves out of acting. The odds are set high, but not at one, so an officer can still come home. I did not fully see why touch was left out of the evidence. I took it as an oversight that the story treats as a gap.

4. Two things.
   - The birds hid from the glow in her left eye, not from her. The violet light that showed up when she opened her eyes was the installation's camera, and the shut-eyes game was them testing it. They had been hiding from the machine since day nine.
   - The relief at −5 is the same kind of false certainty as the 40 was. It comes from the same machine.

5. On day 118 Cistern ran the element-120 test with Perpetua watching as her trusted one, and the machine recorded it. The odds flipped to −5, the task force was stood down, and Cistern's table was spent by being seen. The flock left the workshop and never returned to it. Perpetua wrote a report blaming the issued prior, which lost her the promotion. Months later a clerk, Harriet Baines, told her the service was reviewing the priors. The review would cut 40 to 36.5 for farming worlds and add touch sensors to the refit, which would make hand-teaching visible to the log. Perpetua declined the refit and became a dock instructor. She kept the sealed box Cistern gave her unopened, so that what's inside is never spent.

6.
   - Perpetua Lusk: narrator, field officer of the Concern, on her third mission, trying to avoid a promotion-ending failure.
   - Cistern (Under-the-Cistern): grey assayer bird, a guild member who holds a table of elements. She is forgetting where things are, and she makes Perpetua her trusted one.
   - Under-the-Steps: young bird, named for a buckle she buried. She plays the eye-test game and takes the puzzle piece.
   - Seven-Holes: the flock's eldest. She opposes showing Perpetua anything and never forgives her.
   - Harriet Baines: clerk in the Actuarial Office who tells Perpetua the review will happen.
   - The Desk: the dispatcher Perpetua reports to. It never accepts her narrative.
   - Cistern's mate: dead nine winters. He witnessed her elements up to 117.
   - Her sister: sent the jigsaw puzzle.

7. I am still unsure about the following.
   - What is in the box, and I think I'm meant to stay unsure.
   - Whether Gennet needed the grid.
   - How the test could be so certain: whether it was one event, and why a bird's witness mattered. I never got the logic of "witnessed by a trusted one".
   - What the bird-made gifts, such as the dodecahedron, ampoule and lens, were for beyond showing trust. I'm unsure whether they were also real parts of Cistern's table.
   - How her number could move from sound but not touch, and whether the review fixes that.
   - What the rest of the birds' world does now.
   - The timing of the six-light-hour relay with a four-hour reply.

8. I didn't know these from the story itself.

   - Zygodactyl: I took it as a two-forward, two-back toe arrangement because the text spells that out.

   - Element numbers, "californium", "curium", "witness-cradle", "coincidence plate": I took them as physics furniture and only partly understood them. I understood that 120 was the new element they made.
   - Moot: I guessed a council from context.
   - Windlass, plumb-bob, shim, fathom: partly known already.

9. For the birds, the central change is that their hidden world has been seen, at least by the one machine. For the Concern's officers, the change is the review of the priors and the touch sensors, which will affect every future officer and every world they visit. The touch sensors close the gap Perpetua used, so future officers can't be taught by hand. The lower prior might mean fewer wrecked worlds, but they still have an installed certainty. For other worlds I am not sure, because the story only says it is "modeling" a reduction.
