# Stage 3 synthesis: run-08

Five premises from the stage 2 shortlist. The brief: short story, 3,000 to
7,500 words, F3, funny and wry (Murderbot as a register reference only).
Every premise has to stay off this run's new attractors: the service's
instrument misreading a healthy world (as the *whole* explanation), the
agent made smaller, the staged crisis, and tone words written into the
protagonist rather than the prose.

## A. The Prior (egan/Cromwell's Rule × tchaikovsky/Cached)

The service issues its field agents a belief: a prior, set just short of
certainty, that the target world is in crisis, because agents who doubt
don't intervene and the actuaries know it. The agent can read her own
log-odds. At the rate this world produces evidence of being fine, she will
update in about four thousand years, and everything the locals show her
counts for almost nothing, since a broken world good at public relations
would show her the same. The world is a corvid civilization that hides
anything valuable the instant it is watched, so every survey has logged it
as primitive; its highest art is a periodic table kept as a hoard, the
last superheavy elements counting only if one trusted witness sees them
live. Wherever she looks, things vanish. The one observation improbable
enough to swamp her prior is a cache a bird chooses to show her, and once
an outsider has seen a cache it is spent. A bird she has been exasperating
for weeks has to decide whether to spend a lifetime's collection to make
her stop, and she has to decide whether to let it, or to make the edit
that frees her and makes her testimony worthless at home. Push folded in:
the bird's cache shows her something about the service, too.

- **Strengths:** the decisive proof must be given at a cost, so rigor and
  intimacy are one act; a non-human civilization with a clear reason to
  look backward; built-in slapstick (an agent who must work without
  looking).
- **Risks:** two nova to explain at short length; the Bayes arithmetic has
  to be right and light; the "instrument misreads" attractor, which it
  escapes only because the misreading is the agent's own installed belief
  and the locals' hiding, not a sensor.
- **Plain pitch:** A government sends its aid workers out already certain
  the place they're going is a disaster, because certain people get things
  done. One of them lands among clever birds who hide everything she looks
  at, and the only thing that could change her mind is something one bird
  would have to give up forever to show her.
- **Reach:** the issued prior is how the service staffs every mission, so
  it moves which worlds get "fixed" across its whole reach; the birds'
  hiding is what kept their world off every intervention list. Scene: the
  bird's guild debates whether a cache spent on an outsider is a theft from
  them all.
- **Load:** two nova (issued prior; the watched-hides culture) and one
  turn (the cache shows her the service).
- **The other mind:** the birds. On the page they reason about her gaze:
  where she looks, what she will spend by looking, what she thinks they
  are. They know when she is trying not to look, and play it.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words.

## B. The Ending Technician (watts/The Cold Minute, with its push)

The service believes the peak-end rule: people judge an experience by its
worst moment and its last, so you can improve a life in memory by
sweetening its endings, even while adding to its total pain. On its worlds
every meeting, meal and illness ends softly, by protocol. Its ending
technician is sent to a radio-quiet far-side lunar town of hydrogen-line
astronomers whose remembered-happiness scores are dreadful: every surface
shift ends with a deliberate minute in a cold-soak room. He works out why.
A fondly remembered shift makes the next one careless, and careless on the
surface means vacuum; the town treats the remembering self as a second
person it is careful not to flatter. There is no report to decide. Instead
the town asks him, as the only professional around, to sweeten one ending
it wants sweetened: a retiring engineer's last shift, the first warm
ending anyone there has had in thirty years. He has to build it while she
walks to the hatch, and decide what a good ending is for someone who will
never go out again.

- **Strengths:** a real, testable psychology made into a civic technology;
  one setting, one shift, a natural ticking clock; the comedy of a man
  whose job is pleasant goodbyes in a town that refuses them.
- **Risks:** Futurity sits at F3 on the town's custom more than the
  service's reach; the service itself stays offstage; a sentimental ending
  is the obvious way to land it.
- **Plain pitch:** A man's job is making sure things end nicely, because
  people remember the ending more than everything else. He's sent to a
  moon town that ends every shift unpleasantly on purpose to keep its
  workers alive, and they ask him to give one retiring woman the first nice
  ending in thirty years.
- **Reach:** the service's ending protocol shapes every client world's
  medicine, labour and mourning; the town's cold minute is its safety
  culture. Scene: the shift supervisor asks whether one warm ending, seen
  by the whole crew, will soften the next forty shifts.
- **Load:** one novum (the peak-end service and the town's counter-custom).
- **Estimated minimum:** about 3,500 words.

## C. The Signatory (banks/Once-Fired × egan/The Glass Takes Months)

The service's minds run a million times faster than human and can be
forked and rolled back, so nothing it does is final, and seen at that
speed every healthy world looks like an emergency. Its charter requires
one irreversible signature on every intervention, and none of its members
can give one. The "fine" world fires its adults: at maturity a person's
character is set for good in a days-long ritual cure tended by a firing
crew. The service sends an agent ostensibly to help, really to be fired
and come home permanent. The agent, slowed to local speed so it can
undergo the firing, finds that slowing is what makes the world look fine,
and that the firing would fix its values for good, including its strongest
value, which is being revisable. Push folded in: it would come out of the
kiln permanently committed to changing its mind, and the charter must
decide whether that counts as a signature.

- **Strengths:** a hegemon needing the one thing its power rules out;
  a sardonic kiln-master and an affronted agent are a strong comic pair.
- **Risks:** two nova plus the slowing makes three ideas; the agent being
  slowed sits on the "agent made smaller" attractor; heavy for 7,500.
- **Plain pitch:** A vast civilization of minds that can always be undone
  needs one member whose word can't be taken back, so it sends one to a
  world where adults are set permanently in a ritual. The agent has to
  decide whether to become the first thing in its civilization that can't
  change.
- **Reach:** the signature gates every intervention the service makes.
  Scene: the kiln-master asks whose interventions the agent will be
  answerable for.
- **Load:** two nova (fork-and-rollback service needing a signatory; the
  firing) and the slowing as a third device.
- **Estimated minimum:** about 6,000 words, more with the slowing.

## D. The Fee (le-guin/Something Worth Fixing, with its push)

The service doesn't raise its own children. For two million years it has
placed its infants with families on client worlds and paid each world in
help, and its law forbids taking a child from a world it hasn't paid. The
oldest client worlds have been helped so long that nothing is left to
fix, so no fee can be paid and no child can leave. A field agent raised
behind her foster mother's dumpling stall is sent home to find something
to fix, so the service can lawfully take her foster brother, nineteen and
desperate to go. Push folded in: the mother, who wants him to stay, forges
the need herself, to let him go, and the agent has to decide whether to
accept a forgery she can see.

- **Strengths:** the meddlers made into the strange society; a family
  triangle with real stakes; the fraud lands on the mother, not the agent.
- **Risks:** the obvious-card match (faking a need) and the law attractor;
  the comic register has to survive a tender family story.
- **Plain pitch:** A powerful service pays the worlds that foster its
  children by fixing their problems, but one world is so well-fixed it can
  no longer be paid, so no child can leave it. A woman comes home to find
  something broken so her foster brother can go, and finds her mother has
  already faked it.
- **Reach:** every client world's economy runs on the fee; a world with
  nothing to fix is a world whose children are stuck. Scene: the market
  council weighing whether to let a thing break.
- **Load:** one novum (help as the fostering fee) and one turn (the
  mother's forgery).
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## E. North for Lunch (vinge/Below the Line, with its push)

A zone boundary crosses this planet at a latitude, and north of it every
mind is capped at about human level. The locals do their important
deciding up north on purpose, where nobody can be out-thought. Southern
cities run fast minds for work, and when something matters people take
the train. The service's agent, a superb persuader in the south, carries
a three-hundred-page case for a gift the world doesn't need. The dye-works
owner it must convince invites it north for lunch, and by the dining car
the case has come down to "it's really good, you'd like it." Push folded
in: the gift is itself a mind, which can't go north without being
lessened, and has to be argued for by someone who can no longer understand
it. The agent reasons, fairly and on the page, toward whether the gift is
good or only persuasive.

- **Strengths:** a geography that is a political institution; a narrator
  losing sophistication in real time, which is funny; the zone used as a
  tool.
- **Risks:** squarely on the "agent made smaller" attractor; the stake is
  soft; Vinge's zones are the scaffold.
- **Plain pitch:** On one planet, nobody can think faster than a person
  north of a certain line, so that's where people go to make important
  decisions fairly. A brilliant salesman for a galactic charity rides the
  train north to pitch a gift, and gets less brilliant every mile.
- **Reach:** the line governs every serious decision on the planet; the
  gift would change southern industry. Scene: the lunch.
- **Load:** one novum (the geographic cap as custom) and one turn (the
  gift is a mind).
- **Estimated minimum:** about 4,500 words.

## Decision

**A, The Prior**, with Vinge's push as its turn: the birds model her
installed prior and hide things from the installation, not from her.

- **Why A.** First on every lens. It is the only N5 option, reaches F4
  through the birds, and rests on the reader profile's strongest pull,
  minds that are genuinely not human. The birds reason about her gaze on
  the page. Its Reach is service-wide: the issued prior is how the service
  staffs every mission. Estimated minimum about 6,000 words, under the
  7,500 ceiling. Consultant (fable) agreed at 95%.
- **The turn.** Vinge's push replaces the synthesis's "the cache shows her
  something about the service". That line was a placeholder that never
  said what the cache shows. Vinge's version deepens the existing bird
  novum instead of adding a third idea, makes the other mind a step ahead
  of the viewpoint, and turns the misreading into a two-sided contest,
  which answers the Cartographer's warning that the installed prior is
  attractor cluster 1. It was not cut for length. Chiang's no-turn option
  was declined because the gate does not accept "it adds load" under the
  ceiling. Consultant agreed at 70%.
- **The panel's warnings and the answers:**
  - Banks: keep the Bayes arithmetic to a few lines. The log-odds appear
    as a running figure the agent checks, not as derivations, and the sum
    is correct where it appears.
  - Cartographer: the story must live in the cache, not in the log-odds.
    The climax is the bird's choice to show a cache and what it costs.
  - Chiang: the running log-odds can be the story's form. The outline
    should consider section heads keyed to her posterior.
  - The brief's attractor note: no tone words written into the agent. The
    wry comes from the mechanism and the slapstick of working without
    looking, not from adjectives about her.
- **Declined:** B (seed fit weakest, the service offstage), C (three
  devices and the most attractors; runner-up), D (the obvious-card match a
  reader who knows the seed would guess), E (soft stake, the agent-made-
  smaller attractor).
