# Stage 5 critique: Banks lens

Blind spots: I favour sprawl and set pieces, and short stories aren't my
natural form. Where a note below adds weight, treat it as a swap for
something cut, not an addition. Chiang and Asimov should veto any note that
costs words.

1. **[must-fix] The scale is stated, never seen.** The birds' hidden
   civilization (furnaces, bellows choirs, a particle accelerator, aquifer
   venting) is met only by touch in scene 7, and scene 8 shows a detector.
   The reader never stands in the vastness the whole story rests on. Hessary
   (two billion dead) is also only told. Fix: in scene 8, when her eyes open,
   spend a full paragraph on the hall: hundreds of birds on the bellows,
   singing, lit by furnace glass, under a surface of straw she walked over
   for 118 days. That is the single concrete moment of vastness, and it
   pays off the scruff. Give the Hessary telling in scene 3 one physical
   image from the footage, not a summary.

2. **[must-fix] Sections 3 and 4 ask the same question.** Part II's question
   ("can you know something your certainty won't count?") is answered in
   scene 3 by the pebble and "Touch doesn't go in the log". Scene 4 then
   adds more handovers in the dark with the same result. Fix: cut the
   handover montage in 4 to a sentence. Make the jigsaw misreading the
   scene's engine, because it raises a new question: the flock now thinks
   she is seeking a trusted one, and Cistern has a reason to start. Keep
   Gennet.

3. **[should] The wit thins where the stakes rise.** Scenes 1, 3, 4 and 6
   have a joke each. Scenes 5, 7 and 8 are earnest for about 2,500 words
   running, and 8 carries the number flip, the report, the gift, the
   refit and the coda. Fix: let the Desk be the running comic engine. It is
   allowed two or three messages. Put one in scene 5 (a cheerful
   reminder of her remaining window), and make the coda message
   exquisitely bureaucratic. Let the number headings do deadpan work
   in 5 to 7. Let Lusk be annoyed about the missing jigsaw piece at the
   worst moment.

4. **[should] Scene 8 is overloaded, and the order undercuts it.** Move
   the report and the Desk's coda out of the climax scene into a short
   closing section (scene 9, 300 words, under the cap by trimming 5 and 6).
   Reordering note: put the whisper that moves the number (scene 7) before
   Cistern's day-nine line, so the reader sees the installation's
   hearing, then learns why the birds were testing it. Linear is right
   otherwise; the number headings carry the form.

5. **[should] Who is missing: anyone in the Office.** The story is about
   power that means well, and the Actuarial Office, which set 40 and signs
   off on the bin-and-replace rule, is a voice with a lag. Fix: in the coda,
   let one named clerk answer her report in a way that shows the
   Office meant it, and still wouldn't have caught the error without a
   dead world's worth of evidence. Dirty work, good reasons, a signature.
   Also, the view from below: Under-the-Steps' day nine, told in two lines
   from its side, would earn the pebble.

Reply must-fix: notes 1 and 2.
