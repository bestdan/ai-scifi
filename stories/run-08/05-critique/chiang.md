# Stage 5 critique: Chiang lens

Blind spots: I am weak on pace and comic timing, and I tend to prefer
restraint where this brief wants wit. Banks and Asimov should overrule me
on both.

The climax does grow from the idea. The number is the form, and the
installation scoring its own proof is the question. These are the notes.

1. **[must-fix] Parts III and IV repeat a tradeoff.** Scene 5 (Day 110)
   is Remediation against Cistern's table, with "neither says the
   obvious". Scene 6 (Day 114) is the same tradeoff, now counted by the
   flock. Part III's question, "who pays, and do they agree?", is asked
   twice. Cut scene 5 as a scene. Lusk states the stakes and the proof's
   shape to the moot in scene 6, where it has an audience. Cistern's "I
   made 118 to 120 alone" goes at the end of scene 4, in the dark, as a
   handover: she presses a sample into Lusk's hand and says what it is.
   Stakes still lands before the climax, and the cut frees about 800
   words.

2. **[must-fix] Spend the freed words on scenes 3 and 4.** Scene 3 holds
   Hessary, the Desk's refusal, the eyes-shut game, the pebble, the touch
   channel and trusted sight in 950 words. Scene 4 holds the jigsaw, the
   dark handovers, the edit and Gennet. Each idea the style guide says
   "gets scenes, not sentences" is competing with another. Move Hessary
   back to scene 2, where Lusk is already explaining herself. Leave scene
   3 the game, the pebble and the Desk. Give Gennet room to land as the
   first doubt the installation can't touch.

3. **[should] The cost is stated in the coda, not paid in scene 8.** Eight
   hundred words must carry the detector, the spend, the flip, the report,
   the gift and the coda. The cost to Cistern ("completed and spent in
   one moment") is a sentence. Show what a spent table looks like from
   inside. She has finished it and cannot hide it. Perhaps she leaves the
   workshop facing outward, and Lusk, the one trusted witness, sees her
   do it. Also cut "she has booked passage" to Gennet. It closes her
   wound too neatly, and "she hasn't gone back" is the better open end.

4. **[should] A light formal device.** The headings already enact the
   idea. Add one inversion. Heading 8 opens at 39.9961 and the flip
   happens mid-section, unannounced, so the reader sees the number fall
   in the prose before the heading could. Make the coda's heading the
   number with a gap where touch would sit ("—"). The one thing she
   holds that the number can't count is then visible on the page as an
   absence.

5. **[should] Introductions in scene 1.** The scene introduces Lusk, the
   Concern, the installation, Cistern, "spent" and the number in 850
   words. That is the guide's "few names, one scene" limit stretched.
   Hold "installation" until scene 2. In scene 1 she calls it "my number"
   and "the thing behind my eye". Ensure Cistern's name arrives with her
   job ("the assayer they assigned me"). Separately, plant a vanishing
   after day nine in scene 4 that fits both readings, so the scene 7
   reread works.

The ending leaves her certain the other way, which deepens the question.
Keep that.
