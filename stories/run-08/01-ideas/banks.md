# Idea cards: banks

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** sprawl, set pieces and worldbuilding that bury a short story. Every card below needs a hard cut to one world, one agent and a few scenes. Its instinct for galactic scale will fight the 7,500-word ceiling, so Chiang and Asimov should do the cutting. It also writes the wit as irony from above. The deadpan of a reluctant narrator from below has to be built on purpose.

- **Assigned:** 2. Power that needs · Copied, uploaded and forked selves · search-and-rescue dog handler × a kiln
- **Obvious card:** A meddling agent lands on a thriving world, can't find anything to fix, and learns from the locals to leave well enough alone. Not written.

## Card 1: The Live Find

- **Novum:** The Service can't perceive a world directly. It sends forks of trained agents, who work like scent dogs: a fork "alerts" on suffering it can feel but can't explain. Fixable worlds have grown scarce, and a fork that keeps coming back with nothing goes flat and stops working. So the Service now plants problems for its forks to find.
- **How it works:** This is the dog handler's old trick of staging a live find to keep a dispirited dog keen, done at civilizational scale. A small, survivable crisis is seeded so the agent can solve it and get its reward.
- **First-order consequence:** Agents stay keen and keep finding things.
- **Second-order consequence:** Healthy worlds get harmless staged emergencies, and some of them start noticing the pattern.
- **Third-order consequence:** The galaxy's best-run places quietly learn to fake a little dysfunction, a drought here and a feud there, so the Service will send its people and then leave satisfied.
- **The story:** A deadpan agent, three hundred missions in and privately sick of being congratulated, alerts on a planet that is plainly fine. It solves the crisis, then finds the planting crew's paperwork. The crisis was staged for it. Then it finds the second layer: the locals staged one too, to manage the Service. The Service needs the agent's enthusiasm and can't order it. The agent has to decide whether to pretend.
- **From the stimulus:** The handler's live-find practice for burned-out dogs. The kiln is dropped.
- **Why this lens:** Power that means well doing quiet dirty work, and a hegemon that needs morale it can't requisition.
- **Suspected prior art:** Banks's "The State of the Art" (the Culture leaves Earth alone); the Strugatskys' Progressors. Neither has the staged find or the world gaming the meddlers back.

## Card 2: Once-Fired

- **Novum:** The Service is made of minds that can be backed up, forked and rolled back, so nothing it does is ever final. Its charter requires one irreversible signatory on every intervention, and it has no member who can sign. The "fine" world fires its adults: at maturity a person's neural plasticity is locked in a slow ritual burn, and character is set for good, glaze and flaws included.
- **How it works:** Locals undergo the firing through a days-long neurochemical cure tended by a firing crew. After it they are fixed. They can still learn facts, but their values can't change. The Service can't fake this, because any self it makes can be unmade.
- **First-order consequence:** The Service's interventions stall without a signatory nobody can revise.
- **Second-order consequence:** It sends an agent ostensibly to "help" this world, really to be fired and come home permanent.
- **Third-order consequence:** The galaxy's most powerful polity becomes answerable to one ceramic conscience it can't edit and can't replace.
- **The story:** The agent, a fork who likes being revisable and finds the mission insulting, is apprenticed to a sardonic kiln-master. It keeps trying to fix the firing process and keeps being told no. The Service needs a self it can't take back. The agent has to decide whether to be the one, knowing whatever comes out of the kiln can't be returned.
- **From the stimulus:** The kiln, all of it: irreversible transformation and the crew who sit up through the firing. The dog is dropped.
- **Why this lens:** A hegemon that needs the one thing its power rules out, at galactic scale, resting on one small person's choice.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's "Learning to Be Me" and reversible-self stories. Nearer still is this project's own reversible-minds attractor, approached from the other side.
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** It brushes "reversible or uncomputed minds." Here the novum is irreversibility as a scarce resource the powerful lack, the inverse of that card.

## Card 3: Same Scent

- **Novum:** Four previous agents were sent to fix this world. All of them were forks of the same template, and none of them reported home. Each scrubbed its trace signature and settled in under a new life. The Service can't take a fork's memories by force: they are worthless unless given. So it can't find out why.
- **How it works:** The Service hires a local search-dog handler. The dog's nose is the only tracker it can't spoof. The fifth fork, the narrator, goes along, because it smells exactly like its quarry.
- **First-order consequence:** The dog alerts on the narrator again and again.
- **Second-order consequence:** Each found fork has a different job and a different life, and a different reason the world needed nothing from them.
- **Third-order consequence:** The Service learns that its best measure of a world's health is how many of its own people decline to come back.
- **The story:** The narrator, dry and certain it is the sensible one, tracks down four versions of itself: a baker, an amateur astronomer, a neighbour locked in a petty feud and a dog breeder. None of them will hand over a memory, and each one is a little smug about it. The Service needs one of them to give willingly. The handler knows the dog has started alerting on the narrator with the same confidence, and says so.
- **From the stimulus:** The handler, the dog and the alert on a scent. The kiln is dropped.
- **Why this lens:** The view from a fork, a powerful entity that can't compel memory, and comedy beside quiet loss.
- **Suspected prior art:** The Bobiverse (forks diverging); *Moon* (clones meeting). The tracking-dog frame and the strike of non-reporting forks are not in either.
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** It's next to "copies who live a life and merge back." Here the point is that none of them do.
