# Idea cards: le-guin

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** slow pacing; little interest in hardware and
hard science; a moral seriousness that tips into instruction, which works
against this brief's wry register. Each card puts its comedy in the agent's
competence colliding with a society that makes sense on its own terms, but
someone faster and less earnest should test the jokes.

- **Assigned:** 1. The other answer · Deep time and vast scale · parasitoid wasp × a night market
- **Obvious card:** The agent lands on a peaceful world, can find nothing to
  fix, and slowly learns the locals have been quietly fixing her and her
  service. Not written.

## Card 1: Something Worth Fixing

- **Novum:** The meddling service doesn't raise its own children. For two million years it has placed its infants with families on client worlds, collected them at adulthood, and paid each world in help. The help is a fostering fee, and the service's own law forbids taking a child from a world it hasn't paid.
- **How it works:** The service lives in ships and habitats. Long ago it judged that children raised aboard came out wrong, so it farms out childhood. Families volunteer openly, and a fostered child chooses at twenty whether to go. The fee is real work: cures, orbital repairs, a harvest saved.
- **First-order consequence:** Worlds that raise many service children get a great deal of help.
- **Second-order consequence:** The oldest client worlds have been helped for so long that nothing is left to fix, so the fee can't be paid and no child can leave.
- **Third-order consequence:** On a world with nothing wrong with it, an ambitious young person is stuck.
- **The story:** A field agent was raised on this world herself, behind her foster mother's dumpling stall in the night market. Now she is sent home to find something to fix, so that the service can lawfully take her foster brother, who is nineteen and desperate to go. Her mother wants him to stay. The world is aggressively fine. She can fake a need, which defrauds both her service and her planet, or she can tell him no.
- **From the stimulus:** The wasp lays its young in another body to be raised. The night market became the stall.
- **Why this lens:** It asks who raises the children, and makes the meddlers the strange society.
- **Suspected prior art:** The Strugatskys' *Beetle in the Anthill* (planted embryos); changeling folklore; the Ekumen's envoys. None of them makes help the price of a child.

## Card 2: Closing-Down Sale

- **Novum:** A civilization 60,000 years old has chosen to end. It stopped having children three generations ago, so that its planet passes to a younger species it has nursed toward minds. The service's charter puts survival first, so it sends an agent to save them.
- **How it works:** The heirs descend from a social parasitoid insect and are slowly becoming minded. The old people concluded that their presence bends the heirs into imitators and clients. The last generation is now in its seventies. Its final years are a night market that covers the planet and gives away everything: tools, recipes, cities. These are left where the heirs might find them.
- **First-order consequence:** The world has no schools. Every town is half hospice and half party.
- **Second-order consequence:** The economy is a giveaway. The old keep a quarantine between themselves and the heirs, and they argue over whether to leave writing behind.
- **Third-order consequence:** Grief has turned into hosting, and dying well has become a civic art.
- **The story:** The agent brings fertility fixes, longevity treatments and evacuation, and is politely refused. The person this world fails is its last child: a sixteen-year-old, born late, who will be alone at the end. The old want her taken offworld. She wants to stay and meet the heirs, which would break everything the quarantine protects. The agent's rescue kit sits unopened while stallholders press free trinkets on her.
- **From the stimulus:** The wasp became the heirs' ancestor. The night market became a whole civilization's last party.
- **Why this lens:** It asks what death means, at the scale of a species, and who pays for a good ending.
- **Suspected prior art:** Clarke's *Childhood's End*; Brin's Uplift novels; Stapledon. None of them has a voluntary handover that a rescuer opposes.

## Card 3: Whose Grown-Up Are You

- **Novum:** On this world an adult is two people. When they come of age, two partners bond into one person in two bodies. Anyone single-bodied is a child, legally and socially, whatever their age.
- **How it works:** A neural mesh is seeded at birth and stays inert until puberty. Partners court through adolescence. Once bonded, the mesh keeps both bodies in sync within a few kilometres, and further apart the two drift into halves that merge again when they meet. A bonded pair has one name and one vote.
- **First-order consequence:** Every job is designed for four hands, and single-bodied labour is children's work.
- **Second-order consequence:** When one body dies, the survivor becomes a "half" and goes back to legal childhood, gently looked after.
- **Third-order consequence:** The language has no word for loneliness in an adult, because a single adult is unthinkable.
- **The story:** The agent is 340 years old, single-bodied and very competent. She has come to "restore individuality" to a population the service judges merged without consent. Night-market vendors give her sweets and look around for her grown-up. Her assigned minder is a half whose other body died in a fall. He is the one person the world fails: an adult mind handled as a child. The service could make him a legal single, which would prove that single people are adults and unpick the world's definition of a person. Or she could help him bond again.
- **From the stimulus:** The card travelled far. All that remains is the idea of one life lived inside two bodies, and the market where young people court.
- **Why this lens:** It asks how one becomes an adult, and finds a word the culture lacks.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines (*A Fire Upon the Deep*); Sturgeon's *More Than Human*; Trill joining in *Star Trek*.
