# Idea cards: egan

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens:** it lectures, it flattens emotion, and it lets
a derivation outrank the scene. Comedy is not its native register: the jokes
here come out of the mechanism, and the drafter will have to find the warmth.
Let the First Reader and Chiang push back.

- **Assigned:** 3. The self under edit · The same questions in nonfiction · bell founder × a pressure suit
- **Obvious card:** an agent lands, finds the world thriving, and fakes a
  crisis so her mission isn't scrubbed, until she decides the service is
  the broken one. Not written.

## Card 1: Partials

- **Novum:** The service's agents are cast, not written. A light-sail seed
  grows an overprovisioned mind on site in one pour, and the only edit the
  medium allows afterwards is ablation: a faculty can be cut out, never put
  back.
- **How it works:** Growth from a tiny seed overproduces, as infant brains
  overproduce synapses before pruning. Two near-equal drives in one mind
  beat against each other, like clashing partials in an untuned bell, as a
  mood swing with a measurable period that shortens as the substrate
  settles. The dossier, written forty years ago, lists the cuts for a crisis
  posting: patience, local attachment, a sense of humour.
- **First-order consequence:** Every agent's career is a sequence of losses.
- **Second-order consequence:** Senior agents are slivers, tuned to one
  note. The service is run by single-note minds, and every world sounds to
  them like it needs that note.
- **Third-order consequence:** Fresh agents look at their superiors and see
  their own future.
- **The story:** A wry new agent on a prosperous, noisy terminator-city
  world stalls the cuts, because nothing here needs fixing. But the beating
  is getting faster, and something has to go. She has to choose one cut that
  isn't on the list, and doing without it will define her.
- **From the stimulus:** Bell founding. A bell is tuned on a lathe by taking
  metal off, so the tuning is all subtraction.
- **Why this lens:** a self under edit, with irreversibility as the cost.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's "Reasons to Be Cheerful" (tuning one's own
  mood); the Strugatskys' Progressors and Banks's Special Circumstances for
  the service. Neither has subtractive selves.

## Card 2: Cromwell's Rule

- **Novum:** Before deployment, the service installs in its agents a prior
  of near-certainty that the target world is in crisis. Agents who doubt
  don't intervene, and the actuaries know it.
- **How it works:** Cromwell's rule says that a prior of exactly 1 can
  never be moved by evidence, and the service stops just short of 1. The
  agent can read its own log-odds and do the sum: at the rate this world
  produces evidence that it's fine, she'll update in about 4,000 years.
- **First-order consequence:** She keeps "fixing" small things. The locals
  are politely furious.
- **Second-order consequence:** The harder the locals try to prove they're
  fine, the less it counts. A broken world good at public relations would
  produce the same exhibits, so their likelihood ratio is near 1. She can
  see herself reasoning like a conspiracy theorist, and she can't stop.
- **Third-order consequence:** The only way out is a single observation so
  improbable under "broken" that it swamps the prior. Or an edit. But home
  reads the edit logs, and a mind that deleted its inconvenient belief has
  testimony that's worth nothing.
- **The story:** A deadpan agent and one exasperated local hunt for a
  10⁴⁰-to-1 fact. The cost of failing is that she makes the edit, sees
  clearly, and can never be believed at home. Stay off the disclosure
  attractor: the choice is what she edits, not what she reports.
- **From the stimulus:** The pressure suit. Her belief holds its shape
  against everything pressing in from outside.
- **Why this lens:** reason taken seriously, and a self able to rewrite
  itself.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's "Axiomatic" (belief implants). Not close
  in shape.

## Card 3: The Glass Takes Months

- **Novum:** The service thinks at a million times human speed, and seen
  that fast, every healthy world looks like an emergency.
- **How it works:** This is the statistics of sampling. At high frequency
  you see variance, and only by integrating over time do you see the mean.
  To a fast observer every argument is unresolved, every child is
  mid-tantrum and every market is mid-crash. The agent's energy budget is a
  fixed number of operations, like a suit's fixed air supply.
- **First-order consequence:** At a dinner party, a dropped glass takes her
  subjective months to fall. She drafts three hundred intervention plans
  before it lands.
- **Second-order consequence:** At service speed her budget buys ten
  thousand subjective years, spent inside a week of apparent crisis. Slowed
  to local speed, the same budget buys one human lifetime, and the world
  comes clear.
- **Third-order consequence:** The service sends fixers because it is fast,
  not because the worlds are broken. This is the news-versus-decades error,
  built into an institution's clock rate.
- **The story:** The agent must choose between millennia of noise and
  seventy years of sense. The slow life is the one in which she can make a
  friend, and then she dies, on schedule.
- **From the stimulus:** The pressure suit's finite oxygen. Breathe faster
  and you get less time.
- **Why this lens:** a self able to slow itself, and mortality as a budget.
- **Suspected prior art:** clock rates in *Diaspora* and *Accelerando*;
  *Her*. None argues that speed manufactures crisis.
