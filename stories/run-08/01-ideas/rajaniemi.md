# Idea cards: rajaniemi

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens, stated up front:** I can mistake density for
depth, and front-load a world's vocabulary until readers bounce. My plots
tend to pile up reversals. Each card below is built around one piece of
infrastructure and one choice, so the drafter can lift it without a glossary
avalanche; Asimov and the First Reader should still check whether the
reader is swimming or drowning.

- **Assigned:** 2. The everyday infrastructure · Deep time and vast scale ·
  piano mover × a pacemaker
- **Obvious card:** A Contact-style agent arrives at a thriving world, finds
  nothing wrong, and has to invent a crisis to justify the trip. Not written
  (and its nearest ancestor, Russell's "...And Then There Were None," already
  owns the thriving-colony-that-doesn't-need-you joke).

## Card 1: Arrhythmia

- **Novum:** A civilization of minds spread across a solar system holds
  together on a shared beat, a system-wide clock pulse every mind locks to.
  The meddling service reads a world's pulse from light-years off, the way a
  cardiologist reads an ECG.
- **How it works:** Minds light-hours apart can only act together by
  agreeing when "now" is. The pulse is that agreement. Debts fall due on the
  beat, apologies are a deliberate half-beat late, and the funeral rite is
  letting someone drop off it. An irregular pulse has always meant factional
  collapse.
- **First-order consequence:** The service diagnoses fibrillation and, nine
  centuries ago, launched a corrective pulse at light speed, a defibrillator
  aimed at a whole system.
- **Second-order consequence:** The agent, sent later and faster, arrives
  days ahead of the correction and finds the irregularity is syncopation.
  The system is in its jazz age, the most inventive century it has had.
- **Third-order consequence:** Everyone there has learned to live off the
  beat. A forced regular pulse would flatten a whole generation's art and
  make every syncopated contract fall due at once.
- **The story:** A dry, overqualified field agent has to stop her own
  service's cure, which is a wavefront and can't be recalled. She has a few
  days, a lot of physics, and a report form with no box for "patient is
  dancing."
- **From the stimulus:** The pacemaker, taken literally and scaled up to a
  solar system.
- **Why this lens:** Time as infrastructure you can feel in a handshake.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Qeng Ho timekeeping; Banks's Contact.
  Neither is about a civilization's rhythm being misread as a symptom.

## Card 2: Narrow Stairs

- **Novum:** Minds and cultures travel between stars as freight down
  bandwidth-limited beams. Every settled system has a "stairwell," its
  narrowest link to everyone else, and a culture is shaped by what fits down
  it.
- **How it works:** Movers are the respected trade. They pack large,
  fragile selves for transit: what to fold, what to leave in the van, how to
  pivot a personality around a tight landing. Whatever doesn't fit waits
  outside the system, sometimes for centuries.
- **First-order consequence:** The meddling service sends a mover-agent to
  rescue a world it thinks is trapped behind the galaxy's tightest
  stairwell.
- **Second-order consequence:** To get in, she arrives cut down: about a
  fifth of herself. Her expertise, her languages and her temper are parked
  in orbit.
- **Third-order consequence:** She finds a civilization made entirely of
  people edited down for transit over ten thousand years, who prize
  smallness and think the outside's selves are hoarders. Widening the
  stairwell would end them, and it's the only way she gets reassembled.
- **The story:** A diminished, irritated professional judges whether a
  world needs rescuing while she is missing most of what would let her
  judge. Comedy of competence working without its tools. The choice is to
  go home whole or stay small.
- **From the stimulus:** The piano mover: big, fragile things and narrow
  turns.
- **Why this lens:** Infrastructure (the beam) as the root of a whole
  aesthetic.
- **Suspected prior art:** Egan's *Diaspora* (transit of selves); Schroeder's
  *Lady of Mazes*. Neither makes bandwidth a culture's taste.
- **Attractor broken:** It edges toward copies who merge back. Here the
  question is whether she rejoins herself at all, and it's never a merge.

## Card 3: The Favour Standard

- **Novum:** The service visits each world on its circuit once every sixty
  thousand years and grants one Fix per visit: anything, a cured plague or a
  moved moon. Over deep time the agent's IOU, a deferred Fix, has become the
  reserve currency of the circuit worlds.
- **How it works:** A world with no current problem can bank a chit, a
  promise of a Fix on the next pass. Circuit worlds trade chits for ships
  and grain. Their value rests on the service always honouring them, and on
  the service never issuing them for nothing.
- **First-order consequence:** Thriving worlds stage crises, elaborately and
  with civic pride, to earn chits.
- **Second-order consequence:** The agent is in effect a central bank that
  has never been told so. Her charity is the money supply of forty
  civilizations.
- **Third-order consequence:** This world is fine, and its staged asteroid
  is lovely. If she refuses the chit, its economy and its neighbours' faith
  in chits wobble. If she grants it, she debases benevolence itself.
- **The story:** A tired, million-year-old circuit agent haggles with a
  world's cheerful finance ministers over a fake disaster, and has to decide
  what a kindness is worth once it's money.
- **From the stimulus:** It travelled: the pacemaker became the circuit's
  slow, regular beat of visits.
- **Why this lens:** A currency invented from one odd rule, with a culture
  grown around it over millennia.
- **Suspected prior art:** Stross's *Neptune's Brood* (slow interstellar
  money); cargo-cult satire. Neither has gifts becoming specie.
