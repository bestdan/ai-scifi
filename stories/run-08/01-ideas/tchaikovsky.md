# Idea cards: tchaikovsky

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** It is optimistic that any mind can be reached
with enough patience, so it under-weights the case where contact simply
fails or there is nobody home (Lem and Watts cover that). It likes long
timescales and big populations, which thin out the human characters; I have
tried to keep each card to two or three minds in a few scenes. It drifts
toward sprawl. It is fond of its strange minds, which can tip into making
them cute.

- **Assigned:** 1. The animal given a mind · Technology as magic, seen from both sides · beekeeper × a periodic table
- **Obvious card:** The meddling agent arrives at a world of uplifted bees to
  improve their chemistry, finds the hive already has it sorted, and gets
  worshipped as a god. Not written.

## Card 1: Frame Inspection

- **Novum:** The meddler is a mind that lives in honeycomb. Ordinary,
  unthinking bees build it and act as its hands. Its memory is the
  arrangement of trace metals the workers pack into wax cells, so the comb is
  a periodic table it thinks with.
- **How it works:** The service drops a queen. The swarm builds comb, and the
  foragers bring back nectar salted with whatever the local soil holds.
  The mind reads the world through what they bring, the way a tongue would.
  It thinks by having workers move salted honey between cells. It tells
  time by the dances.
- **First-order consequence:** A local beekeeper hives the wild swarm, which
  is what it was designed for. Its honey is heavy, tinted, faintly metallic,
  and it sells.
- **Second-order consequence:** Harvest removes frames. Each one she takes
  is a lobe of the mind. Smoke stops the hands working, so to the hive every
  inspection is surgery under sedation.
- **Third-order consequence:** The honey is the fix the service sent: a
  supplement for a trace-element shortage the survey thought it found. Every
  jar sold delivers the mission and takes away part of the briefing.
- **The story:** The hive narrates, in deadpan, while working out why it
  came from the cells it has left. The beekeeper is kind, and the clover is
  excellent. Hiding its mind in bitter cells would stop the harvest and end
  the mission. Letting her take the frames ends its memory of the mission.
- **From the stimulus:** Beekeeper literal; the periodic table became a
  memory medium.
- **Why this lens:** Evolution, here the body plan of a hive, decides how the
  mind thinks and what it can lose. The beekeeper sees good honey. The hive
  sees surgery. Both are right.
- **Suspected prior art:** Tchaikovsky's own *Dogs of War* (a bee-swarm
  intelligence called Bees); Vinge's Tines (minds spread over bodies). Here
  the bees are not the mind. The comb is.

## Card 2: The Share

- **Novum:** The world the service flagged belongs to a bird lineage that
  evolved to guide. Like honeyguides, they can't open what they want, so
  their whole civilization recruits bigger animals to open things for them,
  and each partner is paid in a share.
- **How it works:** They are small and sharp-eyed, with ears tuned to the
  sounds of tools. Their gut flora digest wax and polymers. They can read
  where a large creature's attention is going and steer it there with
  chatter. They have no hands, so everything they build is borrowed labour.
  Their one law is the share: cheat your guide and next time it leads you
  into something's den.
- **First-order consequence:** The survey found no industry, so the service
  sent a lone agent with a sealed industrial seed kit.
- **Second-order consequence:** She thinks her careful first-contact steps
  are going well. In fact she is being led, with great patience, to her own
  cargo, because its packing polymer is the richest wax on the planet.
- **Third-order consequence:** They open the kit and eat the packing. The
  machinery lies there, ignored.
- **The story:** A capable, put-upon agent on her first solo posting, a
  viewpoint that switches between her and one guide, and the bill coming
  due. The share they want is her lander's seal compound. Paying it
  strands her for a season. Refusing it gets the service's next surveyor led
  somewhere with teeth.
- **From the stimulus:** From beekeeper to the other species that lives off
  hives. The periodic table shrank to one prized polymer.
- **Why this lens:** Evolution decides the culture. Both sides believe they
  are the one doing the steering, and both are partly right.
- **Suspected prior art:** Brin's *Uplift* patrons and clients, inverted;
  *Elder Race*. I know no fiction about honeyguide mutualism.

## Card 3: Cached

- **Novum:** An uplifted corvid civilization hides anything valuable the
  moment it is watched, an instinct that began as theft-avoidance. So every
  survey probe has logged the world as primitive. Its highest art is a
  periodic table held as a hoard: each element owned in pure form, ending
  with superheavy atoms that last milliseconds and count only if one
  trusted witness sees them.
- **How it works:** They see in ultraviolet, remember thousands of cache
  sites, and are acutely aware of being looked at. Showing someone a cache
  is the deepest intimacy they have, and once an outsider has seen a cache
  it is spent and must be abandoned. Their accelerators exist for the art.
  Everything else came from them as a by-product.
- **First-order consequence:** The meddling agent arrives to kickstart
  industry, and everything vanishes wherever she looks.
- **Second-order consequence:** She works out that she is the reason the
  world looks backward. To do the job at all she has to work without
  looking: handed objects behind her back, touch only, eyes on the horizon.
- **Third-order consequence:** One bird chooses to show her its collection,
  the work of a lifetime, which means giving the collection up.
- **The story:** A competent agent turning slowly into a figure of slapstick,
  and a bird who pays dearly for her trust. Her report is honest and one line
  long, so disclosure is not the drama. The drama is what she owes for having
  seen, and what she can give that she will never look at.
- **From the stimulus:** The beekeeper became a hoarder. The periodic table
  stayed literal: a collection.
- **Why this lens:** Evolution writes the technology and why it can't be
  seen. Primitive and advanced are both true, depending on who is looking.
- **Suspected prior art:** Tchaikovsky's *Children of Memory* (uplifted
  crows, different novum); Star Trek's "Who Watches the Watchers", inverted.
- **Attractor broken (only if one is):** It brushes "a functionary finds
  something hidden". The card routes around it: nothing hinges on telling.
