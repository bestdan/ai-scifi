# Idea cards: chiang

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots.** This lens runs low on action, conflict and plot momentum. It
avoids antagonists, and it tends to be too measured and polished for a
comic register: its restraint can flatten the jokes into wistfulness. Each
card below needs someone pushing back hard, and the deadpan has to come from
a narrator with opinions, not from tidy irony. Let Banks and Watts argue.

- **Assigned:** 2. The form first · Minds that are genuinely not human ·
  portuguese man o' war × a hearing aid
- **Obvious card:** the agent arrives, finds the world thriving, and has to
  fake a crisis so the service will count the mission a success. Not written.

## Card 1: The Undersigned

- **Novum:** The service's field agent is a colonial organism, a siphonophore
  of a few thousand specialist parts, each a small mind with one job. Parts
  can detach and live alone.
- **How it works:** Tact, arithmetic, grammar, locomotion, stinging and the
  service's mandate each live in separate parts. The agent's voice is what
  they produce together.
- **First-order consequence:** On a contented human habitat ring, parts start
  leaving to take local jobs. Tact becomes a wedding planner.
- **Second-order consequence:** The agent's reports to headquarters lose
  whatever faculty just left. After tact goes, the reports turn rude and
  accurate.
- **Third-order consequence:** The ring gains odd new citizens, and the
  agent shrinks toward its one part that cannot be satisfied: the urge to
  help.
- **The story:** Form: the mission's numbered reports, signed "the
  undersigned" with a falling count. The form shows every departure. The
  question is what a helper is once the parts that could see there was
  nothing to fix have left. At the end the mandate files alone.
- **From the stimulus:** The man o' war is many animals mistaken for one.
- **Why this lens:** The form carries the idea, and the feeling grows from it.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Flowers for Algernon*, degrading reports, a
  close match on form. Vinge's Tines; Watts, "The Things"; Banks's Contact.

## Card 2: Gain

- **Novum:** The agent is deaf to the world's medium and hears it through a
  service-issued aid. The aid is tuned to bring distress forward.
- **How it works:** The natives talk in infrasound across a moon's ice
  sheet. The aid translates and weights for pain, grievance and need. It is
  the service's way of finding work.
- **First-order consequence:** At issue gain, the agent hears a planet in
  anguish.
- **Second-order consequence:** Each fitting session it turns the distress
  gain down a notch, and the anguish resolves into what it was: haggling,
  a sport with loud fans, a ten-year comic opera of a feud, flirting.
- **Third-order consequence:** At flat gain the agent hears the world as it
  is. Now it hears its own service's briefings flat too, and they sound
  frantic.
- **The story:** Form: the aid's fitting log, settings and the wearer's
  comments, session by session. The deadpan is in the comments. The question
  is whether the instruments that look for need are what create it. The
  cost: a fixer that can no longer hear need can't be sent anywhere, and
  this agent has nowhere else to go.
- **From the stimulus:** The hearing aid, and how one gets fitted. The man o'
  war's nerve net became the ice sheet that carries the talk.
- **Why this lens:** A question about knowledge, made personal through one
  instrument.
- **Suspected prior art:** Wells, "The Country of the Blind"; Black Mirror,
  "Arkangel" (the filter); the Strugatskys, *Hard to Be a God*.

## Card 3: Intake

- **Novum:** A civilization of colonial persons whose members break up and
  recombine with the seasons. Read by the service's census, a population
  that swings fortyfold every year looks like mass death.
- **How it works:** Specialist bodies drift between persons. In winter a few
  thousand large persons hold a whole coast; in summer millions of small
  ones. Nobody dies, and nobody stays the same person either.
- **First-order consequence:** The agent is sent to stop a "mortality
  crisis."
- **Second-order consequence:** If it reports no crisis, the service's model
  files the world as uncountable. That invites a keener agent next time.
- **Third-order consequence:** The agent asks the world for a favour: hold
  still for one census day as fixed persons. Some of them like being
  individuals and won't come apart. The census has made the first
  individuals this world has ever had.
- **The story:** Form: the service's standard intake questionnaire,
  answered by the world, with the agent's attachment notes. "How many of you
  are there?" gets a different answer on every page. The question is
  whether you can help a people your categories can't count. The cost is the
  small schism the agent caused.
- **From the stimulus:** The man o' war's parts, which are animals and not
  organs.
- **Why this lens:** A premodern-style rigour applied to an alien biology,
  and a thought experiment about personhood.
- **Suspected prior art:** Le Guin, *The Left Hand of Darkness* (seasonal
  persons, an envoy), a real nearness. Vinge's Tines; Tchaikovsky,
  *Children of Ruin*.
