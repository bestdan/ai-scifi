# Idea cards: asimov

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens, stated:** thin interiority; a pull toward
dialogue that explains; little feel for the body, the senses, or weather;
the idea tends to become the character; and a standing assumption that every
problem has a solution a clever person can find. These cards lean on the
drafter and the other lenses for texture, and for an ending that doesn't
resolve neatly. Card 1 at least puts the agent in a body that has to be
felt.

- **Assigned:** 3. The engineer's problem · Technology as magic, seen from both sides · velvet worm × plate tectonics
- **Obvious card:** a meddling-service agent lands, prepares a grand rescue,
  finds the locals are fine, and the joke is the agent's wounded pride. Not
  written.

## Card 1: Weekdays

- **Novum:** The service puts its field agents into bodies grown from local
  fauna, so they can pass as nature. This one is a cat-sized, many-legged
  leaf-litter hunter that spits glue. Its job is to keep a fault under a
  human colony's capital creeping, so it never locks and ruptures, by
  squirting lubricating slime into it at night.
- **How it works:** The service's rule: an intervention must look natural,
  and anything the agent can't attribute to the service counts as geology.
  Something re-locks the fault every few days. The model calls that natural
  hardening, so the agent lubricates harder.
- **First-order consequence:** The fault creeps, and the colony sees a
  geologic anomaly.
- **Second-order consequence:** Tram rails buckle over it, so municipal
  crews grout the fissure every shift. Two benevolent engineering efforts
  undo each other, and each takes the other for the rock.
- **Third-order consequence:** The colonists already lock the fault on
  purpose and release it in small triggered quakes on festival days, with
  the china packed away. The agent's creep is shifting strain onto the next
  segment, which their calendar doesn't cover.
- **The story:** The agent is deadpan, damp and reluctant. It has to work
  out, from inside a worm, why the hardening stops on rest days and vanished
  for a week at midwinter. Undoing its work means being seen in a tram
  tunnel.
- **The reader's question:** What hardens on a six-day cycle and takes
  holidays?
- **From the stimulus:** Both halves taken literally: a glue-spitting body
  and a creeping fault.
- **Why this lens:** a rule (count the unattributed as nature) and the case
  its makers missed, when nature has a payroll.
- **Suspected prior art:** *Star Trek: TNG*, "Pen Pals" (a secret geologic
  fix); Strugatsky, *Hard to Be a God*; Banks, *Inversions*. None has two
  remediations each taking the other for geology.

## Card 2: Roofers

- **Novum:** The service's telescopes see a planet gone into stagnant lid:
  its tectonics stopped as it cooled, so volcanoes no longer return carbon,
  and the models give it a slide into permanent ice. The agent comes to
  crack the lid and restart subduction. The natives are slow, humid,
  many-legged people whose biology makes a glue that can be dissolved and
  drawn out again.
- **How it works:** For two hundred thousand years the natives have run the
  carbon cycle by hand, as ordinary public works: they draw carbon into
  fiber, bury it, dissolve it, burn it. To them a vent is a leak in the
  books.
- **First-order consequence:** Maintenance crews seal the agent's fracture
  bore every night, taking its drill for a burrowing parasite.
- **Second-order consequence:** The agent's instruments show a temperature
  steady for a hundred millennia, which its model calls impossible.
- **Third-order consequence:** The friction is the time horizon. The service
  plans in tens of millions of years, the roofers in centuries, and restarted
  tectonics would bring quakes to a species that has never felt one.
- **The story:** A competent, fed-up engineer-agent and a native foreman who
  thinks it is a pest with opinions. Each sees the other's technology as
  weather.
- **The reader's question:** If nothing geologic returns the carbon, who
  does, and why does the hole close only at night?
- **From the stimulus:** The velvet worm's reversible slime becomes a
  civilization's material; the tectonics are the absent kind.
- **Why this lens:** the engineer's problem, and society as a system on a
  geologic clock.
- **Suspected prior art:** Tchaikovsky, *Children of Time* and *Elder Race*
  (non-human natives, tech seen as magic). I know of nothing that puts a
  planet's carbon cycle in civilian maintenance.

## Card 3: The Mine Is Me

- **Novum:** Ten thousand years ago the service sowed a sleeper agent in
  this world's crust, to guide its civilization when the time came: a slow
  mind spread through a self-repairing crystalline net in the bedrock. It
  wakes for a few days each century.
- **How it works:** The agent reads the world through strain in its own
  net. Its rule says strain is tectonic, because nothing else moves rock.
- **First-order consequence:** Each waking, the net is torn in more places,
  and the agent spends its repair budget regrowing faster than any plate
  moves.
- **Second-order consequence:** The locals are human and thriving. They mine
  "rockwool", a wonder mineral that heals back where it's cut and runs their
  economy. The rockwool is the agent.
- **Third-order consequence:** Their prosperity is the agent's guidance, not
  as intended: it is their ore, not their mentor. Mining towns follow its
  regrowth.
- **The story:** The agent is pompous and slow, with five waking days to
  understand a century. It must reason out why its tears are clean, cluster
  along roads, and deepen in boom decades. Then it chooses: stop regrowing,
  crash their economy and make them listen, or go on being a very good mine.
- **The reader's question:** What tears rock in straight lines and follows
  the price of something?
- **From the stimulus:** Plate tectonics became the agent's false theory of
  its own pain; the worm was dropped.
- **Why this lens:** an engineer diagnosing its own failing structure, every
  clue planted.
- **Suspected prior art:** Clarke, "The Sentinel" and *2001* (a buried
  uplift agent). This one is harvested, not heeded.
