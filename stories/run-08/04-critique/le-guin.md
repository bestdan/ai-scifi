# Stage 4 critique: Le Guin lens (run-08, "Forty")

Blind spots: I am weak on pacing and on the hard-science side (the accelerator,
the odds arithmetic). Egan and Lem should rule on those. I also lean toward
instruction, so discount any note that asks for more moral weight.

The society has a strong core: the moots held facing outward, the taboo on
looking at dug ground, and inheritance by knowing. I'd like to see a chore in
it.

1. **[must-fix] The society has no children in it, and only one old bird.**
   The bible gives a chick-feeding lesson and a fledging age, but no child
   appears in any scene. Cistern's mate is dead, and nobody in her flock is
   young. Add a fledgling, perhaps Cistern's sibling's or a neighbour's, who
   is assigned to watch Lusk's jigsaw from behind and who acts her age. She
   peeks, she is told off, and she is the first to hand Lusk a piece without
   looking. That gives scene 3 a child doing the trust-making, and it gives
   the birds' fear a small, funny cost. Give Lusk a table of her own too: who
   in the Concern raised her, and who waits at home?

2. **[must-fix] The naming rule contradicts the hiding rule.** Birds are named
   for "the place of your first cache, given at fledging". A name that tells
   everyone where your first cache is would be the one thing the birds never
   say aloud. Either the fledging cache is a public, deliberately abandoned
   decoy (a ritual first spend, which suits the culture and costs nothing), or
   the names are something else. State which in scene 1 or 2. Seven-Holes
   doesn't fit the pattern at all. Make it a place name or explain it. It
   reads as a nickname.

3. **[must-fix] Who pays, and do they consent?** Cistern pays, and the moot
   counts the loss in scene 6. But the bible never shows the flock agreeing.
   Seven-Holes says spending one cache steals from every bird, and the bible
   never answers why a shown cache harms the others. Give a reason: for
   example, a spent table is public knowledge in the flock's shared inventory.
   Then write the vote. Someone who loses by it should be left unreconciled at
   the end. Also decide whether the many caches Lusk already spent by fixing
   things count as a cost she owes. They are the first payment, and the story
   should carry them to the end.

4. **[should] The birds' language needs one real strain.** The bible says gaze
   is "the first and loudest fact", but nothing shows what the birds can't say.
   Give them a verb system where every perception verb carries a possessive
   (to see-from, to be seen-from) and no neutral "look" exists. Lusk's
   translation fails on the page, and she can't say "I only looked" without
   saying "I took". That shows the culture through talk and costs no glossary
   entries. Also define "Reach" (Seven-Holes "speaks Reach"). It is used as the
   ledger row name and as a language, and neither is explained. Cut it or add a
   glossary entry with a Job.

5. **[should] Lusk is a function in the wrong places.** Her want (the Office),
   her wound (Gennet) and her puzzle are good. But her change of mind is in
   scene 8, after the proof, so the turn happens to her. Move a smaller
   personal turn earlier: scene 4, when she is tempted to edit, should also
   cost her something about Gennet. She asks Cistern whether the grid on
   Gennet was needed, and Cistern, who can't know, refuses to say. That makes
   scene 8's report the answer to a question the reader has already heard.

6. **[could] The ending risks "contact ends in mutual understanding".** The
   cancelled Remediation and the gift of a cache are tidy. Keep a residue:
   Cistern's table is complete and spent in one moment, and she now has a
   witness who holds it for a generation. But the Concern still sends agents,
   and the Desk's message says only that the Office is "reviewing". Don't have
   it confirm the prior is changed.
