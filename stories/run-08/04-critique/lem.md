# Lem lens: stage 4 critique of the run-08 bible

Blind spots: I favour essay and document over scene, I can undervalue the
comedy of a competent narrator, and I distrust any resolution that warms. Let
Le Guin and Tchaikovsky weigh the bird interiors, and let the First Reader
rule on whether Lusk is a person.

1. [must-fix] **The birds understand too much, and too conveniently.** The
   twist needs them to know, by day nine, that Lusk carries a watcher, that it
   reports home, and that it logs sight and sound but not touch. That is the
   exact hole in the Hessary rules, found by a people with no access to the
   Concern. The bible never says how they know. As written, the alien is
   reading our rulebook. Suggestion: show the discovery as clumsy experiment.
   Cistern tries handing Lusk things from behind, for reasons of her own
   (trust, manners), and notices that Lusk's certainty doesn't move when she
   touches. Then the birds' model of the installation is wrong in some detail
   that never matters (they think it is a second bird). Their mistake is the
   joke, and Lusk never corrects it.

2. [must-fix] **The proof is the installation grading itself.** "Under one in
   10^45" is the installation's own model of a crisis world, the same model
   that logged these birds as primitive and starving. The reader will ask why
   that model is trusted now. The number flips from +40 to -5 and Lusk calls it
   the first uncertainty of her life, but she is simply certain in the other
   direction, on a number she can't audit. Make this explicit in scene 7 and
   don't let her tidy it. Her line "I had never been uncertain in my working
   life" should be followed by her noticing that she still isn't. That is the
   irony the bible lacks, and it lets the ending land without anyone having
   understood the birds.

3. [must-fix] **Scene 2 carries five rules.** Hessary, why not exactly 1, the
   120 days, why a seen cache is spent, and the audit-only rule are all
   explained in one conversation between Lusk and Cistern. Several of them
   are why-not-just questions the reader needs answered earlier, in
   particular "why doesn't Lusk simply tell the Desk the table exists?" (her
   word isn't evidence; only the log is). Split it. Put the Hessary story in
   its own scene (it is the one place she sounds like a person, and the
   style guide asks for scenes, not sentences). Put the audit rule where she
   tries to report something and the Desk replies that it can't be filed.

4. [should] **The ending resolves too much.** Remediation cancelled, table
   done, prior wrong, a gift, and the Office reviewing every issued prior:
   that is the Concern learning, the birds trusted, the heroine converted. It
   edges toward banned "contact that ends in mutual understanding". Make the
   Desk's message a bureaucratic non-answer: the Office will review, and
   meanwhile the next agent is issued 39. Hessary was a real famine, and the
   Concern's rule killed no one on this world, so the story shouldn't convict
   it cleanly. Let Lusk's report be filed and not read.

5. [should] **Let the form carry the gap.** You have day-and-number headings.
   Add the installation's log as the second text: three or four short
   entries (under 40 words each) per section, flat and itemised: "Subject
   avoided observer. Cart removed. Evidence: weak." Every scene where her
   hands learn something has no log entry, or an entry that records only the
   birds turning away. The reader sees what the Concern knows against what
   Lusk knows, and the touch channel is made explicit by what the page
   omits. This is cheaper than explanation and funnier than the voice alone.

6. [could] **Keep one thing about the birds unexplained.** Everything about
   them resolves to one principle (gaze is theft, knowledge is half of
   possession), which is a human privacy culture with the dial turned. Leave
   the table's purpose unexplained to Lusk, and to us. Why the pure elements,
   why 118 to 120 only count live, what Cistern sees in them: Lusk watches
   it and doesn't know. The cache she is given and must never look at should
   stay unlooked at.

**Ledger check:** every row can be made explicit on the page without
spoiling the alien, provided the explicit part is the mechanism (touch, sight,
number) and not the birds' reasons. Rows Twist and The proof need the fixes
in notes 1 and 2.
