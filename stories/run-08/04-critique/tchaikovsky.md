# Stage 4 critique: Tchaikovsky lens (the Naturalist)

Blind spot: I trust that minds can be reached, so I may be too kind to a contact that resolves in one scene. Lem and Watts should push on that. I also read the birds, not Lusk's side, so Egan owns the arithmetic.

The scrub-jay root is the best thing in the bible. Re-caching only in jays that have stolen, forty million years of mutual theft, gaze as the loudest fact about anyone: the culture really does follow from the animal. My notes are about where it stops following.

1. **[must-fix] The body plan doesn't reach the technology.** Furnaces, glass, a particle accelerator and a table of pure elements are made by an animal with a beak and feet, and the bible never says how. Cistern purifies 117 elements and witnesses 118 to 120, but nothing says what her hands are. Give the animal a method: feet for grip, the beak for fine work, tools held in the foot, and a division of labour between two birds, one working while the other faces away. Show it in the workshop scene. The pass-it-from-behind trust ritual could be the same skill: a handover with no look is also how an instrument gets passed in a bench job.

2. **[must-fix] The twist skips the birds' inference.** "We hide from the thing that looks out of you" is the story's turn, and the bible gives the birds no way to reach it. They must detect a second watcher in a creature that looks like one. Biology supplies it. They see ultraviolet and track gaze first, so the installation, behind her left eye, could show up as a gaze that doesn't match her attention: her eyes rest on a wall while something still tracks the cache. Or it could emit in the UV like a survey probe, and the birds already hide from those. Put the discovery in scene 4 or 5 as the birds' own failed experiment, with Cistern doing it quietly. At present the reader gets Cistern's conclusion and none of her reasoning. That is the Elder Race move, two sides seeing one event, and it is currently wasted.

3. **[should] The birds are right about too much, too early.** Their mistakes are mostly the thief misreading, which scene 3 turns into trust. Give them a mistake the biology causes and that costs something. The jigsaw is the obvious one: a picture assembled face up in the open is, to a bird, a hoard of hidden pieces exposed to the sky. They might think she is bait for the survey probes, or testing them. Let one flock act on that wrongly and lose a cache.

4. **[should] Cistern's choice needs more than "she has had no witness."** A mate's death, nine years of an unwitnessed table, is human grief and tidy. Make it avian. A bird who dies with no trusted one takes her caches with her, so the table's real stake is that she is the last who knows it. Lusk's installation is the first witness she can have who will outlive her, because the log goes home and stays. That is the reason the cost is bearable to her, and the bible should say it before scene 7. The price is still real: Cistern spends her own life's work to be recorded by the thing she hid from.

5. **[should] The ending leaves the installation alive and the birds' world surveyed.** If Lusk looks at her own cache, it is spent. Why does Cistern trust a woman whose eye still reports home? Say what happens to it after day 120: switched off, removed, or kept. If it stays, the gift is an act of trust that Cistern knows is risky, and Lusk's "Don't look" becomes the story's joke and its dread together.

6. **[could] Cut the bureaucracy where the birds should be.** Eight scenes, the Hessary history, the Desk, the moot and the arithmetic will crowd 3,000 to 7,500 words. Seven-Holes "speaks Reach", but Reach isn't in the glossary or explained. Either cut the line or say what Reach is and why Lusk can understand it. Keep the birds' humour (gossip told facing away, moots held facing outward) in at least three scenes so they aren't only a foil for a funny narrator.
