# Watts lens: stage 4 critique of the run-08 bible

Blind spots: I lean toward the cold reading and underrate ritual and joy, so
Le Guin should check the intimacy scenes. I'm not the lens for the Bayes
arithmetic or the physics; Egan should check both. I've kept my notes to the
biology and the minds.

1. **[must-fix] "Spent" has no reason while the birds also move caches daily.**
   The bible's ordinary day includes "hide things; move them". Real western
   scrub-jays re-cache the moment the watcher leaves (Emery & Clayton 2001,
   *Nature* 414:443), so a seen cache should be moved, not abandoned. The
   reader will ask "why not just move it?" Suggestion: scene 1 shows the bird
   trying to move the cache while Lusk watches. Moving under a watcher spends
   the new site too, and the bird gives up. Cistern's "You spent it" then
   follows from what the reader has just seen.

2. **[must-fix] Cistern's table can't exist as written.** "Every element
   through 117 in pure form" isn't possible. Everything past fermium is made
   an atom at a time and decays in seconds or less. Suggestion: the table is
   a record of witnessed syntheses. Each element counts when a trusted one
   sees it made, and the cache is the witness's memory plus a few atoms.
   That fits the culture (possession is knowledge) and makes her mate's
   death cost her the witnessing. It also makes the climax something that
   happens, not something displayed.

3. **[must-fix] Day nine has no mechanism, so the twist is asserted.**
   "We hide from the thing that looks out of you" needs a bird experiment on
   the page. Jays track who watched which cache and re-cache only against
   those watchers (Dally, Emery & Clayton 2006, *Science* 312:1662), and
   ravens separate knowers from guessers (Bugnyar 2011). So the birds can
   test it: show Lusk a decoy with her eyes shut and with her eyes open, and
   see which one the installation's reaction follows. Lusk narrates the test
   as a rude game and misses what it proved.

4. **[must-fix] The trust rule restates itself instead of giving a reason.**
   "What a trusted one knows counts as yours" says what, not why. Selective
   logic: a bird holding thousands of sites (the hippocampal load is real in
   caching corvids) loses them with age, and the stolen-from lineage has no
   other insurance. Trust is external memory, and a pair is one memory in two
   heads. Cistern is "old for a bird, grey at the throat": let Lusk find, by
   touch, a site Cistern has forgotten. That gives the table a second
   deadline and explains why "dies with no trusted ones" is the worst death.

5. **[should] Lusk is too coherent.** "She can see herself reasoning like a
   conspiracy theorist and can't stop" is clinical insight (closer to OCD with
   good insight than to a fixed belief). A certain mind would confabulate:
   each vanishing cart is trauma, shame or a trick, with a fluent reason
   every time. Let the narration give the reasons, in the sample paragraph's
   register, and let the reader catch what her hands know. The few-years-later
   teller can laugh at them without having felt them as symptoms.

6. **[should] Scene 8 flinches at the Reach.** A single honest report makes
   the Office "review every prior", and that is a tidy reform. Overconfidence
   is selected for where the prize is large against the cost of error
   (Johnson & Fowler 2011, *Nature* 477:317), and Hessary's two billion dead
   are still the strongest argument. The likelier next step is that the Office
   fixes the hole: it logs touch. Let the Desk's message say so, lightly. The
   birds' trust channel is then also spent, and the price of the ending is
   larger than one table.
