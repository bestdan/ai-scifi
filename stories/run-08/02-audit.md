# Stage 2 audit: run-08

The Cartographer. 30 cards from 10 lenses. Futurity floor F3, judged at a
length of about 5,250 words, the midpoint of 3,000 to 7,500.

## The seed's own map

The seed is charted ground before any card touches it. Every card inherits
this, so I don't repeat it per card unless a card is unusually close.

- Eric Frank Russell, "...And Then There Were None" (*Astounding*, June
  1951), high confidence. A Terran fleet comes to bring a lost colony into
  the empire and finds it thriving without them. It is the canonical "world
  doing fine without help" story, and it is comic. Rajaniemi named it.
- The Strugatskys, *Hard to Be a God* (1964) and the Progressor stories,
  high. They are the benevolent meddling service, played straight.
- Banks, "The State of the Art" (1989) and *Inversions* (1998), high.
  Contact and Special Circumstances, a service that chooses to leave Earth
  alone.
- Arthur C. Clarke, "Rescue Party" (*Astounding*, May 1946), high. Aliens
  arrive to rescue humanity before the Sun goes nova and find it has already
  rescued itself.
- Keith Laumer's Retief stories (from 1960, collected in *Envoy to New
  Worlds*, 1963), high. Comic interstellar diplomatic meddling, with a
  deadpan competent agent inside a foolish service. This is the nearest
  register match after Murderbot.

**What the map leaves open:** almost all of this prior art is about the
service's *decision*: whether to meddle, and whether to leave. Little of it
is about the *mechanism* by which a service comes to believe a fine world
needs it. Fifteen of the thirty cards went there.

Why: N, nothing near. F, the main minds aren't human. R, hiding accelerators from orbital survey is a stretch. Y, the bird's gift costs it a lifetime's work. E, "primitive" becomes an artefact of looking. S, a specific bird's collection.
To push past the map: she has a cache too, something the birds want to see, and showing it costs her as much.

## Convergence

The lenses worked blind. The surfaces spread well: no water, no names on
the attractor list, and no valleys except one beekeeper. **The cores did
not spread.** Three shapes took most of the run.

1. **The service's own instrument manufactures the need. 15 of 30 cards.**
   Weekdays, Roofers, The Mine Is Me, Presenting as Fine, The Cold Minute,
   Cromwell's Rule, The Glass Takes Months, Gain, Intake, Fair Copy, The
   Gradient, The Tun Season, Arrhythmia, The Share and Cached. The
   instruments are a model, a doctrine, a prior, a clock rate, a hearing
   aid, a census, a difference-sensor, a survey record or an ECG. Every one
   answers "why does a good service think a fine world is broken?" with
   "its measurement is biased". It is the seed's most literal mechanism,
   and it is also the second half of two lenses' obvious card (Egan's and
   Lem's: the service is the broken one). **Verdict: an attractor for this
   seed, and a red flag.** Individually several of these cards are
   uncharted in published SF, but a story built on one of them will feel
   like the obvious answer to anyone who has read the seed, and the human
   has. Prefer the cards where the misreading is the setup and the story
   lives somewhere else: The Cold Minute (the town's custom), Cached (the
   cost of being seen), The Tun Season (what survives the fold).
2. **The agent made smaller to fit the job. 10 cards.** The Undersigned
   (parts leave), Partials (ablation), Coin-Op (serial records), Narrow
   Stairs (cut to a fifth), The Tun Season (packing light), Below the Line
   (dimmed), Once-Fired (fixed), The Glass Takes Months (slowed), and, by
   harvest, Frame Inspection and The Mine Is Me. **Verdict: a model
   attractor.** Six different stimuli produced the same arc of a big mind
   choosing what to lose. The comic register pulled there: a competent
   narrator who is less than usual is the easiest deadpan to write. The
   next run should steer away.
3. **Staged crises, faked by the helper or the helped. 4 written cards,
   plus the obvious card of 5 lenses.** The Live Find, The Favour Standard
   (whose third-order consequence is The Live Find's, written blind),
   Coin-Op (staged collapses to summon a record) and Something Worth Fixing
   (the temptation to fake a need). **Verdict: the brief's most literal
   answer.** Watts, Egan, Chiang, Lem and Rajaniemi all wrote "fake a
   crisis to justify the trip" as their obvious card. Something Worth
   Fixing is the closest written match to that obvious card. The Live Find
   and The Favour Standard invert who fakes, which is real distance, but on
   the same ground.
4. **The visitor harvested by the locals. 5 cards.** The Mine Is Me (ore),
   Frame Inspection (honey), The Share (its packing eaten), Coin-Op (a
   vending machine) and Fair Copy (its fix absorbed). It overlaps cluster
   2. **Verdict: an attractor,** and a funny one, but Asimov and
   Tchaikovsky wrote the same joke, about an agent who is the product, on
   different stimuli.
5. **The brief's tone played back as the protagonist.** About 20 cards
   describe their narrator with the brief's register words ("dry",
   "deadpan", "competent", "reluctant", "overqualified"). "Dry,
   overqualified" appears verbatim twice (Fair Copy, Arrhythmia). Fair
   Copy's agent "wants the job closed and to go home", which is close to
   the Murderbot premise the brief excludes. **Verdict: a wording
   attractor,** the brief's twin of "the rubric played back". The register
   belongs in the prose, not in the character's adjectives.
6. **Paperwork as the comic form.** Reports, a fitting log, a
   questionnaire, a report form with no box, edit logs, a notepad: 9
   cards. With the seed's agent already a functionary, this re-enters the
   **functionary-disclosure** attractor: Fair Copy (the lie in the report),
   The Cold Minute (whether to report), The Live Find (whether to pretend),
   Presenting as Fine (the report counted against him) and Something Worth
   Fixing (a fraudulent need). The cluster is hard and was already listed.
   Five cards hit it anyway.

**Existing entries hit again:**
- the functionary deciding whether to disclose (above);
- the certifying institution: Once-Fired's charter signatory, Intake's
  census, Standing Orders' Glasshall copyists;
- law, votes and consent: It Was My Idea's swap counted as consent,
  Once-Fired, Something Worth Fixing's fostering law, and Whose Grown-Up's
  one vote and legal single;
- the past self as authority: It Was My Idea's sealed note, Coin-Op's
  notepad, Partials' forty-year-old dossier;
- copies who merge back: Same Scent and Narrow Stairs, both named;
- reversible minds: Once-Fired, named as its inverse;
- the citation reflex: the Strugatskys' Progressors on 9 cards and Banks's
  Contact on 6, as each card's "suspected prior art" whatever its own
  novum. They are the seed's prior art, not the cards'.

**Not hit:** water, the 400,000-person nation, the attractor names, the
dementia grandparent, the role reversal over a parent, Run-06's devices,
and the rubric played back. Frame Inspection's beekeeper is a one-card
brush with the pastoral polity and isn't counted.

**Did the assignments spread the cards?** They spread the surfaces: worm,
kiln, spectroscopy, bells, siphonophore, night market, scriptorium,
jukebox, pacemaker and bees all left a visible mark, and nobody went to
water. They did not spread the cores. The seed's two literal questions
(why does the service think it's needed, and what does the agent do about
it) drew the instrument-bias answer and the faked-crisis answer from every
lens. The comic register then pulled the agents toward being diminished.

## Collisions

1. **Cromwell's Rule × Cached** (egan × tchaikovsky). Her installed prior
   needs one 10⁴⁰-to-1 observation, and on this world anything she looks at
   vanishes. The only overwhelming evidence available is a cache a bird
   chooses to show her, which costs the bird its life's work. Neither card
   alone makes the decisive proof something that must be given, at a cost.
   Bayesian rigor and intimacy become the same act. Estimated N5 F4 R4 Y5
   E4 S5 → 37/40.
2. **Once-Fired × The Glass Takes Months** (banks × egan). The service
   needs a signatory it can't revise. A fast mind that slows to local speed
   becomes mortal, and a self that can end is the only kind whose signature
   counts. Mortality as the credential is uncharted. Each part is
   irreversibility on its own; together they turn "dies on schedule" into
   the mission's purpose. Estimated N4 F4 R4 Y5 E4 S5 → 35/40.
3. **Below the Line × Cromwell's Rule** (vinge × egan). The installed prior
   lives in fast cognition. North of the line she is too dim to do the sum
   that keeps it in place, and the belief loosens. A belief that survives
   intelligence but not stupidity is uncharted, and it turns Vinge's zone
   into an epistemic tool. Estimated N5 F4 R4 Y4 E5 S3 → 34/40.
4. **Something Worth Fixing × Once-Fired** (le-guin × banks). The service
   fosters its children on client worlds, and this world fires its adults.
   The foster brother who wants to leave would be the service's first
   unrevisable member, the signatory it has never had. Fosterage as the
   pipeline for irreversibility is uncharted. It is heavy for 7,500 words.
   Estimated N4 F4 R3 Y5 E4 S5 → 34/40.
5. **Coin-Op × The Share** (vinge × tchaikovsky). Honeyguide-descended
   locals have learned to stage situations that summon a serial mind's
   records, and each record that comes up owes them a share. Mutualism with
   a jukebox mind is uncharted. It is funny, but it stacks two
   harvested-agent cards. Estimated N4 F4 R4 Y4 E3 S3 → 30/40.

are broken by status, uncharted first.

   Uncharted.

5. **le-guin/Something Worth Fixing.** N4 F3 R4 Y5 E4 S5 → 34/40.
