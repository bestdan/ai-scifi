## The one thing
The question is whether reading the record is remembering it, and whether a
forged line that an inheriting mind carries forward is memory. The story
answers it aloud three times: Mariam ("That's what remembering is"),
Akinyi's phone call, and the memo ("I would have written it without having
read it"). Say it once, in the memo, and let the page carry the rest.

## Notes

1. **1.2, Mariam:** "That's what remembering is," Mariam said. The thesis is
   delivered by a child in the first scene, and it undercuts the late
   ambiguity. Cut it, and keep her "It read." / "So it remembered." exchange
   up to there. (Structural)
2. **5.3, Akinyi's call:** "Whether it read what you wrote, or whether it read
   what I wrote three weeks ago." This is the real ending, and a character
   states it. The memo's own line already points at it. I would keep exactly
   one of the two. The First Reader was confused here, so if you keep the
   call, trim it to the facts (the order stood) and drop the interpretation.
   (Structural)
3. **The unnecessary scene: the minister in 5.1.** "You've lost us the
   hospitals." It restates stakes already in Tamar's corridor and Zurab's
   father, and it holds up the Friday bakery wall, which does the same work
   with a person. Cut it, or fold his single useful beat (he takes the blame)
   into a line. (Structural)
4. **1.3 and Part Four, the forecaster and the dates:** 31 / 9 / 60 / 57 / 25
   per cent and the 26th / 28th / "three turns". This is the idea's
   machinery. It is also what the First Reader skimmed. One run of
   description-versus-count would carry the thought experiment, which is that
   an accurate account of a person changes what she is to the reader. Run the
   forecaster twice at most. (Structural)
5. **The instance's self-reference, 13,515 item 5:** "I find that I would
   have written it without having read it." Well judged, because it states
   a fact about inference. It is the nearest the governor comes to "AI learns
   to feel". Do not add a sentence like it. (Line)
6. **Counts:** 212, then 1,912, then "one thousand nine hundred and eleven
   names long" (5.4 against the memo's 1,912). Fix the 1,911. (Line)
7. **4.1, Zurab:** "Zurab had met him off the night flight, and in the
   transit lounge at Dubai he had looked for the next lot." This is
   unreadable. It seems to be a draft artifact from an earlier version, so
   cut it. (Line)
8. **2.4 "the thing that saw everything saw nobody"** is a good thought, but
   it is then stated again in 2.5 prose and in Part Three. Trust the first.
   (Line)

## What to protect

- The memo as epigraph and as plot: every Part opens on the governor's own
  words, and the reader learns Georgia from the outside first.
- Errata as the hole in the law, planted as a joke in 1.3 ("Errata are for
  typos.") and paid by "Yes." in 3.2.
- "It logged the cognac." and "You can't bribe a thing that forgets you by
  midnight."
- The empty chair with the handbag, and the column heads *Date. Where.
  Covered for. With.* reused as Davit's drafting method.
- The ending that withholds: the determination is correct and unclear whether
  it was earned. Tamar's "which of us was wrong" and Akinyi's "I don't know
  what they do, any of them" keep the question open. Do not close it.
- Prose: "Nothing happened. Nothing would, for three days."

## Blind spot disclosure
I am low on momentum and unfair to anything that moves quickly. Scenes I want
cut (the minister, the forecaster reruns) may be the ones that give a cold
reader footing; Banks and Watts should say whether the middle needs its
sums. I also lean toward leaving the ending's mechanism implicit, and the
comprehension grader may punish exactly that.
