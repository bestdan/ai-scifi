## The one thing
Make the "lying truthfully" exploit legible as machinery. The 31 / 9 / 60 forecaster runs (1.3) are the story's central trick, and the cold reader lost them. Say what the standard turns on. Article 9(2) asks whether the nation "can no longer perform" the function. Forward-looking facts such as "No succession. No legal basis." therefore read as incapacity, and present-tense facts such as "named, verified, working" read as capacity. One plain line from Davit does it. Cut the repeated runs to two.

## Notes
1. **1.3, forecaster scenario (structural):** Same-fact, different-framing scores (9 vs 60) arrive with no mechanism, so the reader cannot tell why accuracy lowers the number. The first reader skimmed the second run. Give the forecaster's reason line, as it appears in 4.5 ("Code s. 14(2). An application lacking Part C is incomplete."). That is the right grain.
2. **Part 5, 13,515 determination (structural):** "Shifts described in Schedules 4 and 7 at Bagratashen appear on the commons roster as worked by other persons. The discrepancy is referred..." is the system turning on the people it just licensed. It gets one flat line and no follow-through. That is the best unexploited consequence in the story: the Armenian nurses who sold their shifts are now a commons audit finding. Pay it off in Eka's or the ward sister's thread, or in the 13,516 memo.
3. **The pending list (structural):** The committee's schedule sits in "the national gazette's pending list" and everyone has read it by the 26th. That lets the Prosecutor see filings three days before the instance, which undercuts Rauf's "Saturday they read it". Either the lag is a Nairobi-only secret, with the national list sealed, or the prosecutor's exposure starts on filing. Choose one and state it.
4. **13,515 item 5, "I find that I would have written it without having read it" (line-level):** This is the instance reflecting on itself, and the bible says it is not human in costume. The standing-order idea needs no help. "This sentence has appeared in each memo since 17 February" is enough, and a literal mind states the count.
5. **1.3 and the hearing's costing stretch (line-level):** The 0.9 per cent of GDP and 214 posts digression earns nothing later, apart from "214 posts" in the costing note. Trim it. The Registry procedure in 1.1 and 3.2 does pay off (the five boxes, "omitted words restored"), so keep it.
6. **Numbers (line-level):** 212, 1,912 and "one thousand nine hundred and eleven names long" will read as an error to a systems reader. Say that Ketevan's entry is appended last and the count was 1,911 before it. Item 9 already hints at this, so make it deliberate.
7. **Davit working (structural):** His craft shows in the "unable" to count rewrite (1.3) and the Świecko discovery (4.5), and both are good. In Part 3 he mostly watches. Let him catch one concrete thing at desk 12, such as a gap in her method or a detail of the form.

## What to protect
- The Świecko schedules planted in 1.2 and turned in 4.5: Schedule C names the drivers, so "Part C" is the lever. That is the story's key exploit, and it was set up as a throwaway.
- "I corrected a numeral." / "There is always a numeral." And the form's five boxes, with "Omitted words restored".
- The inherited standing order that outlives Akinyi's "patch", closed in 13,516 with "It was carried in 22 memos."
- "It logged the cognac." And Rauf: "Then your king owns every road out of here."
- The Registry rap on the desks, "a dry little sound like rain beginning".
- Akinyi's refusal to promise a line: "I would be carrying your line before I'd read the night's tray."

## Blind spot disclosure
I want more mechanism, and the first reader's boredom notes (the meeting-room scene, the costing stretch) are the corrective. Pushing note 1 harder would add exposition to Part 1, which is already the slowest part. Chiang and Asimov should weigh whether one clean line does it. I did not judge the emotional ending (Ketevan, Mariam) or the prose beyond the lines named.
