## The one thing
Let the carrier bag be the form. The cool-bag passed on a rota, Lamara's rota book, Ketevan's ledger and Akinyi's thirty-one exercise books are all one image: keeping and carrying what the record won't hold. The plot machinery crowds that image. Cut the arithmetic (the forecaster runs, the 26th/28th/"read Thursday" counting) to what a reader can feel. Give the freed room to the people who pay.

## Notes

1. **Structural, Part One meeting-room scene (the forecaster, 31/9/60):** This is the only place the story is all spear, a number being optimised. The cold reader skimmed it, and I agree. Davit's sentence-editing ("unable" becomes a count of 70 per cent) is the story's best craft idea. Keep that and cut two of the three forecaster runs.
2. **Structural, the dates:** "twenty-sixth", "twenty-eighth", "three turns", "read on 3 March". A society's calendar should be lived (Saturday = the day Davit's schedule is read), not recited. Anchor deadlines to scene-dated headers and let the dialogue stop counting.
3. **Structural, who pays, the Armenian side:** Lilit, the Armenian nurse whose shift Eka covers and who is "at home with a child or a second job", is paid in cash and is exposed by the determination ("The discrepancy is referred to the commons hospital administration"). She is never seen again. One short scene or line of her afterwards would answer "do they consent?". The Georgian women's consent is well handled: Eka says no, and Davit files her anyway.
4. **Line-level, everyone is eloquent:** Lamara ("Everything here is a favour"), Rauf ("a list with the names rubbed off"), Tamar, Akinyi and the Telavi director all speak in finished aphorisms. Let one of them be inarticulate or dodge. Eka's "It's one thing. If you pull it, it all comes" is the right register: quick, a nurse asking.
5. **Line-level, the theme said aloud:** Tamar's text, "One of us was wrong. I've read it four times and I can't tell which", and the paragraph in 5.5 explaining why Davit stays silent ("a story with a hero and a wronged boy in it, already told"). The scene already does this. Cut the gloss.
6. **Line-level, language:** "unwritten" is spoken once, by Lamara. "Paper is private" is the title, and it is spoken by Mariam. The world's key taboo (never ask for a favour in writing) is lived beautifully in Lamara's kitchen. Give the vocabulary one more domestic use, say a neighbour or a mother, so it reads as a shared word rather than the author's.
7. **Line-level, broken sentence (4.1):** "Zurab had met him off the night flight, and in the transit lounge at Dubai he had looked for the next lot." Nothing makes sense of this. The cold reader flagged it. Cut it.
8. **Line-level, Akinyi:** She is a person (the brown kit, the grandson's number 9, "I was sick in that sink"). But her confession in 3.3 is long and complete. Let her leave one thing out.

## What to protect
- The bus: "It went in the laps of nurses, by rota, a week each, on the bus they took to work." Also the cow-in-a-lift joke the boy never finishes.
- Lamara's wine "in a two-litre water bottle... with a twist of cling film under the cap", and "It's last year's. It's terrible."
- Ketevan's ledger columns, "*Date. Where. Covered for. With.*", and the 16 May 2054 entry, "Alone. Two post-op bad... Both kept. Home 08:40."
- Zurab's father on the bakery wall: "She'll be cross... She'll still come."
- The Armenian ward sister: "I haven't chosen a nurse in ten years." This is the clearest picture of what cession costs.
- Davit says nothing to Mariam about the concert, and Lamara's door stays shut. Don't resolve either.
- The mended radio, "everything visible", set against a world that can't see.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour slowness and gathering, so I may under-weight how much the deadline pressure serves a reader who needs a spine. I can't judge how hard the forecaster logic is to follow for a reader who likes it. Let Egan and Stephenson push back on note 1. I don't judge the mechanism's rigour (the lag, the errata seam, standing orders).
