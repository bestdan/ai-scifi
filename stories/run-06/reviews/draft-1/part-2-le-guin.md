# Part Two: Schedules. Le Guin lens (per-part check)

Blind spots: I read slowly and care little for the lag mechanics. Egan and Asimov should judge whether the 8 Feb erratum timeline holds.

**What to protect.** The bus rota: "Your week," and the bag passing from lap to lap while the cow joke carries on. This is the carrier bag as the novum's social form, and nobody explains it. Also the nurse's "If you write anybody, don't write her. She's on her own with a boy", and the biro correction in the Georgian protocol poster.

1. **Armenia is too clean (structural, must-fix).** The commons hospital "smelled of nothing at all", every post filled, no waiting list, and the only friction is a gruff sister. The bible says ceding cost Armenia its say over care (Tamar's argument). Part Two shows only the benefit. The ward sister and the absent Armenian nurses, who are paid to stay home while Georgians do their work, are the people who pay for it. Give one of them a small, concrete loss, such as a protocol she can no longer change. Otherwise Tamar's stance in Part Four will read as sentiment.

2. **Everyone states the thesis (line-level, must-fix).** Rauf's cousin ("He's bored to death... You're helped, and then you're scheduled") lands because it is a person. Lamara's phone call then says the same thing in the abstract ("I'd get scheduled... a mind in Nairobi decides which ward"). Zurab's "both" repeats it a third time. Cut Lamara's version back to the stove and her mother's letter, and let her call carry her anger at Davit rather than the argument.

3. **Davit files first and asks second (character, decide it).** He promises himself he will read Schedule 3 to Rauf "before anyone else could", but the slip had already gone in at 16:40. Either make that the visible failure, or have him read it to Rauf first. Rauf's "Don't name me" is the consent question the story is about, so the order matters.

4. **The seam is recognised too fast (pacing, carry into Part Three).** Mariam's "it remembered" and the kitchen scene reach the clerk, the desk and Akinyi in about six paragraphs. The reader has been handed the twist. Part Three should let Akinyi's own account re-teach it, with details only she has (the exercise books, Ulaanbaatar), rather than confirming what Davit already deduced.

Taboo to keep in mind: Rauf keeps a written ledger of debts, and the favour-in-writing taboo hasn't shown yet. One line separating private paper from filed paper would settle it.
