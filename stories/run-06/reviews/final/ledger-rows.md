# Graded ledger rows: final

| Row | The fact |
|---|---|
| Premise | A memoryless global governor sees only the written record and must refuse a nation's petition unless the record proves incapacity. To ask for help, Georgia must write down the hidden coping that proves it doesn't need it. |
| Stakes | If cession is granted, hospitals improve and the web is dissolved. If refused, the hospitals keep failing, at 2,100 deaths a year, and the recorded nurses face prosecution. Davit stakes his mother's friends, his own judgement, and her refusal to be written. |
| Why | The charter's drafters made the governor memoryless so that it could hold no grudge, no bribe and no secret, and made it refuse power so that it could never swallow nations. Distrust of every earlier government's conflicts of interest built it. |
| Twist | Before: the memo knew something not yet in the record, and Davit and the reader fear the governor secretly remembers, which would make the charter a lie. After: the "memory" is one clerk's nightly erratum, a human mercy carried through an unlawful seam for nine years. The memos' moments of apparent continuity reread as Akinyi's hand. |
| Ending | The determination refuses cession and licenses the pharmacists' ring as commons carriage of goods, legal and now governed. The nurses aren't covered, because persons aren't carriage. The licence follows from Davit's choice to describe the ring as carriage. Akinyi's line that night was about Patagonia, but her line three weeks earlier kept the ring alive to be written, so her part stays uncertain. The cost: refused hospitals, prosecuted nurses, his mother written down, the ring absorbed by degrees. |
| Reach | Recording moves 1,900 people's unofficial work into the governed world: pharmacists become licensed carriers, retired nurses become scheduled, and 400 nurses become prosecutable. It also moves where Georgia's sovereignty ends, one clause at a time. |
