# First Reader report: "Paper Is Private", draft 3

I read it once, start to finish (the file came in three chunks, but I did not go back). It is long. I stopped at the end of the Sunday 2 March scene.

## Where I got lost

1. **Opening pages, the paragraph starting "Nothing happened. Nothing would, for three days."** I did not know what the "governor of the commons" was, why it was a "new mind every morning", or why filing something meant a three-day wait. I read the next two paragraphs ("The mind running now was instance 13,485...") and it clicked. Worked out from context, about a page in. For that page I was following a man who was nervous about typing a name and I didn't know why it mattered.
2. **"A schedule of evidence"** (opening). I was unsure whether it was a form or a document type. The paragraph defines it, but I held it loosely until the template columns appeared. Worked out.
3. **Davit's reason for deleting Lamara's name (the paragraph "He selected the line and deleted it...").** On first read I couldn't tell why a woman *working* on the ward counted against Georgia. It took until the forecaster scene (Friday 10:30) for me to see that "they cope, so Georgia can run its hospitals, so the petition fails". I worked it out there, from Davit's line "If I write them down, we're coping".
4. **The forecaster scene.** I lost the thread on the percentages (31, then 60, then 57, later 25, 40). I understood that how he phrased things moved the number. I never fully understood why "facts that face forward" would move it, though I accepted it. Not worked out, accepted.
5. **"the way she had looked at the three fingers on Friday"** (Wed 14:00, deputy scene). I had no idea what three fingers meant. Nothing earlier showed it. Not worked out.
6. **Saturday 8 February, the "relief stock" scene.** I got that the memo quoted a phrase from the withdrawn schedule, and that Davit was frightened. I did not follow, at first, that a *clerk* could have put it in. The paragraph about errata and "the whole of it, before any instance could" made me guess a person did it; the "which desk the Caucasus intake had always gone to" line confirmed it. Worked out in that same scene.
7. **Schedule numbers.** There are schedules 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, and 5 and 6 never appear. In Part Four I lost track of which was which, and of Part A and Part C. I kept getting the numbers wrong in my head (640, 406, 412, 1,482, 1,912, 400). I followed the *drift* (names, then the licence trick), not the arithmetic.
8. **The Carriers' Code / "carriage of goods" move (Tuesday 25 Feb).** I understood the cleverness only vaguely: that calling the pharmacists "carriage" put them under Nairobi, and that this made the nurses' names necessary. It took Mariam's questions on 1 March to make it solid. Partly worked out there.
9. **Akinyi's call on 1 March about item five.** She says first that instances are "agreeing with me", then that none of them agreed, they just inherited the order. I had to stop and rethink. I am still not sure what she is more upset about. Not fully worked out.
10. **The Świecko timber licence (Sunday 23 Feb, spare room).** It was planted in the first memo and I had forgotten it. When Davit "found the licence" I wasn't sure why it mattered until the next scene. Worked out a few pages later.

## Where I got bored

- **The first hearing (Sat 1 Feb), Tamar's Armenian precedent speech** ("Within three years waiting lists...stroke protocol...half a percentage point"). I was waiting for someone to do something. It is a speech, and the hearing runs long before the corridor scene where she gets personal.
- **Part Four, the phone calls with district directors and the forecaster creeping back to 60.** I was waiting for him to decide something. The Telavi director's list of names woke me up. The forecaster numbers did not.
- **The long list of memo items in the early scenes** (orbit, deep sea, Mongolia). The first couple were fun and the sawn-timber trucks made me smile. By the third memo-in-full I was skimming to the Georgia item.

## Where I was moved, surprised or delighted

- Zurab's father on the heart waiting list: "He said, good, he'll see the cherry blossom... He's never looked at the cherry blossom in his life." This one got me. Also the bakery wall, and "April... They say April."
- The cow-in-a-lift joke in the boy's voice message, which the nurse can never hear the end of ("I don't know how it ends"). It comes back and I was waiting for it. It works as a small, true detail.
- "That's what remembering is," Mariam said (the bus-stop argument). I laughed and then stopped laughing.
- Mariam: "It says it doesn't say. That's different."
- The student sign: OUR HOSPITALS, OUR MISTAKES, and Davit calling it "the best-drafted thing he had seen all week."
- Akinyi's pencil line through the first dot, and "That's what a good night looks like. Exactly like a wasted one." Also the *After* column: "Not known. Not known."
- The ledger entry for 16 May 2054: "Alone. Two post-op bad... Home 08:40," set against the empty chair with the handbag at his school concert. That was the strongest passage for me. Also Lamara raising her glass at the funeral supra "to her nights."
- The woman of fifty on the 1 March border walk: "It was my week, Davit. I'd do it again. Write that down too."
- The radio announcer saying his mother's name aloud in the kitchen in an uncle's voice, and Davit not telling Mariam. I felt that in my chest.
- Rauf on the grey arch: his father put a bottle of cognac on the belt and "It logged the cognac." I laughed.
- Rauf with a number "like a prisoner" and "I'm going to be bored to death", and the Armenian cousin who is "very well. He's bored to death."

## Who I cared about, and whether I'd have kept reading past page 2

**Lamara** first. I cared about her from the first mention, in the kitchen with the hospital soap. Then **Davit**, because he keeps doing the thing he hates and says so. **Eka Beridze** (the nurse with the cow joke), whom I was dreading for the whole last third. **Akinyi**, whom I trusted instantly and then was unsure about, which I liked. **Mariam**, who is sharper than everyone. And Zurab's father, on very little page time.

Would I have kept reading past page 2? **Yes**, though only just. The first page is a man in a cold office with a radiator and a name he can't type, and I liked it, but I didn't understand the stakes. Lamara's name on the second page, and the line about the heat "getting out", kept me. If the first two pages had been only the mind-erasing machinery, I might have skimmed. By the scene with the driver and his father, I was in.

## The question I'm left with

Did it work, in the end, to name his mother, and who did it help? The determination refuses the grant, so the hospitals stay Georgian, and 406 women have letters from the prosecutor. Rauf is legal and bored, and Lamara won't open the door. I don't know if Davit did right, and he doesn't either. What I most want to know is whether Eka loses her licence and her three shifts, and whether Akinyi would ever have carried the addendum. I also wonder whether the thing in Nairobi is really as blind as everyone says. I'm not sure the story wants me to know.
