## The one thing
Give the exploit an immune system, in one line. The Registry already has a "sealed book" of desk keys (3.1). Nine years and 3,106 errata from one key, always "wrong numeral; omitted words restored", is exactly what a sealed book exists to catch. "Nobody has seen it. The diffs are public" answers the public record. It does not answer the book. Say who may open the book and what it takes (an Appeal Panel order, which needs a complaint, which needs someone who noticed). Then Akinyi's nine years is a hole in a real institution rather than a hole in a convenient one.

## Notes
1. **3.1 sealed book (structural):** see above. It is also the best place for the "one other clerk" question. Two keys in one book would be a pattern.
2. **Part C and the occupation (structural):** Davit is a master of framing true facts. Schedule 7's Part C is drafted as carriers only (4.4). Yet the Prosecutor's notice says Eka was "named in a schedule … as having practised as a nurse". Where did the occupation get in? If Schedule 4's cross-reference supplies it, say so. If Davit chose it, the forced choice isn't forced. He could have left "employed by the Care Service" out of Part C.
3. **3.4 vs 13,503 (line-level):** "He had read the second of them every morning for a week" cannot be true. Memo 13,500 reads "None is scheduled", and the "does not follow" line first appears on 17 February. Cut the week.
4. **1.1 "erased at midnight" (line-level):** erasure is at 04:00 Tbilisi, which is 00:00 UTC. A systems reader will stop on it. Write "midnight UTC".
5. **3.1 intake paragraph (line-level):** "The Registry's work had not changed…" repeats 1.1's stamp-and-batch account. The first reader skimmed it. Keep only the flagged-few-hundred sentence. Also trim the 0.9 per cent of GDP in the hearing; only 214 posts pays off.
6. **Davit working (structural, carried forward):** at desk 12 he still doesn't catch anything. One small catch, such as a gap in her method, would fix it.
