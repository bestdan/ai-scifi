## The one thing
Stop the story from saying "I can't tell" three times. Tamar's text ("One of us was wrong. I've read it four times and I can't tell which"), Akinyi's "So I don't know", and Davit's "I don't know if it was right" each close the same question. The bag, the book and the radio already hold it. Keep Akinyi's, which is earned by her exercise books, and cut Tamar's text. I raised this in round 1 and the plan left it alone.

## Notes

1. **Line-level, the silence gloss (5.5):** "a story with a hero and a wronged boy in it, already told, already over". The kitchen scene shows him closing his mouth. This sentence then tells the reader why. It is a gloss the scene doesn't need.
2. **Structural, the Registry hall (3.1):** the paragraph beginning "The Registry's work had not changed" is a briefing, and the cold reader skimmed it. The groundnut clerk and the desk lamps already give the place. Cut the paragraph to the stamping and the "few hundred" Akinyi reads whole.
3. **Line-level, aphorism:** Rauf ("a list with the names rubbed off") and Lamara ("Everything here is a favour") still deliver finished lines. Eka's "It's one thing. If you pull it, it all comes" is the register to hold: quick, practical, a nurse asking. Let Rauf, once, say something a little clumsy.
4. **Line-level, the vocabulary:** "unwritten" is still spoken once, by Lamara. Give it one more mouth, such as the day sister or Zurab's father's neighbour, so it reads as a word the society shares, not the author's coinage.
5. **Structural, Part Four's calendar:** "Wednesday's the twenty-sixth. The last day." is stated once. The Kibera "Anything filed after the twenty-sixth…" speech then restates it. Let that one go. Davit's anxiety is already visible.
6. **Who pays:** Eka and the 406 are named without being asked. This is the story's best honesty. But Lamara and the 640 are also never asked, and the book says the dead woman refused. Don't repair it. Let a reader feel that Davit spends other people's privacy to buy a road.

## What to protect
- "It went in the laps of nurses, by rota, a week each, on the bus they took to work." The cow-in-a-lift joke the boy never finishes.
- Lamara's wine "in a two-litre water bottle... with a twist of cling film under the cap". "It's last year's. It's terrible."
- The ledger: "*Date. Where. Covered for. With.*" and "*Alone. Two post-op bad. Surgeon would not come out, roads. Both kept. Home 08:40.*" Davit's deliberate search past "dozens of other nights" is the right fix.
- Lamara's rota "ruled in the same four columns", and her shut door.
- The Armenian ward sister: "Now both sides of my river have a letter."
- The radio's valves "everything visible", and Mariam's "Why is Grandma in it?", with Davit's silence left unresolved.

## Blind spot disclosure
I favour gathering over spear and under-weight how much the deadline carries a reader who needs a spine. The First Reader's boredom with the hearing and the drafting scenes supports my cuts, but a mechanism-minded lens may want them. I don't judge the errata and lag logic.
