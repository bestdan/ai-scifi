# Stage 4 critique: Watts lens

Blind spots, stated: I lean bleak and under-rate ritual and joy, so I haven't judged whether the supra, the vine or the radios carry enough warmth. I also can't check the law or the grid engineering. I read for minds and for what traits are for.

1. **[must-fix] Ketevan's dates contradict themselves.** The bible has her born 2019 and dying in December 2096, aged 77. The ending's last memo reads "nurse, 2041–2090, deceased." The memo is the story's last image and must match the record Davit built. Make it 2096. Also, the ledger covers 2058–2071, but Davit was born in 2047, so "all his childhood" runs to 2065 at most. State which years he resents. (Akinyi's 3,300 lines also exceeds nine years of nights, about 3,285, even before nights off. Say "some 3,200".)

2. **[must-fix] Part 4 resolves the wound in one scene.** Davit reads a ledger and "stops seeing her absence as a choice against him." That is the style guide's banned single-paragraph epiphany. Real attachment doesn't update on evidence of worth. A grown child can fully accept that the work mattered and still be angry that she chose it. Let him understand the ledger and still resent her. The honest schedule then costs him something personal, and the filing isn't an act of reconciliation. Writing her name against her will should feel like a betrayal and a tribute at once, and he shouldn't know which.

3. **[must-fix] The ending protects the reader.** Akinyi's call ("Patagonia. Not you") lets the system off: the one human seam did not matter this time. It is tidy, and it clears her of the thing the story has been building toward. Nine years and 3,300 unaudited lines have shaped "thousands of mornings", so nobody can say whether the governor's apparent competence is its own. Her test, "would forgetting it kill someone in 72 hours", is a human heuristic with a human's loss-aversion and availability bias. She remembers the cold of Ulaanbaatar and has no tally of the deaths she didn't prevent. Suggestion: the call stays, but she adds that she can't know what the other 3,299 did, and neither can Davit. The question the story asks becomes whether the instance's behaviour is its own, and the answer is no.

4. **[should] The instance's "I" is cheaper than it looks.** A mind that reads 13,485 memos in its own register, in its own first person, every morning, would build a narrative self. This is the same confabulated continuity seen in anterograde amnesiacs such as Clive Wearing, who write "now I am awake for the first time". The bible says it reads predecessors "the way a historian reads", with no feeling for them. Decide why. Either the memo's "I" is a formal convention and the story says so, or the instance does feel authorship and denies it. The second is stranger and truer to how such systems fail. A single memo line like "These are my numbers; I did not write them" would make the point. Mariam's "it remembered" is good: people assign minds to things that answer them. Keep it.

5. **[should] Ask what the web is for.** An informal network of 640 retired nurses, 23 pharmacies and 400 swappers runs on reciprocity and reputation. Reputation requires someone's memory of who owes what. The village is the memory the governor lacks. The bible has the irony and doesn't use it. Say it on the page, perhaps through Lamara's rota, which is a ledger of debts. Also, 74-year-olds on unpaid nights are a clinical risk (circadian fatigue, error rates). The unrecorded web has no error accounting. Let one scene show a near-miss the record can't see, so the nation's pride is not simply right.

6. **[could] Mariam is a case study.** She is 14, wants Yerevan, and watches Korean drama without subtitles, but the bible gives her a single belief to be wrong about. Give her one thing the memo does that she is right about, such as noticing a pattern the adults skip. She may be the only one who reads it as a text and not as an authority.

Biology check: no neuroscience errors found, because the bible makes few claims. Signature question 3, what is the brain doing that the character doesn't know about, is unanswered for Davit. He believes the web is a symptom of failure, and the story never shows him motivated to believe it. Suggest his resentment is the mechanism.
