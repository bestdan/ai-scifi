# Stage 4 critique: Le Guin lens

Blind spots: I read for society, character and who pays. I am weak on the
legal mechanics and the arithmetic (Egan's ground), and I over-favour slow,
gathering scenes at 35,000 words, where Stephenson and Banks may want pace.

1. **[must-fix] Ketevan's dates and the last line contradict the bible.** The
   Characters section has her born 2019 and dead in December 2096. The ending
   has the memo list "Ketevan Kiknadze, nurse, 2041–2090, deceased". Worse,
   Mariam reads it and "asks who that is". Mariam was 13 when her grandmother
   died fourteen months ago. A child that age knows who her grandmother was.
   Fix the dates. Then change Mariam's question to one only a granddaughter
   would ask: "It says nurse. Baba was a nurse?" She knew the grandmother and
   never knew the work. That is the whole story in one question, and Mariam
   becomes a person and not a bystander.

2. **[must-fix] The people who pay are lists, not faces.** Three groups pay:
   the 2,100 yearly waiting-list dead, the 400 shift-swap nurses, and the
   forty clerks and Akinyi's Patagonians. Only Lamara has a face. Nobody on a
   waiting list appears in a scene, so Davit's costing note "2,100 fewer
   deaths" is a number. Put one waiting patient in Part 1 (the committee
   hearing or a district ward), with a name and a relative who minds. Give one
   of the 400 nurses a scene in Part 4. Otherwise the refusal costs the reader
   nothing, and the tone charter asks that someone pay.

3. **[should] The web is told about, not lived in.** The ordinary day is the
   memo, the bus with a cool-bag, and the turn. The unwritten economy that
   makes the whole novum (Lamara's rota, the car-boot stock, the lending ring)
   is only discussed in Part 2 and handed over as a ledger in Part 4. Add one
   gathering scene in Part 2: Davit rides the Marneuli bus or sits a night
   shift on a district ward. Show who feeds whom, who brings the bread, who
   sleeps in the sluice room. Let the carrier-bag shape do some work beside the
   three-filings contest. The reader should miss the web before Davit exposes
   it.

4. **[should] Armen's place is wrong, which is a respect problem.** Marneuli
   is mostly ethnic Azerbaijani, and its Armenian communities are further west
   (Samtskhe-Javakheti). An Armenian Georgian pharmacist there reads as
   convenience. Make Armen an Azerbaijani Georgian (Arif, say) running the
   ring between Marneuli and Armenian pharmacies. That fits the border web
   better, because the ring then crosses two minorities and the nation's
   majority never sees it. The Lamara-Ketevan friendship also needs a second
   woman who isn't a retired nurse.

5. **[should] The change of heart is too tidy.** The bible resolves Davit and
   Ketevan in Part 4 by his reading a ledger. The style guide bans the
   single-scene epiphany. Lamara, who wants to be prosecuted before gazetted,
   hands him the ledger with no cost shown. Make her give it on a condition
   (he doesn't file it, or he reads it at her table, in front of her), so the
   handover is friction. Let Davit then half-undo his reading. He should still
   be a little angry at his mother afterwards.

6. **[could] The invented words are sparing and good, but "unwritten" does
   the most.** The taboo against asking for a favour in writing is the best
   piece of language in the bible, because it says what these people can't say
   without insult. Use that taboo in dialogue, at a supra where nobody will
   put a toast in writing, and cut the tamada gloss. "Supra" keeps its Job. A
   Georgian word for the stance itself would be better than "unwritten", but
   only if it is one a Georgian would actually say. Otherwise keep the plain
   word.
