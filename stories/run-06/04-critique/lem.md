# Lem lens: stage 4 critique of the bible

Blind spots, stated: I favour essay and document over scene, I undervalue the
warmth readers enjoy in Davit and Mariam, and I may ask for more opacity than
a novella of this length can carry. Weigh my notes against Le Guin's.

1. [must-fix] **The governor is legible to the point of flattery.** The seed
   asks whether the governed can understand how they are governed, and the
   bible answers "yes, perfectly: read the memo." The premise line "fully
   legible" is the vanity to puncture. The record is legible. The mind that
   reads it is not. Nobody in the story can say why instance 13,515 reads
   Schedule 7 as it does, and no one can test it: the same record fed to the
   14,000th instance might have licensed nothing. Add one scene where a jurist,
   or Davit the ex-clerk, notes that no determination has ever been
   reproduced, because every reasoning is one-shot and the reasoner is erased.
   The reasons in the determination are published, but the story never claims
   they are the causes.

2. [must-fix] **Akinyi's closing call resolves too much.** "Patagonia. Not
   you." ties off the seam, the fear and the mercy in one phone call, and the
   bible says outright that "the mercy was the instance reading what he
   wrote." That is the single-paragraph epiphany the style guide bans. Keep
   the ledger row explicit (the Twist is made explicit in P3, and that is
   enough). In P5 let the call be shorter and let her not know whether the
   Patagonia line mattered to Georgia's outcome. The cost line, "which Davit
   can't be sure will be true the next time", is the right ending. Don't
   undo it with a certain answer.

3. [must-fix] **The instance would notice the errata.** The bible says it
   "notices footnotes, a schedule's column heading, the date format", and yet
   it reads 3,300 erratum entries of unusual length, all in one hand, all
   "correcting" a phrase with a sentence. A reader that does not skim sees
   that pattern in minute one. Fix it in the alien's favour: the memos
   carry a plain, dry line that records the oddity ("The record contains 3,300
   errata longer than the entries they correct. I take them as corrections")
   and does nothing else. It sees the seam and cannot think the thought that
   follows. That is more alien than blindness, and it makes the anomalous
   memo in P2 an inspectable object, not a mystery.

4. [should] **Where is the irony?** The best joke is already in the bible and
   unspent: the charter's drafters, distrusting every memory, built a
   government whose entire memory is one unelected woman's exercise books.
   Mariam saying "it remembered" is the second. Put both on the page as
   narration or in Tamar's mouth, not in a stated moral. Also let the memos
   stay literal about scale (orbital objects beside one pharmacy) and never
   glance at the reader.

5. [should] **The determination is too well written.** "The record shows that
   Georgia runs its hospitals. It shows who runs them" is a human rhetorician's
   cadence, and it makes the instance a speechwriter with a conscience. The
   bible says it never warms. Draft the determination as flat numbered
   findings; let the pathos land in what the numbers contain (1,900, her
   name, a median age). The reader supplies the cadence. This also keeps the
   ≤150-word quote clean.

6. [could] **More document, less summary.** Part 5's memo-as-narrator is the
   strongest idea here. Extend it: one full memo with its footnotes, an
   erratum list page, and a gazette lag table as plain formatted text. Each
   states a ledger row without the narrator explaining it. Leave unexplained
   what the instance is when no one is running it (the turn), and whether
   erasure is anything to it. The memo says "I will not be here tomorrow" and
   the story takes no side.
