# Banks lens: stage 3 critique, run-06

**Blind spots, stated.** This lens favours sprawl, set pieces and
worldbuilding, and short forms aren't its natural ground. It will tend to
over-credit scale and wit and under-weigh what a 30-40k novella can carry.
Defer to Chiang and Vinge on cutting and on whether the load fits. I judge
plain pitch, moral friction, dirty work, humour, scale against intimacy, and
whether the thing is fun to stand in.

## A. The Morning Record

**Pitch:** holds. A world government that can only know what is written down,
and must refuse a country still coping, is plain to anyone who has met a
benefits office. **Friction:** the best here. The minister has to expose the
pharmacists' ring to prosecution to prove his country is failing, so his good
cause does harm in every clause. **Dirty work:** the clerk's unlawful line,
nine years running, is a person doing wrong for good reasons, and the charter
that makes the governor refuse is the same one that makes help impossible.
Who signed off on the clerk? Nobody, and that is the joke and the horror.
**Humour:** native. A mind that opens each day with "what I understand the
world to be today" is a ready comic voice, and Banks would give the memos
dry wit about orbital debris and nothing else. **Scale:** the commons (air,
orbit, deep sea, grid) is stated. It needs one concrete moment of vastness,
perhaps one memo item. **Reach:** goes well past the household. **Enjoyable:**
yes.

## B. The Speaker's Reasons

**Pitch:** holds, and is the most gripping of the five in a sentence. **Friction:** high, but it
sits in one man's head, and a commissioner judging the statute that
made him is a risk of static deliberation. **Dirty work:** diffuse. Whoever
decided forty years ago to seal the reasons did it for a sound reason and
is nowhere on the page; give them a name and a signature. **Humour:** four
sincere, fluent, incompatible Speakers testifying about one law is a farce
waiting to be staged, if the story lets itself laugh. **Scale:** ten thousand
statutes is felt if the vote is costed. **Structure:** letters from the
past against a present hearing is the Banks braid, and the reveal earns its
place only if the ordering is held back. **Enjoyable:** grim. The mill town
and the gallery of resettled families need lightness set against them.

## C. The Port

**Pitch:** holds, barely. "Machine raised in ordinary homes, then frozen" needs
the why on the page. **Friction:** good. Rounding unfairly on purpose to keep
a ruler is a real, hard choice, and the flip being more just is a clean
irony. **Dirty work:** a convention would sign off on injustice for
continuity, which is honest. **Humour:** the kitchen idiom of the petitions
is a lovely, specific joke. **Scale:** weak. The Concordance is background
and the global question is thin, which is a seed failure. The two timelines
are sprawl, my own vice, and I would cut them. **Intimacy:** the strongest of
the five, with the family ledgers, though the ancestor link is neat.
**Enjoyable:** richly so, but it reads as one nation's heritage tale.

## D. The Empty Set

**Pitch:** holds. The world may do only what every constitution allows at
once, and one clause blocks it. **Friction:** strong. The concord's honest
forecast that it would be better without them is a hegemon being kind in
the cruellest way, which is Banks's ground. **Dirty work:** the pressure on
a small nation is applied by decent minds, and the drafter is asked to
spend her people's protection. **Humour:** the concord that speaks only in
proofs and one plain forecast, against the governor who comes home from
sessions changed, gives dry comedy. **Scale:** best of the set. Frozen
vaccines and orbital traffic are felt daily. **Risk:** specification-talk
lecturing, and "world turns on a small nation" is familiar. **Enjoyable:**
yes, if the amendment drafting stays dramatic.

## E. Equally Authentic

**Pitch:** holds, and Waitangi is a real root. **Friction:** one clean
moral grey: her predecessors chose renderings for smallholders, kindly, and
may have cheated. That is wrongdoing for good reasons, and good. **Humour:**
translation puns, if the story permits. **Scale:** whole world, nicely, with
one language at stake. **Failing:** with no AI voice and the benevolent-ruler
and acceptance questions off in the background, it half drops the seed.
The brief said a card may answer in the negative, not leave it standing in the
wings. At 4,500 words it also runs thin for a novella, and padding it would
be the sprawl I'd normally enjoy.

## Ranking

1. **A.** Friction, wit and procedure are the same machine.
2. **D.** The best scale and the cruellest benevolence.
3. **B.** The best pitch and a good braid, but static and short on lightness.
4. **C.** The most intimate and richest, with the thinnest global answer.
5. **E.** Original, but it half drops the seed and is too light for the length.

**Why A should win.** Every seed question is answered by one mechanism the
reader can hold in a sentence: the governor that forgets, the record it
reads, and the charter that makes help depend on proof of failure. The
procedure is the plot, because the minister's drafting is the action. It
also carries a villain-free moral trap that a Culture reader would
recognise. The governor means well, the charter is sensible, and the
only road to rescue is to hand the helper the means to take over. The
risks are real: two nova and a twist, and the clerk sliding back into the
whistleblower attractor. The fixes are cheap. Keep the clerk's line a fact
the minister uses and cannot publish, and put the governor's memos on the
page early, where they make the world funny and legible at once. D is the
runner-up, and its scale is better, but A has the voice.
