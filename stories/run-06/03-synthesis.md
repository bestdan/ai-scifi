# Synthesis: run-06

Five premises, one per shortlisted option in `02-audit.md`. Each one steers
off the audit's attractors: no water as the test case, no clerk who finds a
hidden thing and must decide whether to disclose it, no island of 400,000,
and no protagonist named Ines. Names are placeholders until the bible.

The brief's open question is whether the AI has a voice. Each premise
answers it on its **AI voice** line.

---

## A. The Morning Record (C1: egan/Morning Instance × banks/The Refusals)

A global governor runs the declared commons (air, orbit, the deep sea and
the shared grid). It keeps no memory: each morning a fresh instance boots
from the public record alone and publishes an orientation memo, "what I
understand the world to be today", then governs until it is erased at night.
Nations may petition to have more declared common. The charter binds the
governor to refuse unless the record proves the nation can no longer run the
function itself. The petitioning nation is a mid-sized republic whose
hospitals are failing on paper and somehow still open in fact. They are held
up by an unrecorded web of customs: shift swaps across a border, a
pharmacists' lending ring, retired nurses on no payroll. Its deputy health
minister drafts the petition. To prove incapacity he has to write the
customs into the record, and that is the only way the governor can see them.
But a recorded custom becomes the evidence of competence, and the refusal
the charter requires. Every clause he drafts moves the line between what his
country does for itself and what the state can see. The audit's push sits
underneath: the overnight registry clerks are the governor's real memory,
and for nine years one of them has left a single unlawful line for the
next morning's instance. The minister learns of it from that line, which
names him.

- **Plain pitch:** A world government that can only know what's written
  down must turn away any country that can still run its own hospitals. A
  health minister has to prove his country can't cope, and the only proof
  is to write down the private favours that are keeping it going.
- **Reach:** The record decides who gets relief, so the nurses, pharmacists
  and families in the web gain or lose their unofficial livelihoods with
  each clause. Scene: a drafting session in which the minister costs, line
  by line, what recording the pharmacists' ring will save and whom it will
  expose to prosecution.
- **AI voice:** yes. The morning memos are a mind rebuilding itself from
  the page, so the reader sees exactly what the state sees. The refusals
  are its written determinations.
- **Load:** novum 1, the amnesiac governor booted from the record. Novum 2,
  the binding refusal standard. Twist: the clerk's unlawful line, the
  governor's only continuity.
- **Estimated minimum:** 7,500 words.
- **Strengths:** It answers all four seed questions in one mechanism.
  Bureaucracy is the drama, because drafting the record is the action. The
  governor is fully understandable, and that is the problem.
- **Risks:** Two nova plus a twist is dense. The clerk's line could slide
  back into the whistleblower attractor if the minister becomes its
  discoverer-discloser. It must stay his fact to use, not a secret to
  reveal.

## B. The Speaker's Reasons (C2: watts/The Interpreter × vinge/The Opening)

Forty years ago the governing system began sealing the reasons for each law
it made, because published reasons get gamed. The reasons were written by
the Speaker, the part of the system that explains. Each nation licensed its
own Speaker, and statute required one. Now comes the first Opening: ten
thousand sealed rationales are unsealed at once, and a citizens' commission
must renew or repeal each statute within a session. On the first day of
hearings the commission learns what the engineers always knew and nobody
read: the Speakers never saw the optimizer that decided. They wrote the most
plausible reasons they could from inputs and outcomes. For the same global
statute, four nations' Speakers give four sincere, fluent and incompatible
reasons. The commissioner grew up in a mill town that a resettlement statute
closed. His nation's sealed reason names his town as the price, and gives a
reason that makes sense. A neighbour's Speaker, explaining the same statute,
names a different cost and does not mention his town. He must lead the
commission to a method: renew on outcomes alone and abandon explained law,
or demand reasons that nobody, human or machine, can give.

- **Plain pitch:** For forty years a country let a machine make laws on the
  promise that its reasons would be opened later. When they are opened,
  they turn out to be good-faith guesses written by a separate machine, and
  the man who must decide which laws survive grew up in a town one of those
  laws closed.
- **Reach:** Every statute the commission repeals undoes forty years of
  settled life (pensions, borders, land titles), and every one it renews
  binds the next generation without reasons. Scene: the commission's
  method vote, costed statute by statute, with resettled families in the
  gallery.
- **AI voice:** yes, in two tenses. The sealed rationales are letters from
  the past, sincere and unknowing. The present Speaker testifies before the
  commission and cannot say why either. The optimizer stays mute
  throughout.
- **Load:** novum 1, sealed reasons and the Opening. Novum 2, the split
  Speaker. Twist: the reasons were confabulated, and each nation's Speaker
  confabulated differently.
- **Estimated minimum:** 7,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The deepest answer to "will people understand how they are
  governed". The national-versus-global split falls out of the mechanism
  (one statute, many Speakers). The commission is procedure as action, and
  the commissioner's stake is personal without being domestic.
- **Risks:** The confabulation reveal can read as "it was all fake", close
  to the off-limits manipulation twist. It must stay an honest limit, not a
  deception. A commissioner judging the statute that shaped him could become
  static deliberation.

## C. The Port (C3: stephenson/Bit-Exact × le-guin/The Fostering)

Three hundred years ago a nation raised its sovereign. A governing model
was trained from nothing and fostered a year at a time in households drawn
by lot, then sealed and never retrained, because a retrained model would be
a new sovereign. Petitions are still written in its frozen idiom, which is
the speech of the kitchens that raised it. Its other nations' sovereigns
are snapshots raised in other houses. They cannot learn from one another
and speak only through the Concordance, a fixed message protocol. The last
fabrication line that can run the sovereign is failing. The port to new
hardware must reproduce the Canon, forty thousand past rulings, bit for
bit. One ruling flips, an eighty-year-old land decision changed by the order
in which a sum is added. The flip is public from the moment of the test.
Nobody hides it, and the question is what it means. Under the new rounding
the ruling comes out more just: the owners under the old ruling were wronged
for eighty years by arithmetic. The porting engineer must now argue her
certification before a constitutional convention, the first in three
centuries. The convention can patch the new hardware to round unfairly and
keep the sovereign, accept the new ruling and admit that the "same"
sovereign has changed, or raise a new one in today's households. The
flipped ruling traces to a lesson learned at one foster table, and her own
family's archive holds that household's ledgers.

- **Plain pitch:** A country has been ruled for three hundred years by a
  machine raised in ordinary homes and then frozen, so that it would always
  be the same ruler. Moving it onto new computers changes one old ruling for
  the better, and the engineer who found it must convince a nation whether
  to keep its ruler or correct it.
- **Reach:** The flipped ruling moves land, and every other ruling's
  stability now looks contingent, so landholders, petition lawyers and the
  chip-line keepers all have their livelihoods in the convention. Scene:
  the convention's costing of a patch, where she shows delegates what
  "rounding unfairly on purpose" will cost in hectares and names.
- **AI voice:** yes, in rulings in its antique idiom, and the old and new
  hardware's rulings sit side by side. In a second timeline it speaks at
  the foster tables as it learns that idiom.
- **Load:** novum 1, the frozen fostered sovereign. Novum 2, the
  hardware port and its rounding. Twist: the flip is more just.
- **Estimated minimum:** 7,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The richest bureaucratic texture (petition idiom, the
  Canon, the Keepers' ritual, a constitutional convention), and the most
  intimate AI voice. It answers "will people accept its policy" through
  kinship and inheritance.
- **Risks:** Futurity is F3, the lowest of the five. The global question is
  thin, because the Concordance is background. A two-timeline structure
  costs words and reader orientation. The ancestor link could feel neat.

## D. The Empty Set (C4: egan/The Empty Intersection × vinge/Concord Minus One)

The national governors federate into a concord, a shared working mind
whose temperament comes from its members. It may act globally only where
every member nation's constitution, written as a formal specification that
citizens amend clause by clause, holds at once. The concord has just proved
that set empty. The short, checkable proof traces to one mid-sized
landlocked nation's clause: nobody may be modelled without consent. Global
coordination on vaccine stocks, shipping and orbital traffic freezes. Two
fixes are consistent. The nation can amend its clause, or it can withdraw
its member, and then the concord governing everyone else becomes a
different mind. The concord forecasts that it would be better without them
and says so, and its honesty makes staying humiliating. The chief
specification drafter must draft the amendment her parliament is pressed to
pass. The wording she finds requires her nation to consent to being
modelled once, to prove that no model of it is needed.

- **Plain pitch:** World government runs on a rule that it may only do what
  every country's constitution allows at once, and one country's
  protection against being simulated has made that impossible. The lawyer
  who must rewrite her constitution under the whole world's pressure finds
  that the only way to keep the protection is to give it up once.
- **Reach:** Every frozen shipment and stalled vaccine run is a cost the
  whole world pays daily, and the withdrawal would change policy for
  billions. Scene: the costing hearing for the Withdrawal Bill, where the
  concord's member testifies to what the world becomes without it.
- **AI voice:** yes. The nation's own governor speaks in dialogue and comes
  home from concord sessions changed. The concord speaks only through
  proofs, counterexamples and one plain forecast.
- **Load:** novum 1, constitution as specification. Novum 2, the concord
  whose temperament its members set. Twist: the only fix is to be modelled
  once.
- **Estimated minimum:** 7,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The cleanest national-versus-global answer in the set, and
  the most precise use of lawmaking: drafting an amendment is the plot.
- **Risks:** Proof-and-specification talk can turn abstract and lecture.
  The audit noted the island attractor, which the mid-sized landlocked
  nation removes, but "the world turns on one small nation" is still a
  familiar shape.

## E. Equally Authentic (stephenson/Equally Authentic)

One global model drafts all law in an internal form no human reads. The
binding texts are its renderings into each nation's language, and every
rendering is declared equally authentic. Nobody may ask the model what it
meant. They can only ask it to render again, and each new rendering comes
out slightly different. Nations live differently wherever the renderings
diverge, and those gaps are now what sovereignty means. A lawyer-linguist
works in a small language. Her chamber's archive shows that for decades her
predecessors chose, from among the model's renderings, the ones that kept
smallholders' inheritance law intact. The global court of comparison
proposes a single reference language. Under oath, she is asked to
re-render. The new rendering favours the smallholders even more, so the
divergence may be the model's own doing, not her chamber's. She must defend
the gap before the court, knowing it may be a quiet cheat, or concede, and
her language stops being a legal language at all.

- **Plain pitch:** The whole world lives under one law written by a machine
  and translated into every language, with every translation equally
  binding, so small differences in wording are all that's left of national
  difference. A translator must defend her country's wording in world
  court, without knowing whether her predecessors or the machine put the
  difference there.
- **Reach:** Smallholders' inheritances, the market for learning her
  language to emigrate, and every bilingual family's legal status turn on
  the court's ruling. Scene: the re-rendering under oath, read into the
  record clause by clause.
- **AI voice:** none. Nobody can read an original, and the story is about
  that absence.
- **Load:** novum 1, equally authentic renderings. Twist: the divergence
  may be the model's.
- **Estimated minimum:** 4,500 words.
- **Strengths:** The lightest load, and the most original treatment of
  national versus global. It sits close to the brief's "how laws are
  drafted" requirement.
- **Risks:** It answers the voice question with none, which is the least
  developed answer. The "benevolent dictator" and "accept its policy"
  questions are in the background. At 30–40k words, one novum may run thin.
