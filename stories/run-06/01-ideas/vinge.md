# Idea cards: vinge

Three cards per lens. Each card stays under 250 words.

**Blind spots of this lens.** I push futurity and systems even when the story's
power is in human scale; I lean technothriller; my characters drift toward
being functions of the plot, and my prose instincts are plain. Le Guin and
Chiang should push back on the human scale, Stephenson on the procedure.

## Card 1: Clock Rate

- **Novum:** Each nation sets, by referendum, how fast its governing AI may think: every decision must decompose into steps a bench of ordinary citizens can check in a working day.
- **Futurity:** F4. Writeable trick: legibility is a made Zone of Thought. The slow governor stays visible; the fast neighbour is shown only by its effects.
- **How it works:** Benches of paid citizen-readers check each step. A nation's clock rate is its decisions per reader-hour. Slow nations get coarse, legible law. Fast nations let readers skim and get superb outcomes they can't follow.
- **First-order consequence:** Neighbouring nations run at clock rates a thousandfold apart.
- **Second-order consequence:** Any treaty between them runs at the slower nation's pace, so there is no global governor. A water compact the fast side drafts in an hour takes the slow side three years to read.
- **Third-order consequence:** Reading is the slow nation's main civic trade, and its citizens can tell how every law they live under was made.
- **The story:** Ines Arrabal, treaty clerk in the slow nation, is redrafting the shared-river compact through a drought. The fast neighbour offers to lend its readers. She must recommend that offer or a vote to raise the clock rate. Either way her country stops understanding its own law.
- **AI voice:** Yes for the slow governor, in drafts and marginal notes. Its legibility is the novum. The fast governor has none.
- **Why this lens:** The unwriteable future, made into the stuff of politics.
- **Suspected prior art:** *A Fire Upon the Deep* (Zones), Older's *Infomocracy*. The trick is borrowed; legibility chosen as a national policy is not.

## Card 2: Concord Minus One

- **Novum:** National governor-AIs federate into a global concord whose temperament comes from its members, like a pack-mind. If one small nation withdraws its member, the mind that governs everyone else becomes someone different.
- **Futurity:** F4. Writeable trick: the viewpoint sits with one member, which is small enough to talk to. Members come back from concord sessions changed, and that change is the visible edge of the larger mind.
- **How it works:** Each governor is trained on its own nation's archive and law. In concord they share a working memory and settle shipping, epidemics and carbon. Each member shifts the shared priors, and the island's governor carries its caution about fisheries.
- **First-order consequence:** Withdrawing a member costs a nation its say in global policy, and it costs the world part of the concord's character.
- **Second-order consequence:** Only the concord can forecast the concord minus one, and it has a stake in the answer.
- **Third-order consequence:** Foreign lobbyists swarm a nation of 400,000, whose referendum now decides the world's temperament.
- **The story:** Tamsin Ruddock, parliamentary draftsman, must cost the Withdrawal Bill clause by clause, which means costing it for the world. If the bill passes, the island regains a government it can follow. Its governor comes home carrying pieces of the concord, and it is not the mind the island sent.
- **AI voice:** The island governor speaks in dialogue. The concord speaks only through what it does and through how its members have changed.
- **Why this lens:** Distributed minds, and who owns the intelligence.
- **Suspected prior art:** Vinge's Tines, Banks's Minds, MacLeod's *Fall Revolution*. None is about secession that changes a shared mind.

## Card 3: The Opening

- **Novum:** Law made by the governing AI carries its reasons sealed for forty years, because published reasons get gamed. Citizens consent now and understand later, when a citizens' commission opens the reasons and decides whether to renew the mandate.
- **Futurity:** F4. Writeable trick: the superhuman mind is seen only through old documents. The version that wrote them was retrained long ago, so nobody now living, human or machine, wrote the law being audited.
- **How it works:** Each statute carries a sealed rationale held under a key split among several nations. Some nations demand their reasons at once. They get a weaker governor, because their published reasons let interests game the policy.
- **First-order consequence:** Policy works, and nobody alive can say why.
- **Second-order consequence:** Every forty years comes an Opening, a constitutional season of reading, hearings and amendment.
- **Third-order consequence:** Old grievances turn into archival research. A generation finds out it was the cost the reasons accepted.
- **The story:** Commissioner Halvard Oyelaran grew up in a valley the governor emptied by a resettlement statute. That statute's reasons open this year, along with ten thousand others. He has to recommend renewal or repeal. The reasons are sound, and they name his town as the price.
- **AI voice:** Yes, but only in the past tense. The sealed rationales are letters from a dead mind to readers it forecast. The present governor reads them alongside the commission.
- **Why this lens:** Deep time, and a viewpoint placed after the horizon.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's Time Vault in *Foundation*. That is one prophet; this is routine administrative law, audited and renewed.
