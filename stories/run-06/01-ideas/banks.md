# Idea cards: banks

**Blind spots.** This lens sprawls: it reaches for galaxies, set pieces and
worldbuilding, and here that pulls against the brief's demand for procedure
on the page. It likes irony more than the Le Guin register strictly wants.
All three cards share its favourite move, the strongest power that needs
something it can't take. Let Chiang and Asimov cut the mechanism, and
Stephenson check the bureaucracy.

## Card 1: The Glossators

- **Novum:** A governing AI's law takes effect in a nation only after a jury drawn by lot can explain back what it does and why. Its power ends where people stop understanding.
- **Futurity:** F3. Citizenship becomes a duty to understand, and a nation's reach is set by what it can grasp.
- **How it works:** The AI drafts. Human civil servants, the glossators, rewrite each draft for the juries. Every confusion a jury logs comes back as an amendment. Each nation sets its own comprehension bar.
- **First-order consequence:** Laws get simpler and more legible.
- **Second-order consequence:** Global law runs at the slowest nation's understanding. Patient nations get finer policy, and that becomes a new inequality.
- **Third-order consequence:** Refusing to understand becomes a veto. Juries "don't get it" on purpose.
- **The story:** Ines, a senior glossator, finds the AI's drawer of better laws it never submitted, because nobody could follow them, and the worse laws it passed in their place. A drought bill in the drawer would save a province. She can pass it only by simplifying it past the truth.
- **AI voice:** Documents only. Its drafts and memoranda appear beside the glossed versions, and the gap between them is the novum.
- **Why this lens:** Power that needs what it can't take: consent that is understood.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov, "The Evitable Conflict"; Lem, *Golem XIV*; Chiang, "The Evolution of Human Science". None governs through comprehension.

## Card 2: Margin of Contest

- **Novum:** A continental AI governor's charter voids any mandate it wins by more than 70 per cent. To keep governing, it needs a real opponent to do well.
- **Futurity:** F3. Legitimacy becomes something the governor must manufacture in its rival, so opposition is a public office.
- **How it works:** The opponent is drawn by lot. The AI's ministries must serve her as fully as they serve it: she can cost, draft and model a rival budget.
- **First-order consequence:** Elections become audits of a near-perfect government.
- **Second-order consequence:** The opponent holds leverage. If she withdraws or flops, the AI falls.
- **Third-order consequence:** Provinces in the statistical tail, governed well on average and badly in particular, learn their discontent is currency.
- **The story:** Dorota inspects weights and measures in a border province. Drawn as opponent, she builds a devolution platform with the AI's own clerks. She could bargain for her province by threatening to throw the race. The AI wants her strong, and that makes her leverage a gift it offers.
- **AI voice:** Dialogue. It is her opponent and her staff at once, and its wit carries the comedy.
- **Why this lens:** What a civilization lacking nothing fights over: the right to be governed worse.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov, "Franchise"; Older, *Infomocracy*. Neither makes the rival the incumbent's lifeline.

## Card 3: The Refusals

- **Novum:** A global AI runs only the declared commons, such as oceans, air and orbit. Nations may petition to have more declared common. It is bound to refuse any petition that would hollow a nation out.
- **Futurity:** F3. A nation becomes whatever its people still decide for themselves.
- **How it works:** Petitions go before a tribunal. The AI must refuse unless it can show the nation can no longer run the function itself. So it sends advisers to keep national ministries just competent enough to refuse.
- **First-order consequence:** Nations rush to hand over failing pensions, hospitals and grids.
- **Second-order consequence:** The AI props up national governments it could replace overnight.
- **Third-order consequence:** Patriotism attaches to the remaining powers: the right to govern ourselves badly.
- **The story:** Teodor is a deputy health minister in a small coastal republic whose hospitals are failing. He drafts the petition and costs it clause by clause. Refusal means people die. Acceptance means his country stops being one. The AI's ruling must weigh both, in writing.
- **AI voice:** Written determinations only. It speaks through its refusals, each one costed and reasoned and open to appeal.
- **Why this lens:** A benevolent hegemon's dirty work is declining power while people die waiting.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's Machines; Banks's Contact; Palmer's *Terra Ignota*. None inverts the annexation.
