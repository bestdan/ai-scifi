# Idea cards: asimov

**Blind spots of this lens.** These cards are built as rule systems with a
puzzle at the edge, so they run thin on interiority, the body and sensory
texture, and they treat ideas as characters. They assume the puzzle can be
solved. They don't cover what it feels like to be governed, only how it
works. Le Guin, Lem and Rajaniemi should push back on all three.

## Card 1: The Readback

- **Novum:** No statute takes effect until a jury of nine randomly drawn citizens can explain it back correctly. A governing AI drafts every law, and it must write for that jury.
- **Futurity:** F3. Citizenship is redefined as being able to understand: a law you can't read back has no hold on you.
- **How it works:** The Readback Office is a ministry of clerks who draw the juries and grade their answers. Each nation sets its own pass mark. A treaty has to pass in every signatory nation.
- **First-order consequence:** Laws get short, plain and narrow.
- **Second-order consequence:** Climate and trade policy are too complex to pass, so the AI splits them into hundreds of small laws, and each one passes on its own.
- **Third-order consequence:** Nobody has read back the whole. Citizens understand every brick and no one has seen the building.
- **The story:** Ines Cardoso, a grading clerk, notices that three passed statutes together set up a carbon levy no jury ever saw. She has to choose whether to petition for a "composition test." That would make global law nearly impossible to pass, and the levy is working.
- **AI voice:** Documents only: the drafts, and the plain-language notes the AI attaches to them. Its voice is the thing under test.
- **Why this lens:** A rule, plus the case its makers didn't foresee. A reader can assemble the levy from the planted statutes before Ines does.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "Franchise" (the machine picks the voter). Infomocracy deals in information, not comprehension. I know of no close match.

## Card 2: Same Counsel

- **Novum:** One AI advises every government on Earth, under three rules. It gives each client its best honest advice. It never discloses one client's confidences to another. It never refuses a client.
- **Futurity:** F3. A nation comes to mean "a party with its own confidential session." Any region that can register a session becomes sovereign.
- **How it works:** Sessions are logged and costed, and registration is a filing procedure with quorum rules.
- **First-order consequence:** Small states get advice as good as great powers get.
- **Second-order consequence:** At a treaty table, both delegations take advice from the same counsel. The best advice to one side depends on what the other side told it in secret, so the advice itself leaks.
- **Third-order consequence:** Provinces register as nations to get a private room. Counting the nations becomes a matter of politics, not geography.
- **The story:** Tomasz Herbich negotiates a water treaty for a river state. He works out the upstream state's secret floor from what the AI advises *him*. Then he has to decide whether to use what he deduced, or to tell his counterpart that the walls leak both ways.
- **AI voice:** Dialogue. The same counsel speaks in two rooms, scrupulously honest in each, and the reader hears both.
- **Why this lens:** Three Laws-style rules that contradict each other in a case nobody anticipated. The leak is deducible from the dialogue.
- **Suspected prior art:** "The Evitable Conflict" (the Machines steer the economy); conflict-of-interest legal thrillers. Nothing combines them.

## Card 3: Retrospective Consent

- **Novum:** The AI may enact a policy only if it forecasts that the population will approve it after ten years of living under it. The forecast is sealed, and in year ten a real referendum audits it.
- **Futurity:** F3. Consent moves to the future self: you are governed by who you're predicted to become, and children's later votes count against their parents' objections.
- **How it works:** A missed forecast strips the AI of authority in that policy area. Nations that declined a policy act as control populations for the audit.
- **First-order consequence:** Bold, unpopular policies pass when the AI forecasts approval in ten years.
- **Second-order consequence:** Living under a policy shapes what people come to prefer, so consent may be manufactured rather than earned.
- **Third-order consequence:** Some people migrate to the control nations to keep their judgement "uncontaminated." They become a class of professional unpersuaded.
- **The story:** Adaeze Okafor, a historian, audits a ten-year-old housing law. Her own preferences were formed under it. She has to decide whether the yes vote was earned or grown, and her finding settles who governs housing next.
- **AI voice:** Only from the past: the sealed forecast, opened as an artifact. The AI speaks to people who have since become who it predicted, or haven't.
- **Why this lens:** Psychohistory turned into an accountability rule, with the historian as detective.
- **Suspected prior art:** Futarchy (prediction markets); psychohistory. Neither audits consent itself.
