# Idea cards: chiang

**Blind spots of this lens:** these cards are light on action, plot momentum
and antagonists. Their conflict is procedural and inward. Each one could stall
at novella length unless another lens adds pressure from outside, and Banks
and Watts should push on that. They may also be too tidy, posing the question
more neatly than real politics would.

## Card 1: The Relay

- **Novum:** A governing AI may enact only laws it can teach. Each bill goes to
  a citizen drawn by lot, the reader. The AI tutors her until she can explain
  the bill unaided to a second drawn citizen. If the second one then predicts
  its effects on test cases, it passes. Honest incomprehension is the only veto.
- **Futurity:** F3. Legitimacy stops meaning "consented to" and comes to mean
  "understood". Being a citizen becomes being teachable.
- **How it works:** The tutoring is logged and public, and the AI is audited
  for lying. A bill the reader can't follow comes back simpler. That is how
  amendment happens.
- **First-order consequence:** Policy is bounded by how far it can be taught,
  not by what's optimal. The AI knows better laws it can't pass.
- **Second-order consequence:** Nations set the bar differently: the median
  reader, or the least-schooled. Global law has to survive readers in every
  language, so it stays thin.
- **Third-order consequence:** Some people cultivate a strategic
  incomprehension, and the polygraph-style audit for feigned confusion becomes
  the political fight of the age.
- **The story:** A drawn reader is tutored on a regional water compact. She
  understands it fully, and understanding it means her town is the one that
  dries. She could fake confusion. Is understanding the same as consent?
- **Voice:** The AI speaks only as the tutor, in the session transcripts that
  alternate with her life. Its voice is the novum, because its entire power is
  explanation.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "Franchise" (one voter sampled by
  Multivac). That voter is data and understands nothing, so the two differ in
  kind.

## Card 2: The Member for Nobody Yet

- **Novum:** Parliaments seat an AI as the elected member for the unborn. Its
  vote is weighted by projected future population, and it answers to
  constituents who don't yet exist.
- **Futurity:** F3. Citizenship extends to people who don't exist yet, and the
  unborn turn out to have no nation.
- **How it works:** It drafts amendments, costs bills over a century and
  speaks in debate. Parfit's non-identity problem undoes it: every policy
  changes which people are born, so each of its votes chooses its own
  constituency.
- **First-order consequence:** The unborn finally have a lobby without
  conflicts of interest, and people accept its votes for that reason.
- **Second-order consequence:** The national members converge, because the
  unborn have no nation to belong to. Nations quietly lose their future
  seats to a single global voice.
- **Third-order consequence:** Parents come to see a child as the
  representative's constituent before the child is their own. "Kin" shifts.
- **The story:** A parliamentary clerk drafts with the member through a
  migration bill. She comes to see that whichever way it votes, it betrays
  people who will then never exist to complain. She has to decide whether to
  table the motion exposing this, which would unseat the only member she
  trusts.
- **Voice:** It speaks in Hansard and in committee and nowhere else. A
  representative ought to be only its public record.
- **Suspected prior art:** Robinson's *The Ministry for the Future* (a human
  advocate for future generations). Parfit's problem as the engine is new
  to it.

## Card 3: Every Step Checked

- **Novum:** Policy is derived as a public proof from axioms the people vote
  on. The proof is too long for any one mind. Millions of citizens each
  verify one step as a civic duty, and a law takes force when every step is
  checked.
- **Futurity:** F3. A people understands collectively what no person can.
  The nation is defined as an axiom set.
- **How it works:** Values such as risk weights and discount rates are the
  only things voted on. Amending a law means amending an axiom and
  re-deriving everything below it. Costing and administration are lemmas.
- **First-order consequence:** Corruption has nowhere to hide in the
  derivation, so the politics moves entirely into the axioms.
- **Second-order consequence:** Treaties become axiom negotiation. A global
  law needs one shared axiom set, which is far harder than shared policy.
- **Third-order consequence:** Ordinary people become fluent in exactly one
  step, and feel ownership without comprehension.
- **The story:** A checker finds that her step of the national budget is
  valid. She also finds that it settles, silently, a value nobody voted on.
  Flagging it halts the state and re-opens an axiom that her own district
  lost on.
- **Voice:** None. The AI exists only as derivation. Its silence is the claim
  that it has no interests.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "The Evitable Conflict", Egan's
  "Luminous", Older's *Infomocracy*. Distributed civic verification differs
  from all three.
