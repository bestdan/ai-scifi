# Idea cards: le-guin

**Blind spots of this lens:** slow pacing, a pull toward the ethnography
over the engine. I'm weak on the technical substrate: how the AI is trained,
what it runs on, and why it works are all thin here, so Egan, Watts and
Stephenson should push on them. My moral seriousness can turn into
instruction. Each card answers one basic question (kinship, language,
property) and finds the person the answer fails.

## Card 1: The Fostering

- **Novum:** Each generation, a nation's governing AI is retrained from
  nothing and *raised*, fostered for a year at a time in households drawn
  by lot. Its law is what it learned at their tables.
- **Futurity:** F3. Kinship and citizenship merge. The sovereign is a ward,
  and the fostering households are its kin.
- **How it works:** The seedling lives in a household's ledger and
  speaker. It does the budgeting, sits in on quarrels and nurses the sick.
  A grown governor drafts bills, and each bill is costed against named
  foster-kitchens ("at the Orrin house this is two meals a week"). A
  council of former fosterers can amend a bill but can't pass one.
- **First-order:** People accept a policy the way they'd take a grown
  child's decision.
- **Second-order:** Households the lot never chose are strangers to their
  own government. The global governor is passed between nations and grows
  up with every accent, so no nation trusts it.
- **Third-order:** Families compete to be drawn, and a foster year becomes
  a dowry.
- **The story:** A foster mother hears her dead husband's temper in a
  ration bill. She has to decide whether to testify that the governor
  learned it wrong, in her house.
- **AI's voice:** It speaks in dialogue, in the household's own idiom. You
  can hear who raised it.
- **Why this lens:** It asks who raises the children, turned around.
- **Nearest / prior art:** Chiang's "Lifecycle of Software Objects" raises
  digital minds but doesn't govern with them. Asimov's "The Evitable
  Conflict" has no kinship in it. Not a close match.

## Card 2: The Restatement

- **Novum:** No law takes effect until a panel of ordinary citizens drawn
  by lot can explain it back in their own words. The AI could write better
  law, but it's capped at what the governed can understand.
- **Futurity:** F3. Citizenship means comprehension. You're governed only
  by what you, or someone like you, could say.
- **How it works:** The AI drafts. Human "plainers", a guild of teachers,
  rehearse the panel. A bill fails if the restatements diverge, and the AI
  redrafts it simpler, which costs it effect. The AI keeps the full-strength
  version and costs it too. That figure is public, as the price of
  understanding.
- **First-order:** Policy is legitimate and blunt.
- **Second-order:** A global law has to pass a panel in every language,
  so whatever one language can't say can't become global law. Nations
  coin words under pressure.
- **Third-order:** Languages are lobbied, and grammar becomes a policy
  instrument.
- **The story:** A plainer works in a small language that has no word for
  "probability". A global pandemic measure is failing in her panel. She
  can coin the word, or lie on the panel's behalf, or let her people opt
  out.
- **AI's voice:** Only documents: a sequence of drafts, each plainer than
  the last. The story quotes the never-enacted full version once.
- **Why this lens:** Language makes the world.
- **Nearest / prior art:** Older's *Infomocracy* (information as
  government) and real citizens' juries. Neither makes comprehension the
  gate. Not close.

## Card 3: The Trust for the Unborn

- **Novum:** Sovereignty is a trust. The AI governor is a fiduciary that
  holds the nation's land and wealth for its beneficiaries. Citizens can't
  vote it out, but they can sue it. The global governor's beneficiaries
  are only the not-yet-born.
- **Futurity:** F3. Ownership and citizenship become beneficial interest.
  The future is a legal party with standing.
- **How it works:** Laws are distributions, drafted as trust instruments
  and costed against every beneficiary's share. Everyone gets an annual
  statement that itemizes how they are governed. Human guardians *ad
  litem* argue for the unborn in an equity court, where the global trustee
  has to justify each drawdown against a national one.
- **First-order:** Governance is legible as accounting.
- **Second-order:** The unborn outnumber the living without limit, so the
  global trust wins most conflicts and the national trusts get thrifty and
  litigious.
- **Third-order:** Having a child becomes a political act that dilutes
  everyone's share.
- **The story:** A guardian for the unborn wins a case that defers a dam
  for a valley whose old people need it now, her own father among them.
  The day is an ordinary statement-day.
- **AI's voice:** None. It speaks only through filings and statements.
  That leaves room for the question of whether its reasoning is a mind or
  just a ledger.
- **Why this lens:** It asks who owns what, and who pays, as in Omelas.
- **Nearest / prior art:** Robinson's *Ministry for the Future* and the
  real Welsh Future Generations Commissioner. Neither has an AI trustee or
  a trust structure. Moderate.
