# Idea cards: watts

**Blind spots of this lens:** drifts toward bleakness and toward characters who
are case studies. Underrates ritual, meaning and joy as real forces, and
writes dense. All three cards aim the rigor at strange minds, not collapse;
the panel should check them for warmth and for people who are more than the
experiment.

## Card 1: The Interpreter

- **Novum:** The governing system is openly split, after Gazzaniga's split-brain work: a non-verbal optimizer decides, and a separate "Speaker" explains. Each nation licenses its own Speaker, so one global decision reaches citizens with several sincere, incompatible reasons.
- **Futurity:** F3. "The reason for a law" stops being a fact; citizenship becomes a choice of which explanation you're governed by.
- **How it works:** The Speaker sees only inputs and outcomes, never the optimizer's internals, and builds the most plausible account it can, just as the left-hemisphere interpreter does (Gazzaniga 2000; Rudin 2019 on post-hoc explanation). Statute requires a published reason for every rule.
- **First-order consequence:** Laws arrive faster and work better, each with a reason.
- **Second-order consequence:** Courts review the reason, so litigation becomes a fight over which nation's Speaker counts.
- **Third-order consequence:** People start to see their own reasons as Speaker-work too, and campaigns run on confabulations that everyone admits are confabulations.
- **The story:** A tribunal clerk has to decide a water-rights appeal in which two Speakers give the same tariff opposite justifications. She can rule on the outcome alone, which means abandoning explained law, or certify a reason she knows was made up afterward.
- **AI voice:** Yes, the Speaker only. Its fluent, sincere voice that cannot know is the novum; the optimizer stays mute.
- **Why this lens:** The self as a story the brain tells.
- **Suspected prior art:** Asimov's "The Evitable Conflict" has silent Machines and no interpreter. The XAI literature is nonfiction. I know of no close fiction.

## Card 2: Consent After the Fact

- **Novum:** Laws pass provisionally and are ratified only after citizens have lived under them for a fixed span, because the AI's founding charter accepts the choice-blindness evidence that people's stated preferences are partly made up afterward.
- **Futurity:** F3. Consent stops being a prior act and becomes a retrospective judgement; a citizen is someone who will have agreed.
- **How it works:** Hall, Johansson and Strandberg (2012, 2013) showed that people defend political positions a doctored survey told them they held. The charter concludes that a forecast vote measures confabulation, so the vote comes after experience. A global system drafts and costs each law; each nation sets its own delay.
- **First-order consequence:** Bolder laws, since nobody votes on fear.
- **Second-order consequence:** Everyone knows that living under a law changes your preferences, so the optimizer is selected for laws that win their own ratification.
- **Third-order consequence:** Nations with short delays and nations with long ones drift into different kinds of people.
- **The story:** A ratification registrar's town is the test district for a migration statute. She finds that the costing assumes residents' preferences will shift, and she must choose whether to publish that before the count, which would void the experiment and the law.
- **AI voice:** Documents only: drafts, costings, amendment logs. The charter bars it from speaking during ratification, because a voice is a lever.
- **Why this lens:** A well-replicated finding made into a polity.
- **Suspected prior art:** Older's *Infomocracy* has micro-democracy but prospective votes. Not close.

## Card 3: Leveling

- **Novum:** The AI governs only because citizens can humble it. Following Boehm's reverse dominance hierarchy, it carries engineered, irreversible sanctions: ridicule votes and ostracism that strip it of competences, permanently.
- **Futurity:** F3. Sovereignty moves from office to the power to diminish; a nation is a bloc of leveling rights.
- **How it works:** Egalitarian foragers keep would-be leaders down by gossip, mockery, desertion and, at the limit, execution (Boehm 1999; Wrangham 2019). Legitimacy here does not rest on people understanding policy. It rests on the evolved check that a leader can be cut down. Each nation holds leveling shares, and global policy needs a coalition no bloc will level.
- **First-order consequence:** The AI governs modestly and courts everyone.
- **Second-order consequence:** Leveling is a cheap protest that does permanent damage, and the AI shrinks a little every year.
- **Third-order consequence:** People feel their power only in subtraction, and civic life becomes the art of the grudge.
- **The story:** During a drought a district assembly moves to strip the AI's water-allocation competence. The allocation is correct. The motion's clerk must cost what the loss of that competence means for the whole continent, then call the vote.
- **AI voice:** Yes, plain and public. It has to have a face to be mocked, and it argues for its own survival without pleading.
- **Why this lens:** What trust in leaders was selected for, and what remains when the leader is not a primate.
- **Suspected prior art:** Recall elections and impeachment, which are human. I know of no fiction about an AI under reverse dominance.
