**Q1. Why can't Georgia simply ask the governor for help with its hospitals?**
- a) The governor takes over only nations that cede every function at once, and Georgia won't cede its borders.
- b) The governor must refuse unless the record proves Georgia can't cope. To make that case Georgia has to write down its hidden coping, the unpaid nurses and the pharmacists, which is exactly what shows it can.
- c) The governor remembers Georgia's earlier petitions, and the new one has to wait out that history.
- d) The petition had to be filed before the gazette closed, and the forecaster says it's already too late.
- e) Can't say.

**Q2. If cession is refused, what follows?**
- a) The schedules are sealed, and nothing named in them is ever acted on.
- b) The 214 ministry posts, Davit's among them, are cut.
- c) The hospitals keep failing, at about 2,100 deaths a year, and the nurses named in the schedules face prosecution anyway.
- d) Georgia's border hospitals are folded into Armenia's commons roster.
- e) Can't say.

**Q3. Why was the governor built to forget everything every night?**
- a) So it could hold no grudge, take no bribe and keep no secret: its drafters distrusted every earlier government's conflicts of interest.
- b) So no instance could be blamed for an earlier instance's decision.
- c) Because of Ulaanbaatar, where a remembered order killed 41 people.
- d) Because the one machine in Nairobi can hold only a day's record.
- e) Can't say.

**Q4. What was behind the memo that seemed to know something not yet in the record?**
- a) The governor secretly carries memory between instances, which the charter forbids.
- b) Tamar's counter-schedule, which reached the record early through the Gazette Office.

- d) Akinyi, a night clerk in Nairobi, writing one "erratum" a night for nine years.
- e) Can't say.

**Q6. What does writing it all down change beyond this petition?**

## 4. Process takeaways (confirmed by the human: "land all")

1. **Stage 4: every world rule needs an in-world reason on the page.** The
   gazette lag had no reason even in the bible; the single machine's reason
   (instances aren't reproducible) stayed in the bible. A bible check:
   "why is it this way, and where does the reader learn why?"
2. **Stage 4/5: the world's shape needs a felt history.** Why one machine,
   why Nairobi, why some functions and not others. The human's question
   halfway through was the question the bible answered only in passing.
3. **Stage 5: an outline check for repetition.** The lenses scored
   structure, but none flagged the same tradeoff recurring. Ask each part
   what new question it raises.
4. **Stage 3: test the AI-voice choice against "does it seem to think?"**
   The memos satisfied the brief's question formally but read as a
   transcript. Consider giving the AI interiority where the premise is
   about it.
5. **Stage 7: the gate misses what the human forgets.** The cold reader
   passed Why and Who's who; the human didn't. Picking beats recall, so
   the gate over-predicts on recall rows and under-predicts on thinly
   dramatised ones.
6. **All drafting: under-description.** The human's standing note: ideas
   given a few sentences read as throwaway, and more colour is wanted.
