# The brief's tone is checked on an opening sample before the full draft

## Context

Run-08's brief asked for "funny and wry". No stage checked a draft against
the brief's Tone: it lived in the brief and the bible's Register, and no
lens question or rubric axis carried it. The signal appeared anyway. At
stage 5 Banks wrote "Wit thins", and on draft 1 Chiang wrote "The wry
teller disappears". Neither note touched a graded row, so no revision plan
carried it. At the debrief the human said "The tone didn't come across as
funny or wry", and wanted it caught "early enough on to catch issues rather
than at the end when it's already written" (`stories/run-08/debrief.md`).

## Decision

The drafter writes the opening scene, about 800 words, before the rest.
A fresh `first-reader` names its tone in three words, and one lens (Banks
for comic, by default) compares that with the brief. If they don't match,
the drafter rewrites the sample once, then goes on. At stage 7 the cold
reader is asked the tone as an ungraded tenth question, and a miss becomes
a revision-plan item.

## Consequences

- Good, because the voice is set before 7,000 words are built on it.
- Good, because a cold reader's words judge the tone, not a lens that has
  read the Register and expects it.
- Bad, because it adds a reader and a lens to every story, and up to one
  extra drafting turn.
- Bad, because an opening can be in tone while the middle drifts, which is
  what Chiang saw. The stage 7 question is the backstop.

## Revisit when

Two runs pass the sample check and the human still finds the tone missing,
or the sample check never once fails.

## Confirmation

Nothing beyond the stage file: the orchestrator logs the check in
`STATUS.md`.

## Alternatives

- A tone axis on the full draft only: rejected, as that is where run-08 had
  the signal and couldn't use it.
- The human reads the sample: rejected, as in proxy mode it shows the
  premise.
