# A partial on a dramatised row passes the comprehension gate

## Context

The gate passed only when every graded row was "got it". On three runs a
cold reader was partial on rows that the page carries in a scene, and the
human, reading days later, got them: run-05, run-06, and run-08, where the
final reader was partial on Premise, Stakes, Twist, Ending and Reach and
the human picked all five correctly. The partials were usually one clause
left out: draft 2's Stakes and Ending each "omit one clause", and the final
Twist answer omitted "teaching by touch" while its line was unchanged since
it passed. Run-08's gate failed every round, fired the loop cap, and spent
round 3, whose success test then failed partly on reader variance.

## Decision

A partial passes when the bible's "Shown in" scene dramatises the row in
the draft, the grader marks the partial **omits** rather than **errs**, and
no lost-list entry concerns the row. The orchestrator, who has read the
draft, judges whether the row is dramatised and logs the scene; the grader
still grades blind. A row the page only states still needs "got it", and a
passing partial stays in the revision plan as a note.

## Consequences

- Good, because the gate stops spending revision rounds on recall.
- Good, because it stays strict on stated rows, where run-06 showed the
  reader over-predicting the human.
- Bad, because "dramatised" is the orchestrator's judgement, and the
  orchestrator wrote the ledger.

## Revisit when

A human misses a row the gate passed as a dramatised partial.

## Confirmation

The grader marks every partial **omits** or **errs**; the orchestrator logs
the scene it checked with each gate decision.

## Alternatives

- Keep "every row got it" and rely on the loop cap: rejected, as it cost
  run-08 a round on three runs' evidence.
- Count every partial as a pass: rejected, as stated rows over-predict the
  human.
