# A term for an office, role or channel is clear from what it does, not from a gloss

## Context

Run-08's "the Desk" is the dispatcher the narrator reports to. The bible
defined it as "a voice with a six-hour lag", and polish glossed it at first
use as "my dispatcher at sector headquarters". The final cold reader still
couldn't tell "whether the Desk is a person", and the human answered "No
idea" (`stories/run-08/debrief.md`). An institution met only through its
messages is a term a gloss doesn't fix.

## Decision

For an office, a role or a channel, the bible's **Clear at first use by**
names what it does on the page at that first use (it answers, it refuses,
it lags), never an appositive gloss. The style guide's rule 3 says the
same, so the drafter and line editor are bound by it.

## Consequences

- Good, because the reader learns the term the way the narrator does.
- Bad, because it costs a few lines of scene where a gloss cost five words.

## Revisit when

A term shown in action at first use is still missed by a reader and the
human.

## Confirmation

Nothing: the stage 7 Glossary check and the grader's Terms row catch a
miss, not the rule itself.
