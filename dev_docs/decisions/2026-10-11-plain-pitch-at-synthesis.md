# A premise must survive a plain pitch at synthesis

## Context

Run-02's premise was chosen at stage 3 on novelty, futurity and load. After
four review rounds the human's first questions in the debrief were:

> Why the heck are they making bells? Why would that benefit from
> intelligence boosting? What's the tension?

None of the panel's stage 3 questions asked whether the premise could be
stated plainly to someone outside the genre, and no later stage raised it.

## Decision

Each premise in `03-synthesis.md` carries a **Plain pitch**: two sentences
to a smart reader outside the genre, with no coined words, saying why this
world does this, why it matters to this person's life or trade, and what
they are torn between. The stage 3 gate rejects a premise whose plain pitch
doesn't hold up, or whose novum doesn't bear directly on what the
protagonist does all day. Banks checks the pitch, and Chiang asks whether a
reader would care about this person in the first page. Downstream, the
bible lists three to five facts the reader needs about the world, the
outline places them in the first third, and each graded ledger row is both
stated and shown.

## Consequences

- Good, because the problem is caught when it costs one paragraph to fix,
  not a drafted story.
- Bad, because a premise strange enough to resist a plain pitch may be
  rejected even when the story could have made it clear.

## Revisit when

- A debrief finds the human couldn't say why the world does what it does,
  although the plain pitch passed.
- The pitch rejects most premises a run produces.

## Confirmation

Nothing beyond the stage 3 gate text; it rests on the orchestrator and the
Banks and Chiang lenses applying it.

## Alternatives

- **Catch an unclear premise at stage 7:** by then the story is drafted,
  and run-02's four review rounds didn't raise it.
