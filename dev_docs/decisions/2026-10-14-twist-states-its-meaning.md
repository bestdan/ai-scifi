# The twist's stated line says what the reveal means

## Context

In two runs running, the cold reader caught the twist and the human didn't.

- **Run-03:** the reader recalled the Tuesday entry; the human read it as
  "Just another entry tbh, I didn't track it was a reveal, more like an
  inconsistency or oddity" (`stories/run-03/debrief.md`).
- **Run-04** (branch `bestdan/story-run-04`, parked): the reader called the
  robot arm's double tap "the best moment for me. My stomach dropped with
  hers." The human: "I don't think there were any big reveals towards the
  end. At least not that I picked up on." The page gave the image ("It
  tapped twice.") and left the meaning, that her own habit now lived in the
  firm's model of a beginner, to the reader.

The bible's rule was already "stated and shown". In both stories the stated
line stated the fact, not what it changed.

## Decision

For the Twist row, the stated line says in plain words what the reveal
changes, spoken or thought by a character. The image alone counts as the
"shown" half. Added to the payoff ledger's rules in
`templates/story-bible.md`.

## Consequences

- Good, because a reader who takes the image for a detail still gets the
  reveal.
- Bad, because a twist explained on the page loses some of its sting for
  the reader who caught the image. The line can come a beat later, after
  the image has had its moment.

## Revisit when

- A debrief again finds the human missed a twist whose meaning was stated:
  the next lever is the reader model (a weaker reader, or two readers), as
  the 2026-10-11 gate decision proposed.

## Confirmation

Nothing beyond review: the drafter and the stage 4 lenses read the bible
template's rule. The comprehension grader already grades Twist on the
causal link, not on recall of the image.

## Alternatives

- **A weaker First Reader model:** may make the gate predict the human, but
  it changes the measure, not the story the human reads. Kept as the next
  step.
