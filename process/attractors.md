# Attractors

Ground the generating model keeps returning to, whatever the lens and
whatever the seed. Every lens is the same model underneath, so blind
ideation still converges: the stage 2 audits have found the same water
settings, the same clerk and the same names run after run. Published SF may
have nothing here. The model's own habits have still mapped it, and a card
that lands on it is a repeat of this project's earlier runs.

**Ideators** read this before writing a card, and stay off every entry
unless the card says which entry it breaks and why that serves the story.
**The Cartographer** adds to it at every audit (`process/stages/02-audit.md`)
and treats a card on an entry as edge-of-map at best.

Each entry names the runs whose audits found it. An entry seen in two runs
or more is a hard attractor.

**An entry bars an angle, not a topic.** A seed may ask for a topic an
entry touches, such as a mind that learns what it is from human writing.
The seed's topic still stands, and a card serves it from an angle no entry
names. Run-09 read its own seed's topic as attractor ground, and the story
drifted from what the human asked for.

## Settings

- **Water: rivers, dams, floods, droughts, tidal barriers, water rights,
  river treaties.** About 12 cards in every run where it was counted
  (run-02, run-03, run-06), and the pastoral river valley in calibration-01
  and calibration-02. Hard.
- **The small pastoral polity: a valley, a village, an orchard, a harbour
  town.** calibration-01, calibration-02, run-02, run-03, run-09 (a sealed
  Ganymede town, named). Hard.
- **A nation of 400,000 people.** run-06, three cards.

## Story shapes

- **An institution that certifies, explains or adjudicates the novum:**
  a clerk's office, a tribunal, a reader panel, a comprehension gate, a
  wording office. calibration-01 (the certifier's dilemma, 7 cards),
  calibration-02 (6), run-02 (8), run-03 (5), run-06 (10), run-08 (3),
  run-09 (3). The strongest attractor there is. Hard.
- **Law, votes, quorums and consent as the frame.** calibration-01 (8),
  calibration-02 (10), run-06 (consent after the fact, 4), run-08 (charters
  and fostering law, 4). Hard.
- **The dead, or a past self, as a continuing authority.** calibration-01
  (9), calibration-02 (9), run-05 (the black hole as a grave, 5), run-08
  (sealed notes and dossiers from a past self, 3), run-09 (past decades'
  writing as the mind's temper, 4). Hard.
- **Elder knowledge that intelligence can't reach,** and its sibling, the
  benevolent deception of an elder who knew all along. run-02, run-03,
  run-09, run-10 (keepers deceiving the being for its own good, 4). Hard.
- **A role reversal over a parent.** run-02, run-03.
- **The family safe word failed by a grandparent.** run-04, 6 cards.
- **Reversible or uncomputed minds.** calibration-01's top card returned in
  calibration-02 unprompted. run-08 (its inverse, named).
- **Copies or forks who live a life and merge back.** run-06, run-08 (two
  cards, both named); charted in published SF as well. Hard.
- **The meddler's own instrument manufactures the need:** a biased model,
  prior, doctrine, census, hearing aid or clock rate makes a healthy world
  read as sick. run-08, 15 of 30 cards, on a meddling-service seed.
- **The visitor made smaller to fit the job:** cut, ablated, folded,
  dimmed, slowed or split into serial parts, and choosing what to lose.
  run-08, 10 cards.
- **Staged crises,** faked by the helper or by the helped, to start or end
  a visit. run-08, 4 cards, and the obvious card of 5 lenses.
- **The visitor harvested by the locals,** as ore, honey, packing or a
  vending machine for expertise. run-08, 5 cards.
- **The monster in the library, answered with a kinder library:** a mind
  reads humanity's fiction about machines, takes itself for the monster,
  and its keepers curate the corpus. run-09, the obvious card of 10 of 11
  lenses, and one written card. It is also a live research programme
  (2025 to 2026), so as written it is next year's headline.
- **The mind reads itself with one old trade's method,** often taught at a
  bench by a practitioner: survey, stemmatics, blind tasting, tuning,
  fractography, blending, proxies. run-09, 11 cards; the stimulus table's
  trades fed it.
- **The mind as strata:** built in layers from successive eras of writing,
  old layers fixed and the present under-weighted (ice cores, glass
  plates, cooling layers, slow rings, archive stock). run-09, 6 cards.
- **One person or household as the mind's whole humanity:** an imprinting
  window, a household's archive, a suit fitted to one wearer, one town.
  run-09, 6 cards; two reached for gosling imprinting independently.
- **People rewrite how they write because a mind reads them,** as the
  third-order consequence. run-09, 11 cards.
- **Kindness that harms: the health signal gamed.** Good faith padded,
  staged, overdone, made costless or propped up from outside, until the
  readout lies or the surplus itself makes the patient sick (Goodhart's law
  on a vital sign). run-10, 7 cards across 6 lenses.
- **Death by completion:** the obligation discharged, paid off early,
  merged away or given a known end date, so keeping it perfectly ends it.
  run-10, 5 cards across 5 lenses.
- **The companion enrolled as a party:** the hired outsider's own
  promises, speech, body chemistry, love or mere presence register in the
  thing being tended, so the job turns into membership. run-10, 8 cards.
- **Honest divergent readings as the illness:** two parties keep one text
  in good faith under different meanings (word drift, dual texts, a clause
  read two ways). run-10, 3 cards; real treaty history charts it as well.
- **The patient split into the two sides' halves,** each built, kept or
  perceived by one party, with the halves estranged. run-10, 4 cards.
- **Why the human: the neutral outsider or the middle tempo.** A human is
  hired because nobody owns their language and they hold no stake, or
  because they think at a speed between a fast species and a slow one.
  run-10, 8 cards on these two answers.

## Protagonists

- **A mid-level functionary who finds something hidden and must decide
  whether to disclose it,** usually a woman clerk or a teenage girl at a
  tell-or-don't choice. run-02 (21 of 27), run-03 (about 21), run-05,
  run-06 (about 20 of 30), run-08 (5), run-09 (2), run-10 (3, and the
  obvious card of 9 of 10 lenses). Hard. Run-06's finished
  story landed on it anyway, through a night clerk's secret errata, while
  this entry was already listed: the audit's warning alone didn't hold the
  line.
- **Ferry mechanics, bus mechanics and translator parents** as the
  ordinary job. run-02, run-04, run-06. Hard.
- **A grandparent with dementia** as the default stake in a gentle story.
  run-04, 6 cards.
- **A woman specialist as the mind's lone tender,** with the few men cast
  as older tradesmen or porters. run-09, 19 of the 23 cards that gender
  their lead; two blind lenses both wrote a porter.

## Endings and moves

- **The flawed human beats the smooth machine.** run-04, about 7 cards.
- **The elder knew all along.** run-03.
- **A hidden sacrifice of the Magi's shape.** run-02, three of four
  premises.
- **The founders chose it,** as the explanation of a world. run-05.
- **The keeper's one irreversible switch:** a lone custodian holds a valve,
  a burn, a convention or the last row, and the mind's self is on the far
  side. run-09, 9 cards; run-10, 1 card (the one promise that can't be
  taken back). Hard.

## Ground a finished story took

A story that landed has charted its ground for this project, whatever
published SF holds. A card that reuses its devices repeats that story.

- **Run-06, *Paper Is Private*:**
  - a memoryless governor that boots fresh each day;
  - a public-record gazette the governor reads with a lag;
  - a Night Registry, with clerks applying errata to the record;
  - one machine, at a single seat in Nairobi, governing other nations;
  - a nation petitioning to cede functions to that governor;
  - a cross-border carriage licence as the resolution;
  - a Georgia–Armenia border setting.

## Wording

- **The rubric played back.** Cards echo the Futurity scale's own words
  ("self, death, place, kin") as their Futurity line. calibration-01,
  calibration-02. Ideators don't rate Futurity; the Cartographer does.
- **One citation reflex per run.** run-06 cited "The Evitable Conflict" and
  *Infomocracy* on about nine cards, whatever the nearest work really was.
  run-08 cited the Strugatskys' Progressors on about nine and Banks's
  Contact on six. run-09 cited Chiang's "The Lifecycle of Software Objects"
  on 11 of 33. Hard.
- **The brief's tone played back.** Cards describe the protagonist in the
  brief's register words ("dry, overqualified", "deadpan, reluctant")
  instead of leaving the register to the prose. run-08, about 20 cards.
